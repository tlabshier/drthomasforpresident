By: Thomas Lee Abshier, ND

It has become common in America for many people to engage in sexual affairs and/or pornography (both of these practices are referred to as "fornication" in the Bible) and/or practice homosexuality. The general tone of the culture has moved toward accepting fornication and homosexuality as alternative lifestyles or relationship choices similar to traditional marriage.

God may indeed judge and hold every man accountable for every idle word, but the consequences and judgment we face in this life are more immediate than divine condemnation at the end of life. God’s judgment and way of Truth permeate the very substance and nature of the universe, and societal beliefs, personal perspective, and common wisdom have little effect on moderating the outcome of a person's beliefs.

God defines right and wrong by His very being, and the creation automatically responds to the actions and hearts of men. His Law is embedded within the very fiber and substance of the creation.

I believe the Bible is God’s revelation of His nature, and revelation of the nature of His creation, and in it we see the sexually free lifestyles as sinful, and producing consequences. And, if the revelation is True, then those consequences will affect both the individual and the nation that refuses to follow God's loving instructions. The Bible describes God as a lawful and just, but desiring to extend grace when there is confession and repentance. But, confession, repentance, and grace is meaningless without the Law defining the standard of Righteousness.

Below are scriptures that give evidence to the Biblical doctrine that God created sex for the purpose of procreating only within a sacred act of marital covenant commitment. Sexuality is sanctified when expressed between a man and woman as they promise and live with sexual fidelity in lifelong companionship.

Some may interpret the scriptures of the Mosaic Law of the Old Testament referring to the penalties prescribed for adulterers and homosexuals as intolerant or hate speech. But, the Law of God is not determined or legislated by man. God has simply informed us about how He constructed the moral laws and consequences of the universe. He understood that sexual purity would create stable and happy families with wise children and hence strong and prosperous societies. In the early 1800s Alexis de Tocqueville said of America: "Not until I went into the churches of America and heard her pulpits flame with righteousness, did I understand the secret of her genius and power. America is great because America is good, and if America ever ceases to be good, America will cease to be great."

God created the human heart. He knew that sexual immorality would ruin lives, destroy the family unit, and seriously degrade the society. We can now observe the outcome of embracing the sexual revolution of the 60s as it comes to fruition in America today. By giving us His Law, God has lovingly warned us that sexual sin has serious repercussions in the spiritual realm that can destroy the fabric of society. It is compassionate of God to try to call us back to Himself by warning us of how serious these sins are in His eyes. Sexual impurity places us in a position of deserving spiritual and physical death. It is for this reason that Jesus Christ went to the cross. His suffering and death were established as an acceptable substitutionary sacrifice for the punishment and death that we deserved.

As Christians, we are under a new dispensation of the Mosaic Law. The sexual sins are still serious in God's eyes, but we are to deal compassionately with the violators of His Law. This new way of dealing with the violator of the sexual Law is illustrated in the New Testament by the story of how Jesus dealt with the woman caught in the act of adultery. He said, "Whoever has not sinned should cast the first stone." When no one came forward to stone her He said to her, "Woman, where are your accusers? Has no one condemned you?" She said, "No one Lord." He then said, "Neither do I condemn you, go and sin no more."

We are to be likewise compassionate to those who are caught in the all-too-common snare of sexual sin. But, just because this is a sin that has beset many of us does not mean that we should ignore or condone it. Rather, we are to be compassionate to those who fall prey to this sin, and we are to help to lead the offender in the ways of purity and righteousness. We are to support, love, and encourage him/her to resolve to sin no more.
