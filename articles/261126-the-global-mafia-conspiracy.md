# 261126 - The Global Mafia Conspiracy

# Neither Fear Ye Their Fear


### Concentrated money power is real. A five-hundred-year master plot is not, and believing in one makes free people weaker.

Thomas Lee Abshier, ND | 26 November 2026


---


This article is based on an interview with Mees Baaijen, a retired veterinarian and author of *The Predators versus The People*. His thesis is simple and total. For at least five hundred years, he says, the world has been run by one invisible government, a cartel of dynastic banking families he calls "Glafia" (Global Mafia). In his telling, Glafia is behind the Reformation, the Industrial Revolution, colonialism, the French and Russian Revolutions, both World Wars, Hitler, communism, 9/11, climate policy, and COVID. It moved its seat of power from Genoa and Venice to Spain, then to Holland, Britain, and America, and it is now moving it to China. Nations, democracies, and churches are only its instruments.

The piece reached me by way of a physician's newsletter, which cross-posted it as "one interesting view of who runs the world." Many good people I know find this picture persuasive, and part of it is true. So I want to answer it carefully: what is right in it, where it fails, and why the failure matters to anyone who hopes to govern himself.

## Where I Stand

I stand with those who say that concentrated wealth has captured much of our public life, and that the people who hold it coordinate more than they admit. I do not stand with the claim that one syndicate has authored every war, revolution, and reformation for five centuries. That claim fails on the evidence, on logic, and on Scripture.

There are real areas of uncertainty. We do not know how much of what we see is deliberate coordination and how much is many powerful men pursuing the same interests by instinct. Private meetings leave thin records, and that thinness cuts both ways: it hides real deals, and it lets a theorist fill the silence with whatever he expects to find. I will keep those limits in view. But the totalizing thesis is not a modest overstatement of a true concern. It is a different claim, and a harmful one.

## The Question Under the Question

What is this theory reaching for? I think it is reaching for things many Americans feel in their bones:


 	- that their vote changes little, because the same interests win whichever party wins;

 	- that wars are fought by the sons of the poor for the profit of the rich;

 	- that the same names sit at every table, whether in finance, media, government, or the "philanthropy" that sets policy without ever standing for election;

 	- that the money system is designed by lenders and for lenders.


None of that is paranoia. Scripture says it plainly: *"The rich ruleth over the poor, and the borrower is servant to the lender."* **(Proverbs 22:7)** It also tells us that rulers do take counsel together against what is right: *"The kings of the earth set themselves, and the rulers take counsel together, against the LORD, and against his anointed."* **(Psalm 2:2)** And it tells us that the struggle has a spiritual dimension that money only partly explains: *"For we wrestle not against flesh and blood, but against principalities, against powers, against the rulers of the darkness of this world, against spiritual wickedness in high places."* **(Ephesians 6:12)**

So the right answer to Baaijen is not "there is no conspiracy." There are many. Men plot, and they always have. The question is whether one plot explains everything.

## Why the Concern Remains Plausible

Here is the strongest part of the case, stated as fairly as I can.

**Ownership is more concentrated than most citizens know.** Three asset managers, BlackRock, Vanguard, and State Street, are together the largest shareholder in 88 percent of S&P 500 companies. Their average combined stake in those companies rose from about 5 percent in 1998 to over 20 percent in 2017, and they cast roughly a quarter of the votes at S&P 500 shareholder meetings. Often the same firm is the top shareholder of competitors in the same industry, such as Apple and Microsoft, or ExxonMobil and Chevron. The scholars who documented this compare it to the age of J. P. Morgan. That is not a theory. It is in the filings.

**Capital does migrate from one center of power to the next.** Baaijen builds on Giovanni Arrighi's *The Long Twentieth Century*, which is serious scholarship. Arrighi traces "systemic cycles of accumulation" from Genoa to Holland to Britain to the United States. The pattern is real. But Arrighi describes the structural movement of capital, which is many actors chasing returns across centuries. He does not describe a single family council directing it. Baaijen keeps Arrighi's map and adds a pilot Arrighi never claimed to find.

**Western money did flow into Germany between the wars.** American loans under the Dawes and Young plans helped rebuild German industry, and American firms and financiers did business in Germany well into the Nazi era. Antony Sutton's *Wall Street and the Rise of Hitler* makes the strongest version of this case. Bankers profit from war, sometimes on both sides, and they have rarely paid for it. That is a legitimate indictment.

**The closed rooms exist.** The Bilderberg meetings, the Council on Foreign Relations, and the World Economic Forum are real gatherings of people with real power who prefer to talk without the public listening. Public officials attend them. Citizens are right to ask what is said there.

Add the money motive, the revolving door between regulators and the firms they regulate, and the unnatural feeling of watching a "consensus" form overnight across every major institution, and you can see why a total explanation is tempting. It feels like it finally names what we have all sensed.

## Where the Thesis Breaks


### It cannot be wrong

The interviewers asked Baaijen a fair question: isn't this an after-the-fact story, where no event could ever disprove it? His answer, in substance, was "this is what I found." Look at how the theory handles evidence. When living standards rose for four centuries, that was Glafia's "bread and circuses," a confidence game. When living standards fall, that is Glafia dismantling the middle class. Suffrage and universal education were "pyrrhic." Population growth was Glafia's doing; so is depopulation. Freedom is Glafia's trick, and tyranny is Glafia's goal.

A theory that explains every outcome and its opposite explains nothing. I hold my own physics to the standard of prediction: a theory earns belief by saying what will happen and being at risk of being wrong. History deserves the same discipline. A claim that survives every possible fact has not been confirmed by the facts. It has been sealed off from them.

### Specific claims that the record contradicts

When I can check the theory's specific claims against primary evidence, they do not hold.

**The Bank of England.** Baaijen calls state ownership of the Bank of England a "fairy tale." The Bank was founded in 1694 as a private, stockholder-owned corporation to lend £1.2 million to the Crown for war with France. It remained privately owned until Parliament nationalised it in 1946. The founding subscription list survives and is public, more than 1,200 names, most of them modest merchants. The Act of 1946 is public law. On this point the claim is false.

There is a fair question underneath it, though: does a state-owned central bank serve lenders more than citizens? I think that question deserves a public answer, and I would ask it of our own Federal Reserve. But it has to be asked honestly, not answered with a myth.

**Cromwell and the readmission of the Jews.** Baaijen says Jewish bankers "bribed Cromwell to let them back in." The documentary record tells a different story. In 1655 Menasseh ben Israel, a rabbi and printer from Amsterdam, came openly to London to petition for readmission. He had already published his case to Cromwell in print. A conference of clergy, lawyers, and merchants met in December 1655 and ended without agreement. London merchants opposed readmission because they feared the competition. Cromwell's own motives, as historians reconstruct them, were theological and economic. Like many Puritans, he believed the conversion of the Jews must come before Christ's return, and he wanted the trade. The Jews were then tolerated quietly, without a formal law. The article offers no evidence of a bribe. The claim runs contrary to the record we have.

### The ethnic thread

Baaijen insists that Glafia is "not bound to any nation, religion, race, or ethnicity." Yet at nearly every turning point of his story, Jewish bankers appear: behind William the Conqueror, buying Cromwell, founding the Bank of England, and, through "Zionist proxies," setting up Hitler. That is the oldest pattern in this genre of writing. It is the pattern of the forged *Protocols of the Elders of Zion*, and it has been used again and again to justify the murder of ordinary Jewish families who controlled nothing.

Whatever a man thinks of particular bankers, or of the policies of the State of Israel, assigning a hidden hand to a people is bearing false witness against one's neighbor: *"Thou shalt not bear false witness against thy neighbour."* **(Exodus 20:16)** Greed has no ethnicity. *"For all have sinned, and come short of the glory of God."* **(Romans 3:23)** The banker who profits from war is accountable for his own sin, and the man next door who shares his ancestry is not.

### The claim of deliberate depopulation

The article claims the mRNA vaccines were a "stealth" depopulation measure. Whether those products caused more harm than was admitted is a real question. I take it seriously, and it deserves its own careful treatment. But "they harmed people" and "they were designed to kill people" are different claims. The second is a claim about intent, and the article offers no evidence of intent. It offers only the inference that a bad outcome must have been planned. That is the same move as everywhere else in the theory: an outcome, then a motive supplied after the fact.

### The theological problem

This is the deepest failure, and the one that matters most to me. The Glafia theory gives to a few hundred families a power that belongs to God alone. In this picture they see everything, plan across centuries, and bend every nation, church, and revolution to their will. That is a counterfeit sovereignty.

Scripture answers it directly. When the people of Judah were seized with fear of a great alliance against them, God told Isaiah: *"Say ye not, A confederacy, to all them to whom this people shall say, A confederacy; neither fear ye their fear, nor be afraid."* **(Isaiah 8:12)** The kings of the earth do take counsel together, but *"He that sitteth in the heavens shall laugh: the Lord shall have them in derision."* **(Psalm 2:4)** And the most proud king of his age learned, at great cost, *"that the most High ruleth in the kingdom of men, and giveth it to whomsoever he will."* **(Daniel 4:17)**

The theory also excuses us. If they script everything, then nothing I do matters, and nothing I fail to do is my fault. To his credit, Baaijen quotes Charles Eisenstein near the end: we have "two enemies: THEY and WE," and most of us are both perpetrator and victim at different times. That is the truest line in the interview, and it undercuts the rest of it. If WE are part of the problem, then THEY are not all-powerful. The failures of the last century were not only imposed on us. Many were chosen by us: the debt we voted for, the wars we cheered, the comforts we would not give up, the God we stopped obeying.

## Why This Matters for Self-Government

A belief in total control does not make people braver. It makes them despair or withdraw. Why vote, why serve on a school board, why sit on a jury, if the outcome was written in Venice in 1500?

But the powers that matter most in a free republic are the ones no syndicate controls. Twelve ordinary citizens in a jury box can refuse to convict a neighbor under an unjust law, and no banker can overrule them. Jury nullification is a cornerstone of liberty for exactly that reason. A local school board answers to the parents who show up. A congregation that teaches its children the truth cannot be bought out. Real power still lies with people who use it, and it is lost mostly by people who stop using it because they have been told it was never theirs.

## What I Would Do

My role as a candidate is leadership by voice: positions taken with reasons, held open to a better argument. I test each one by three questions: Is it righteous? If so, is it loving? Would I want it done to me? Here is what I would urge.


 	- **Give shareholder votes back to the savers.** The shares held in index funds belong, in substance, to the millions of Americans whose retirement savings bought them. Their votes should follow them, not sit with three fund managers by default. Some firms have begun offering this choice. It should become the norm. It is righteous because it returns authority to its owners, and loving because it stops treating citizens as passive money.

 	- **Bring the closed rooms into the light where public officials are present.** A private citizen may meet with whomever he likes. But when a sitting official attends a private policy conference, the public should know that he went, who paid, and what policy was discussed. I would want my own servants to tell me where they were and on whose ticket.

 	- **Ask the money question in public.** What is the Federal Reserve for, whom does it serve, and to whom does it answer? Those questions deserve open hearings and plain answers, not mythology from either its defenders or its accusers.

 	- **Refuse the money myself.** I take no donations. A candidate who owes no one can say what he sees.


## Conclusion

Mees Baaijen is right that predators exist and that ordinary people have been robbed by them. He is wrong that they are gods. The first truth should make us vigilant. The second error would make us helpless, and a helpless people is exactly what predators want.

So I will say what Isaiah said: do not call everything a confederacy, and do not fear what the frightened fear. Fear God, tell the truth about the powerful and about ourselves, and use the power that is still in our hands: the vote, the jury box, the school board, the pulpit, and the purse. *"Sanctify the LORD of hosts himself; and let him be your fear, and let him be your dread."* **(Isaiah 8:13)**

## Sources and Their Limits

My sourcing leans on the mainstream record: the Bank of England's own archive, academic studies of share ownership, and historians of the Cromwellian period. That record is curated by institutions, some of which have an interest in how their history is told. I treat the Bank of England's account of itself as interested-party testimony, but the 1694 subscriber list and the Bank of England Act of 1946 are primary documents that any researcher can check. On the readmission of the Jews, the historical literature is broad and includes Jewish, Puritan, and economic historians who disagree on emphasis but agree on the basic events. If there is evidence of a bribe to Cromwell, it is not cited in the article, and I could not find it. On interwar finance, the strongest dissenting work is Antony Sutton's, and I have tried to present its case fairly. Private meetings among powerful men leave thin records by design, so there may be coordination I cannot see. But an absence of records cannot by itself be counted as evidence for any particular plot.
