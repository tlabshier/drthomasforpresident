# 261009 - A Strong Defensive China Policy

# China: Love the People, Resist the Regime


*A position paper from Dr. Thomas Lee Abshier, ND | 09 October 2026 *


A [recent WND article](https://www.wnd.com/2026/09/we-are-world-war-xi/?utm_source=wnd-news-alerts.beehiiv.com&utm_medium=newsletter&utm_campaign=is-putin-responsible-for-starting-world-war-iii&_bhlid=774a7a54281258203a27a400012ee68a1fa01da8) by Dr. Karen Sigemund and Dr. Bradley Thayer argues that the war in Ukraine is better understood as one theater in a larger contest, and that the principal beneficiary of that contest sits not in Moscow but in Beijing. They call it “World War Xi.” I think the core of their argument is right. That argument has implications for American policy, in terms of what we should and should not do, think, and say toward China. To avoid making bad decisions, we must not misjudge China as a rival in either direction. We must see the Chinese Communist Party as it actually is, and respond in a way that is both strong and righteous.


## The moral starting point


Every position I take on this site rests on one question: would my neighbor feel loved by this act in the public square?


The Chinese people are our neighbors. So are the Uyghurs in Xinjiang, the Christians in China’s house churches, the people of Hong Kong who lost the freedoms they were promised, and the 23 million people of Taiwan who govern themselves. So are the American families who have buried a son or daughter killed by fentanyl made from chemicals shipped out of China. Love for all of these neighbors requires two things that sound opposed but are not. It requires goodwill toward the Chinese nation and firm resistance to the regime that rules it.


The Chinese Communist Party is not the Chinese people. It is a political organization of roughly a hundred million members that has held a monopoly on power since 1949, answers to no voter, and treats its citizens’ conscience as territory to be occupied. Opposing it is not opposing China. It is to take the side of the Chinese who cannot safely oppose it themselves.


## What the regime is doing


Here is what I believe can be shown, and what I believe a prudent American should take seriously.


**It is underwriting Russia’s war.** In February 2022, weeks before Russia invaded Ukraine, Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin declared a partnership with “no limits.” Since then Beijing has bought Russian oil and gas that Europe will not, supplied the machine tools, electronics, and other dual-use components that keep Russian arms factories running, and given Moscow diplomatic cover. Whether Xi gave Putin an explicit green light is debatable. That China has made the war sustainable for Russia is not. Russia is now the junior partner in that relationship, and every year the war continues, the dependence deepens.


**It benefits from the war’s drain on America.** Munitions that the United States sends to Ukraine and the Middle East, and the attention its intelligence services must give to those conflicts, are munitions and attention not available in the western Pacific. A smaller American stockpile weakens deterrence over Taiwan. This is not a conspiracy theory; it is simple arithmetic, and the Chinese leadership can do that calculation.


**It is preparing for coercion in Asia.** China has built the world’s largest navy by number of ships, conducts large-scale exercises rehearsing a blockade of Taiwan, and repeatedly uses water cannons and ramming against Philippine vessels inside the Philippines’ own exclusive economic zone. A nation that intended only defense would not need to do these things.


**It persecutes its own people for their faith and their ethnicity.** In Xinjiang, more than a million Uyghurs and other Muslims have passed through detention camps, and forced labor has entered global supply chains for cotton, polysilicon, and other goods. Christian congregations that will not submit to Party oversight are raided, and their pastors are imprisoned. In 2019, authorities sentenced Pastor Wang Yi of Early Rain Covenant Church to nine years. The leaders of Beijing’s Zion Church were detained in 2025. Tibetans and Falun Gong practitioners have suffered the same pattern for decades.


**It works to shape opinion abroad.** The Party’s United Front Work Department cultivates influence among foreign elites, diaspora communities, universities, and businesses. China has operated unofficial “police service stations” in foreign cities, including New York, to pressure dissidents. These are documented facts, several of which have been established in American courts.


**It is the source of the precursor chemicals behind the fentanyl crisis.** Most of the chemicals used to make the fentanyl that kills tens of thousands of Americans a year have originated in China, often routed through Mexican cartels. Beijing has had the power to shut much of this trade down and for years chose not to use it fully, cooperating only when it served its bargaining position.


## Where I part company with the alarm


The article that prompted this paper also claims that I cannot verify. It implies that the COVID outbreak was timed to a Party declaration. It says the Party interfered in the 2020 election to put President Biden in office. It describes Chinese nationals crossing the southern border as a prepositioned invasion force. The Party’s conduct during COVID was dishonest. It hid information and leaned on the World Health Organization. Border crossings by Chinese nationals did rise sharply in 2023 and 2024, and that deserves scrutiny.


## What I would do


**1. Stop financing the regime’s military.** American savings should not fund companies that build weapons for the People’s Liberation Army or surveillance systems for Xinjiang. I would extend and enforce restrictions on outbound investment in Chinese military, surveillance, and advanced-technology firms, and require pension funds and index providers that hold public money to exclude them. A man should not unknowingly pay for the cell that holds his brother in Christ.


**2. Bring critical production home, starting with energy.** America depends on China for rare earth elements, battery materials, solar components, and many of the active ingredients in our medicines. Relying on a rival for what you need to survive isn’t trade; it is a leash. Independence rests on cheap, abundant energy, because energy makes manufacturing possible. That is why my platform begins with thorium molten-salt reactors. America invented this technology at Oak Ridge in the 1960s and then shelved it. China picked up our research and now operates an experimental thorium reactor in the Gobi Desert. We should not lose the race to build the power source we pioneered. With abundant power, refining, chemistry, and pharmaceutical production can return to American soil.


**3. Hold Beijing accountable for fentanyl, and heal the demand.** Tariffs and sanctions on the firms and officials who permit precursor exports are justified, and Beijing should make cooperation a condition of any trade relief. But no foreign policy will save a nation whose people are reaching for oblivion. The deeper answer is moral and spiritual renewal at home: families, churches, and communities that give a young man a reason to live.


**4. Deter war by being ready for it.** The surest way to keep peace in the Pacific is to make aggression plainly unprofitable. That means rebuilding our munitions stockpiles and the industrial capacity to make them, strengthening our partnerships with Japan, the Philippines, Australia, India, and South Korea, and helping Taiwan become hard to conquer. Deterrence fails when an adversary believes we are weak or distracted. It succeeds when he believes we are strong and attentive.


**5. Resist influence operations without abandoning due process.** Organizations acting for the Chinese state should register as foreign agents, and universities should disclose foreign funding, close Confucius Institutes, and end similar programs under Party control. Unofficial police stations and harassment of dissidents should be prosecuted vigorously. All of this must be done through law, evidence, and courts, never through suspicion based on race or national origin.


**6. Stand with the persecuted.** I would enforce the ban on goods made with Uyghur forced labor, speak the names of imprisoned pastors and dissidents in public diplomacy, and keep America’s doors open to those who flee religious and political persecution in China. Refugees from the Party are among the best witnesses against it.


**7. Keep the lines open.** Firmness is not hostility toward the Chinese nation. I would maintain direct military communication with Beijing to prevent accidents from becoming wars, keep ordinary trade in non-strategic goods, and welcome Chinese visitors and scholars who come in good faith. The Cold War with the Soviet Union ended without a direct war between the two powers in part because each side kept talking. We should do the same.


## The strongest objections


Some will say this is a recipe for a new Cold War that could turn hot. I take that seriously. My answer is that weakness invites war more reliably than strength does, and that every measure above is defensive: none requires us to attack anyone, and the seventh is designed specifically to keep a confrontation from becoming a catastrophe.


Others will say that bringing production home will raise prices. In the short term it will, for some goods. I believe the price of independence is worth paying, and that abundant energy will bring those costs down over time. But this is a real cost, and Americans deserve to hear it plainly rather than be promised that security is free.


Others will argue that the Party is less unified and less capable than it looks, with a shrinking population, heavy local debt, and a slowing economy, and that America should be patient rather than alarmed. There is truth in this. A weakening rival can also be more dangerous, tempted to act before its window closes. Patience and preparation are not opposites.


I hold these positions because I think the reasons support them, and I’m open to a better argument. If you think I have the balance wrong, I want to hear why.


## Conclusion


The Chinese Communist Party is the most capable adversary the United States has faced in my lifetime. We should respond with clear sight, not fear or exaggeration. We should cut off its access to our money, end our dependence on its factories, deter its aggression, expose its influence by lawful means, and stand openly with those it persecutes. And through all of it, we should remember that the people of China are not our enemies. One day the Party will be gone, as every tyranny eventually is. When that day comes, I want the Chinese people to remember that America was on their side.
