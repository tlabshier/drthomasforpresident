# 261103 - David Kupelian's Election-Day Letter

# Given Over: A Response to David Kupelian’s Election-Day Letter


*Dr. Thomas Abshier · 3 November 2026*


**Resource:** David Kupelian, Editor in Chief, WND News Center, donor letter to readers dated 3 November 2026 (WorldNetDaily, [wnd.com](https://www.wnd.com/)).


---


## The letter


On the morning of the 2026 midterm election, David Kupelian of WorldNetDaily sent his readers a letter asking a question millions of Americans are asking: why does so much of our public life look like madness? He lists the evidence as he sees it: Democrats who seem to favor illegal aliens over citizens, repeated attempts on the President’s life by people who had been told he was Hitler, the charge that ordinary Americans are racists, abortion defended to the last moment of pregnancy, children steered toward chemical and surgical “transition,” law enforcement called the Gestapo, noncitizens voting, Satanic after-school clubs in public schools, and a press that covers for all of it.


He grants the usual explanations — hatred of Trump, woke ideology, emotional thinking, contempt for America, university indoctrination — and then offers what he calls the one explanation beneath them all. He quotes Romans 1, where Paul describes people who suppress what they know of God and are “given over” by Him to their own disorder, and he quotes Aleksandr Solzhenitsyn: “Men have forgotten God; that’s why all this has happened.” He closes by naming the news media as the culprit that let the Left escape accountability, and asks for donations to keep WND alive.


I stand with Mr. Kupelian on his central claim, and I would go further than he did. The disorder we are living through has a spiritual root, and Romans 1 describes it as accurately as any text ever written. But what he sees in candidate after candidate is not a scattering of personal failures. It is written down. One of America’s two governing parties has adopted, as its formal platform, the approval of the very practices Romans 1 names as the marks of a people given over. That is the point of this essay. A few of his particulars overstate the details, and I will note them. None of them touches the thesis.


## The question under the question


Underneath the list of outrages is a simpler question: *Is there a moral cause for what is happening to us, and can it be reversed?*


The answer to both halves is yes. The cause is that a large part of the nation has chosen to set aside what it knows of God, and has organized that choice into a political program that it votes into law. The remedy is repentance, and it begins with the people who call themselves by God’s name and have lent that name to the program.


## What Romans 1 says


Paul’s diagnosis runs in three steps. People know something of God from the creation itself. They suppress that knowledge because it demands something of them. And so God, three times in the chapter, “gave them up” (Romans 1:24, 26, 28) — to impurity, to dishonorable passions, and finally to a mind that can no longer reason morally:

>
“And even as they did not like to retain God in their knowledge, God gave them over to a reprobate mind, to do those things which are not convenient.” (Romans 1:28, KJV)


This is judgment by consent. God does not have to strike a rebellious people; He only has to let go.


Then Paul names the last and deepest step:

>
“Who knowing the judgment of God, that they which commit such things are worthy of death, not only do the same, but have pleasure in them that do them.” (Romans 1:32, KJV)


Doing wrong is the condition of every man. Approving wrong — celebrating it, protecting it, funding it, and calling those who object hateful — is the mark of the reprobate mind. That is what a party platform is. It is approval, written down, debated, and adopted by vote. A platform is not a slip or a weakness of the flesh. It is a public declaration of what a party intends to make law.


## The platform, plank by plank


Exaggeration is lying by proportion, so I will state the platform as written. The 2024 Democratic platform calls for restoring and enshrining a legal right to abortion. It pledges to repeal the Hyde Amendment so that federal tax dollars can pay for abortion. It promises to protect “gender-affirming care” and to oppose state bans on it, including bans for minors. It pledges to pass the Equality Act, which would make sexual orientation and gender identity protected classes under federal civil-rights law. And it commits the party to codifying same-sex marriage in federal law.


Each of these inverts something Scripture plainly teaches.


- God forms the child in the womb (Psalm 139:13–16) and hates “hands that shed innocent blood” (Proverbs 6:16–17). The platform makes the shedding of that blood a right and asks every taxpayer to pay for it.

- God “made them male and female” (Genesis 1:27; Matthew 19:4). The platform protects the medical alteration of children to deny it.

- God defined marriage as one man and one woman becoming one flesh (Genesis 2:24; Matthew 19:5–6). The platform seeks to redefine it in federal law, and the party celebrates that redefinition with an annual month of pride.


A platform that sets out to protect and fund the opposite of each of these is not neutral toward God’s order. It is organized against it. Romans 1:32 is not an analogy for this platform. It is a description of it.


This stand and precept is central to everything I teach. God does not watch human life from outside, like a judge in a gallery. He is self-existent; nothing exists outside Him; and He lives every person’s life from within. In a real sense, we are God’s experience of the world. That is why His law is not arbitrary: He keeps it, and asks us to keep it, because He feels every choice we make from the inside. God feels the sin of the abortionist and the agony of the child. He also feels the confusion of the teenager who has been told his body is wrong. He feels the injustice of the person who ran for office to serve, and won the election in the votes but lost in the counting. A law that blesses the killing of a child is not an abstraction to Him. He lives it, a million times a year.


## Christian and Democrat


Here I must say plainly what Christians have been too timid to say to their friends: you cannot call yourself a Democrat and a Christian without contradicting yourself. To hold Jesus as Lord is, at the very least, to hold His standards as your own, even when you fail to meet them. To lend your name, your vote, and your loyalty to a party that has organized itself against those standards is to say, in effect, “Lord, everything You intend to establish in Your creation is wrong, and we will establish the opposite.” Jesus warned against exactly this:

>
“Not every one that saith unto me, Lord, Lord, shall enter into the kingdom of heaven; but he that doeth the will of my Father which is in heaven.” (Matthew 7:21, KJV)


I cannot see any man’s heart, and I do not pronounce on his soul. God will sort out each individual’s judgment. But I can read what a man stands for by the loyalty he expresses, and a vote expresses loyalty. A Democrat in Congress votes with his party when these issues come to the floor. A citizen who votes for him has, knowingly or not, put his hand to that vote.


The usual defense is that the Democratic Party cares for the poor, the homeless, the environment, the immigrant. Scripture cares for the poor too. But God has already answered the man who offers good works with bloody hands:

>
“And when ye spread forth your hands, I will hide mine eyes from you: yea, when ye make many prayers, I will not hear: your hands are full of blood. Wash you, make you clean; put away the evil of your doings from before mine eyes; cease to do evil; learn to do well; seek judgment, relieve the oppressed, judge the fatherless, plead for the widow.” (Isaiah 1:15–17, KJV)


Notice the order. Cease to do evil first; then relieve the oppressed. Tax policy, housing, and clean water do not outweigh the blood of a million children a year. To rank them above it is to miss the proportion and gravity of the offense in supporting what God hates.


Nor can confusion be offered as an excuse that God will overlook. God is holy. He does not admit sin into His Kingdom, whether it is committed in malice or in fashion. To stand against His nature and expect Him to wink at it is to presume on His mercy, and mercy presumed upon is not mercy received. The mercy God offers comes through repentance and the blood of His Son, not through a quiet hope that He will not mind.


A nation that votes these practices into law is choosing, in Moses’s words, between blessing and curse: *“I have set before you life and death, blessing and cursing: therefore choose life, that both thou and thy seed may live”* **(Deuteronomy 30:19)**. To impose the platform on the whole of society is to call down the curse on one’s own people.


## On judging


Some will answer, “Judge not.” But Jesus’s warning in Matthew 7 is about hypocrisy, not about silence. He goes on: “*first cast out the beam out of thine own eye; and then shalt thou see clearly to cast out the mote out of thy brother’s eye”* **(Matthew 7:5)**. The point is to see clearly and then to judge rightly. Elsewhere He commands it: *“Judge not according to the appearance, but judge righteous judgment”* **(John 7:24)**. Paul rebuked the Corinthians for refusing to judge sin within the church **(1 Corinthians 5:12)**. Much of the New Testament is instruction in judging rightly.


To call a sin a sin, when it is a sin, does not condemn the speaker. It condemns the speaker by his own words if he practices what he condemns. And it is an act of love: *“Thou shalt not hate thy brother in thine heart: thou shalt in any wise rebuke thy neighbour, and not suffer sin upon him”* **(Leviticus 19:17)**. If my friend is lending his name to what God hates, the loving thing is to tell him, plainly and without contempt. I would want the same done for me.


## The Republican Party


The Republican Party has fallen short of the glory of God; so has every man in it. There is a difference between a man who holds God’s standard and fails it and a man who holds the opposite standard and succeeds at it. The first needs repentance and grace. The second needs to change sides. Works matter, but they do not save; only repentance and the acceptance of Christ’s sacrifice do. But works testify to a man’s commitment and alliance to His Lordship.


But that difference holds only as long as the Republican Party actually holds the standard, and it has been slipping. The 2024 Republican platform softened its abortion language, leaving the question to the states and dropping its long-standing call for federal protection of unborn life, and it softened its language on marriage. That retreat is to be opposed with full force. A party that claims God’s standard and then quietly lowers it is on the road the other party has already traveled. Christian voters should hold the Republican Party to the standard it claims, and should advocate, lobby, and organize to embed the principles of Biblically revealed Godliness in its platform.


## Where the letter overstates the details


My editorial rule is to grade each claim by the kind of evidence behind it. Mr. Kupelian’s thesis stands, but several of his particulars need correction, and a careful argument is stronger without them.


**Abortion.** He is right that abortion has gone *up* since *Dobbs*. The Society of Family Planning’s #WeCount project counted about 1.13 million abortions in the U.S. healthcare system in 2025, above the pre-*Dobbs* level, nearly three in ten by telehealth, mostly mailed pills. In states with total bans, nearly all abortions came by mail from doctors in other states protected by “shield laws,” and Texas and Louisiana recorded more abortions in 2025 than before *Dobbs*. Pro-life organizations cite the same figures. “Full term” needs proportion: federal data show about one percent of abortions occur at or after 21 weeks, and abortions literally at full term are vanishingly rare. But the substance of his charge stands, because the party’s position is to oppose any federal limit at all.


**Children and “transition.”** Here his concern is fully supported. England’s Cass Review (2024) and a U.S. Department of Health and Human Services review (2025) both found the evidence for medical transition of minors weak, and in 2025 the Supreme Court upheld Tennessee’s ban in *United States v. Skrmetti*. Sterilizing a confused child is not loving.


**Immigration and noncitizen voting.** Not quite all Democrats: the Laken Riley Act of January 2025, requiring detention of illegal immigrants charged with theft or violent crimes, passed with the votes of dozens of House Democrats and twelve Senate Democrats. On noncitizen voting, audits so far find the problem real, but documented cases in official records are small. When Texas checked 18 million registered voters against the federal SAVE database in 2025, it flagged 2,724 possible noncitizens — under two hundredths of one percent — and later review found that many of those flagged were citizens. Alabama found about two dozen noncitizens who had voted; Maricopa County, Arizona, confirmed eight over more than a decade. Every illegal vote cancels a citizen’s vote and should be prosecuted. Verifying citizenship at registration is a reasonable reform. Likewise, verifying ID, using paper ballots to allow audits, counting immediately after polls close, and updating and verifying voter rolls are important for election integrity, and in-person (vs. mail-in) ballots make vote manipulation more difficult.


**Violence.** The attempts on President Trump’s life are real and repeated: Butler, Pennsylvania, in July 2024; the West Palm Beach golf course in September 2024, whose would-be assassin wrote that Trump was a threat to democracy; an armed man killed at Mar-a-Lago in February 2026; and gunfire at the White House Correspondents’ Dinner in April 2026. In September 2025, Charlie Kirk was assassinated while speaking at a Utah university. Comparing a political opponent to Hitler tells unstable men that killing him would be heroic. Political violence has struck on more than one side — Minnesota legislator Melissa Hortman, a Democrat, was murdered with her husband in June 2025. It is tragic when a political figure is murdered


**“Liberals think emotionally, conservatives logically.”** The research usually cited on this question does not measure what it is taken to measure. Peter Ditto’s 2019 meta-analysis of 51 experiments, the largest test of the question, measured partisan bias — how much people favor evidence that supports their own side — and found it on both sides to a similar degree. It did not measure emotion, and it could not tell whether an emotion was warranted. That is the real question. Anger at a platform that funds the killing of children is not a failure of reason; it is reason working as it should, as grief is the right response to a death. Jesus looked on the hard-hearted “with anger” (Mark 3:5), and Paul wrote, “Be ye angry, and sin not” (Ephesians 4:26). The difference that matters is what happens when the reasoning is put on the table; there, the evidence leans toward Mr. Kupelian’s experience. In a 2021 survey by the American Enterprise Institute’s Survey Center on American Life, 28 percent of liberals said they had ended a friendship over politics, against 10 percent of conservatives. In a 2020 Cato Institute survey, 77 percent of conservatives said the political climate kept them from saying what they believe, against 42 percent of strong liberals, and half of strong liberals supported firing a business executive for donating to Donald Trump. Many conservatives know the pattern firsthand: raise the Democratic platform calmly, oppose it with reasons, and the conversation ends in unfriending, shunning, or being disfellowshipped. When an issue cannot be discussed without ending the relationship, no one can reach across the aisle. A test that counts emotion on both sides without asking what the emotion is about misses its significance entirely.


**After School Satan Clubs.** These exist, run by The Satanic Temple, which opens clubs only where a Christian Good News Club already meets, using the equal-access rule Christians won in *Good News Club v. Milford Central School* (2001). Enrollment has been small, and at least one current reference source reports none operating in the 2026–27 school year; I could not confirm his figure of eight states. The mockery is real; the answer is to make our own clubs so plainly good that the mockery has nothing to reflect.


**The press.** Here the public is with him. Gallup found in 2025 that only 28 percent of Americans trust the mass media to report fully, accurately, and fairly — the lowest in the poll’s history — and only 8 percent of Republicans. Jefferson was right that a free press is the security of a free people, and that principle protects WND as much as any outlet.


## Applying the test


I evaluate public acts with three questions. First, is it righteous — does it match what God has established? Second, if so, is it loving? Third, would I want it done to me — measured not by my momentary appetite but by the self I am trying to become before God?


The Democrat platform fails the first question on every plank set out above, and so it never reaches the second. Its defenders call it loving — love for women, for the confused, for those who wish to marry as they please — but a love that requires the death of a child or the sterilization of a teenager is not love. And the third question answers itself: if I were the child, I would not want it done to me.


Telling a Christian friend that he cannot stand with that platform and stand with Christ passes all three. It is righteous, because it is true. It is loving, because it warns him before the judgment rather than after. And if I were the one lending my name to the opposite of God’s will, I would want a friend to tell me.


## Why the concern is well-founded


**The sense of unnaturalness is real.** Practices that would have been unthinkable to nearly every American a generation ago — that a man may marry a man, a woman marry a woman, that a man may become a woman by declaration, that a doctor may mail abortion pills across state lines in defiance of local law, that a child may be put on a path to sterility before he can drive — are now defended as settled rights. When a culture loses the ability to see what was obvious to every prior generation, “a reprobate mind” is the right description.


**Money is at work.** Abortion pills by mail are a business. Pediatric gender medicine became a revenue line for clinics. Where money flows toward a behavior, the behavior grows, and those who profit fund the politicians who protect them. Consideration of the place money holds in motivating action is to examine the ecology of sin. When sin is monetized, the lust for money is added to the sin of murder for convenience, the sin of sexuality without commitment, all of which are aspects of a society without the love of God and giving Him the pleasure of living His Way. Money provides another incentive to sin.


**Doctrine leads to law, and law leads to behavior.** The doctrine at the center of our moment is that the self defines reality — that identity, life, and truth are whatever the individual declares. That doctrine produced the platform; the platform produced the law; and a population raised under the law acts on it. Tracing the policies back to the doctrine, and the doctrine back to the forgetting of God, is not paranoia. It is the argument of Romans 1.


**Solzhenitsyn saw it coming.** Mr. Kupelian’s quotation comes from Solzhenitsyn’s 1983 Templeton Prize address in London, where he repeated a judgment he had heard from old people in his childhood. In that address, he warned that the West was arriving by its own road at the same place the Soviet Union had reached, and for the same reason: men had forgotten God. In a two-party republic, the forgetting has a platform, and the platform has a party.


## What a president can do


A president cannot repeal Romans 1. He can lead by voice. He can name the platform for what it is, from the most visible platform in the nation, and call Christians to choose between their party and their Lord. He can hold his own party to the standard it claims and oppose its retreat. He can protect children from irreversible medical harm, verify citizenship at registration with a human check before any voter is removed, and prosecute illegal votes. He can defend a free press. And he can say plainly that America’s renewal begins in its churches, with people who know God and must stop lending their names to what He hates.


## Election Day


Today Americans vote, and they should, because party affiliation matters and the choice is real. But no election alone will reverse the process Paul describes. Romans 1 ends with a reprobate mind — in the Greek, a mind that has failed the test. The answer comes eleven chapters later: *“be not conformed to this world: but be ye transformed by the renewing of your mind”* **(Romans 12:2)**. The given-over mind is answered by the renewed mind, and the renewed mind is given, one person at a time, to anyone who repents and turns back to the God who has been living his life with him all along.


That is the argument as I see it. I hold it open to a better one.

**Limits.** This essay leans on the mainstream record — government data, polling firms, academic journals, and major news outlets — and that record is curated by institutions whose trustworthiness is part of what Mr. Kupelian is questioning. The platform summaries here come from secondary reporting by outlets on both sides of the issues; they agree on what the platform says, but readers should check the platform text itself. WND’s accounts of being deplatformed are its own testimony, treated here as credible leads. Scholars contest the partisan-bias research. If a reader has primary evidence that changes any of these judgments, I want to see it.
