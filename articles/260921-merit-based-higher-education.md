# One Measure for Every Applicant

## A Position Paper on Race, Merit, and Federal Support of Universities

*Dr. Thomas | September 21, 2026*

*Written in response to Jarrett Stepman, "The New York Times Accidentally Revealed Why Higher Ed Won’t Get Rid of DEI," The Daily Signal, September 20, 2026 ([link under Resources](https://www.dailysignal.com/2026/09/20/why-colleges-deserve-to-lose-federal-support-over-dei/?utm_source=TDS_Email&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=the-new-york-times-accidentally-revealed-why-higher-ed-won-t-get-rid-of-dei&_bhlid=4cbd5098ccf9b0e777b8b44ec822e10efda72cda)).*

---

## The Occasion

On September 16, the New York Times published an opinion essay by Miriam Pawel defending the University of California's 2020 decision to drop the SAT from admissions. Her case: applications from high-achieving Black and Latino students rose, the campuses became more selective, and students are graduating at record rates. She concedes that many students now arrive without basic math skills, and she blames K-12 schools.

Four days later, Jarrett Stepman answered in The Daily Signal. He reads the essay as a confession: universities know the standard has fallen and do not care, because racial outcomes matter more to them than merit. He concludes that universities will never reform themselves, and he applauds the Treasury Department's September 3 proposal to remove federal tax-exempt status from private schools that use race in admissions, scholarships, or other programs.

I agree with Mr. Stepman's central principle and with much of his remedy. I disagree with his proportions. This paper states the moral ground first, then the facts, then the law, then what I would do.

---

## The Moral Foundation

Every policy I propose must pass one test: would my neighbor feel loved by this act in the public square? In this matter, there are three neighbors.

**The applicant who is passed over.** A young man who has done the work and is turned away because of his ancestry has been told that he is a category and not a person. He did not choose his ancestors. He wronged no one. He has been made to pay a debt that is not his. He will not feel loved, and he will be right.

**The applicant who is admitted under a lowered bar.** This is the neighbor most often forgotten. To place a student in a program for which he has not been prepared is not kindness. It sets him beside classmates who are years ahead of him, hands him a debt, and attaches to his degree a question he did not earn and cannot remove. Every graduate of his group, including the ones who would have been admitted under any standard, inherits the same question. An honest measurement, given early, with real help offered, is the loving act. A flattering measurement is not.

**The neighbor downstream.** The patient, the client, the man driving across the bridge. He depends on the diploma meaning what it says.

Scripture states the principle without partiality in either direction:* "Thou shalt not respect the person of the poor, nor honour the person of the mighty: but in righteousness shalt thou judge thy neighbour"* **(Leviticus 19:15)**. And again: *"A false balance is abomination to the LORD: but a just weight is his delight"* **(Proverbs 11:1)**. One weight, one measure, for every man. That is the standard. It forbids favoring the powerful. It equally forbids tilting the scale for the weak, because a tilted scale is a false scale no matter whom it favors.

The compassion behind these programs is real. The injuries of history were real, and the present gap in preparation is real. My disagreement is with the remedy. A wrong done to one man because of his race is not repaired by a wrong done to another man because of his.

---

## What Is True in Mr. Stepman's Article

**The university itself documents the decline in preparation.** In November 2025, a joint faculty and administration working group at UC San Diego reported that the number of entering freshmen whose math skills fall below middle-school level had risen nearly thirtyfold between 2020 and 2025, to roughly one in eight of the entering class. A quarter of the students placed in the remedial course had a 4.0 grade average in high school math. The report names several causes: pandemic learning loss, grade inflation, and changes in admissions. With grades inflated and the common test removed, the admissions office lost the one instrument that did not depend on the applicant's high school's generosity.

**The university's own faculty advised against dropping the test.** The Regents overruled a faculty task force that had recommended keeping it.

**A graduation rate is not independent evidence.** The institution that awards the degree also sets the standard for the degree. When the body being graded is also the grader, a record graduation rate tells us little. The placement exam is the outside check, and the outside check is the one that failed.

**Evasion of the law is a real concern.** In *Students for Fair Admissions v. Harvard* (2023), the Supreme Court held that race-based admissions violate the Equal Protection Clause and Title VI of the Civil Rights Act. The Court allowed an applicant to write about how race has affected his own life, as evidence of his character, and warned that essays may not become a back door to the forbidden practice. What may not be done directly may not be done indirectly. When schools relabel programs or choose measures to achieve a racial result, they defy the law.

---

## Where the Article Is Out of Proportion

Hyperbole is a lie told by proportion. An argument that is right in principle is weakened when its facts are stretched, and I would rather correct a friend's argument than repeat its errors.

**The Treasury rule does not reach the University of California.** The proposed regulation concerns the 501(c)(3) tax exemption of *private* schools. The University of California is a public institution. The article moves from a complaint about UC to a remedy that does not apply to UC.

**California already forbids racial preferences in its public universities.** The voters enacted Proposition 209 in 1996, and in 2020 they refused to repeal it. The SAT decision was race-neutral on its face. The honest charge is therefore not "open racial preference." It is that an objective measure was removed, against faculty advice, in part for the sake of a demographic result. That is a serious charge. It should be made as it is.

**Motive is asserted rather than shown.** Phrases such as "globalist, race-obsessed leftists" and "institutional seppuku" persuade no one who is not already persuaded. Most people who support these programs are moved by a genuine wish to help those who were left behind. They deserve an argument, not an epithet.

**"Primary reason for being" overstates.** American universities still teach, heal, and discover on a very large scale. The claim is narrower and sufficient: racial sorting and ideological conformity have been given a place in these institutions that the law forbids and the public, which pays for them, never approved.

---

## The Law, and a Caution

The government has clear authority here. The Fourteenth Amendment binds public universities. Title VI binds every school that accepts federal money. In *Bob Jones University v. United States* (1983), the Supreme Court held that the IRS may deny tax exemption to a school whose racial policies violate established public policy. The Treasury proposal applies that same precedent in the other direction: if exemption could be withdrawn for discriminating against Black students, it can be withdrawn for discriminating against white and Asian students. That is one measure, applied evenly, and I support it. The proposal also leaves schools free to help disadvantaged students by race-neutral means such as income, hardship, and first-generation status, and it preserves the freedom of religious schools to select by genuine religious affiliation.

Now the caution. The power to revoke a tax exemption for offending "public policy" is a loaded instrument, and the hand that holds it changes every four or eight years. A future administration with a different definition of public policy could turn it on religious schools, on churches, on any institution whose convictions are out of favor. Conservatives who cheer this tool today should think carefully about who will hold it tomorrow.

The safeguard is to tie the penalty to conduct, not to ideas. A school should lose its exemption for a specific, proven act of racial discrimination as defined by statute and by the Supreme Court, established through due process, with the right of appeal. It should never lose it for what its faculty teach, what its students believe, or what vocabulary its brochures use. The final rule should say so plainly. The comment period is open until November 3, 2026, and that is the place to say it.

---

## Positions

- **One standard of admission, hiring, and aid, without regard to race.** This is already the law. I would enforce it evenly, against preference in any direction.

- **Federal funds and federal tax exemption are conditional on obeying civil rights law.** I support the principle of the Treasury proposal, with enforcement limited to proven discriminatory conduct, never viewpoint, and with full due process.

- **Sunlight before mandates.** A President has no business dictating an admissions test to a state university. That decision belongs to the people of that state. But any institution receiving federal aid should publish, each year, its placement-exam results, its remedial enrollment, and its graduates' outcomes on independent licensing and professional examinations. Let the citizens and the Regents see what they are paying for and decide.

- **Help the disadvantaged directly and early.** Assistance based on income, hardship, and family circumstance reaches the child who needs it, of every race, and insults no one.

- **Repair the injury where it occurs.** Ms. Pawel is correct on one point: the failure begins in the K-12 schools. A student who arrives at eighteen unable to do sixth-grade arithmetic was failed twelve years in a row by adults who passed him along. Parents must be free to choose the school, or the home, that will actually teach their child. A preference at eighteen conceals that failure. It does not repair it.

- **Loosen the degree's monopoly.** Where federal hiring can test for skill directly, it should do so rather than requiring a diploma. A man should be able to prove what he can do.

---

## Held Open

This is my position, and these are my reasons. I hold them as an advocate, not as a final authority. If someone can show me a way to repair an old injustice without committing a new one against a person who did no wrong, or show me that the facts above are mistaken, I want to hear the argument. Until then: one weight, one measure, and real help, given early, to every child who needs it.
