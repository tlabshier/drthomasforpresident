# 261012 - Diesel, MAHA, Iran-Israel, AI

# Proportion in a Crisis: A Response to Mike Adams on Diesel, Hormuz, MAHA, and Machines


*Dr. Thomas — Position Paper, 12 October 2026*


**Resource:** Mike Adams, *Bright Videos News, Oct 12, 2026 — Trump Begs Russia for Diesel Bailout + MAHA Gets a Lashing + AI Gets Super Smart* (with an interview of Robert Scott Bell): [https://brightvideos.com/play/vid-d10b5815-35bc-44c3-8693-caefe0f41b89/index.html](https://brightvideos.com/play/vid-d10b5815-35bc-44c3-8693-caefe0f41b89/index.html)

## Why Respond at All


Mike Adams speaks to a large audience that distrusts the institutions that failed them, and for good reason. His show today covered four things that matter to every American household: the price of diesel, the war in the Persian Gulf, the split inside the health freedom movement, and the coming of capable machines. I want to take him seriously, which means checking what he said, agreeing where he is right, disagreeing where he is wrong, and offering something better than alarm.


My test for public speech is the same one I apply to policy. First: is it righteous — is it true and lawful before God? Only then: is it loving toward my neighbor? The ninth commandment forbids false witness, and a quiet form of false witness uses true facts in false proportion. A real danger described three times its size is still a lie because it moves people to act on a world that doesn’t exist. Exaggeration is lying by proportion. So the question for each of Adams’ claims is not only “did this happen?” but “is it as big as he says?”


Strong words are fine when the threat warrants them. Some of the threats he named do warrant them. Others do not, and a few of his statements cross from warning into contempt for whole peoples. I will say which is which.


---


## Part I — What Adams Gets Right


Credit first, because much of the core is sound.


**The energy damage is real and long-lived.** Iranian strikes in March knocked out two of Qatar’s fourteen LNG trains — about 17 percent of its export capacity — and QatarEnergy itself says repairs will take three to five years. Adams’ further point, that the clock does not start until repairs can begin safely, is reasonable. Houthi forces have repeatedly struck Saudi Aramco’s Jazan and Yanbu facilities since late July, and Saudi Arabia confirmed an attack on its East-West pipeline on 10 September and suspended operations. These are not rumors.


**The U.S. does not control the Strait of Hormuz.** On 10 October, Iran’s Revolutionary Guard said a supertanker exploded after hitting a mine while using what Tehran calls an unauthorized route; U.S. Central Command has disputed Iranian versions of similar incidents, but no one disputes that ships are being hit. Reuters documented at least twelve attacks, attempted strikes, or harassment incidents near the strait in a single week (28 September – 5 October). Any claim that the strait is fully restored is false.


**The Russian diesel deal is a real reversal.** The President announced on 9 October that Russia would supply more than 300,000 tons of diesel immediately, 500,000 in November, a million tons after that, and three million more later. This came one month after he signed a sweeping Russia sanctions law, and Treasury immediately issued a temporary license for Russian diesel to reach world markets. Adams is right that Americans have not been told what, if anything, was given in return.


**Adams is right that the tonnage sounds bigger than it is.** In fact he understated his own point (see Part II).


**Local resilience is wise.** His advice to reduce dependence — solar charging, storing fuel bought cheap, growing food, keeping household machines off the network — is prudent stewardship, whatever one thinks of his forecasts.


**One honest dissenter matters.** His closing illustration from the Asch conformity experiments is sound in its main point: when a single other person in the room says the obvious thing, the pressure to conform collapses.

## Part III — Commentary


### 1. The crisis is real; the forecast is not proven


Adams’ energy segment is mostly correct about events and mostly unproven about the future. He predicts a “lost decade,” population collapse from famine, nations disappearing, kingdoms falling. Each of these is possible. None follows from the facts he gave. Crude is still moving at three-quarters of prewar rates through a contested strait; Saudi Arabia has a bypass pipeline it repairs after each attack; the United States produces far more crude and natural gas than it consumes and has long been a net exporter of diesel. That is a severe price shock, not the end of the industrial world.


Why does the difference matter? Because fear is not neutral. A frightened people will accept almost any concentration of power that promises safety — the very thing Adams says he opposes. Panic is the friend of centralization. Calm, accurate accounting is the friend of liberty.


### 2. On Israel: criticize a government, never wish a people gone


Adams said the world would benefit if Israel were “defeated and vanishes,” and that there can be no stability “as long as Israel exists.” I reject this without qualification. Israel is a nation of about ten million souls. To wish its disappearance is to wish for the death or dispersion of a people, and no reading of Scripture or of natural law permits that wish. It fails the first test before it reaches the second.


It is entirely legitimate to ask whether America should be in this war, whether our interests and Israel’s diverged, and whether an ally’s actions have been just. Those questions deserve open argument. But the record itself undercuts the “puppet” story: U.S. officials reportedly warned Iran that Israel might assassinate its negotiators because Washington wanted the talks to succeed. That is a portrait of two governments with different aims, not of one controlling the other. “Cabal” language replaces evidence with suspicion of an entire people, and it has a long and bloody history. A movement that wants to be trusted on vaccines cannot afford to be untrustworthy about Jews.


### 3. On MAHA, mRNA, and the fight inside the movement


There is a real grievance here. Many people voted on a promise that the mRNA products would face hard scrutiny, and they see instead a department that still supports the platform while launching studies. Dr. Bowden’s letter states that grievance plainly, and political pressure by citizens on their officials is exactly how a republic is supposed to work.


But a movement that asks the public to trust its judgment on medicine must be the most careful people in the room about evidence. The “shedding” claim does not meet that standard. Neither does calling every opponent a fraud. Adams’ strongest point — “I cannot tolerate bad-faith actors who lie to us” — is right. His application of it is too broad: he moves from “I disagree with their strategy” to “they are compromised” without showing the step between. Robert Scott Bell’s counsel, the lifeguard’s “quick reverse” — pause and assess before you dive in — is the wiser rule. Our Lord gave the same order in Matthew 18: go to your brother first, privately, before you go public. Bell and his producer were accused of being paid operatives on the strength of one republished article. That was false witness, and the people who made the charge owe them an apology.


### 4. On hoping for collapse


Adams says he hopes for “the rapid dismantling” of the American system and judges that billions may die “because of sheer ignorance,” for which he and his allies bear no responsibility. Bell’s answer was better: he does not wish suffering on anyone, collapse is ugly, and the goal is an orderly unwinding — Ron Paul’s argument. I stand with Bell. A Christian may expect judgment; he may not cheer it. The prophets wept over Jerusalem even while announcing its fall. And the people who suffer most in a collapse are the poor, the old, the sick, and children — not the elites Adams is angry at. The loving course is the one Bell named: prepare people, and unwind the overreach deliberately while there is still time to do it without bloodshed.


### 5. On artificial intelligence and robots


I should note that this paper was drafted with the help of an AI system, Claude, made by Anthropic — the company whose warnings Adams calls a “doom psyop.” Readers should weigh that, and I invite them to check every source below.


On the substance, Adams and I largely agree on remedies even where we differ on diagnosis. Government regulation of AI is at high risk of capture by the largest companies, a point I have made in earlier papers on this site. Local, open, inspectable systems — and household robots that are small, offline by default, and physically switchable — are sensible safeguards regardless of whether the main danger is the machines themselves or the people who control them. Where I part from him is in setting a “10 percent chance AI kills us” against a “100 percent chance globalists are trying to.” The first is a serious estimate argued by researchers; the second is an accusation offered without evidence. Both deserve to be weighed by evidence, not by which side one dislikes.

## Part IV — Alternative Solutions


My platform rests on *A New ERA*: Energy, Righteousness, and Accountability. Each applies here.


### Energy: honest, domestic, abundant


- **Tell the truth about supply.** Publish weekly, in plain numbers, how much diesel the country produces, consumes, exports, and imports, and how much any foreign deal actually adds. A nine-day supply should not be sold as a rescue, nor mocked as nothing; it should be stated.

- **Use the domestic levers before foreign ones.** America refines more diesel than it burns. The shortage at the pump is a price problem set by a world market, not a physical absence. Short-term relief lies in temporary waivers of the Jones Act so Gulf Coast refiners can ship diesel by sea to East Coast and agricultural markets; in clearing permitting bottlenecks for idled refinery capacity; and in priority allocation agreements for farm fuel during planting and harvest — voluntary, contract-based, not a blanket export ban that would invite retaliation and raise world prices further.

- **Build a real strategic diesel reserve.** The nation holds a crude reserve but almost no reserve of finished diesel, the fuel that actually moves food. A modest distillate reserve stored near farm regions would turn the next shock into an inconvenience.

- **Lay the long foundation: thorium molten-salt reactors.** Liquid-fuel thorium reactors can supply abundant, steady heat and electricity at home, insulated from any strait anywhere. Electricity cheap enough can make hydrogen and ammonia fertilizer domestically, power rail and short-haul freight, and free farmers from fuel imports. This is the real answer to Hormuz: be the nation that does not need it.

- **Welcome distributed solar and EVs without mandating them.** Adams’ employee charging station is a good example of private initiative. Government’s role is to remove obstacles, not to choose the vehicle.


### War and peace: constitutional and keeping our word


- **Return war to Congress.** Whether the war with Iran was wise is a question the Constitution assigns to the people’s representatives. A declared war, debated in the open, is accountable; an undeclared one is not.

- **Support a reciprocal, verified energy ceasefire in Ukraine** — refineries for power grids, as Zelensky himself proposed — and say plainly that Russia’s attacks on Ukrainian homes’ heat and light are part of the same ledger.

- **Keep our word.** A nation that negotiates in one room and strikes in another teaches every adversary that agreements are worthless. Trustworthiness is a strategic asset, and it is also simply righteous.

- **Disclose the terms.** Any agreement trading sanctions relief for fuel should be published in full. The people pay for it; the people should see it.


### Health freedom: liberty with responsibility


- **Separate medicine and state in the right sense:** no mandates for any medical product; full informed consent with plain disclosure of known risks; protection for families who decline.

- **End the liability shield.** Vaccine manufacturers should answer in court for injuries like every other manufacturer. If a product is safe, the market and the courts will show it; if not, the injured deserve recourse.

- **Open the data.** Raw safety data from federally funded and federally licensed products should be available to independent researchers. Studies run inside the same agencies that promoted the products will never earn back trust on their own.

- **Free the healing professions.** Naturopaths, homeopaths, herbalists, and nutrition counselors should be free to practice and advertise truthfully, with honest disclosure of their training, and patients free to choose them. Competition, not monopoly licensing, protects the public.

- **Hold the movement to its own standard.** Every claim we make should be one we would defend before a skeptical jury. Our credibility is our only currency against institutions with vastly more money.


### Machines: tools that answer to their owners


- **Right to local operation:** buyers of robots and AI devices should be able to run them fully offline, with a physical power cut-off.

- **No federal licensing regime for AI models** that would hand the largest firms a moat; instead, ordinary liability for harms caused, as with any product.

- **Open standards** so that skills and training — like the laundry and weeding routines Adams plans to share — can pass freely between households.


---


## Conclusion


Mike Adams is right that the energy shock is serious, that the government has not been candid, that the health freedom movement was promised more than it received, and that free people must prepare themselves rather than wait to be rescued. He is wrong when he multiplies real dangers into certain catastrophe, when he assigns guilt to a whole people, and when he treats suspicion as proof against his own allies.


The answer to a lying government is not louder speech in the other direction. It is accurate speech, held to a higher standard than the government holds itself. Tell the truth in its true size. Prepare without panic. Argue with your brother before you accuse him. Build — energy, food, medicine, and machines — that free men can own and control. That is the ordered test applied: first what is righteous before God, then what is loving to our neighbor. I hold these positions open to a better argument, and I invite one.
