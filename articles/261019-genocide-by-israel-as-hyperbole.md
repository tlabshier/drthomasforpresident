# 261019 - Genocide by Israel as Hyperbole

# The Genocide Charge and the Just Weight


**Dr. Thomas |** **October 19, 2026**

**Category:** Foreign Policy · Truth in Public Speech · Righteousness


---


## Why I Am Writing


This week Joan Swirsky published a column titled [*“Yes, There’s a Genocide Being Waged in Gaza!”*](https://newswithviews.com/yes-theres-a-genocide-being-waged-in-gaza/) Her point, delivered with irony, is that a genocidal war is indeed being fought in the Middle East, but that the party intending genocide is Hamas and its patrons, not Israel.


On that central claim I agree with her. But I want to make the case differently. The genocide charge against Israel is, at bottom, a failure of proportion: a word built to measure the gravest crime men commit has been placed on a scale where it does not belong. Another failure of proportion does not answer a failure of proportion. So I will argue by reason first and feeling second, concede what must be conceded, and state plainly where the people who disagree with me have their strongest ground.


## The Moral Ground: A Just Weight


Before any law or statistic, a prior question remains: what does God require of a man who speaks about his neighbor in public?

>
*“A false balance is abomination to the LORD: but a just weight is his delight.”* — **Proverbs 11:1**


>
*“Ye shall do no unrighteousness in judgment: thou shalt not respect the person of the poor, nor honour the person of the mighty: but in righteousness shalt thou judge thy neighbour.”* — **Leviticus 19:15**


>
*“Thou shalt not bear false witness against thy neighbour.”* — **Exodus 20:16**


Words are weights. When we call an act “genocide,” we are placing it on the same scale as the Holocaust, Rwanda, and Srebrenica. If the act belongs there, silence is a sin. If it does not, the word is a false weight, and Scripture calls that an abomination.** Leviticus 19:15** cuts both ways: we may not tilt judgment toward the weak because they suffer, nor toward the strong because they are our allies. Righteous judgment follows the evidence and nothing else.


That is the standard I will apply here, and it is the standard I would hold the United States government to.


## What Genocide Means


The word has a precise legal meaning. The 1948 Genocide Convention defines genocide as certain acts “committed with intent to destroy, in whole or in part, a national, ethnical, racial or religious group, as such.” The defining element is **intent to destroy the group**. Killing in war, even killing that is excessive or unlawful, is not, by that fact, genocide. War crimes and crimes against humanity are separate categories with separate tests, and they are grave in their own right.


The International Court of Justice has not ruled on whether Israel committed genocide. South Africa brought its case in December 2023. In 2024, the Court issued provisional orders directing Israel to prevent acts prohibited by the Convention and to allow humanitarian aid, but those orders protect rights while the case is heard; they are not a finding of guilt. Israel filed its formal reply in March 2026 and contested the Court’s jurisdiction. Under the current schedule, written pleadings continue until May 2029. Anyone who tells you the matter has been legally decided, in either direction, is not telling you the truth.


## Why the Charge Fails Against Israel


**1. The intent element is not shown.** Genocide requires that a state set out to destroy a people as such. Israel’s stated war aim was the destruction of Hamas as a military and governing force and the return of hostages after the massacre of October 7, 2023. On that anniversary this month, more than seventy-five retired senior American military officers and military lawyers, including a former NATO Supreme Allied Commander and a former Marine Corps Commandant, published an open letter rejecting the genocide charge. Their argument is the right one: civilian deaths in combat, however terrible, do not by themselves establish an intent to destroy a people. They point to Israeli practices that a genocidal army would not adopt: advance warnings, evacuation orders, protected corridors, pauses in fighting, and legal review of targets. One may argue about how well those measures worked in particular cases. One cannot easily reconcile them with a plan to annihilate.


**2. Numbers alone do not settle it, and the numbers do not fit the charge.** The Gaza Health Ministry, which is run by Hamas and does not distinguish fighters from civilians, reports roughly 74,000 deaths through mid-October 2026, out of a pre-war population of about 2.2 million. That is around 3 percent. Every one of those deaths is a human being made in the image of God, and I will not speak of them as a statistic to be won. But Dr. Michael Brown’s comparison is fair: in Poland, about 90 percent of the Jewish population was murdered. When one word covers both, it stops meaning anything. The retired officers make the companion point: genocide is not a quantitative crime, so no casualty count proves it, and no casualty count disproves it. The question is intent, and the shape of the war does not show it.


**3. The population was not being destroyed before the war.** Gaza’s population grew from roughly 1.1 million in 2000 to about 2.1 million in 2023, one of the world’s higher growth rates. Whatever one says about the blockade or the occupation, it was not a program to destroy the group.


## Where Genocidal Intent Is Written Plainly


The intent that the charge cannot find in Israel’s conduct is stated openly on the other side.


Hamas’s 1988 founding charter, in Article 7, quotes a tradition that the Last Day will not come until Muslims fight and kill the Jews, with stones and trees calling out that a Jew is hiding behind them. That is not a political grievance about borders. It is a religious summons to kill a people as such, which is precisely the intent the Convention describes. Hamas issued a revised political document in 2017 that softened its language, but it did not renounce the goal of eliminating Israel.


On October 7, that intent was put into practice. About 1,200 people were murdered, most of them civilians, in homes, at a music festival, and in kibbutzim. Some 250 were taken hostage. A United Nations report in 2024 found reasonable grounds to believe that rape and other sexual violence occurred during the attack. Battle orders captured afterward, as reported on Israeli television and cited by Dr. Uri Rosset, directed fighters to kill as many people as possible and burn buildings with their occupants inside if they could not be taken. When the attackers had the opportunity to kill Jews, they did so without distinction of age or sex.


If we are going to use the word genocide in this war, that is where the evidence points.


## Why the Charge Spreads Anyway


Swirsky leans on the idea of projection, and it has some merit. The Soviet writer Vasily Grossman observed, in substance, that the crimes men accuse the Jews of are often the crimes they themselves commit or intend. For some of Israel’s accusers, including the regime in Tehran and the organizations it funds, that is exactly what is happening.


But I don’t believe that is the whole explanation. Many people who use the word are not anti-Semites. They have seen images of dead children, flattened neighborhoods, and hungry families, and they reach for the strongest word they know because nothing smaller seems equal to what they feel. Their grief is real, and their compassion is to their credit. Their error is one of measure, not of heart, and the right response is to correct the measure without mocking the heart.


## The Three Questions


I test every public position by three questions, in order.


**Is it righteous?** To accuse a nation of genocide without showing intent to destroy a people is to use a false weight. It is bearing false witness against a neighbor nation. It is not righteous.


**If so, is it loving?** It fails here too, and not only toward Israel. The genocide label also harms Gazans and every future victim of genocide. When we spend the word on a case that does not meet the standard, we weaken it for the cases that do. The world was slow to act in Rwanda; it is slow today in Sudan, where UN investigators described the destruction at El Fasher as bearing the hallmarks of genocide. A word worn smooth by overuse will not stop the next massacre.


**Would I want it done to me?** Suppose my own country were invaded, a thousand of my neighbors murdered in their beds, and hostages dragged across the border, and when we fought back, the world accused us of the very crime our attackers had announced as their aim. I would call that an injustice. Now suppose I were a father in Gaza whose children died in the war. I would want the truth told about who began the war, who hid weapons beneath hospitals and homes, and who refused to surrender the hostages that could have ended it. I would not want my children’s deaths turned into a slogan. And I would want my grief honored, not dismissed as propaganda. Righteousness demands both at once.


## What I Would Do as President


These positions are held for reasons and are open to a better argument.


- **Use the Convention’s standard, and say so.** The United States government should apply the word genocide only where intent to destroy a group is shown, and should explain its reasoning in public each time, whether it is defending an ally or accusing an enemy.

- **Name genocidal ideology where it is stated.** Hamas’s founding religious summons to kill Jews should be named for what it is. No organization with that aim should receive American recognition, funding, or a seat at a negotiating table as a legitimate governing authority until it renounces that aim in deed and word.

- **Keep the categories separate.** Investigate and, where proven, prosecute allegations that particular strikes or units violated the laws of war. A nation that fights justly should welcome that, because it is what distinguishes its conduct from its enemy’s. Refusing the genocide label is not the same as refusing scrutiny.

- **Treat civilian protection and humanitarian access as duties in themselves.** Feeding the hungry and protecting the innocent do not depend on settling the genocide question. They are owed regardless.

- **Protect the precedent for American soldiers.** As the retired officers warned, if civilian deaths in a war against an enemy that hides among civilians become proof of genocide, every future adversary will be rewarded for using human shields, and American forces will one day face the same charge.


## The Strongest Case on the Other Side


Leadership means stating the opposing argument at its strongest.


Those who say Israel has committed genocide do not rest only on casualty figures. A UN Commission of Inquiry concluded in September 2025 that Israel had committed genocide, and reaffirmed that finding in June 2026, citing what it called deliberate targeting of children. The International Association of Genocide Scholars passed a resolution to the same effect in August 2025. Several Israeli scholars of the Holocaust and genocide, including Omer Bartov and Amos Goldberg, have reached the same conclusion, as have some Israeli human rights organizations. They point to statements by certain Israeli officials calling for the destruction of Gaza or denying any distinction between Hamas and the population, to restrictions on aid that the ICJ ordered lifted, to famine conditions documented in 2025, and to the scale of destruction of homes and civic life. Navi Pillay, who chaired the UN commission that reached the genocide finding, was awarded the Nobel Peace Prize this month.


This is serious, and it deserves an answer, not contempt. My answer is this. Inflammatory statements by individual officials are wrong and should be condemned, but the legal test asks whether destruction of the group was the policy of the state, and the evidence of warnings, evacuations, aid corridors, and a stated war aim directed at Hamas points the other way. Famine and destruction, where they occurred, are grievous and may raise questions under the laws of war, but they do not by themselves establish intent to destroy a people. And the highest court charged with deciding the question has not decided it. If the evidence ever showed that destroying Gaza’s people as a people was the policy of the Israeli state, I would say so, because a just weight does not change when the defendant is a friend.


## Conclusion


Swirsky ends by observing that if Hamas had not attacked on October 7, 2023, the war’s casualties would have been zero. That is true. But the deeper reason to reject the genocide charge against Israel is not that Israel is our ally, nor that its enemies are wicked. The charge does not meet the measure, and a righteous people does not use false measures, even against those it disagrees with.


Let the word keep its weight, so that when it is needed, it will still be heard.

## Notes: Where I Differ from the Source Article


I agree with Swirsky’s conclusion, but several of her supporting claims do not hold up, and a paper that asks others to use a just weight must use one itself.


- **“Infants put in ovens.”** This claim, from a first responder’s account in late 2023, was never confirmed by Israeli authorities, and Israeli journalists and fact-checkers concluded there was no evidence for it. The documented atrocities of October 7 are sufficient; we do not need unverified ones.

- **Gaza’s population “from one million to five million.”** Gaza’s population before the war was about 2.1 to 2.2 million. The figure of over five million matches Gaza and the West Bank combined.

- **“Dispersed in a diaspora for the last 4,000 years.”** The Babylonian exile began about 2,600 years ago, and the Roman dispersion about 1,950 years ago.

- **The Nobel laureate.** The 2026 Peace Prize went to Navi Pillay, a South African jurist and former UN High Commissioner for Human Rights, who chaired the UN commission that found genocide. One may strongly disagree with her conclusion. Calling her an anti-Semite is a charge that requires evidence, and the column offers none.

- **“Israel is starving the Palestinians” as “now-disproven.”** International monitors declared famine conditions in parts of Gaza in 2025. Who bears responsibility for those conditions is genuinely disputed; their occurrence is not disproven, and pre-war obesity data do not settle it.

- **“Apartheid” as “now-disproven.”** This remains a contested claim, not a disproven one. I don’t accept the label, but the column doesn’t refute it.

- **Hostages.** About 250 were taken, not 240.

- **The list of empires.** Some on the list persecuted Jews severely; others did not set out to destroy them. The Persian Empire under Cyrus ended the Babylonian exile and permitted the rebuilding of the Temple.

- **Tone.** Phrases such as “cesspool,” “bloated Arab potentates,” and the claim that hatred of Jews is at root jealousy are assertions, not arguments, and they weaken the case. I have left them out.
