# The President in Kalispell


### Weighing the claims, and weighing the solutions


*A position paper occasioned by President Trump’s campaign rally in Kalispell, Montana, on October 14, 2026*


Thomas Lee Abshier, ND — 15 October 2026


---


## Plain language summary


The President came to my hometown and spoke for about seventy minutes in support of Kurt Alme for the Senate and Aaron Flint for Congress. I checked his major claims against the best available public data.


**Much of what he said about crime, the border, gun rights, and several Montana projects holds up.** Violent crime fell more in 2025 than in any year on record. Border Patrol has gone more than a year without releasing border crossers into the country. The Janicki plant in Great Falls is real. As of last week, the federal registration requirement for suppressors and short-barreled rifles is no longer being enforced.


**Much of what he said about the economy, prices, trade, taxes, drug prices, and the two wars does not hold up.** Inflation is 3.7 percent, not 1.7. Gasoline is higher than on most days of the prior administration. The “$22 trillion” in investment is a list of announcements that the White House itself puts at about half that. The death tax was not eliminated. Tips, overtime, and Social Security were not made tax-free; they received capped, temporary deductions. Americans do not pay the lowest drug prices in the world. The Iran war is not over, and the Strait of Hormuz is still contested.


**The promise that troubles me most is the $5,000 check** Republicans offer every adult citizen if they hold Congress. It would cost about $1.2 trillion; the tariff money to pay for it doesn’t exist, so we’d borrow from our children and hand it to voters on the condition of a vote. I would not do it.


**For each problem he named, I set out what he proposes and what I would do instead,** tested by three questions: Is it righteous? If so, is it loving? Would I want it done to me? My answers rest on the framework of my candidacy: A New ERA—Energy, Righteousness, and Accountability.


---


## Why I am writing about a rally


A rally is not a policy paper, and I will not grade it as though it were one. A man speaking for seventy minutes without notes to a friendly crowd to win an election twenty days away will round up, tell stories, and enjoy himself. That is the genre, and I do not hold him to a standard I could not meet on a stage.


But this was not an ordinary speaker. He is the President; the rally was in Kalispell, and my neighbors will vote on the claims he made. If the numbers are right, my neighbors should know it and give him credit. If they are wrong, my neighbors should know that too, because a vote cast on a false weight is a vote stolen from the man who cast it.


A month ago I published *[A Just Weight](https://drthomasforpresident.com/2026/09/260914-examination-of-trumps-record/)*, examining a list of this administration’s twenty-five achievements. I proposed five tests for any political scorecard: Is it enacted or only announced? Is it correlation or causation? Are we counting actions or weighing effects? Is it durable or reversible? Compared to what? I apply the same tests here, and I invite readers to apply them to me.


I add one thing this time. The President did not only report results; he made promises and named enemies. Promises and accusations are moral acts, and we must weigh them by a moral standard, not only a factual one. The standard I use is the one I have adopted for every position paper on this site:


- **Is it righteous?** Is it what God would establish — just, lawful, true?

- **If so, is it loving?** Does it actually serve the neighbor’s good, rather than merely humor him?

- **Would I want it done to me?** If I stood in the other man’s place — the taxpayer, the farmer, the foreign citizen, the political opponent — would I, committed to righteousness, accept this as fair?


---


## PART ONE: What holds up


Credit first, and without reluctance.


**The crime drop is real and historic.** The FBI’s final 2025 data, released in August, show violent crime falling 9.7 percent, the largest one-year decline since national estimates began in 1936. Murder fell 18.1 percent, to a rate of 4.1 per 100,000, tying 1955 and 1956 for the lowest in the FBI’s series. The claim that it is the lowest in “125 years” reaches back into public-health records from before 1930 that are not strictly comparable; the Council on Criminal Justice thinks it likely, and other analysts say it cannot be known for certain. The honest statement is “the lowest in at least seventy years, and possibly far longer.” That is remarkable enough. As I noted last month, the decline began in 2022, so it’s fair to say it continued and accelerated on his watch—not that he caused all of it.


**The border numbers are real.** Southwest border apprehensions totaled about 137,000 in fiscal 2026, a historic low, and CBP reports sixteen consecutive months without Border Patrol releases into the interior. “Zero illegal aliens admitted” is too absolute — evaders still get through, and a Washington Examiner investigation in February found some releases despite the official claim — but the direction is not in dispute. I said in my own [immigration paper](https://drthomasforpresident.com/2026/08/260829-illegal-immigration-and-border-enforcement/) that the line has been substantially secured, and I repeat it.


**The gun-rights items are substantially true.** The One Big Beautiful Bill Act set the federal tax on suppressors, short-barreled rifles, and short-barreled shotguns to zero as of January 1. In August, a federal court in Texas held that registration requirements for zero-tax items exceed the taxing power; the Justice Department did not appeal, and on October 9 the ATF announced it would no longer enforce registration and transfer approval for these items. The administration also repealed the prior administration’s “zero tolerance” revocation policy for gun dealers, and in February the VA stopped reporting veterans who merely have a fiduciary managing their benefits to the background-check system — restoring rights to roughly 200,000 veterans who had been adjudicated of nothing.


**The Montana items are mostly real.** In June, Janicki Industries announced an $800 million, two-million-square-foot aerospace and defense facility in Great Falls, with more than 2,000 jobs at full buildout; construction began in July. Interior’s 2026–27 hunting rule opened new hunting at Grass Lake, Hailstone, and Medicine Lake refuges. The rally announced the “super range” for advanced weapons testing; no site, size, budget, or timeline has been published yet, so it remains, by my first test, an announcement. The Colstrip plant did receive a two-year exemption from the 2024 mercury rule, which its owners and the Governor said threatened its operation. The executive order allowing dyed farm diesel in road vehicles is real, signed about October 5. And the $1,776 “warrior dividend” was in fact paid to about 1.45 million service members last December.


**The income and poverty figures are records, with caveats.** The Census Bureau reported in September that real median household income in 2025 reached $87,460, the highest since the series began, and that the official poverty rate fell to 10.2 percent, the lowest since 1959. Those are genuine. They describe 2025, not today; the Supplemental Poverty Measure, which most economists prefer, was flat at 13.1 percent; and the current picture is weaker — September added only 29,000 jobs, unemployment is 4.2 percent, and wages are now rising more slowly than prices.


**The lumber point is largely right.** American mills now supply nearly three-quarters of domestic demand, a share not seen since the 1970s. That is a market-share claim, not a volume record, and it comes from the domestic industry’s own trade group, but the direction is real, and it matters in a county like ours.

## PART TWO: What does not hold up


Here the weights are false, and some are badly false.


### Prices


**“The worst inflation in American history.”** Inflation under the prior administration peaked at 9.1 percent in June 2022, the highest in forty years. It was not the highest in history: inflation reached 14.8 percent in 1980 and 23.7 percent in 1920. The cumulative price rise over those four years was about 21 percent, so “19 percent” is in the right range if that is what he meant.


**“The last three months, we have 1.7.”** This is false. The Bureau of Labor Statistics released September’s numbers the morning of the rally: 3.7 percent over the year, with core inflation at 2.6 percent. Over the last three months, prices rose at roughly 4.5 percent annually. CNN rated the identical “1.7 percent” claim false in May. Newsweek noted the contradiction in its coverage of this rally.


**“Your prices have come way down.”** Not for fuel. Gasoline averaged $4.33 nationally on the day of the rally, up from $3.12 a year ago; September set a record for any September, and diesel hit an all-time high above $6.50. That is mostly the Iran war, which is why I take up the war below. But a man who blames his predecessor for “the highest energy prices in history” — itself only true in nominal dollars — cannot then claim prices have fallen when they have risen by a third on his own watch.


### Investment and markets


**“$22.2 trillion being invested.”** The White House’s own running tally was about $11.2 trillion in early October, half the figure claimed. That tally counts announcements, pledges, purchase agreements, and trade commitments, some double-counted. The Cato Institute found nearly $3 trillion of it was not investment at all. Actual foreign direct investment, as measured by the Commerce Department, ran in the hundreds of billions per year. This is exactly the failure I described last month: **an announcement isn’t an investment, and a number too large to be plausible is a liability, even when it favors you.**


**“401(k)s up $50,000 to $60,000 each.”** False. Fidelity’s average balance rose about $18,000 over the past year, but large accounts pull up the average; the median gain is much smaller. PolitiFact rated an earlier, smaller version of this claim mostly false.


### Tariffs and the $5,000 check


**“Tariffs don’t cause inflation.”** The Federal Reserve Bank of New York found this month that about 90 percent of tariff costs were passed through to American import prices, adding nearly three points to goods inflation by February. The Tax Foundation estimates the cost at roughly $820 per household this year. American importers pay tariffs and mostly pass them on to American buyers. That does not make every tariff wrong — I defend a particular kind below — but it makes this sentence false.


**The legal ground has shifted under the tariff program.** In February the Supreme Court held, six to three in *Learning Resources v. Trump*, that the emergency-powers statute does not authorize tariffs. The administration is refunding about $165 billion collected under that authority; in May and June, refunds exceeded new collections. The Congressional Budget Office now projects that the administration’s trade policy will *add* about $0.9 trillion to deficits over the next decade.


**“I am going to give all adult citizens $5,000.”** With about 245 million adult citizens, the check would cost about $1.23 trillion, not $1 trillion. Total customs revenue in fiscal 2025 was about $195 billion, and net 2026 revenue is lower because of the refunds. No bill, no CBO score, and no named source of funds. I return to the moral question in Part Four.


**“The $28 billion came out of China, from the tariffs.”** False. The first-term farm payments were made through the Commodity Credit Corporation’s borrowing authority at the Treasury. China did not pay them. American importers paid the tariffs, and American taxpayers paid the farmers.


### Taxes


**“The largest tax cuts in American history.”** In raw dollars, about $4.5 trillion over ten years, yes. As a share of the economy, about sixth since 1940; the 1981 cut was roughly twice as large.


**“No tax on tips, no tax on overtime, no tax on Social Security.”** What the law actually does: a deduction for tips up to $25,000; a deduction for the overtime *premium* (the “half” in time-and-a-half) up to $12,500, or $25,000 for a couple; and, for seniors, not an exemption of Social Security at all but an extra $6,000 deduction for each person over 65. All three phase out at higher incomes and expire after 2028. They are real tax relief. They are not “no tax,” and they are not permanent.


**“We eliminated the death tax.”** False. The law made the exemption permanent at $15 million per person, $30 million per couple, indexed to inflation. The 40 percent rate remains above that line. This matters because of his story about farm families losing land to banks, and, worse, because of the tax. It is a moving story, and I share his hatred of a tax that falls at a graveside. But the Department of Agriculture estimated that under the prior exemption only about 120 of roughly 41,000 farm estates in a year owed any estate tax at all — about 0.3 percent. Under the new exemption, fewer still. The tax was wrong in principle; it was never the reason most family farms are lost.


### Drug prices


**“We went from the highest drug prices in the world to the lowest,” with “price differences of 300, 400, 500, 600, and even 700 percent.”** False. A price cannot fall more than 100 percent. Americans still pay, on average, nearly three times what other developed nations pay, and more than four times for brand-name drugs. Reuters found that about a third of the drugs on the new TrumpRx site were cheaper in Britain. The “most favored nation” arrangements are voluntary deals with 16 companies, and cash prices paid through TrumpRx don’t count toward a patient’s insurance deductible. There are real reductions — GLP-1 weight-loss drugs, some fertility drugs, insulin — and those deserve credit. “Lowest in the world” does not.


### The wars


**Iran: “It’s going to be over very quickly.”** The war began on February 28 with strikes that killed the Supreme Leader and much of the senior command — that part of the President’s account is true. But a ceasefire in April and an agreement in June both collapsed, limited strikes resumed in September, and as of this week talks are stalled. The U.S. Navy is blockading Iranian ports while Iran continues to strike tankers in the Strait of Hormuz. Brent crude is above $100. The Congressional Research Service counts 18 American service members killed and more than 800 wounded. Iran’s conventional navy is largely destroyed — the U.S. and Israel claim 155 vessels destroyed or damaged — but its Revolutionary Guard still operates small boats and minelayers, which is precisely why Hormuz remains dangerous. On October 8, the President said the U.S. would take no further military action until after the midterm elections. A war paused for an election is not over.


**Venezuela: “It took us 48 minutes.”** Officials say the January 3 raid that captured Maduro lasted about two and a half hours. It was a skilled operation. But **“we paid for the war many, many times over”** is not supported: the best estimate of oil sold through U.S.-controlled accounts is about $13 billion gross, and PolitiFact rated the larger claim false. And **“the United States and Venezuela together have 64 percent of the oil in the world”** is false: Venezuela holds about 17 to 19 percent of proven reserves and the United States about 3 percent, together roughly 22 percent.


### Climate, coal, and China


**“China makes the windmills but doesn’t have any wind farms.”** False. China installed about 119 gigawatts of wind power in 2025 alone, a world record, and holds roughly 44 percent of the world’s wind capacity. The point he was reaching for is true and stronger than what he said: China is also building coal plants at a record pace — about 95 gigawatts commissioned last year, far more than “48 plants.” China builds everything, because it wants power.


**“The planet is actually cooling, and the ice caps are actually larger.”** False. NOAA ranks 2025 the third-warmest year since 1850. The 2025 Arctic sea-ice maximum was the lowest in the satellite record. I say this as a man who has published [skepticism of the climate-crisis narrative](https://drthomasforpresident.com/2023/09/scientists-skeptical-of-climate-crisis/) on this site. One can believe — as I do — that much proposed climate policy is costly, coercive, and aimed at the wrong target, without denying a thermometer. Bad data doesn’t strengthen the case against bad policy.


### The border numbers that are not true


**“They allowed 25 million people to pour across our borders.”** Total Border Patrol and port encounters during the prior administration were just under 11 million, and that figure includes people expelled, people removed, and people counted more than once. Even adding the House Republicans’ own estimate of about 2.2 million evaders still doesn’t approach 25 million.


**“They emptied their prisons and mental institutions into our country.”** No evidence has been produced for this, and fact-checkers, including the restrictionist Center for Immigration Studies, have said they know of none.


**“11,888 murderers.”** No public source exists for this exact figure. It appears to derive from a 2024 ICE letter counting 13,099 noncitizens with homicide convictions on its “non-detained docket.” That phrase means only “not in ICE custody”; many are serving sentences in state prisons. The data go back more than forty years, include legal permanent residents, and do not count murderers admitted by one administration.


**I make these corrections from the restrictionist side of the debate.** I want the law enforced. The true numbers are bad enough to justify enforcement; inflated ones hand the opposition an easy reason to dismiss the real problem.


### Elections


**“In 2020, they rigged the election.”** Courts, state audits, and the President’s own Justice Department found no fraud sufficient to change the outcome. I support voter identification and an auditable paper trail for every ballot—it has been in my platform since 2010—precisely so questions like this can be settled by evidence instead of assertion. But accusing someone of stealing a national election, repeated for six years without evidence to support it, is not a small exaggeration.


**“We won by the biggest margins… numbers nobody’s ever seen.”** He won the 2024 popular vote by about 2.3 million votes, 1.5 percentage points—a real, clean victory and the fifth-smallest popular-vote margin of the thirty-two presidential elections since 1900. His 312 electoral votes were fewer than in 2008 or 2012 and far fewer than in 1984.

## PART THREE: The proportion problem


I have written that hyperbole is *lying by proportion*. A statement can contain a true fact and still deceive, because it represents a small thing as large or a contested thing as settled. Strong words aren’t wrong when the threat warrants them; naming what God detests isn’t hype. The test is proportion.


By that test, the rally failed most in how it treated the men and women on the other side of the ballot.


**The Senate opponent.** Alani Bankhead, an Air Force lieutenant colonel, was called a “Democrat communist” who campaigns to defund the police. Her own published positions are these: she would shut down ICE and rebuild a replacement from scratch; she would end wealth-based pretrial detention; she supports impeachment and has signed a pledge to convict. I disagree with all three, and I would argue against each on this site. But she does not propose cutting police budgets — she proposes crisis co-responders and use-of-force standards — and nothing in her platform is communism. To call a decorated officer a communist because she holds liberal views on criminal justice is to use the heaviest word in the political vocabulary for a disagreement that deserves an argument.


**The House opponent.** Sam Forstag, a smokejumper and union representative, was called a “radical left maniac” and a career lobbyist for the “anti-American ACLU.” He was, in fact, a registered lobbyist from 2021 to 2023 for the ACLU of Montana, Planned Parenthood, the Montana Library Association, and the City of Missoula, among others, and he lobbied in 2023 for Bozeman’s request to let its voters decide on a local sales tax. Those are fair subjects for criticism, and Montanans may weigh them. “Maniac” is not an argument.


**The protester.** One man in a crowd of thousands interrupted, and the President said such people are paid $1,000 by the radical left. No evidence was offered. It may be true of some protesters somewhere; he asserted it about this one with nothing to back it up.


**The whole opposition.** “They hate us.” “They actually want to destroy America.” “The only way they can win is if they cheat.” Half the country votes for the other party. Among them are people I know and love. Many of them are wrong on matters I consider grave. They do not hate their country, and telling them so persuades none of them and hardens all of them.


Why does this matter more than any single statistic? Because a republic depends on citizens who disagree being able to reason together, and that ability depends on each side describing the other accurately. Our Lord commands us to love our enemies, not misrepresent them.* “Thou shalt not bear false witness against thy neighbour”* **(Exodus 20:16)** does not contain an exception for neighbors in the other party.


There is one more line I cannot pass over. The President asked the crowd to “pretend that I’m on the ballot” and, later, “Remember, I am on the ballot.” I understand why he said it; enthusiasm drives turnout. But it asks people to vote for a man rather than a principle, and it builds a habit that is the opposite of what a free people needs. I have asked those who read this site to write me in only if they are certain I would win, and otherwise to vote for the best man on the ballot. **Vote for Mr. Alme or Mr. Flint if, on the merits, they are the better men for Montana.** That is a sufficient reason. You don’t need to vote for someone who isn’t running.

## PART FOUR: The problems, and what would actually solve them


The President named real problems. People are hurting at the pump. Trade has been unfair to American producers for forty years. The border was overrun. Drug prices are a scandal. Iran was an aggressor. These deserve solutions, and he deserves credit where his solutions work. For each, I state his approach, weigh it, and give mine.


### 1. Energy and prices


**His approach:** reopen coal, keep old plants running by emergency order, drill, let farm diesel onto the roads, and end the Iran war “one way or the other.”


**Weighed:** The instinct — that cheap, abundant energy is the foundation of prosperity — is correct, and it is the first letter of my own platform. But a reprieve for a fifty-year-old coal plant is a holding action, not a foundation. The dyed-diesel order defers a federal tax that is still owed and leaves state taxes in place, so a farmer crossing a state line can be fined. And the biggest driver of today’s fuel prices isn’t regulation; it is a war in the Persian Gulf.


**What I would do:**


- **Build the foundation.** Commit the nation to abundant, cheap, safe power — above all, the thorium molten-salt reactor, which runs at atmospheric pressure, has no high-pressure water to flash to steam, drains passively to a safe state if it overheats, and burns a fuel the country has in abundance. Clear the licensing path so that a proven design can be built in years, not decades. This is the “E” in A New ERA, and every other economic goal sits on it.

- **Protect the household while the foundation is built.** Make the dyed-diesel relief a statute, not a deferral, so farmers and truckers don’t end up owing a back tax in January.

- **Tell the truth about prices.** Publish inflation figures with the energy component shown separately, every month, so the country can see what the war costs at the pump instead of being told that prices have fallen.


**Tested:** Abundant energy is righteous stewardship of what God placed in the earth; it is loving because energy costs hit the poor hardest, and I would want it done for me as a man paying for his own fuel.


### 2. Inflation and the debt


**His approach:** grow out of it, and claim it is already solved.


**Weighed:** The figure he cited is false, and he ignores the deeper cause. At its root, inflation is the government spending money it does not have and covering the gap by creating more of it. As I argued in *[Moral Capitalism](https://drthomasforpresident.com/2026/10/261005-moral-capitalism/)*, that is a form of counterfeiting: it takes value silently from every saver and wage-earner and transfers it to the spender. The tax cuts, the war, and the proposed checks all add to the deficit; CBO projects the trade program alone will add $0.9 trillion.


**What I would do:** Bring federal spending down to revenue on a published schedule, beginning with subsidies to industry; require that every new program carry its own funding; and make every expenditure expire unless reauthorized, as I proposed in *[Reforming the Administrative State](https://drthomasforpresident.com/2026/10/261006-administrative-state-reform/)*.


**Tested:** Debasing the currency fails the first question; in the most literal sense, it is a false weight. *“Divers weights, and divers measures, both of them are alike abomination to the LORD”* **(Proverbs 20:10)**.


### 3. The $5,000 “Trump dividend”


**His approach:** If Republicans win both houses, send every adult citizen $5,000, paid from tariff revenue and economic growth, to be spent in the United States.


**Weighed against the facts:** The cost is about $1.23 trillion. Customs revenue is a fraction of that and is currently being refunded. The money would therefore be borrowed. The “spend it in America” condition is unenforceable on fungible money.


**Weighed against the three questions:**


- *Is it righteous?* It would take $1.2 trillion from the next generation—through borrowing they must repay—and hand it to this generation on the condition that this generation votes a particular way. That is not a dividend. A dividend is a share of profits actually earned. This is a loan taken out in our children’s names and paid to their parents as an inducement. I cannot call that righteous.

- *Is it loving?* It is pleasant, which is not the same thing. Scripture warns against the man who humors his neighbor rather than telling him the truth **(Leviticus 19:17)**. A check that worsens the inflation it claims to relieve is humoring.

- *Would I want it done to me?* If I were the child who would pay it back, no.


**What I would do:** Decline to offer it. If tariff revenue ever produces a genuine surplus, apply it first to the debt, because the debt is already a claim against every citizen’s future earnings. Paying it down is the only “dividend” that does not borrow from someone else to pay it.


### 4. Trade and tariffs


**His approach:** high tariffs, imposed by executive action, used both to raise revenue and to pressure foreign governments; and, when they hurt farmers, Treasury-funded bailouts.


**Weighed:** His instinct about trade is right, and Montana’s sawmills are proof. For forty years, competitors have undersold American producers while bearing none of the costs we impose on ourselves. But tariffs set and reset by decree are unstable; the Supreme Court has now struck down the main authority he used; businesses cannot plan around a rate that changes with an announcement; and farmers who lose export markets in retaliation then become dependent on federal checks. That is a cycle, not a policy.


**What I would do:** Adopt the *[fair-trade](https://drthomasforpresident.com/2019/03/fair-trade/)* principle I have held since 2004. We impose *reverse tariffs* on our own producers — regulatory, liability, and labor costs that foreign competitors do not bear. The just remedy is a tariff on imports **equal to the cost burden we impose on our own producers**, and no more:


- set by Congress, by statute, so that it is durable and does not depend on emergency powers;

- calculated openly, sector by sector, with the method published;

- falling automatically as a trading partner’s standards rise to ours, so the tariff rewards decency rather than punishing it;

- paired with steady reduction of the reverse tariffs at home, so that we lower our own producers’ costs rather than only raising others’.


**Tested:** A tariff that only equalizes a burden we ourselves imposed is righteous — it is a just weight. It is loving to the American worker without being cruel to the foreign one, who gains access to our market by meeting the standards we claim to believe in. And I would accept it as fair if I were the foreign producer.


### 5. Taxes, including the death tax


**His approach:** temporary deductions for tips, overtime, and seniors; a higher estate-tax exemption; and a promise to make it all permanent.


**Weighed:** The relief is real, and I do not begrudge any waiter or welder his deduction. But every carve-out complicates the code, rewards one kind of work over another, and creates a cliff when it expires in 2028. The estate-tax change is good as far as it goes, but it didn’t eliminate the tax, and he shouldn’t tell the families he is courting that it did.


**What I would do:** Replace the patchwork with one rate — a flat tax or a national sales tax, as my platform has held since 2010 — so that tips, overtime, and wages are all taxed alike, at a low rate, with no deduction to lobby for and no expiration date to fear. **Abolish the estate tax outright.** Taxing a man’s lifetime of already-taxed savings at death is wrong in principle, whatever the exemption.


**Tested:** One rate for every citizen is the clearest form of equal justice under law, and I would want to be taxed exactly as my neighbor is.


### 6. The border and crime


**His approach:** secure the line, remove criminal aliens, pass a statute barring any future president from opening the border, prosecute traffickers up the chain to the cartels, and seek the death penalty for large-scale dealers and traffickers.


**Weighed:** This is the strongest part of his record, and Mr. Alme described the prosecutorial strategy well. The proposed statute is exactly right in form: it converts an executive achievement into law, which is the durability test I care about most. What is still missing from every list — his, and every administration’s for forty years — is enforcement against the employers who knowingly hire illegal labor.


**What I would do:** Support the statute. Add mandatory E-Verify with personal criminal liability for owners and officers who knowingly hire illegal labor, because the job is the magnet. Publish an honest count of the unauthorized population every quarter, with the method open to audit, so that neither side can inflate or shrink the problem. Fund immigration courts until they decide asylum claims in weeks. On capital punishment for large-scale trafficking, I hold that the gravest crimes may justly carry the gravest penalty, but only with the protections of a full jury, and with the jury’s ancient right to refuse to convict under a law it judges unjust — the check I described in *[Whose Law?](https://drthomasforpresident.com/2026/09/260908-jury-nullification-debate/)*.


**Tested:** Protecting the citizen is the government’s first duty; enforcing against the employer rather than only the laborer is the loving form of enforcement because it lands on the one who profits; and I want the same rules, published in advance, applied to me.


### 7. Health care and drug prices


**His approach:** voluntary “most favored nation” deals with drug companies, a government drug-price website, and a plan to send health subsidies “directly to you” instead of to insurers.


**Weighed:** The direction of the health plan — money controlled by the patient rather than the insurer — is right, and it is close to the medical savings accounts in my own platform. But the plan has no legislative text, and the enhanced subsidies it would replace lapsed in January. The drug deals produced real savings on some medicines, but they are voluntary, reversible, and far from “lowest in the world.”


**What I would do:**


- **Put the money in the patient’s hands by statute**, in an account he owns and may spend on any care he chooses, conventional or alternative.

- **Measure what works.** Build the national outcome-feedback system I have proposed since 2010 — the *[anecdotal database](https://drthomasforpresident.com/2024/12/reforming-healthcare-the-anecdotal-database/)* — so that patients and doctors can see which treatments actually helped people like them, for what cost. Price falls fastest where results are visible.

- **Break the regulatory moat** that lets a few manufacturers set prices, by allowing competing, liability-bearing certifiers alongside the FDA, as I set out in the administrative-state paper.


**Tested:** Letting a man spend his own health money on what he judges best, with honest information, is both righteous and loving; it is what I would want for myself.


### 8. The wars: Iran and Venezuela


**His approach:** overwhelming force, decapitation of leadership, and a negotiated end “one way or the other”; in Venezuela, seizure of the government and control of the oil.


**Weighed:** I do not grieve the destruction of the Iranian regime’s capacity to threaten its neighbors; preventing a nuclear Iran is a legitimate aim. But Congress never declared either war. The Iran war has cost 18 American lives and paused for an election rather than ending. And the boast that we “paid for the war” with Venezuela’s oil should trouble every Christian who hears it. Taking another nation’s resources as the price of our intervention is the logic of plunder. It may be legal under some arrangement; it is not righteous, and I would not want a stronger power to do the same to my own country.


**What I would do:** Restore the constitutional architecture of war that I set out in *[The Nuclear Option](https://drthomasforpresident.com/2026/08/260829-the-nuclear-option/)*: no sustained hostilities without a declaration or specific authorization from Congress; publish the war’s costs and casualties to the public monthly; and pursue an end to the Iran war that secures the Strait and verifies the end of the weapons program, rather than one timed to the electoral calendar. Venezuela’s oil belongs to the Venezuelan people; any arrangement should be transparent, temporary, and accountable to them, with an end date.


### 9. Guns


**His approach:** zero tax on suppressors and short-barreled arms, non-enforcement of registration, restored rights for veterans and dealers.


**Weighed:** I agree with every item, and the veterans’ change corrects a real injustice. But by my own durability test, the most important of these rests on enforcement discretion. The ATF itself warns that the policy could be reversed, and that unregistered items would then be contraband. A citizen who acts on a promise of non-enforcement may become a felon under the next administration’s memo.


**What I would do:** Ask Congress to remove suppressors and short-barreled long guns from the National Firearms Act by statute, so that no change of administration can turn a lawful owner into a criminal. Support the Swiss-style principle in my platform: training and readiness as the duty of free citizens, not merely their privilege.


### 10. Elections and the official language


**His approach:** the SAVE America Act — voter identification and proof of citizenship — and an executive order designating English as the official language.


**Weighed:** I support both aims, and have since 2010. But the English order is an executive order and can be revoked with a signature; it should be law. An unproven charge that 2020 was stolen weakens the case for election integrity, not strengthens it.


**What I would do:** Pass voter identification and a hand-countable paper ballot for every federal race, with public audits; and enact English as the official language by statute.

## PART FIVE: Held open


These are my positions, and I’m open to a better argument. In particular:


- **The crime decline.** I credit it as real and say it began before this administration. If careful work shows the administration’s enforcement strategy caused a measurable part of the 2025 drop, I will say so.

- **The tariff measure.** My equalizing tariff depends on estimating the regulatory burden in each sector. Reasonable people will dispute those estimates. I would rather argue about a published method than about an announced number.

- **The Iran war.** I do not have a President’s intelligence. If the course of the war shows that a pause before an election was the wisest military choice, I will acknowledge it.

- **Every figure in this paper.** I have cited my sources. If any is wrong, tell me, and I will correct it publicly.

## The measure that matters most


Suppose every boast at the rally had been true. Suppose prices had fallen, the wars had ended, and the drug prices were the lowest on earth. A nation can have all of that and still be a people who will not tell each other the truth. The President is not the cause of that condition; he is, in part, its product. We reward the men who tell us what we want to hear, about ourselves and about our opponents, and then we are surprised when our leaders do it.


The remedy is not a better candidate. It is a better people — citizens who check the numbers, who give credit where it is due even to men they oppose, who correct their own side out loud, and who refuse to call a neighbor a communist because he votes differently. If that people exists, any honest President can lead it. If it does not, no President can save it.
