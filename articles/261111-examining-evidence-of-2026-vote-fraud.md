# 261111 - Examining Evidence of 2026 Vote Fraud

# The 85/15 "Algorithm":


### A Pattern Is Not a Proof

*By Thomas Lee Abshier, ND — November 12, 2026*

A post is going around Facebook with the headline "TRUMP SHARES DEMOCRAT 85/15 CHEATING ALGORITHM USED IN THE MIDTERMS." It says that for every 1,000 Trump votes, 150 were "algorithmically switched" to Democrats, that the same ratio shows up in every state that went blue, and that "the numbers do not vary by state size or by the type of race." It ends by saying the certified results of the 2026 midterms are open to immediate challenge.

I have been asked about it several times this week, so I'll give my answer plainly. I stand on this side: **the 85/15 chart is not evidence that votes were switched, and the Facebook version of it is false in its central particulars.** The question under the post is fair. Can Americans trust the count? It deserves a better answer than a chart. I'll say below what that answer should look like.


---


## Where the claim came from

The story didn't start with Donald Trump, and it didn't start with a discovered piece of software. On November 6, an account called "Truth Is Freedom" posted a chart on X covering 59 Senate and governor races. It lined states up from reddest to bluest by their 2024 presidential results and fitted one formula to them:


 	- Republican 2026 share = 0.85 × Trump's 2024 share

 	- Democrat 2026 share = Harris's 2024 share + 0.15 × Trump's 2024 share


The author reported that Republicans kept about 85% of Trump's share in Senate races and 86% in governor races, that the formula "explains 88%" of the variation, and that the pattern "demands answers" through hand counts, poll-book comparisons, and voter-file checks. President Trump then reshared the post on Truth Social. As far as I can find, he added no written explanation and made no direct accusation. The Gateway Pundit named it the "85/15 Algorithm." Anonymous accounts turned it into "Trump identified the Democrats' cheating algorithm," and that is the version now on Facebook.

So the original chart was a curve fitted to results, together with a request for an investigation. Each retelling added certainty that the evidence never had. One more fact is worth knowing. On election night, before the chart existed, the President said: "Congratulations to the Democrats. They did a good job." He was also reported to have said he would accept the results. The man who said he had "identified the algorithm" had already congratulated the winners.

## What the chart actually measures

The chart measures vote **shares**, which are percentages. It does not measure votes. "150 of every 1,000 Trump votes were switched" sounds like a count of ballots, but the chart cannot tell you what any ballot did. A Republican getting 85% of Trump's percentage is consistent with at least three different things:


 	- Some Trump voters voted for the Democrat this time.

 	- Some Trump voters stayed home, as midterm voters often do when the president's name is not on the ballot.

 	- Votes were altered.


The chart cannot tell these three apart. Only the paper, the poll books, and the voter files can. That is why the chart's own author asked for hand counts, and he was right to ask.

The Democratic half of the formula adds nothing. In two-party shares, whatever the Republican does not get, the Democrat gets. Once you write the Republican line, the Democratic line follows automatically. It is not a second, independent confirmation that votes "moved."

The 88% figure also sounds more impressive than it is. States that were red in 2024 were mostly still red in 2026, and blue states were still blue. Any reasonable model built on the 2024 results will "explain" most of the 2026 variation, because partisan lean carries over from one election to the next. A high fit tells you that red states stayed redder than blue states. It does not tell you a machine was involved.

## Why a proportional drop is what a wave election looks like

Picture a national mood acting on Trump's 2024 coalition. Suppose that in every state, about one Trump voter in seven either stayed home or crossed over. You would get exactly an "85/15" pattern. A state where Trump won 70% would fall to about 60%, and a state where he won 40% would fall to 34%. The drop would be largest in the reddest states, because they have the most Trump voters to lose. No algorithm is needed. The pattern only requires that disappointed voters in Wyoming behave somewhat like disappointed voters in Ohio.

There was plenty to disappoint them. Trump's approval was near 39–40% going into the election, roughly ten points below his 2024 vote. Polls found voters focused on high prices, the war in Iran, and corruption. A plurality said their vote for Congress was a vote against the President. Large midterm swings against the president's party are also not new. Since 2000, the swing between presidential and midterm elections has ranged from about 4 points (2002) to over 17 points (2010). In 2018, Trump's own first midterm, total votes for House Republicans fell by about 19% from 2016. That was roughly the same proportional drop, eight years before anyone called it an algorithm.

None of this proves the 2026 count was clean. It does show that the pattern in the chart is what an ordinary, severe wave election produces. A pattern that ordinary causes produce cannot, by itself, prove an extraordinary cause.

## What the published results say

The Facebook post says "the numbers do not vary by state size or by the type of race." That claim can be checked against the published results, and it fails.


 	- The chart's own author reported a fit of 88%, not 100%. By his own numbers, 12% of the variation is unexplained, so the numbers do vary.

 	- When the fact-checking site Lead Stories rebuilt the chart from Wall Street Journal results, only a handful of states landed within a point or two of the formula. They were mostly a cluster of deep-red states: West Virginia, Oklahoma, Alabama, Kentucky, Arkansas, Tennessee, and Mississippi.

 	- Wyoming's Republican ran ahead of the formula, and Mississippi's ran behind it.

 	- In Maine, the Republican ran *ahead of Trump's 2024 share*. That is the opposite of a 15% cut.

 	- The original chart left out Alaska, Arkansas, and Montana. Its 20–80% vertical axis and stretched horizontal axis also made the lines look closer together than they are.


An algorithm that skims a fixed 15% does not let one state's Republican beat Trump's number. On the claim that the ratio is "identical in every state," the primary evidence contradicts the post, and I call that claim false. On the claim that votes were "switched," there is no evidence either way in the chart, because the chart is not the kind of evidence that could show it. I call that claim unsupported. "Unsupported" is a different verdict from "disproven." It is also not a basis for overturning an election.

## The strongest case on the other side

I want to give the dissenting analysts their best argument, because they deserve to be heard at full strength.

Their case runs like this. The fit is unusually tight in the deep-red states. The multiplier stays between 0.846 and 0.853 when any single state is dropped. It reportedly did better than standard swing models. A follow-up analysis put the odds of one feature of the pattern at about 1 in 649 under its author's historical assumptions. The seats that flipped (Texas, Kansas, Iowa, Ohio, South Carolina, and North Carolina) are just the ones the formula predicts would fall below 50% once Trump's 2024 share is under about 58.5%. They argue that real electorates are messier than this, and that something this tidy calls for an explanation.

My answer is that "calls for an explanation" is correct, and the ordinary explanation is the one given above: a broad national swing that hits each state roughly in proportion to its Trump vote. The "1 in 649" figure is only as good as the historical assumptions behind it. A wave this size breaks historical assumptions by definition, and even the outlet reporting it noted that 85% of a vote share does not mean 15% of voters switched. As for the flipped seats lining up with the formula, that is close to circular. A formula fitted to the results will predict the results it was fitted to.

## Why the concern is still plausible

I don't think the people sharing this post are fools, and I don't want to treat them that way. Their concern has real roots.


 	- **The swing feels unnatural.** In 2024, Republicans won the presidency, both houses, and all seven battleground states. Two years later Democrats took Senate seats in Kansas (for the first time since 1932) and Texas (for the first time since 1988). To someone who knocked doors in 2024, that change can look impossible.

 	- **Counting is opaque to the ordinary citizen.** Most votes are read by machines that the voter never sees inside. When the counting cannot be observed, trust has to rest on audits, and audits have not always been clean. One academic review of Georgia's 2020 hand-count "audit" found that the machine counts and the audit tallies disagreed substantially, even about how many ballots were cast. That finding did not show the outcome was wrong. It did show that an official assurance of a "100% audit" was not the same as a clean audit.

 	- **The stakes are enormous.** Control of the Senate decides judges, budgets, and wars. Wherever that much power and money are at stake, a temptation to cheat exists, and denials from the people who run elections are the testimony of interested parties.

 	- **A moral collapse has a political cost.** When a nation stops believing that its neighbors tell the truth, every loss looks like theft. That suspicion is itself a symptom worth taking seriously.


So the rumor is reaching for something real: an election system citizens can **verify** for themselves, so they do not have to trust anyone. That desire is right, and I share it.

## The righteous response

My test for any public act is whether it is righteous, whether it is loving, and whether I would want it done to me. Spreading the Facebook version of this claim fails the first test. The Scripture is plain: *"Thou shalt not bear false witness against thy neighbour."* **(Exodus 20:16)** The post accuses tens of thousands of election workers, Republican and Democrat alike, of a coordinated crime, and its evidence is a curve fit that cannot tell a stolen vote from a voter who stayed home. If my county clerk were accused that way, I would want the accuser to bring evidence first and the accusation second.

The same standard cuts the other way. *"A false balance is abomination to the LORD: but a just weight is his delight."* **(Proverbs 11:1)** A just count is a moral obligation, not a bureaucratic nicety, and citizens have every right to demand proof that the balance is true. *"He that is first in his own cause seemeth just; but his neighbour cometh and searcheth him."* **(Proverbs 18:17)** Searching is the citizen's duty. But the search has to be real: done with the ballots, not with a chart.

Here is what I would ask every reader to do:


 	- **Don't share the Facebook version.** It claims an identity of numbers that the published results contradict, and it puts words in the President's mouth.

 	- **Demand verification, not certainty.** Ask your state and county for hand-countable paper ballots, public precinct-level results, poll-book reconciliation, and randomly selected hand-count audits watched by observers from both parties. These are the same remedies the chart's author asked for, and they are the only kind of evidence that could settle the question.

 	- **If an audit finds a discrepancy, follow it to the end.** If it finds none, accept the result and turn to the harder work: winning back the neighbors who left.


I hold this position as an argument, not a decree. If someone shows me ballot-level evidence that votes were altered, I will change my view and say so publicly. Until then, I won't build a nation's trust in its elections on a ratio. The Republican defeat of 2026 has explanations that are more ordinary, more painful, and more useful to face: the prices people pay, a long war, and a leadership that many of its own voters no longer trusted. Facing those is the path back. Blaming an algorithm is a way to avoid them.
