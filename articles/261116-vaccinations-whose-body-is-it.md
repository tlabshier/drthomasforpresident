# 261116 - Vaccinations - Whose Body Is It?

# Whose Body Is It?


### The Amish, the Vaccine Mandate, and the Limits of the State

17 November 2026 · *Thomas Lee Abshier, ND*

I stand with the Amish. My reason is not that I am sure every vaccine is harmful, or that measles is harmless. It is that I do not believe God has given civil government authority over the inside of a man's body, nor over the body of a child whom God has entrusted to his parents.

A friend recently shared a Facebook post headed "BREAKING." It said that Amish schools in New York face crushing fines over vaccine mandates and that Amish families may have to flee the state. I checked the record. The core of the post is true, though the story is not new and some of its numbers are garbled. I set out the facts in the next section.

The facts are not the deepest part of the story. Beneath the fines lies a question older than any statute: *whose body is it?* When the State requires a substance to be injected into a person under threat of penalty, it is claiming jurisdiction over that person's flesh. I want to ask whether God has given the State that jurisdiction. I hold the position I take here firmly, and I hold it open to a better argument.

## What Happened in New York

New York allowed religious exemptions from school vaccination for decades. In June 2019, during a large measles outbreak, the legislature repealed them. Since then the only exemption is a medical one, and the law applies to private and religious schools as well as public ones.

The Amish run small, one-room schools on their own land, attended only by Amish children and funded without state aid. Three of them refused to enforce the mandate: Dygert Road School near Canajoharie, Pleasant View School in Heuvelton, and Shady Lane School in Clymer. State auditors visited in late 2021. In March 2022 the Department of Health found that the schools had let unvaccinated children attend past the 14-day grace period.

The Department treats each day an unvaccinated child attends as a separate violation, with a maximum fine of $2,000 each. According to the federal appeals court's opinion, the fines came to $118,000:

An administrative judge recommended against the fines for lack of adequate notice. The Department rejected that recommendation.

The Facebook post that brought this to my attention got the heart of it right. It garbled the arithmetic. It was not "one more day" that pushed the total to $118,000; the State counted a single day per child. The real danger is what lies ahead: every further day of attendance is, in the State's view, a fresh violation. The schools' lawyers say the Amish cannot pay without losing their land, and that they will leave New York if they lose in court.

The parents and schools sued in *Miller v. McDonald*. The courts have ruled this way so far:


 	- The federal district court dismissed the case in 2024, and the Second Circuit affirmed in 2025.

 	- On December 8, 2025, the Supreme Court vacated that judgment. It sent the case back to be reconsidered in light of *Mahmoud v.** Taylor*, its 2025 decision on parents' religious rights in schooling.

 	- On June 30, 2026, the Second Circuit ruled for New York again. It held that a vaccine requirement is a health measure that does not regulate religious instruction.

 	- On September 28, 2026, the Amish filed a second petition (No. 26-438). Among other things, it asks the Supreme Court to overrule *Employment Division v. Smith*, the 1990 decision that lets "neutral and generally applicable" laws burden religious practice with little scrutiny.

 	- On November 2, 2026, Alabama and sixteen other states filed a brief in support of the Amish.


As I write, the Court has not decided whether to hear the case.

## Whose Is the Body?

When the Pharisees tried to trap Jesus over taxes, He asked whose image was stamped on the coin. They answered, Caesar's. He replied:

*"Render therefore unto Caesar the things which are Caesar's; and unto God the things that are God's."* **(Matthew 22:21)**

The principle is that whatever bears an image belongs to the one whose image it bears. The coin bears Caesar's image, so tribute goes to Caesar. Man bears another image:

*"So God created man in his own image, in the image of God created he him; male and female created he them."* **(Genesis 1:27)**

If the coin belongs to Caesar because it bears his likeness, then man belongs to God because he bears God's. Paul says so plainly of the believer's body:

*"What? know ye not that your body is the temple of the Holy Ghost which is in you, which ye have of God, and ye are not your own? **For ye are bought with a price: therefore glorify God in your body, and in your spirit, which are God's."* **(1 Corinthians 6:19–20)**

This is the foundation of everything that follows. My body is not my own in the sense of being mine to abuse. But neither does it belong to the State. It belongs to God, who has given it into my keeping, and I will answer to Him for how I keep it. When a government claims the right to put a substance into that body over my objection, it claims a jurisdiction that God gave to me as His steward, not to Caesar.

Government is a latecomer in the order of things. God made man, then the family, and only later permitted the nation and its rulers. The later institution does not acquire rights over the earlier one simply by being larger or by holding the sword.

## What the State Is For

God did not want Israel to have a king. When the people demanded one, God told Samuel:

*"They have not rejected thee, but they have rejected me, that I should not reign over them."* **(1 Samuel 8:7)**

God's design was to rule each person directly, through conscience and His law. He let them have a king, but first He warned them what a king would do, and the first thing named was the taking of their children:

*"This will be the manner of the king that shall reign over you: He will take your sons, and appoint them for himself, for his chariots, and to be his horsemen; and some shall run before his chariots."* **(1 Samuel 8:11)**

In the New Testament, Paul tells us to submit to the governing authorities and to pay them tribute. He also tells us what they are for:

*"For he is the minister of God to thee for good. But if thou do that which is evil, be afraid; for he beareth not the sword in vain: for he is the minister of God, a revenger to execute wrath upon him that doeth evil."* **(Romans 13:4)**

*"For this cause pay ye tribute also: for they are God's ministers, attending continually upon this very thing."* **(Romans 13:6)**

The sword is given to restrain evil: to stop a man from offending against his neighbor. It is not given to compel good. Compelling good is the work of conscience, and conscience answers to God. When the State begins to compel what it judges to be good for a man's own body, it has stepped from the office of minister into the office of master. Since government is God's minister, its task is to do what God would do in that office, no more.

American law already concedes much of this, even if our courts rarely say why.


 	- **A competent adult may refuse life-saving treatment.** The Supreme Court recognized this right in *Cruzan* (1990), and it is why a Jehovah's Witness may refuse a blood transfusion and die. The State has no jurisdiction to force a remedy on a man to save his own life.

 	- **No one may be compelled to accept bodily risk to save another.** In *McFall v. **Shimp* (1978), Robert McFall was dying of aplastic anemia, and his cousin David Shimp was the only known bone-marrow match. Shimp refused. The judge called his refusal morally indefensible, but he would not order it. To force one man's body to serve another's, he reasoned, would open a door with no limit. McFall died two weeks later.

 	- **Even *Jacobson v. Massachusetts* (1905)**, the case defenders of mandates always cite, upheld a $5 fine for refusing smallpox vaccination. No one was held down.


So the law already rejects forced treatment "for your own good." It already refuses to conscript one body to save another. What remains of the case for mandates rests on one claim: that an unvaccinated person endangers his neighbors. That claim deserves a serious answer, and I give one below.

## Risk for Risk: Why a Vaccine Is Not a Transfusion

Courts do override parents in some medical emergencies. When a Jehovah's Witness child is bleeding to death, judges routinely order the transfusion. Defenders of mandates ask why a vaccine should be treated differently. I believe the difference is real.

A transfusion answers a present and nearly certain danger. The child is dying now, and the remedy carries a small risk. A vaccine answers a danger that may never come. It lowers the chance of catching a disease the child may never be exposed to. It also adds a small risk of injury that the child would not face if left alone.

Our own government admits that risk. Since 1986, federal law has run a compensation program for people injured by vaccines, paid for by a tax on every dose. A government does not build a compensation fund for injuries that do not happen.

A mandate, then, is not a rescue. It is the State ordering a person to accept one gamble in place of another. The question it poses is: *may the State compel a man to take on a risk that could injure or kill him, when he has chosen to set himself apart from the danger?* If the law will not compel David Shimp to accept the small risks of marrow donation to save his dying cousin, I do not see where it finds authority to compel an Amish child to accept the risks of injection to protect strangers from an exposure that may never occur.

Honesty requires me to say that the two gambles are not equal once a person is exposed. In the public-health record, measles kills roughly 1 to 3 of every 1,000 children who catch it, and causes brain inflammation in about 1 in 1,000. Serious reactions to the measles vaccine are far rarer. If the disease were certain to arrive, the arithmetic would favor the vaccine.

But the arithmetic contains a hidden term: the likelihood of exposure at all. A community that separates itself lowers that likelihood. The State's calculation assumes every child lives in the general population. The Amish child does not. Weighing that probability is properly the parents' judgment, made before God for their own children. It is not a calculation the State is entitled to make for them and then enforce with fines.

## What God Prescribed for Contagion

If government is God's minister, the question is what God Himself did when contagious disease threatened His people. The law of Moses answers directly. The priest examined the person with the plague and, if it was uncertain, shut him up for seven days to watch it **(Leviticus 13:4–5)**. If the disease was confirmed:

*"All the days wherein the plague shall be in him he shall be defiled; he is unclean: he shall dwell alone; without the camp shall his habitation be."* **(Leviticus 13:46)**

The pattern is separation of the sick. God did not command that the healthy be forced to take a remedy. He commanded that the one carrying the disease be kept from carrying it to his neighbor. That is exactly the kind of act the sword exists for: restraining a man from harming another.

This gives us a principle that honors both the conscience of the one who refuses and the safety of his neighbor. **The State may separate the sick. It may not seize the bodies of the well.** Quarantine and isolation during an outbreak are within its office. Compelled injection of healthy people, as the price of educating their own children, is not.

The Amish have already done what Leviticus describes, and done it in advance. They live apart. Their schools are on their own land and are attended only by their own children. If any community in New York has kept its contagion "without the camp," it is this one. Fining them by the child and by the day is not public health. It is a penalty for refusing the State's remedy.

## Who Stewards the Child?

The hardest form of the question is about children. A child did not choose his parents' faith or the risk they accept on his behalf. So who decides for him, his parents or the State?

The question is often put as whether the State or the parents "own" the child. I think both answers are wrong. Children are not owned by anyone but God:

*"Lo, children are an heritage of the LORD: and the fruit of the womb is his reward."* **(Psalm 127:3)**

A heritage from the Lord is a trust. God has placed each child into the keeping of his father and mother, and He has told them what that keeping requires:

*"And, ye fathers, provoke not your children to wrath: but bring them up in the nurture and admonition of the Lord."* **(Ephesians 6:4)**

The parents are stewards, and they will answer to God for their stewardship. The State is not a co-steward. Its office toward the family is the same as its office toward any man: to restrain offense. When a father beats his son or starves his daughter, he commits an offense against his neighbor, and the child is his nearest neighbor. The sword rightly falls there. The same holds when parents refuse care for a child who is gravely ill right now.

I see a danger in going further. Any claim that the State may overrule parents whenever it judges its own choice better makes every child, in the end, a ward of the government. A people whose children belong to the collective are not free. They are subject to the will of whoever governs the collective. That is not the liberty we have in Christ, who bought us and made us answerable to God rather than to men.

So the honest test is this: **is declining a vaccine an offense against the child, or a judgment of conscience about the child's good?** A parent who weighs a remote risk of disease against a small risk of injury, inside a community that has set itself apart, and who decides before God to decline, has not abused his child. He has done what stewards do: made a hard judgment and accepted responsibility for it. Reasonable people may judge differently. But a difference in judgment is not an offense, and only offense calls for the sword.

## The Hard Cases, Faced Honestly

An argument that ignores the strongest evidence against it does not deserve to persuade anyone. Here is that evidence.

**Separation leaks.** In 2014, two young Amish men returned to Ohio from typhoon-relief work in the Philippines, where measles was raging. They were carrying the disease. Over the next four months, 383 people in nine Ohio counties caught measles; 380 of them were Amish. The researchers found that most transmission happened within households. There were 9 hospitalizations and no deaths.

**Children can die.** In 2025, measles spread through the Mennonite community of Gaines County, Texas. By the time the outbreak ended, 762 Texans had been infected, and two unvaccinated school-age girls from that community had died. They were the first U.S. measles deaths in a decade. Some in the community believe the girls died of underlying conditions or poor hospital care rather than measles. The state reported that the second child had no underlying conditions. I treat both accounts as testimony from interested parties, but I cannot dismiss the deaths.

**"Your vaccine protects you" is not a complete answer.** Two doses of the measles vaccine prevent the disease in about 97 percent of people, not all. Infants under about a year old cannot yet be vaccinated. So an unvaccinated child can endanger someone even when everyone else has done what the State asks.

These facts sharpen the question rather than settle it. Notice where the danger fell in both outbreaks: almost entirely inside the communities that had made the choice. In an Amish school, the people most at risk are mostly the community's own children and infants. The hard case is therefore not the Amish endangering New York. It is the Amish child, and the Amish infant, whose parents have chosen for them.

My answer is the one I gave above. God has entrusted those children to their parents, and the parents will answer to Him. The State's rightful role is not small: it can tell the truth about the risks on both sides, offer the vaccine freely to any who want it, and, when disease actually appears, separate the sick as Leviticus prescribes. What it may not do is take the stewardship away from the parents because it would have chosen differently.

I recognize this is the point where a reasonable person may part with me. If a better argument shows that declining vaccination is itself an offense against the child, as starving him would be, I will have to reconsider. I have not yet seen that argument made.

## Why the Concern Remains

Even after facing the hard cases, I believe the Amish concern is well founded, for five reasons.


 	- **The State has made itself judge of theology.** By ending religious exemptions while keeping medical ones, New York decided that a doctor's objection counts and a believer's does not. Once the State begins sorting conscientious objections into valid and invalid ones, it has taken a seat that the First Amendment was written to keep empty. The question in a free country is not whether the Amish reading of Scripture is correct. It is whether the government has jurisdiction to overrule a conscience it disagrees with.

 	- **The penalty is built like an eviction.** A fine of up to $2,000 per child, per day, applied to one-room schools run by farmers, is not sized to change a policy. It is sized to end a way of life. The State's own administrative judge recommended against the fines for lack of notice, and the Department overruled him. When a penalty can only be paid by selling the land, the community is being told to leave.

 	- **The court called it a health measure, and that misses what is lost.** The Second Circuit reasoned that the law does not touch religious instruction. But its effect is to close the schools in which that instruction happens. A rule that leaves the faith untouched in principle while shutting the schoolhouse in practice burdens the faith all the same.

 	- **The incentives run one way.** Federal law since 1986 has largely shielded vaccine makers from ordinary injury lawsuits and routed claims to the government's compensation program. Mandates guarantee those makers a market. I do not claim this explains New York's law. I do say that when the people who profit from a product cannot easily be sued over it, and the State requires everyone to buy it, citizens are right to examine the arrangement closely.

 	- **It fails the test I apply to every public act.** I ask three questions. Is it righteous: does God establish it? If so, is it loving? And would I want it done to me? Scripture gives the State the sword against evil, not authority over the bodies of the well, so the first question is not satisfied. A neighbor fined off his land for keeping his child's body as he believes God requires would not feel loved. And I would not want the State to inject my own child over my objection, enforced by fines until I sold my home.


There is also something people sense before they can argue it: putting a substance into a healthy child by force of law feels unnatural. That intuition is not an argument by itself. But it is worth heeding, because it points toward the line I have tried to draw. The State may restrain the sick from harming others. It may not treat the healthy as raw material for its plans.

## Where I Stand, and What I Would Do

I stand with the Amish families of New York. I hold that the body belongs to God, that children are entrusted by God to their parents, and that the State's sword is given to restrain offense, not to compel what it judges to be good. Within that order, I hold that civil government may separate the sick in an outbreak. It may not require the well to be injected as the price of educating their own children.

School policy belongs to the states and to local communities, not to Washington. A President's main tool here is his voice, and I would use it. As President I would:


 	- **Urge the Supreme Court, through the Solicitor General, to hear *Miller v. McDonald*** and to reconsider *Employment Division v. Smith*, so that laws burdening religious exercise must again meet a serious test.

 	- **Call on New York and every state without one to restore the religious exemption**, and on all states to adopt the principle of Leviticus: separate the sick during an actual outbreak rather than mandate injection of the healthy at all times.

 	- **Tell the truth on both sides of the ledger.** Federal health agencies should publish the benefits and the injury risks of each vaccine plainly, so that parents can exercise their stewardship with full information, and should make vaccines freely available to all who choose them.

 	- **Oppose any federal funding conditioned on states removing religious exemptions.**


I offer this as an argument, not a decree. If someone shows me from Scripture or from evidence that I have drawn the line in the wrong place, I will listen. Until then, I will stand where I believe God has placed the line: the State may restrain the hand that strikes a neighbor, and it may not lay its hand on a body that belongs to God.

## Sources and Their Limits

The facts in this essay lean on the mainstream record: court opinions, the Supreme Court docket, public-health studies, and established news outlets. Here is where that record may be incomplete.


 	- **The fine amounts and their method** come from the Second Circuit's opinion, which summarizes the State's own administrative record. The per-school student counts are inferred from the State's stated method, not reported directly.

 	- **The measles death and complication rates and the vaccine's effectiveness** come from public-health agencies, which are also the advocates of mandates. I treat their figures as the best available, not as disinterested.

 	- **Vaccine injury rates** are harder to know than disease rates. Federal reporting of injuries is largely passive, and the compensation program records only claims that are filed and won. The true rate may be higher than official figures suggest; it is unlikely to be lower.

 	- **The Texas deaths** are reported by the state and by news outlets; members of the community dispute the cause. I have presented both.

 	- **The Amish plan to leave New York** is their lawyers' public statement, made during litigation. It is a stated intention, not an accomplished fact.
