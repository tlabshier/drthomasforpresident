# Transformation of a Nation: Peace, War, and Solutions at the Edge

**An essay responding to the problems raised in the 28 August 2026 conversation between [Tucker Carlson and Joe Kent.](https://tuckercarlson.com/tucker-show-joe-kent-082826?utm_campaign=20260831_aug31dailybrief&utm_medium=email&utm_source=iterable&utm_content=joemaxwell)**

---

## Introduction: Naming the Problems

A widely viewed conversation between Tucker Carlson and Joe Kent has put into words the sense that our nation is drifting toward catastrophe — possibly nuclear catastrophe — through wars with no defined outcome or congressional declaration, directed by processes no voter can see, funded by incentives no citizen would approve, with unthinkable consequences if taken to their end.

The conversation makes many specific claims — about deliberations inside the Pentagon, about who directs the war against Russia, about the influence of foreign governments and donors over American policy, about why particular decisions were made. I cannot verify each allegation. Some may be overstated, some may prove worse than stated. But the *problems* the conversation names do not depend on any single allegation being precisely correct, because every one of them is a problem of structure and of heart, visible in the public record of the last twenty-five years: wars entered without defined objectives and exited without honesty; the erosion of the nuclear taboo in public speech; the capture of policy by money; the atrophy of Congress's war powers; the loss of civilian mastery over the permanent security apparatus; the treatment of allied interests as commands; and beneath all of it, a governing class in which too few will say "no" at the cost of their careers, and a citizenry too distracted and too divided to make them.

This essay is written according to the discipline that governs everything on this site. Exposure has its place, and we let others do that work. Our work is the other half — to ask, of every problem, for the solution that heals: *What could be done here that is long-term curative?* What follows are solutions — constitutional, diplomatic, structural, and spiritual — offered in the conviction that a nation, like a soul, is not saved by hating its diseases but by turning toward health. "Come now, and let us reason together, saith the LORD" (Isaiah 1:18).
## I. The First Solution Is a Posture: Sobriety Without Panic

The conversation opens with a true observation about human nature: we treat gunshots like fireworks and house fires like burnt toast. Denial is the mind's first anesthetic, and nations use it as freely as individuals. But the answer to denial is not its opposite — panic — which paralyzes judgment as surely as denial postpones it. Scripture prescribes the third posture: "God hath not given us the spirit of fear; but of power, and of love, and of a sound mind" (2 Timothy 1:7). And: "Watch ye, stand fast in the faith, quit you like men, be strong" (1 Corinthians 16:13).

A sound mind looks at the nuclear question directly and states the truth without flinching: there is no such thing as a small nuclear war between great powers, no "tactical" use that stays tactical once the taboo is broken, and no national objective — none — worth the risk of ending the human story. The first solution, then, costs nothing and is available today to every citizen and every official: *stop treating the unthinkable as a policy option in our speech.* Words are the first weapons staged. A President's rhetoric of "total destruction," a briefing slide that prices annihilation as an option, a commentator's casual talk of limited strikes — these are not analysis; they are the normalization that precedes catastrophe. The tongue "is a little member, and boasteth great things. Behold, how great a matter a little fire kindleth!" (James 3:5). A nation that will not speak of first use as speakable has taken the first real step back from it.
## II. Restore the Constitutional Architecture of War

Nearly every problem in the conversation traces to a single structural failure: America now enters, escalates, and prolongs wars without the constitutional act designed to prevent exactly this. The Founders lodged the war power in Congress — "The Congress shall have Power … To declare War" (Article I, Section 8) — precisely because they had watched kings spend nations into ruin through wars of pride and faction. A declaration forces three things no executive process forces: a public *definition* of the enemy and the objective, a public *vote* that puts every representative on record before the blood flows, and a public *ownership* of the costs.

Joe Kent's diagnosis of the Iran war — that we entered without stating what victory was, then moved the goalposts repeatedly — is the textbook description of an undeclared war. The cure is as old as the parchment:

**No sustained military campaign without a congressional declaration or authorization that states the objective in one sentence, the conditions of termination, and the sunset date.** If the objective cannot be written in one sentence, the war cannot be won, because no one knows what winning is. If Congress will not vote for it in daylight, the Republic has no business doing it in the dark. And when the stated objective is achieved — or proven unachievable — the authorization expires, and the war ends, without requiring anyone to perform the theater of "victory."

Our Lord Himself gave nations the auditing rule: "Or what king, going to make war against another king, sitteth not down first, and consulteth whether he be able with ten thousand to meet him that cometh against him with twenty thousand? Or else, while the other is yet a great way off, he sendeth an ambassage, and desireth conditions of peace" (Luke 14:31–32). Count the cost *first* — publicly, honestly, arithmetically — and where the count fails, send the embassy. This is not weakness; it is the Word of God on statecraft.
## III. Rebuild the Nuclear Firewall

Whatever occurred in any particular meeting, the public record is sufficient: doctrines are being revised, threats of civilizational destruction have been uttered by heads of state on public platforms, and the vocabulary of "tactical" nuclear use has re-entered respectable discussion for the first time in a generation. The firewall between conventional and nuclear war — the taboo that has protected every child on earth since 1945 — is a *maintained* structure, and it is not being maintained. Solutions:

**First, a declaratory covenant.** The United States should adopt, by statute and by presidential declaration together, a No First Use policy: nuclear weapons exist to guarantee that they are never used, and America will never be the nation that reopens that door. Critics say ambiguity deters; eighty years of evidence says the taboo deters, and the taboo is dying of ambiguity. Let America lead the recovery of the norm it once wrote.

**Second, a procedural firewall.** No single human being — no president of any party, however trusted — should hold unilateral authority to initiate nuclear use where no nuclear attack is underway. First use, if it is ever to be lawful at all, must require the same constitutional act as war itself: the affirmative consent of Congress. Retaliatory authority under attack remains as it is; initiating authority must never again rest on one man's mood, one faction's pressure, or one bad night.

**Third, an ethical standard for service.** Any official who proposes offensive nuclear use against a non-nuclear adversary has, by that act, disqualified himself from the stewardship of such weapons — not as a matter of criminal theater, but as the simple application of the fiduciary principle: the trustee who proposes burning down the trust is removed from the trust. And every officer and civil servant in the chain must know, in law and in culture, that refusing to plan the indefensible is protected, honored service.

"I have set before you life and death, blessing and cursing: therefore choose life, that both thou and thy seed may live" (Deuteronomy 30:19). That verse is the whole of nuclear policy, if we will have it.
## IV. End the Two Wars: The Ambassage of Peace

The conversation's most practical finding is also its most hopeful: both conflicts remain resolvable by decision — hard decisions, humbling decisions, but decisions within our power. The path in each case is the one the King described: objectives, as escalated, cannot be achieved at any acceptable cost; therefore redefine the objective honestly, and send the embassy.

**Iran.** The achievable American interests are three: the Strait of Hormuz open, no Iranian nuclear weapon, and no American war of occupation in Persia. All three are negotiable; none is obtainable by escalation, and the third is forfeited by it. The curative course: state that regime change is not an American objective — because it is not an American *right*; "the LORD … hath determined the times before appointed, and the bounds of their habitation" (Acts 17:26) — and trade what Iran needs (sanctions relief, unfrozen assets, security from decapitation) for what the region and the world need (the Strait open, verifiable non-weaponization, an end to proxy attacks). Enforce it with inspection, not invasion. And then pursue the deeper policy that no missile can accomplish: *transformation over containment.* Seventy percent of Iran is under forty, educated, and starving for freedom. Reach the Iranian people — by every broadcast, network, and channel that cannot be confiscated — with respect, with truth, with commerce where possible, and yes, with the gospel of Jesus Christ, which is already growing in Iran faster than nearly anywhere on earth. Change the people, and in time the government must follow; manage the government forever, and you must manage it forever. Persia has heard from God before: Cyrus, whom the LORD said, "He is my shepherd, and shall perform all my pleasure" (Isaiah 44:28). Our quarrel is not with the Persian people, and our policy should prove it.

**Russia and Ukraine.** Honesty first, because policy built on flattering lies kills by the hundred thousand: control of the Donbas is not existential to the United States. It never was. What *is* vital to the United States — and to every nation — is that this war not become a NATO–Russia war, and that the nuclear threshold never be approached. From honesty, the settlement follows the shape every sober analyst has seen for years: an armistice on the line of contact; ironclad, internationally guaranteed security for what remains of Ukraine without NATO membership — the very issue over which the war began; the end of Western strikes into Russia and Russian strikes into Ukraine; elections in Ukraine, so that the nation being defended actually governs itself; and a reconstruction compact that rebuilds Ukrainian towns rather than dividing Ukrainian assets among foreign funds. America's role is to stop feeding the war it can no longer pretend merely to observe: end the offensive intelligence targeting, condition every dollar on movement toward the table, and put the full weight of the presidency behind the ceasefire. The extermination of a generation of Ukrainian men — and now the conscription of Ukraine's daughters — for a stalemate everyone privately acknowledges, is not the defense of a nation; it is the consumption of one. "Blessed are the peacemakers: for they shall be called the children of God" (Matthew 5:9). Note well: not the appeasers, and not the crusaders — the *makers* of peace, which is work, craft, and courage.

And to those who say negotiated settlement is "losing": the conversation itself contains the answer. Empires that cannot admit limits destroy themselves attempting the impossible — Napoleon at Moscow, and worse. The strongest thing a great nation can do is the thing pride cannot do: "Pride goeth before destruction, and an haughty spirit before a fall" (Proverbs 16:18); "Better is the end of a thing than the beginning thereof: and the patient in spirit is better than the proud in spirit" (Ecclesiastes 7:8). America does not need these wars to be great. America needs to be *good* to be great — and good nations do not feed meat grinders.
## V. Break the Purchase of Policy: The Prostitution Analysis

Beneath both wars, the conversation keeps striking the same rock: incentives. Weapons contractors whose revenues require conflict; an intelligence apparatus whose budgets require an enemy; legislators whose campaigns require donors; donors whose donations require wars. I have written elsewhere that this system deserves its true name. When a legislator trades his vote for campaign money, the transaction is not "adjacent to" prostitution — it *is* prostitution on the largest scale: the sale of what must never be sold, with the price merely under negotiation. Such a man uses his position as the nation's guardian of its treasure to trade national loss for personal benefit.

Structural solutions exist, and a serious nation would enact them: full same-day transparency of every dollar in politics; the closure of the revolving door between the Pentagon, the agencies, and the contractors they fund (a real interval and disconnection with influence); war-industry profits audited and capped during hostilities, so that no company's quarterly earnings ever again depend on a war continuing; and foreign-influence laws enforced equally against every nation's lobby — every nation's, ally and adversary alike — because the principle is universal: *American war policy may be advised by friends, but it may be purchased by no one.* We can love an ally and still say what the conversation struggles to say cleanly: an alliance is a friendship between sovereigns, not a power of attorney. The measure is symmetrical and fair — no foreign government's electoral calendar, no donor network's passions, no lobby's leverage, foreign or domestic, may set the objectives for which American children are asked to die. That standard defends the alliance too;  for nothing destroys an alliance faster than the suspicion of vassalage.

But there is a deeper analysis: the structural reforms, though necessary, are secondary. A bought government is the symptom; a buyable people is the disease. The lobbyist's check has power only over the man with a price. *"For the love of money is the root of all evil: which while some coveted after, they have erred from the faith"* (1 Timothy 6:10). The final solution to graft is not a regulation; it is a citizenry — and therefore a governing class drawn from it — of the *unbuyable*: men and women who have settled, before the offer comes, that they are not for sale in any currency: not money, not office, not survival, not reputation. "They overcame him by the blood of the Lamb, and by the word of their testimony; and they loved not their lives unto the death" (Revelation 12:11). A world where the billion is offered, and everyone says no is a world where the power of money simply evaporates — for money's power was never anything but the purchase of consent. That world is built one transformed heart at a time, and building it is the actual platform of this candidacy.
## VI. Restore Civilian Mastery and the Courage to Resign

The conversation reveals indicators that we now have a government that its own elected head cannot steer — handwritten notes passed to aides because the system cannot be trusted, wars continuing on autopilot because no one shut off the machinery, and an administrative culture in which, as Kent puts it, the moral compass has eroded to "go along with whatever the boss says." Solutions run in both directions — mastery from above, conscience from below:

**Mastery from above.** Elected control of the security state cannot be a slogan; it must be machinery. Every covert action and offensive intelligence program should carry the personal written authorization of the President and expire automatically unless renewed — so that nothing lethal ever again "keeps churning in the background" by default. The agencies' operational leadership must include enough appointees who actually understand the buildings they run to make direction real. And Congress must recover its purse-string discipline: programs that evade oversight lose funding, period. A republic in which "no elected leader has control" is not a republic, whatever its ceremonies; restoring that control is not a partisan project but the precondition of every other one.

**Conscience from below.** Jethro gave Moses the personnel policy of heaven: "Thou shalt provide out of all the people able men, such as fear God, men of truth, hating covetousness" (Exodus 18:21). Fear of God before fear of the boss; truth before access; hatred of covetousness before ambition — staff a government on those three filters and most of these problems never form. We must honor, in law and in culture, the official who says "I will not plan that" — protect him, promote his kind, and tell his story to our children. Daniel served a pagan empire faithfully for decades and was found "faithful, neither was there any error or fault found in him" (Daniel 6:4) — yet three times he refused the state outright, at the risk of the furnace and the lions' den, when it commanded what God forbade. That is the complete job description of the public servant: maximal faithfulness, absolute limits. A government full of Daniels cannot be marched into an indefensible war, because at every desk the order meets a conscience that outranks it.
## VII. The Immune System: A People No Propaganda Can Stampede

Every solution above can be undone by one thing: a public that can be stampeded. Wars of choice are marketed, and the marketing works because each citizen, alone before his screen, cannot check what he is told. The remedy I have carried for forty years is the reason this candidacy exists. In 1986 I had a vision for what I call the *voting network* — the deliberate construction of channels by which citizens compare notes laterally, neighbor to neighbor, community to community, until the whole village has conferred and no authority can shout down what everyone can see. When the child says the emperor has no clothes and the whole crowd has already compared notes, the parade is over. A populace immunized against propaganda is the only permanent check on the war machinery, the moral decay, and cancel culture because it is the only check that cannot be captured—there is no headquarters to buy.

This is also why the daily work of this campaign is not rallies but *counsel*: taking each day's news and asking, in public, "What would be long-term curative here? What would Jesus Christ say to the nation about this?" — and inviting every American into the practice of asking it themselves. Truth is found by triangulation — many independent witnesses converging — and a nation that practices triangulation together cannot be herded into Armageddon by a slide deck. "Prove all things; hold fast that which is good" (1 Thessalonians 5:21).
## VIII. The Root Solution: National Repentance and the Renewal of Hearts

Now let us go to the bottom, because every solution above — declarations, firewalls, treaties, reforms, networks — is a fence around a field, and the field itself is the human heart. "From whence come wars and fightings among you? come they not hence, even of your lusts that war in your members? Ye lust, and have not: ye kill, and desire to have, and cannot obtain" (James 4:1–2). The apostle's diagnosis of war has never been improved upon. Empires overreach because pride overreaches; policy is bought because hearts are for sale; wars continue because grievance and greed renew their subscriptions daily. Structural reform without heart reform is a new lock on a door whose owner keeps selling the key.

Therefore, the deepest plank of this platform is that America's security problem is, at root, a righteousness problem, and its solution is, at root, a return. "Righteousness exalteth a nation: but sin is a reproach to any people" (Proverbs 14:34). "If my people, which are called by my name, shall humble themselves, and pray, and seek my face, and turn from their wicked ways; then will I hear from heaven, and will forgive their sin, and will heal their land" (2 Chronicles 7:14). The healing of the land is covenanted to the humbling of the people — not first to the brilliance of its diplomats, though we should have brilliant diplomats; not first to the wisdom of its doctrines, though we should have wise doctrines; first to the turning of its heart.

So the program of renewal runs beneath the program of reform: the transformation of hearts, one by one, widely — through the gospel of Jesus Christ preached, taught, and lived; through fellowships where truth is triangulated in honest conversation; through the raising of children in the Word; through a citizenry learning again to be good, so that its government may again be good, so that its strength may again be safe to possess. Tocqueville's old observation remains the operating truth of this Republic: America is great because America is good, and when America ceases to be good, she will cease to be great. The wars, the graft, the drift toward the unthinkable — these are what ceasing looks like from inside. The return is available at any hour. "For all this his anger is not turned away, but his hand is stretched out still" (Isaiah 9:12).
## Conclusion: Choose Life

Here, then, is the answer this site gives to the fears that conversation voiced — not a denial of the danger, and not a wallowing in it, but a path out of it:

Speak of nuclear war as unspeakable, and rebuild the firewall in law. Count the cost of every war in daylight before it begins, and let no war continue that cannot state its object in a sentence. Send the ambassage of peace to Tehran and to Moscow — trading pride for the Strait, the Donbas illusion for the survival of Ukraine's next generation — and let America be strong enough to stop. Name the purchase of policy what it is, close the revolving doors, and grow the only incorruptible thing: unbuyable souls. Put the elected republic back in command of its machinery, and staff it with men who fear God, love truth, and hate covetousness. Build the networks by which a free people confers and cannot be stampeded. And beneath it all, turn — humble ourselves, pray, seek His face, and let the God who heals lands heal this one.

"I have set before you life and death, blessing and cursing: therefore choose life, that both thou and thy seed may live" (Deuteronomy 30:19).

That is the platform. Choose life — for your children, for the Ukrainian conscript and the Russian mother, for the Persian student and the Israeli child, for the soldier and the citizen alike. It is not naïve; it is the only realism left. The alternative is being soberly discussed in conference rooms, and it must never leave them but by dying there.
