# 261018 Coercion in Government

# Neither Bought nor Bullied: What the Guilfoyle–Deters Affair Should Teach Us


*Dr. Thomas | 18 October 2026*


## What happened


On October 7, *The Wall Street Journal* published text and Signal messages provided by Eric Deters, a Kentucky donor and former congressional candidate, which appear to show Kimberly Guilfoyle, then U.S. Ambassador to Greece, asking him to pay roughly $100,000 on her American Express account before her July 2025 Senate confirmation hearing, while discussing her access to senior officials and urging him not to put their financial dealings in writing. Deters says he refused. He also told the *Journal* that over several years he paid Ms. Guilfoyle about $300,000, Donald Trump Jr. $200,000, and Eric Trump $100,000 to speak at his Freedom Fest events, and that after the 2024 election he sought Ms. Guilfoyle’s help with disputes he had with federal agencies.


On October 13, the White House asked Ms. Guilfoyle to resign, and she did. She denies seeking any improper payment; her lawyers have called the messages fake, and Mr. Deters a blackmailer. On October 15, Mr. Deters sued her for defamation and promised a “16-minute recording” that he said might “bring down the entire Trump administration” and end a future Vance–Rubio ticket. He has said publicly that his real aim is to force the extradition of Dr. Abubakar Atiq Durrani, a spine surgeon indicted in 2013 for health-care fraud who fled to Pakistan — and that if the administration does not act, he will “never shut up.” He calls this “a promise, not a threat.”


On October 16, he released the recording. It is not a recording of Ms. Guilfoyle. It is a phone call in which a third party in Greece accuses her of taking bribes and other misconduct. None of it has been tested.


I would ordinarily leave most of this story alone. But three moral questions sit inside it, and each of them will come up again under every administration, so I will take a position on each.


## The test


I judge every public act by three questions, in order:


- **Is it righteous?** — Is it what God establishes as just, not merely what is legal or useful?

- **If so, is it loving?** — Does it do real good to the neighbor, not merely please him?

- **Would I want it done to me?** — Not by my appetites of the moment, but by the self I want to be before God, feeling the other man’s position in my own body, mind, and heart.


## First question: selling access, and buying it


If the messages are authentic, a nominee for an ambassadorship solicited a private payment from a man who wanted her help with the government. That fails the first question outright. *“Thou shalt take no gift: for the gift blindeth the wise, and perverteth the words of the righteous”* **(Exodus 23:8)**. An office of public trust is held for the whole people. The moment its holder accepts private money from one citizen with business before that office, every other citizen has been quietly disinherited.


I want to be equally plain about the other side of the transaction, because public outrage tends to land on one party only. By his own account, Mr. Deters paid large speaking fees to people close to the President and then asked for help with federal agencies. Whatever the legal status of those payments, a man who pays the household of power and then asks the household for favors is trying to buy what isn’t for sale. The giver of the gift is under the same judgment as the taker. Both halves fail the first question, and so the second and third never arise.


Ms. Guilfoyle denies it all, and she has not had a trial. I am not pronouncing on her guilt. I am stating the standard by which her conduct, and his, should be judged once the facts are known.


## Second question: leverage dressed as justice


Mr. Deters says he is “playing hardball” for the patients Dr. Durrani is accused of harming. If the accusations in that 2013 indictment are true — that a surgeon talked patients into unnecessary spinal operations and billed public and private insurers millions for them — then those patients were grievously wronged, and their government owes them a serious effort to bring the accused to trial. On that much I agree with him.


But the method fails. “Do what I demand, or I will keep releasing damaging material and never stop talking” is a coercive structure, whatever we call it and whether it is lawful. Apply the third question: would any of us want the government’s prosecutorial decisions — about us, or about the man who wronged us — to be set by whoever holds the most embarrassing tape? A just cause does not sanctify an unjust means. If extradition is right, it is right because the evidence and the law require it, and it should be pursued openly through the Justice Department, the State Department, and Congress. If it is decided because a donor turned up the volume, then the next decision will be made the same way, for a worse cause.


A government that can be bought and a government that can be bullied are the same government. Both have handed their judgment to whoever has the leverage.


## Third question: the “bombshell”


The recording was promised as proof that could topple an administration. What was released was one man repeating another man’s accusations. That is not evidence; it is hearsay about hearsay.


Scripture is unusually specific here.* “Thou shalt not raise a false report”* **(Exodus 23:1)**. *“At the mouth of two witnesses, or at the mouth of three witnesses, shall the matter be established”* **(Deuteronomy 19:15)**. *“He that is first in his own cause seemeth just; but his neighbour cometh and searcheth him”* **(Proverbs 18:17)**. And: *“Thou shalt not go up and down as a talebearer among thy people”*** (Leviticus 19:16)**.


Spreading untested accusations of bribery and drug use fails the first question, and it plainly fails the third — none of us would want our own reputation tried by whatever a stranger said on a recorded phone call. This is also where citizens carry their own responsibility. I hold that a juror has not only the power but the duty to judge both the facts and the justice of the law before him. That same posture belongs to every citizen in the public square: withhold the verdict until witnesses can be questioned and the matter is established, and refuse to pass along what you cannot establish. The share button is a form of testimony.


None of this means you should ignore the accusations. Serious allegations against a former ambassador— bribery and anything touching the disclosure of government information—should go to investigators who can put witnesses under oath. That is exactly the point: they belong in a forum where they can be tested, not in a livestream.


## What I would do


Righteous government is not only a matter of personal virtue in office; you can build it into the structure. I advocate the following, and I hold each open to a better argument:


- **Treat solicitation by a nominee as disqualifying.** Any request by a nominee or appointee for a private payment, loan, or debt relief from a person with business before the government should be a reportable offense, and the person solicited should have a protected way to report it — rather than the choice between paying, staying silent, or going to the press.

- **Disclose the household, not just the official.** Disclose paid speaking engagements, gifts, and debts involving the immediate families of the President, Vice President, cabinet members, and ambassadors when the payer has, or later seeks, business with the government. Influence travels through families as easily as through offices.

- **Decide prosecutions on the record, not on pressure.** Answer requests to pursue a fugitive like Dr. Durrani on the merits, in writing, and make them public once doing so would not compromise the case—so neither a donor’s favor nor a donor’s threat can move the decision.

- **Investigate allegations where they can be tested.** Accusations like those in the released recording should be referred to inspectors general and the Justice Department, with the accuser’s witnesses under oath. A charge worth making is worth making where perjury is a crime.


## The deeper point


The ordinary reaction to a story like this is to pick a side: defend the official because she is on our team, or cheer the donor because he is exposing corruption. Both reactions miss the moral center. The question is not which party to this quarrel we prefer. The question is whether we will have a government that answers to righteousness — one that cannot be purchased by a gift or steered by a threat — and a citizenry that refuses to convict on rumor. Neither party meets that standard. It belongs to God, and it binds all of us, beginning with the man writing this.
