# 261110 - Hegseth Pending Impeachment Analyzed

# Impeachment Without Inquiry:


### The Hegseth Articles and the Duty Congress Keeps Avoiding

*A position paper
Dr Thomas · November 10, 2026*

**Source article:** [The latest: Hegseth slams his potential impeachment as a political spectacle as House vote looms](https://www.msn.com/en-us/news/other/the-latest-hegseth-slams-his-potential-impeachment-as-a-political-spectacle-as-house-vote-looms/ar-AA2h8TZP)


---


## Where I stand

This evening the House of Representatives votes on whether to set aside eight articles of impeachment against Secretary of War Pete Hegseth. If the motion to table or refer fails, the House could vote on the articles themselves as early as tomorrow.

I stand against impeaching Secretary Hegseth on these articles, in this way, at this time. I also hold that the grievances behind them are real, that two of them concern innocent blood and the freedom of speech and must be answered, and that the deepest failure on display is not the Secretary’s but Congress’s own. The House is being asked to punish the man who carried out the President’s war because the House has never had the courage to vote on the war itself.

I hold that position open to a better argument. Here are my reasons.


---


## What the articles charge

Representative Thomas Massie of Kentucky first read his 34-page resolution on the House floor on September 15. The Speaker sent the House home early for the campaign recess before the vote the rules required, and Massie reintroduced the resolution on November 9, the first day back after the midterm elections.

The eight articles fall into four groups:


 	- **The Iran war (Articles I–III).** The war began on February 28 without a declaration of war or a statutory authorization. The charge is that the Secretary violated the War Powers Resolution of 1973 at three points: when the war began, when it passed the law’s 60-day limit, and when it continued after the House voted to bring the troops home. The administration has answered that the spring ceasefire reset the 60-day clock, and that the War Powers Resolution is unconstitutional anyway.

 	- **Civilian casualties (Article IV).** The charge is that the Secretary disregarded the laws meant to limit civilian deaths. The central example is the February 28 strike on the Shajareh Tayyebeh girls’ elementary school in Minab, Iran. Iranian state media report at least 168 dead, most of them children. A preliminary U.S. assessment found American forces were likely responsible but did not target the school on purpose. The Pentagon has not released its final findings.

 	- **Speech (Article VI).** The charge is that the Secretary used his office to punish Senator Mark Kelly, a retired Navy captain, for telling service members they may refuse unlawful orders.

 	- **Other operations.** The remaining articles concern the lethal strikes on suspected drug boats in the Caribbean and eastern Pacific, the January seizure of Venezuela’s Nicolás Maduro, and the 52-day bombing campaign in Yemen, each described as action without lawful authority.


Secretary Hegseth answered all of this at his Pentagon news conference this morning. He called the effort “a political spectacle” and said the charges are policy disagreements, “not high crimes, not misdemeanors.” Vice President Vance and Speaker Johnson said the same. Massie says that if the articles reach the floor, he will ask for each one to be voted on separately, so members can vote their conscience on each charge.


---


## The question under the question

To answer this rightly, I have to ask what the impeachment is reaching for. It is not chiefly about one man. The country has been at war for more than eight months. That war has cost tens of billions of dollars, closed the Strait of Hormuz, and driven up fuel prices at home. Last week the voters handed both houses of Congress to the other party in an election many in both parties tie at least partly to the war. And through it all, Congress has never once voted to authorize or declare the war. The House has voted to end it three times; the Senate has let those votes die.

So the impeachment is the House trying to take back a power it gave away by neglect. **Article I, Section 8** of the Constitution gives Congress, and only Congress, the power “to declare War.” The Founders put it there on purpose: the branch closest to the people, the one that has to raise the taxes and send the sons, decides whether the nation goes to war. When that power goes unused, the executive fills the gap, and Congress is left with only its most drastic tool: removal.

I share the hunger behind this impeachment. I do not share the remedy, because it treats the symptom and lets the disease go.


---


## Why I would vote no


### First: an indictment without an inquiry

An impeachment is the House acting as a grand jury. It brings charges that the Senate then tries. A grand jury hears evidence first. These articles have had no committee hearing, no subpoena, no sworn witness, and no chance for the accused to answer under oath. They were read aloud from the floor and brought straight to a vote under privileged status.

Scripture sets the standard for any judgment of a man: *“One witness shall not rise up against a man for any iniquity, or for any sin… at the mouth of two witnesses, or at the mouth of three witnesses, shall the matter be established”* **(Deuteronomy 19:15)**. And again: *“He that answereth a matter before he heareth it, it is folly and shame unto him”* **(Proverbs 18:13)**.

This brings me to my third test of any public act: would I want it done to me? I would not want to be charged with high crimes, before the whole nation, on the strength of one man’s reading of a document, with no hearing first. Neither would any member voting tonight. That alone does not prove Secretary Hegseth innocent. It means the process is not yet one a righteous man can sign.

Democratic leaders said the same thing two months ago, when they declined to impeach the President on a resolution that had had no investigative work behind it. The principle does not change with the accused’s party.

### Second: the wrong officer for the main charge

The first three articles, the heart of the case, charge the Secretary with making war without Congress’s consent. But the Secretary of War does not decide to make war. The President, as commander-in-chief, gave the orders; Secretary of State Rubio said this afternoon that Hegseth carried out “a direct order from the commander in chief.” Whether the President had the authority to give those orders is a fair and serious constitutional question. But it is a question about the President’s power, and Congress should settle it by law and vote, not by removing the man who carried out the orders.

If a Cabinet officer can be removed for carrying out a President’s contested war policy, then every future House majority will have a tool for punishing any administration’s policy through its officers. We saw the beginning of that in 2024, when the House impeached Homeland Security Secretary Alejandro Mayorkas over the administration’s border policy, and the Senate dismissed the articles without a trial. I opposed that administration’s border policy. I would still say the Mayorkas impeachment set a poor precedent, for the same reason I say it here. Policy is answered by legislation, by the purse, and by elections. Impeachment is for crimes and for corruption of the office.

### Third: the vote will not do what it claims

Removal takes two-thirds of the Senate, and Republicans hold the Senate until January. The Senate has never convicted a Cabinet secretary. Even Massie concedes the point by framing his resolution as a marker in history. A vote everyone knows will fail does not hold anyone accountable. It lets members record a position while the war goes on unchanged and the real decision stays unmade.


---


## Why the concern still stands

Voting no does not mean the charges are empty. Two of them in particular deserve answers, and I will not let my position on the process blur that.

**The children of Minab.** At least 168 people, most of them little girls, died in one strike. The U.S. military’s own preliminary assessment says we were likely responsible. Eight months on, the final findings have not been released. Scripture is plain about innocent blood whose cause is unknown. When a slain man is found and no one knows who struck him, the elders of the nearest city must come forward, deal with the matter publicly, and declare before God: *“Our hands have not shed this blood, neither have our eyes seen it”* **(Deuteronomy 21:7)**. The nation that did the striking may not keep silent. I believe God lives inside every person’s experience, so He was present in each of those children, and He feels each death. A nation that wants His blessing on its war must account for the innocent who died in it. The Pentagon should release the Minab findings now, in full, with names of who decided what, and whether the rules meant to protect civilians were followed or set aside. The Secretary’s own public scorn for “stupid rules of engagement” makes that question fair.

**The speech of a retired officer.** A service member’s duty to refuse a plainly unlawful order is not partisan. It is the lesson of Nuremberg and part of American military law. If the Secretary used his office to punish a United States senator for saying so, that is a matter of the First Amendment and of Congress’s independence, and it deserves a sworn hearing.

**The boat strikes and the Maduro seizure.** The administration says they answered imminent threats and that the Maduro capture was a law-enforcement action against a fugitive under indictment. Its critics call the boat strikes killings without trial. My standard is that a warrant for lethal force requires an actual attack or a real threat of attack. Whether these operations met that standard is a question of fact, and facts are established by evidence, not by press conferences on either side.

Each of these concerns is plausible, and none is answered by Secretary Hegseth calling his accuser “disgruntled” or by the President calling Massie a “TOTAL LOSER.” Name-calling is not an answer to a charge of innocent blood.


---


## What Congress owes the country instead

Accountability is the A in the New ERA Vision. It is the step that turns a righteous standard from an aspiration into something enforced. Here is what accountability looks like in this case:


 	- **Vote on the war.** Congress should bring an authorization for the use of military force in Iran to the floor of both houses, with the aims stated and the costs counted, and vote it up or down. If the war is just, and I believe the threat from an Iranian regime seeking nuclear weapons is real, then make the case and own the vote. If the war is not worth its cost, end it by law and by the power of the purse. Either way, the people’s representatives will have done the duty the Constitution assigns them.

 	- **Investigate before you indict.** The incoming Congress has already said it will investigate the Department of War. Do it properly: committee hearings, subpoenas, sworn testimony, the Minab findings, the targeting records, the Kelly matter. If that inquiry turns up evidence of crimes, or of willful disregard for the laws protecting civilians, then bring articles of impeachment on that record, and I will support them.

 	- **Release the Minab report.** The Secretary does not need to wait for Congress. If he is confident he has done nothing wrong, as he said this morning, the quickest way to show it is to publish the findings.


---


## The moral test

**Is it righteous?** To charge a man with high crimes without hearing evidence is not righteous, whatever he may have done. But righteousness also requires that innocent blood be accounted for, and that Congress do its own duty and not leave war to the executive alone.

**Is it loving?** Love for the Secretary means not condemning him unheard. Love for the families of Minab means not letting their dead disappear into a classified file. Love for our soldiers means not leaving them to fight a war their own Congress will neither authorize nor end.

**Would I want it done to me?** I would not want to be impeached on a reading. I would want my day of evidence, and I would also want, if I had done wrong, to be called to answer for it. The path I have described offers both.

My counsel to the House tonight: set these articles aside, and then open the inquiry and hold the vote on the war that should have come in February.

## Sources and their limits

This paper relies on mainstream reporting from November 10, 2026, mainly the Associated Press live coverage linked above, along with CBS News, CNN, Stars and Stripes, Newsweek, NBC News, and Fox News reporting on the September and November filings. The summary of the eight articles comes from news accounts of the resolution, not from my own reading of its full 34-page text. The casualty figure for Minab comes from Iranian state media, an interested party, and the Pentagon’s preliminary assessment is likewise an interested party’s account. Neither has been tested by an independent inquiry, which is exactly why I am calling for one. Both the administration’s claim that the boat strikes answered imminent threats and its critics’ claim that they were killings without trial remain, so far as the public record shows, assertions that no one has tested under oath. Mainstream outlets have sources and editorial choices of their own, and anything material that remains classified could change the picture in either direction. The House vote had not taken place when this was written.
