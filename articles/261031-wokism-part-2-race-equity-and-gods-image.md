# 261031 - Wokism Part 2 - Race, Equity, and God's Image

# The False Gods of Wokism


## Part 2: One Blood — Race, Equity, and the Image of God


by Thomas Lee Abshier, ND 11/1/2026


## Plain language summary


The woke religion teaches that race is the deepest fact about a person: it decides whether he is an oppressor or a victim, what he can know, and what he owes. Its remedy for past discrimination is new discrimination in the opposite direction. The Bible teaches the opposite. God “hath made of one blood all nations of men” (Acts 17:26), and in the very next breath Paul says that in God “we live, and move, and have our being” (Acts 17:28). God lives the life of every person of every race from the inside, so contempt for any man, for the color of his skin, is contempt for the One who lives that man’s life. That condemns the old racism and the new racism alike. Justice means judging every man by what he has done and helping every man by what he needs, with one measure for all. Real wrongs should be punished where they are proven, case by case, but guilt cannot be inherited by skin. The Christian answer to racial division is not a new hierarchy. It is reconciliation, the breaking down of the wall between peoples that Christ accomplished on the Cross.


---


## The doctrine stated fairly


In Part 1, I described the woke religion’s core belief: society is a structure of oppressor and oppressed groups, and a person’s standing follows from his group. Race is where that belief was first developed and where it is most fully applied.


Stated as fairly as I can, the racial doctrine holds that:


- **Racism is a system, not mainly a personal sin.** It lives in laws, institutions, and habits, and it operates even when no individual intends harm.

- **Disparities prove discrimination.** When outcomes between races differ, in income, discipline, arrests, test scores, or admissions, the system is presumed to be the cause.

- **There is no neutral ground.** Ibram X. Kendi argues that every policy and every idea is either racist or antiracist; to claim neutrality is to defend the status quo.

- **White people are complicit by default.** Robin DiAngelo’s *White Fragility* (2018) teaches that white people are socialized into racism, and that their discomfort at being told so is itself evidence of it.

- **The remedy is equity:** treating groups differently to make their outcomes equal. In Kendi’s words in *How to Be an Antiracist*, the remedy to past discrimination is present discrimination.


The steelman goes like this: “Slavery lasted two and a half centuries. Jim Crow lasted nearly a century more. Redlining, segregated schools, and exclusion from the postwar housing boom kept Black families from building wealth when white families did. These are not ancient history; people now living remember them. You cannot run a race for three hundred years with one side in chains, then unlock the chains and call the race fair.”


That argument contains real truth, and a Christian must hear it. The sins it names were real. Many who professed Christ committed them or defended them. The church should not be quick to defend itself.


But the conclusion the woke religion draws from those sins, that guilt and innocence now belong to races, is not justice. It is the same error turned around.


## One blood, and the One who lives every life


When Paul stood before the philosophers of Athens, he made two claims in a single breath. First: God *“hath made of one blood all nations of men for to dwell on all the face of the earth”* (Acts 17:26). Second: *“In him we live, and move, and have our being”* (Acts 17:28).


Those two verses, side by side, answer the racial question at its root.


The first establishes that humanity is one family. There are no lesser races and no greater ones. Every man descends from the same first parents, bears the same image of God (Genesis 1:27), and stands under the same judgment and the same offer of grace.


The second goes further. As I explained in Part 1, God does not watch our lives from a distance; He lives them with us, from the inside. Every experience a man has, God has with him. That means God has lived the life of every slave who felt the lash, and the life of every child turned away from a school because of his skin. He has felt every insult, every closed door, and every humiliation, from inside the one who suffered it.


It also means that racial contempt is never only a sin against a man. It is a sin against God. *“He that oppresseth the poor reproacheth his Maker”* (Proverbs 14:31). Jesus said, *“Inasmuch as ye have done it unto one of the least of these my brethren, ye have done it unto me”* (Matthew 25:40). When a man despises another for the color of his skin, he despises a life that God is living. God feels that contempt, and His holiness recoils from it.


This is the ground on which the old racism stands condemned. It is also the ground on which the new racism stands condemned, and the woke religion cannot see this because it has no God who lives every life. In its framework, some lives are oppressor lives and some are victim lives, and contempt for the first kind is permitted, even required. But God lives the life of the white child told on his first day of training that he is complicit in oppression, just as He lived the life of the Black child turned away from the white school. He feels both. Contempt by skin, in either direction, wounds the same God.


The commandment to love God with all our heart is not an abstraction here. If God lives every life, then honoring every man as a person, judged by what he has done and not by what he is, is a way of loving God. It gives Him joy to see His image honored in each of His children.


## Testing the doctrine


### 1. Disparity is not proof of discrimination


The doctrine’s central inference, that unequal outcomes prove discrimination, does not survive examination.


Thomas Sowell, the economist, spent decades documenting that unequal outcomes between groups are the norm across the world and throughout history, including among groups that faced no discrimination and groups that outperformed their supposed oppressors. In *Discrimination and Disparities* (2018), he shows that differences in geography, family structure, age, culture, and the timing of a group’s arrival all produce different outcomes. Discrimination is one possible cause among many. It must be proven in each case; it cannot be presumed from the numbers.


This matters because the presumption has consequences. If every disparity is discrimination, then every institution with unequal outcomes is guilty, and every remedy must be racial. The doctrine cannot lose an argument, because any fact counts as evidence for it. A belief that no evidence could ever disprove is not a finding. It is an article of faith.


### 2. “No neutral ground” abolishes justice


Kendi’s claim that no policy is neutral sounds bold, but follow it to the end. If there is no neutral rule, then there is no fair rule, only rules that favor one race or another. The question is no longer what is right but who wins.


The Christian and the American tradition rest on the opposite claim: there is a just law above every race, and it applies to all equally. *“Ye shall do no unrighteousness in judgment: thou shalt not respect the person of the poor, nor honour the person of the mighty: but in righteousness shalt thou judge thy neighbour”* (Leviticus 19:15). God Himself is no respecter of persons (Acts 10:34). The Civil Rights Act of 1964 wrote that principle into federal law: no discrimination by race, in any direction.


In *Students for Fair Admissions v. Harvard* (2023), the Supreme Court held that race-conscious admissions at Harvard and the University of North Carolina violated the Equal Protection Clause and the Civil Rights Act. The principle the Court restored is not new. It is the principle of Leviticus 19:15: one measure for every person.


### 3. Inherited guilt cannot be repented of


Part 1 showed that the woke religion moves guilt from deeds to categories. Race is where that move does the most damage.


A man born in 2005 did not own slaves, write Jim Crow laws, or draw redlining maps. Under the woke doctrine he is still complicit, and his only path is endless confession. He cannot repent, because he has not chosen the sin. He cannot be forgiven, because the doctrine has no one to forgive him. So he is left with two options: perpetual guilt or quiet resentment. Neither produces reconciliation.


Scripture refuses inherited guilt: *“The son shall not bear the iniquity of the father”* (Ezekiel 18:20). Every man answers for his own deeds. That is not a way of escaping history. It is the only way to end the cycle in which each generation punishes the next for what the last one did.


### 4. Equity without a deposit


The woke meaning of equity is equal outcomes, produced by unequal treatment. It treats the fruits of effort, discipline, and sacrifice as if they fell from the sky in unequal portions and had to be redistributed by race.


I have given the word a Christian meaning elsewhere. Equity is like the equity in a house: what you have deposited. God’s love is offered to every man without condition, but to receive its fruit, a man must choose God’s way. Every cup of water given in His name, every evil repaid with good, is a deposit in the storehouse. There is no equity without a deposit. That principle respects every person as a moral agent capable of choosing, building, and receiving. The woke version treats people as passive recipients of whatever their group is owed.


### 5. The neighbor harmed by a lowered bar


In *One Measure for Every Applicant* (September 2026), I described three neighbors affected by racial preference in admissions. Two are obvious: the applicant passed over because of his ancestry, and the public that depends on competent doctors, engineers, and pilots. The third is most often forgotten: the student admitted under a lower standard. Placing a student where he has not been prepared, beside classmates years ahead of him, is not kindness. It hands him debt and a question attached to his degree that he did not earn, and that every graduate of his group, including those who would have been admitted under any standard, inherits with him.


Racial preference claims to love the people it helps. Honest measurement given early, with real help offered, loves them better.


## Applying the test


**Is it righteous?** No. God judges every man by his deeds, and He is no respecter of persons (Ezekiel 18:20; Acts 10:34). A doctrine that assigns guilt and merit by race contradicts the character of the Judge.


**Is it loving?** No. It teaches children to see one another as oppressors and victims before they see each other as persons. It burdens the innocent with guilt they cannot discharge, and it tells the people it claims to help that they cannot succeed by the same standard as everyone else.


**Would I want it done to me?** No man, of any race, would want to be judged by his skin. Every one of us would want to be judged by what he has done, and helped according to what he needs. The test cuts both ways, which is why it is the right test.


## Why the concern is not overblown


A critic will say that racial preferences are a small correction for a large injustice, and that the woke framework is mostly words. I disagree.


**It is institutional.** Over the last decade, race-based training, hiring preferences, diversity statements for faculty applicants, and equity mandates in school discipline spread through universities, corporations, hospitals, and public schools. A doctrine administered by the institutions that hire, educate, and treat the public is not a matter of words.


**It is paid for.** A large industry of consultants and administrators earns its living by finding racism in institutions. An industry whose income depends on finding a problem will find it. That does not prove every finding false, but it places the burden of proof on those who profit from the diagnosis.


**It shapes the spirit of a nation.** A people taught that their neighbors are enemies by birth will learn to see them that way. *“A house divided against itself cannot stand”* (Mark 3:25). A nation that sorts its citizens by race in law, education, and employment is building the division it claims to cure.


## Where the argument must be careful


Some discrimination is real and present, not only past. Controlled studies in which identical résumés are sent out under different names have found that employers respond differently by race. Where an employer, a lender, a landlord, or a police officer discriminates by race, that is a sin and should be a proven offense, prosecuted case by case. My argument is not that racism has vanished. It is that the remedy for a wrong done to a person is justice for that person, not a new wrong done to someone else.


Some disparities also have historical causes. The answer is to help persons according to need, which will reach the descendants of past injustice without assigning guilt to anyone by skin.


These boundaries do not weaken the thesis. They sharpen it: punish proven wrongs, help real needs, and never judge a man by his race.


## What I would do


The policy follows from the principle:


- **One measure for every person.** Enforce the Civil Rights Act as written, in every direction. No public institution, and no institution receiving public benefits, should sort people by race in admissions, hiring, contracting, or discipline.

- **Help by need, not by race.** Where help is warranted, give it according to a person’s actual circumstances: income, family situation, the quality of the school he came from. That reaches the disadvantaged of every race without teaching anyone that his skin defines him.

- **Prosecute real discrimination.** Proven discrimination against any person should be pursued vigorously. The test is evidence of a wrong done to a person, not a disparity in a spreadsheet.

- **Lead by voice.** The president cannot change hearts by statute. He can speak, clearly and often, the truth that God made of one blood all nations of men, and that every citizen will be judged by what he does and not by what he is.


## The better way: reconciliation


The Christian answer to racial division is not a better hierarchy. It is reconciliation.


*“For he is our peace, who hath made both one, and hath broken down the middle wall of partition between us”* (Ephesians 2:14). Paul wrote that about the deepest ethnic division of his world, between Jew and Gentile. The Cross did not erase their differences. It removed the wall. At-one-ment means that what sin divided, alignment with Christ reunites.


The final picture of Scripture is not a colorless humanity. It is *“a great multitude, which no man could number, of all nations, and kindreds, and people, and tongues”* standing together before the throne (Revelation 7:9). Diversity, rightly understood, is giving the same love, God’s love, to all people, in every place we go. Inclusion, rightly understood, is bringing people into God’s family. Those are Christian words. The woke religion borrowed them and emptied them.


The woke religion offers the descendants of slaves a permanent grievance, and the descendants of everyone else a permanent debt. The gospel offers both the same thing: forgiveness for what they have actually done, healing for what was done to them, and a place at one table. Christians led the movement to abolish slavery, and a Baptist preacher led the movement to end Jim Crow. The church’s best moments on race came when it took its own Scripture seriously. It must do so again.


## Where to push


- **Can a colorblind rule be just in a society shaped by past racial injustice?** I say yes, if help is given by need. Show me a case where need-based help fails and only race-based help works.

- **Is “systemic racism” ever a useful idea?** I grant that laws and institutions can embody injustice. My objection is to inferring it from every disparity and assigning guilt by race. Where should that line be drawn?

- **Does “God lives every life” really condemn both kinds of racism equally?** I claim it does. Show me a reason God would feel contempt toward one race as an injury and contempt toward another as justice.
