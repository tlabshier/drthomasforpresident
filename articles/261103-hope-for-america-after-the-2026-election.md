# 261103 - Hope for America After the 2026 Election

# America Is Not Finished


### An answer to Steven Yates on the morning after the 2026 midterms


Thomas Lee Abshier, ND — 4 November 2026


*Occasioned by Steven Yates, [“The U.S. Is Now Toast (Officially or Unofficially),”](https://substack.com/home/post/p-223504078) published on his Substack, Navigating the New Normal, the morning after the 3 November election. Dr. Yates is a trained philosopher and a serious critic of both parties. His essay deserves a serious answer.*


---


## Plain language summary


Yesterday the Democrats won both houses of Congress by a wide margin. Steven Yates argues that President Trump lost the Republicans their majority by failing on four promises: the border, putting Americans first, staying out of foreign wars, and releasing the Epstein files. He predicts the Democrats will do no better, because both parties answer to the same moneyed class, and he concludes that the country is finished.


I agree with much of his diagnosis. The war and the cost of living drove this result. The border was secured, but the promises to finish the wall and remove those here illegally were not kept, and enforcement in the streets put ICE under great scrutiny. The Epstein files have been handled in a way that invites suspicion. And money has too much say in Washington.


Dr. Yates did not mention it, but perhaps the biggest disappointment was the SAVE America Act. The House passed it with every Republican vote, and it died in the Senate with a Republican majority, where four Republicans joined the Democrats against it, and the leadership would not set aside the filibuster to pass it. This is another indication that we did not have a Republican majority; we had a majority that was named Republican but too often legislated like Democrats. That is my underlying diagnosis: beneath the war and the prices, the populace was disgusted by Republicans who voted and legislated like Democrats, so they voted out the Republicans and put in Democrats.


While Dr. Yates identified the factors that led to the Republicans’ defeat, I disagree with his conclusion that America is finished. A few of his facts are wrong, and some of his language is out of proportion to the evidence, but those are minor. My disagreement is that his despair is not justified. The deeper problem is that voters are not being offered a real choice: both parties have retreated from God’s standards, one openly and the other quietly. A nation is not finished while its people can still repent, vote, serve on juries, and govern their own towns. I set out below what I would ask of the new Congress, what I propose as the hope and promise of my platform, and why I believe the remedy is righteousness.


*A note on how this paper was made: I drafted it with the help of Claude, an AI assistant, which checked the factual claims against the public record. The positions and the final text are mine.*


---


## PART ONE: What happened


The result is not in dispute. Democrats took control of both the House and the Senate on 3 November, flipping Senate seats in states such as Kansas, Iowa, Texas, Ohio, Maine, and North Carolina. NBC News projects that Democrats will hold their widest House margin in more than three decades. The President has accepted the result. Vice President Vance has publicly blamed the losses on the Iran war.


Republican strategists saw this coming. As early as April, a source close to the White House told Politico, *“This war in Iran almost cements the fact that we lose the midterms in November — the Senate and House.”* Through the summer, gasoline stayed above four dollars a gallon, and voters increasingly tied the war to what they paid for groceries and travel. Consumer prices rose 3.7 percent in the twelve months to September.


So Dr. Yates is right about the immediate cause. The voters did not suddenly embrace the Democratic program. They punished the party in power for a war that was supposed to last weeks and has lasted eight months, and for prices that rose while they were told things were improving. Whether something deeper was also at work is a question I take up in Part Three.


---


## PART TWO: Where Dr. Yates is right


I want to give him the credit he has coming before I disagree with him, because he is saying several things that most of official Washington will not say.


**Affordability is not a leftist issue.** He is right that the cost of a roof, a meal, the electric bill, and a tank of diesel is ordinary people’s first concern, and that a party that treats it as someone else’s talking point deserves to lose. Scripture does not treat the price of bread as a minor matter: *“A false balance is abomination to the LORD: but a just weight is his delight”* **(Proverbs 11:1)**. A government that debases its money or drives up the cost of necessities by its own choices is putting its thumb on the scale against the poor.


**The border was secured, but the promises behind it were not kept.** I argued in August that the immigration laws are statutes passed by the people’s representatives, that they had gone unenforced for decades, and that enforcing them is a duty. I also argued that enforcement must land first on the employers who profit from illegal labor, not on the powerless. The administration did the hardest part: illegal crossings at the southern border have fallen to the lowest levels in half a century, and Dr. Yates is fair to credit it. But the wall was not finished, removals of those already here fell far short of what was promised, and the employers were left largely untouched.


**The war was begun without the consent of the people’s representatives.** On this point, Dr. Yates and I agree on the fault, if not on everything else. Joe Kent, the director of the National Counterterrorism Center, resigned in March, writing, *“I cannot in good conscience support the ongoing war in Iran. Iran posed no imminent threat to our nation.”* Article I gives Congress the power to declare war. No such vote was taken. I have argued since the summer that Congress must authorize the war, with its objectives and red lines voted on in advance, or it should not be fought.


**The Epstein files have been handled in a way that invites suspicion.** Congress ordered their release. The Department of Justice missed its deadline, released the material in batches with heavy redactions, and a federal judge has been reviewing whether the department over-redacted. When the government of a free people withholds evidence about the sexual abuse of children by the powerful, the burden of proof shifts to the government. Dr. Yates is right that the public is entitled to be suspicious.


**Money has too much say in Washington.** He is right about this too, and I return to it in Part Four.


---


## PART THREE: Where he overstates, and what lies beneath


**Some of his facts are wrong.** These are not large errors, but a man who asks his readers to distrust the official story must be scrupulous with his own.


- He writes that Tulsi Gabbard criticized the claim that Iran was close to a nuclear weapon. The record shows the opposite. In June 2025, she wrote that *“Iran is at the point that it can produce a nuclear weapon within weeks to months, if they decide to finalize the assembly,”* and on the day Joe Kent resigned, she publicly backed the President’s authority to judge what counts as an imminent threat. She did leave the administration in May 2026, but the dissent on Iran was Kent’s, not hers.

- He writes that “most” of those roughed up by ICE turned out to be citizens. Renee Good and Alex Pretti, both killed in Minneapolis in January, were citizens, and there were others. I have found no count that establishes that most were citizens. The claim may be true, but he does not show it, and he should not state it as fact.

- He calls John Thune, the Senate Majority Leader, a Democrat. Senator Thune is a Republican from South Dakota. Whether or not Dr. Yates meant it, the slip is apt, and on the point it makes, he and I completely agree: the Republicans have too often governed like Democrats. I return to this below.

- He writes that Peter Thiel is “now based in Argentina.” Reports in May said that Mr. Thiel had temporarily relocated his family to Buenos Aires while a billionaire-tax measure was pending in California. That is a fair example of the wealthy keeping an exit open. It is not proof that America is governed from abroad.


**Some of his language is out of proportion.** He calls one cabinet officer a “bimbo,” another a “psycho,” and a third a stooge. I disagree with many of the people he names, and some of them have done real harm. But contempt is not an argument, and it costs the writer the very readers he most needs to persuade. Exaggeration is a form of lying by proportion. If Kristi Noem ran an enforcement campaign that hired badly and killed citizens, say that, and show it. The name-calling adds nothing to the case and subtracts from the writer’s credibility.


**His conclusion does not follow from his evidence.** “Toast” means finished, with nothing left and nothing possible. Nothing in his essay shows that. It shows a party that failed its voters and was removed. That is not proof that the system is working well. It proves voters still have the power to remove those who fail them. A plutocracy does not hand both houses of Congress to the opposition because gasoline is too expensive.


**A deeper issue is at play.** Voters in Kansas and Texas sent Democrats to Washington. Three explanations are possible, and they are not exclusive. The first is the one in Part One: the war and the prices. The second is that the electorate has reversed its moral judgment and now prefers sexual license, abortion, and gender confusion. The third is that voters have become disgusted with a party that promised to hold child predators accountable, to end wars by presidential fiat, to secure elections, and to stop unlimited immigration, and then did not write those promises into law.


I believe the third explanation carries more weight than the commentators will admit. Republicans held the House, the Senate, and the White House for two years. They passed a tax-and-spending bill that the Congressional Budget Office estimated would add trillions of dollars to the debt. They did not pass a law requiring the release of the Epstein files until public pressure forced it, did not vote on the war, and did not put the border into statute so that the next administration could not undo it. The House passed the SAVE America Act with every Republican vote; it died in the Senate, where four Republicans joined the Democrats, and the leadership would not set aside the filibuster to pass it. Whatever their reasons, the effect was to leave our elections open to the abuses the bill was written to stop. Spending without restraint and promising without legislating is how Democrats govern. The voters had been given a party that talked like Republicans and legislated like Democrats, and many of them voted for the Democrats to get rid of it.


That points to the deep dysfunction in both our electorate and our legislative class. **We are not being given true alternatives.** We are offered the Democrats and the RINOs, Democrats disguised as Republicans. And the retreat is not only in the legislating. It is in the platform itself. The 2024 Republican platform, rewritten at the urging of the Trump campaign, dropped its definition of marriage as the union of one man and one woman and its call for federal protection of unborn life, leaving abortion to the states. That is an abomination, and I detest it. A party that claims God’s standard and then quietly lowers it is on the road the other party has already traveled. This must change. We need a real choice, not an echo disguised as a choice.

## PART FOUR: The question under the question


Dr. Yates’s real claim is not about Trump or the Democrats. It is the question under all four of his complaints: **Do the American people still govern their country, or is it owned?**


He cites the study by Martin Gilens and Benjamin Page, published in 2014, which examined about 1,800 policy questions over two decades and found that when the preferences of the affluent and of organized business diverged from those of the average citizen, policy followed the affluent, and the average citizen’s preferences had almost no independent effect.


**I stand with Dr. Yates’s concern.** I believe that the moneyed interests of both parties were bending federal policy toward themselves long before my lifetime, and I have said so in paper after paper: on cheap labor, on corporate capture of AI regulation, on members of Congress trading stocks, and on the Federal Reserve’s service to the banks.


The areas of uncertainty are these. Other scholars have reanalyzed the same data and found that on most questions the affluent and the middle class want the same things, so the study measures disagreement only on a minority of issues, and the gap is smaller than the headline suggests. Correlation is also not causation: a policy that follows the wishes of the wealthy may have been adopted for other reasons. So the study does not prove that America is an oligarchy in the strict sense. It shows that where the wealthy and the ordinary citizen disagree, the ordinary citizen usually loses.


**Why the concern remains plausible** despite those caveats:


- The money motive is obvious and measurable. Campaigns cost hundreds of millions of dollars, and the people who supply that money expect access. No one needs a conspiracy to explain why legislators listen to their funders.

- The pattern holds across administrations of both parties. Amnesty without enforcement in 1986, cheap-labor tolerance for forty years, bank rescues in 2008, and a war begun this year without a vote of Congress all served narrow interests, while the costs fell on people who were never asked.

- Most Americans have an intuitive sense that something is wrong, and that intuition has been consistent for a generation. When the governed feel ignored for that long, the burden falls on the governors to show they are not.


So I agree with the warning. Where I part from Dr. Yates is in what follows.


---


## PART FIVE: Why the republic is not finished


Dr. Yates ends by telling Americans that they are on their own, that no one in the establishment will save them, and that no outsider has the resources to do so. The first two statements are true. The conclusion he draws from them is not.


**No party was ever going to save us.** *“Put not your trust in princes, nor in the son of man, in whom there is no help”* **(Psalm 146:3)** was written about kings, and it applies to presidents and congressional majorities. The hope that Donald Trump would cleanse Washington was always a hope placed in the wrong place. So is the Democrats’ celebration this morning. But our renewal does not depend on finding the right man for the White House. If we are faithful, the right man will reflect the Godliness of the nation. If we are blessed, a righteous leader may come who will lead the people from their rebellion against God into righteousness and worship of Him. The more likely scenario is that a willing people who desire Godliness will see a man’s character and platform, and will vote for one who is Godly and promises to lead them into a deeper and purer expression of the Kingdom on Earth.


**A free people is not finished while it keeps its means of self-government.** Americans still elect their school boards, county commissioners, and sheriffs. They still sit on juries, and a jury may refuse to convict a citizen under an unjust law, which is the oldest check a free people holds against its rulers. They still choose which banks to use, which businesses to support, what to teach their children, and whether to tell the truth. Every one of those choices is within the reach of ordinary people, and none requires BlackRock’s permission.


**Empires fall when righteousness fails, not when elections go badly.** Dr. Yates is right that empires rise and fall. Scripture tells us why: *“Righteousness exalteth a nation: but sin is a reproach to any people”* **(Proverbs 14:34)**. The question is not whether the right party holds Congress. It is whether the American people will turn back to God, govern themselves by His law, and demand the same of the men they send to Washington. That is the R in The New ERA Vision. It is the solid rock upon which we should build our nation, and it will hold.


**The economic picture is hard, but it is not hopeless.** Dr. Yates says inflation cannot be stopped, because shutting off the money supply would freeze the credit system. He is right that a sudden stop would be a catastrophe. But there are choices between printing without limit and a sudden stop. The Federal Reserve should increase the money supply in proportion to measures such as productivity, with the goal of money that holds a stable value. A goal of 2 percent inflation is theft by policy: a dollar that loses 2 percent of its value every year is a false balance.


The deepest cause of rising prices this year is not the Federal Reserve alone. It is energy: the war closed the Strait of Hormuz, oil passed a hundred dollars a barrel, and every transport and fertilizer cost followed. Abundant domestic energy is the most powerful deflationary force available to a nation. That is why energy is the E in The New ERA Vision, and why I have argued for an immediate national program to build conventional pressurized-water reactors, along with strong support for bringing thorium molten-salt reactors to practical small modular and full-scale designs. Add a credible, multi-year path to a balanced budget, an end to wars of choice, whose cost we all pay at the pump, and a gradual return toward money that holds its value, and inflation can be brought down without collapsing the banks. That is slower and less satisfying than a crash or a miracle. It is also how real recoveries happen.

## PART SIX: What I ask of the new Congress


Dr. Yates predicts that the new majority will continue the war, fail on inflation, and keep the Epstein files sealed. He may be right. But a prediction does not determine reality. We still have the power to influence Congress. Its members may be bought by corporations and obligations; they may be anti-God ideologues; they may be committed to socialism, globalism, and technological control of the entire populace. Regardless, they are people, flesh-and-blood humans with a soul and a spirit that have a conscience, and God can speak to them and convince them to turn from their rebellious ways against Him.


We can all pray for their transformation. We can write letters. We can form groups such as our Renaissance Fellowships. We can debate the Godly pattern we wish to establish and discuss the issues. We can take action by asking those in our circle who live in each member’s district to write letters, and by enrolling all who believe in The New ERA Vision. We need to stand for what is right. We need to organize our communities, advocate, and enroll others in Godly stands on the issues. And we should ask of each policy the same three questions I ask of every policy: Is it righteous? If so, is it loving? Would I want it done to me?


- **Vote on the war.** Hold an up-or-down vote on the Iran war in January: its objectives, its limits, and the conditions under which force will resume. If the new majority will not authorize it, it must say so plainly and act on that.The only place for war is preventing an imminent threat, either to our homeland or to a nation unrighteously victimized. I hold that Iran meets that test, not because an attack on America was days away last February, but because a regime that preaches martyrdom, does not keep its word, and is a short step from a nuclear weapon is a standing threat to its neighbors and to us. Congress may weigh that judgment differently, and that is exactly why the decision belongs to Congress. The President’s use of force should be limited as the Constitution specifies.My own plan is set out in “Winning the Long War with Iran” and “The Missing Lever.” You cannot negotiate in good faith with men who do not keep their word and who practice deception as policy. So target the leaders, and keep targeting them, until they allow unlimited inspections. At the same time, begin a new campaign of Christian Soldier warfare, a war of persuasion, never of the sword, where the primary battle is for the hearts of the people. I would carry that campaign of truth and the Gospel, as a wartime outreach, to isolated and overtly hostile nations such as Iran and North Korea, and as a peacetime outreach to troubled populations in every part of the world. Without winning their hearts and transforming their minds, whatever peace we gain by armed conflict must be maintained at endless cost, as the old behavior that caused the conflict returns.

- **Release the Epstein files under court supervision.** Protect the names of victims. Release everything else. Where the Department of Justice claims a redaction is required, let a federal judge decide, on the record. Then prosecute whoever the evidence shows abused children, regardless of party, wealth, or friendship with any president.

- **Enforce immigration law and finish the wall.** For forty years both parties tolerated a border that leaked, and even that modest flow should have been intolerable. Under the Biden administration, the leak became a gusher: more than two million encounters a year at the southwest border, and images of crowds crossing in open view. That was not subtle. It was an open invasion, and it should have been met as one; the Constitution charges the federal government to protect every state against invasion (Article IV, Section 4), with military force if necessary. Whether the authors of that policy were trying to destroy America or simply had a heart for the unemployed of the whole world, I do not know. But a policy that opens the border is a betrayal of loyalty to our very existence as a nation, and we are now left cleaning up the invasion it allowed.Prosecuting the employers who hire illegal labor would do more to end illegal immigration than any number of street raids. Removal of aliens once inside the country is expensive and dangerous. The agents who risk their lives to remove those who trespassed deserve our support, and those who trespassed should be removed.Where agents have used excessive force, that is a discipline problem, not a policy problem. Agents should be disciplined and retrained as appropriate, and referred to the courts as for any other criminal act; screening and training agents is the commander’s responsibility. We must trust the courts to judge rightly the deaths of two American citizens in Minneapolis. In my reading of the accounts, if the evidence shows that the motorist was attempting to run the officer over, that is mitigation; the court must decide. An officer who risks his life to protect the nation deserves every support we can offer, not a rush to conviction. No one has shown that these abuses reflect systemic racism against minorities. If evidence shows it, we should address it; until then, the misconduct of some agents is not an indictment of the whole effort. We should investigate charges of excessive force and systemic injustice alike. But we must not bend to the will of those who are lawless and anti-border in their support of those who have invaded our country. The bottom line is that either we are a nation with borders and control of who lives inside them, or we are not a nation.

- **Take back the tariff power.** The Constitution gives Congress the power to tax imports. Take it back, and set tariffs by law. I would not abolish them. I would set a fair-trade tariff equal to the cost our own labor, safety, and environmental rules impose on American producers, so that the American worker and the foreign one are measured by the same weight.

- **Ban stock trading by members of Congress.** This is the simplest test of whether the new majority is serious about the concern Dr. Yates raises. If members will not give up trading on what they know, nothing else they say about the power of money should be believed.

- **Stop spending money we do not have.** Publish a credible path to a balanced budget, and stick to it.


These demands do not depend on party. I have made every one of them of the Republican majority, and I make them now of the Democratic majority that takes office in January.

## Conclusion


Dr. Yates has seen clearly what went wrong. I part with him where diagnosis turns into despair. A country whose voters can still throw out a majority that failed them is not finished. A people who can still repent, still serve on juries, still raise their children in the fear of God, and still tell the truth to their rulers has not lost the means of its own renewal. It has only to use them.


The question for the next two years is not whether the Democrats are more moral, Godly, and righteous than the Republicans. We know the answer. The Democratic platform no longer names God, and it stands for abortion, sexual license, and the confusion of the sexes. These are not policy preferences indifferent to God. They openly defy His law, and whoever embraces that platform has taken a stand against Him. A man cannot call himself a Christian and a Democrat and be confident that he will stand in the judgment. Jesus warned that many who claim to have done mighty works in His name will hear Him say, *“I never knew you: depart from me, ye that work iniquity”* **(Matthew 7:23)**. God’s standards are not up for debate. What is open to argument is only how we persuade a nation to keep them. I leave the judgment of each man’s soul to God, who alone judges it. My judgment of the Democratic Party is that its platform, and by alliance its candidates and members, have taken a stand as enemies of God. I say it as one who was once His enemy myself, as every believer was: *“For if, when we were enemies, we were reconciled to God by the death of his Son, much more, being reconciled, we shall be saved by his life”* **(Romans 5:10)**.


Nor does the line run neatly between the parties. The Republican platform of 2024 retreated on marriage and on life, and that retreat is part of why voters saw no real choice. The question is whether the American people will demand righteousness of those who govern them, and practice it themselves. Yesterday’s vote suggests that too many do not see the difference, or do not weigh it. There is a difference, and a nation whose moral compass is bent toward rebellion against God will suffer for not seeing it. That must change, and it must change in the voters before it changes in Washington.


The President keeps his veto until January 2029, so the new Congress cannot easily repeal the laws now on the books. But it can block, defund, and investigate, and it will use that power against Godly policy wherever it can. We will suffer for it. My hope is that in the two years before the 2028 election, the nation will turn its heart toward God. Every man can change in a moment, repent, and turn toward Him. That is why I run.

## Sources and their limits


The factual claims in this paper rest mainly on the mainstream record: national news coverage of the election results, reporting on the Minneapolis shootings and the lawsuits that followed, coverage of Joe Kent’s resignation and Tulsi Gabbard’s statements, Bureau of Labor Statistics price data, reporting on the Epstein file releases, reporting on the SAVE America Act, and reporting on the 2024 party platforms. That record is not neutral. On the Minneapolis shootings in particular, the government’s account and eyewitness accounts conflict, and official statements are the testimony of an interested party. On the Epstein files, the redacted material is, by definition, unavailable, and what it contains can only be inferred. On the question of oligarchy, the academic literature is itself divided, and the data any study can examine are limited to policies that reached a recorded decision. The platform summaries rely on secondary reporting, and readers should check the platform texts themselves. Where press or government curation could have hidden evidence, I have tried to say so, and I have labeled a claim false only when primary evidence contradicts it. I could not verify the exact wording of the statements Dr. Yates attributes to Senators Schumer and Thune, and I have not relied on them.
