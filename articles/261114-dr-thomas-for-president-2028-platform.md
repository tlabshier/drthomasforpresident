## Tensions Between Earlier and Current Writing

Some of this older material is still public. Joelle has already warned that critics will read your whole body of work looking for contradictions *(P/261005-moral-capitalism)*. These are the conflicts I found:


 	- **Religious tests.** *Reverse Everson* (2019) proposes a Biblical-Christian test for federal judges and impeachment of justices who rule against the bill. The 2026 papers say Article VI forbids any religious test and require allegiance, not a profession of faith. **Recommendation:** retire or annotate Action #4 and recast Action #3.

 	- **Abortion: state or nation, and exceptions.** The 2010 platform calls abortion a states'-rights issue and allows only a life-of-the-mother exception. The 2026 book prosecutes the doctor and the mother and allows no rape exception, but does not say whether that law is federal or state. **Open question for you:** a physical picture of who passes and enforces the law.

 	- **Sexual-conduct laws.** The 2010 platform leaves laws on adultery, homosexuality, and related conduct to the states, and the 2019 Lawrence essay calls the decision a "coup." The 2026 book says explicitly that it does not propose a return to those criminal laws. The 2026 position should govern, and the older posts need an annotation.

 	- **Taxation.** The 2010 platform proposes a 17% flat tax, Fair Tax, or national sales tax. A site category proposes repealing the Sixteenth Amendment. The 2026 papers propose rent on land, minerals, and spectrum and removing taxes on labor. These are three different systems. Choose one as the plank and list the others as transition steps.

 	- **The military at home.** *Death Penalty for Sedition* (2025) treats troops who refuse orders to suppress riots as seditious. The 2026 immigration paper refuses to use the military for civil enforcement and invokes Posse Comitatus. The two can be reconciled (insurrection is not civil enforcement), but neither paper says so.

 	- **Energy technology.** Methanol and hydrogen (2010), then solar, wind, and biomass (2019), then thorium molten-salt reactors (2026, not yet in the repos). The older energy pages should point to the current foundation.
