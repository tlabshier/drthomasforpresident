# Why I Run When Winning is Unlikely

### The Philosophy of a Witness Candidacy

Thomas Lee Abshier, ND — 26 August 2026

---

## The question

People ask me some version of the same question: *You will not be on a ballot. You accept no money. You have no party, no machine, no consultants, no path. Why are you running for President?*

I have a vision I want to share about the nature of God, the substance of the universe, and how Godly business, politics, and relationships can transform the nation. My success is not measured by **whether I take the office. I am running to place a paradigm, a plan, a vision before my country.** The presidential race is the one forum where the whole country considers, at once, who will lead them toward the nation each of us wants. If I were elected, it would be a miracle. The candidacy is the work I can do; the miracle is up to God. The office would come by providence and by individuals being touched by the message, not by strategy or campaign wisdom.

---

## Three kinds of candidacy

There are at least three reasons a man stands for election.

**The first is to win the office.** This is the ordinary kind. Its logic is arithmetic: assemble a coalition large enough to cross fifty percent, and say only what enlarges the coalition. The discipline of this candidacy is subtraction. You remove whatever costs you votes. The candidate must look acceptable to everyone and must raise money from someone. I do not blame the men who run this way. The arithmetic is real. But the arithmetic is also why almost nothing true and costly ever gets said from a national stage.

**The second is to move a party.** This is the pressure candidacy. You cannot win, but you can take enough delegates or enough attention that the winner must adopt part of your platform to close the wound. Much of American political history is written by candidates of this type. The Socialist Party's Norman Thomas never came close to the presidency, yet many of the planks he ran on in the 1920s and 1930s became law within a generation, enacted by the men who had beaten him. The pressure candidate loses the office and wins the argument.

**The third is to change a paradigm.** This is the witness candidacy, and it is mine. Its purpose is not to take power and not to bend a party, but to place a curative proposition in front of a people. I have a dream that America is a land where Christ is King of every man's heart, where the command to love God first and neighbor as self is the first law held by every mind. The proposition is this:
> 
> 

*America's problems are downstream of America's holiness. No policy, however clever, will heal a people who have stopped wanting to be good. And if the people return to God, most of what we now argue about will dissolve. The battlefield is the human heart. Win there, and the rest is work; lose there, and no amount of work will do.*
> 

That is a claim about reality. It is either true or it is false. An election cannot settle it, and it does not become false when the election is lost.

---

## Why the presidency? Why not simply write essays?

If the aim is to teach, to propose, and to instill a vision of what could be, why not just write? I have written for forty years. I intend to keep writing. But the question is fair: why attach the writing to a candidacy?

Three reasons.

**First, the office demands completeness.** A pamphleteer may write about whatever moves him this week. A candidate for President must consider the entire body of human group experience. He must have a workable answer for defense, the money supply, the border, education, the courts, energy, the poor, the unborn, and our enemies. The frame of the presidency forces a man to convert a pile of opinions into a governing philosophy — one that must not contradict itself across a hundred subjects. Everything is interconnected, and the presidency is the discipline that exposes the interconnection. No other discipline does it as thoroughly.

**Second, a candidacy is an invitation to self-examination and a call to action.** An essay asks to be read. A candidacy asks to be judged and acted upon, and judgment requires the reader to take a position of his own. When a man reads my platform, he is not merely absorbing an argument; he is being asked whether he would entrust the country to it. That is what is ultimately at stake in every election. And behind the political question stands the personal one: I am asking each person to evaluate his life, to accept Jesus Christ as Savior and Lord, and to bring others into that allegiance. That is a far more serious act of attention, and it produces a far more serious kind of disagreement.

**Third, this is free speech in its original American sense.** I am on no ballot. I accept no donations. I have been advised there is no FEC jurisdiction where there is no ballot access and no money taken. What remains is a citizen saying publicly what he believes the country should be, under his own name, at his own cost, with nothing to gain but the benefit of living in a country where every man lives fully to please God by loving his neighbor. That is not a loophole. That is the thing the First Amendment was written to protect, and it is the highest purpose of political speech.

---

## The freedom of having nothing to lose

There is a strange grace in a candidacy that cannot be won. Because no office is at stake, there is nothing to lose by honesty and nothing to gain by shading the message to win a constituency. This position allows full disclosure of what I believe would actually make America, and the world, great.

I do not have to soften a position to hold a donor. I do not have to keep together a coalition assembled on false pretenses. I do not have to pretend to a certainty I lack, or hide a certainty I have. I can say the thing I actually believe about God, about sexuality, about money, about war, and let it be weighed on its merits by whoever is willing to weigh it.

This is the only condition under which a fully honest political philosophy can presently be stated in America. The men with a real chance of winning cannot afford to state one. So the work falls to men who have no chance of winning.

My aim is to make the argument compelling enough that it cannot be ignored, and to enroll enough people in it that it spreads by individual witness and personal persuasion. I intend to leave behind resources that help people learn and defend the propositions of the righteous society I envision — a society where people choose goodness out of a transformed heart. I do not expect this without the power of God. This program will work only if the individual heart is transformed by committing to Jesus Christ as Lord, and by the shepherding and character development that follow as men implant the Word in their hearts and walk in His ways.

---

## What I am actually asking the voter to do

Because this campaign is not an arithmetic exercise, its instructions to the voter are unusual.

**Do not write in my name in order to register a protest.** A vote cast for me out of disgust with the alternatives is a vote subtracted from the most Godly man who can actually win. That is a loss, not a witness. Vote for the best man on the ballot.

**Do the work that would have to precede any such election anyway.** Read the Bible daily. Repent of what needs repenting. Repair your marriage and make it thrive; be happy; serve each other as though God Himself were receiving your words and actions. Raise your children in the ways of Godliness, giving them the reasons to believe, and teaching them that God is the only consciousness and that He experiences all. Learn the issues well enough to argue them from first principles. Find two people and bring them along. Form a cell. Meet weekly, on Zoom or in person. Hold each other accountable. Ask, in each situation, what Godliness requires.

**And understand what would have to be true for me to win.** If a majority of the electorate wrote in the name of a man running on repentance and on Godliness enacted in legislation, adjudication, and the execution of the law, it would mean the Third Great Awakening had already happened. The purpose of such a campaign is revival, and its victory would be the evidence that revival had already come. I am running to lead the nation toward that revival. If the awakening happens, I will win. Or rather, we all would win. The bride of Christ, the Church, would be without spot or blemish, and ready for His return. **Ephesians 5:27**

That is the real resolution of the paradox in the title. I am running to bring America to Christ. I am holding a nationwide revival. I am describing the condition under which a man like me *could* win, and asking you to help create it. If you agree with my vision, if you believe I have the character and the ability to balance the hundreds of problems a President must hold at once, if you find my solutions for America real and effective, then I am willing to serve.

---

## The measure of success

If I never receive a vote, this work will still have been worth a life.

What is being built here is a **corpus** — an organized body of thought on the whole range of human and national questions, answered from one consistent premise, in public, under a real name, with the reasoning shown. Position papers on defense, education, money, justice, and law. A physics that proposes how God's omnipresence could be literally true and underlie the structure of matter. A theology of creation, evil, and atonement. A curriculum. A voting network (a framework for participatory democracy). Books elaborating the policy, the theory, the contingencies, and the philosophy of as many issues and perspectives as I can address in the years I have left.

Any one of those would be a life's work. Together they are a template for the transformation of a nation.

My hope is not that people will follow me, but that they will follow Christ — that they will accept Him as Lord and Savior and be reborn in Him. And my hope is that others will take courage to speak these things in their own circumstances: a man running for county commissioner, a school board member, a pastor, a young person forty years from now who dreams of making the world the place he actually wants to live in. Let him find that the hard thinking has been done, the philosophy justified, the objections already faced — so that he can build his life, his platform, and a coalition of righteous men who will support the work. All of my writing is copyrighted for fair use precisely so that it can be taken and used to start a campaign that is not mine.

Plato and Aristotle did not win elections. They left something to build from. That is the more durable form of victory, and it is what I am implementing with my books, videos, essays, and candidacy.

I do not feel an obligation to do more than witness the truth of the Way of Christ, the omnipresent and all-encompassing existence of God, and the practicality of living out the implications of that reality. I feel an obligation to create a template for society, learning, science, and religion. That much I am doing, and I am doing it as well as I know how.

## Objections to my candidacy

**"This is vanity dressed as ministry."** No man can prove his motives to another. What I can do is remove the ordinary rewards of vanity: no money is accepted, no ballot is sought, no crowd is gathered, no salary is drawn. What is left is a great deal of writing that few may read. If it is vanity, it is remarkably poor vanity. But I hold the question open rather than closed, and I invite all to keep asking it of me.

**"A candidacy with no ballot access is a category error — call it what it is and just write."** The word "candidate" carries an implied claim of contesting an election, and I do not contest one in the ordinary sense. I am contesting with the battle cry of the Revolutionary War: *No King but King Jesus.* But the objection deserves a direct answer as well, and it is the one given above: the frame of the office forces the work to be complete. An essayist may answer the questions that interest him. A candidate must answer all of them, consistently, and stand behind the whole. That discipline is not available any other way, and it is why I keep the word. The promise of service is real. The likelihood of a majority of Americans writing in my name is small, and would require divine assistance. I pray for that grace — not for me, but for my nation. I write because writing is the vehicle I have. If I am given a stage, I will take it. If not, I will keep influencing in every way I am given the opportunity.

**"You will discredit the message by attaching it to a candidacy no one can take seriously."** Possibly. It is a genuine risk, and I accept it knowingly. The alternative risk is that the message is never stated at national scale, because everyone who could state it has something to lose.

**"A Christian should not seek to establish his religion through the machinery of the state."** This is not an objection from unbelievers only. It is a serious position within the Christian tradition itself, held by Roger Williams, by the Baptists who pressed hardest for the First Amendment, and by many devout Americans today, who argue that coerced righteousness is not righteousness, that the church is corrupted whenever it borrows the sword, and that religious liberty for all is the surer protection for the faithful. I do not agree with where they land — I hold that all legislation is legislated morality, that the only question is *whose*, and that the God of the Bible is the right standard. But I want the reader to know that the disagreement is real, that it is held in good faith by serious Christians and serious secularists alike, and that the strongest form of it is not the caricature. My positions on this site are arguments, offered for examination. As I have written elsewhere, a theocracy of men claiming infallible direction is not desirable. Until the Lord returns, we must reason together, and share the light of our lives, which is the real testimony of a life committed to the Way of Christ.

**"Your standard would exclude most of your countrymen."** It would exclude no one from the argument, and that is the point. I am asking for persuasion, not compulsion; for revival, not enforcement. Whether the moral vision I hold could be enacted without becoming coercive is a fair question to press me on, and I would rather be pressed on it here than have it assumed.

## What I am running for

I am running for office to bring attention to the proposition that America's machine will run right only when her people are healed; that healing is personal before it is political; that policy follows character, and character follows belief, and belief must be pointed in the right direction — which I hold to be Jesus Christ as Lord and Savior.

The idea that America was founded on Christian principles is not a fringe invention. Justice Brewer wrote for a unanimous Supreme Court in 1892 that this is a Christian nation, though he wrote it as an observation alongside the ruling rather than as the ruling itself. A movement of ministers that began gathering in 1863 sought to write the acknowledgment of God and of Christ into the preamble of the Constitution; a delegation put it to Lincoln in 1864, and he received them cordially, saying he approved the general aspect of their movement but needed time to deliberate on the particulars. Congress never acted, and in 1874 the House Judiciary Committee declined the amendment as inexpedient. So the proposition has always been contested. But it has always been in the room, argued by serious men in the highest chambers of the republic — and the modern claim that it was never American at all is the innovation, not the tradition.

What did change was 1947. In *Everson v. Board of Education*, the Court gave us the metaphor of a high wall of separation, and that metaphor has since been used to strip any mention of God from public policy and public education. The phrase is not in the Constitution. Those who carry that banner present it as though it were. We must recover the conviction that a nation may be governed by Christian principles and reason, openly from the Bible. We must reject the arrangement in which Jesus may be named in church but not in the public square, while the church in turn is warned not to speak of politics for fear of its 501(c)(3) status.

Not all belief leads toward good ends. Men should be convinced in their minds as well as their hearts of the truth of what they hold. Belief follows from having a reason to believe — which is why I have spent forty years trying to give men a reason.

I am running to leave behind an organized body of thought that answers the country's questions from one premise, all the way down. I pray this body of work receives enough attention to live, to influence, and to encourage the next generation to pick it up and carry it further.

And I am running because I was asked to pray *Thy will be done on Earth as it is in Heaven*, and I believe that prayer can become a reality. It feels like an assignment. A man who prays it and then does nothing has not been transformed. This is what doing something looks like for me: writing, reasoning, standing publicly for what I hold, and asking my countrymen to consider it.

When election day comes, write in my name only if you are certain I would win — only if every indication is that the country has changed, and a vast groundswell has formed around this candidacy and its policies. Until that day, vote for the best man who can win, live and speak as a reborn man, and declare Jesus as Lord openly and proudly.
