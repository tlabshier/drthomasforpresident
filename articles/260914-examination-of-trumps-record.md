# A Just Weight

### On receipts, scorecards, and the discipline of counting honestly

*A position paper occasioned by Rod D. Martin's "Trump's Top 25 Achievements: The List Every Conservative Should Know and Use"*

Thomas Lee Abshier, ND — 14 September 2026

---

## Why I am answering a Facebook post with a position paper

Because the post is doing something serious, and because how a people keeps score is a question of character before it is a question of politics.

Mr. Martin's list is not a rant. It is an attempt at exactly what I have been asking for in this series: specifics, checkable, laid out so a citizen can examine them. He explicitly says it is not a list of promises but of laws enacted, cases won, and measurable results. That is the right standard, and he deserves to be held to it — not dismissed, and not swallowed whole either.

I published a position paper two weeks ago arguing that immigration enforcement had been betrayed. This list says the border was fixed. A serious man does not simply choose the source that suits him. He asks how both can be in front of him, and whether both might be partly true.

So this paper does three things. It gives Mr. Martin the credit he has coming on the items that hold up. It offers five tests I would apply to any political scorecard, my own included. And it takes up the passage at the end of his essay that I believe does more damage to his cause than any Democrat could.

---

## PART ONE: What checks out

I went and looked. Two of his biggest claims are substantially true, and anyone on my side of the aisle who has been waving them away should stop.

**The crime numbers are real.** The FBI's 2025 data show the violent crime rate falling 9.7 percent, the largest year-to-year decline since the Bureau began national estimates in 1936. Murder and nonnegligent manslaughter fell 18.1 percent, robbery 18.5 percent, rape 7.6 percent, aggravated assault 7.2 percent. The murder rate reached 4.1 per 100,000, tying 1955 and 1956 for the lowest in the Bureau's series, and producing the fewest estimated murders since 1968, when the country was some forty percent smaller. The data covered over 17,000 agencies and 96 percent of the population, and the nonpartisan Council on Criminal Justice found the same direction independently. Property crime fell 12.4 percent. This is not spin. It is a genuinely extraordinary year, and it deserves to be said plainly.

**Negative net migration is real.** Brookings — no friend of this administration — estimates net migration between negative 10,000 and negative 295,000 for 2025, the first negative year in at least half a century, and the Census Bureau's own figures point the same way. Border encounters collapsed: Southwest apprehensions of 237,538 in FY2025, the lowest since 1970, against 2.1 million the year before. Whatever one thinks of the methods, the direction of the border numbers is not in dispute, and I said in my own immigration paper that the border has been substantially secured. I repeat it here.

Credit where it is owed. Now the harder part.

## PART TWO: Five tests for any scorecard

These are the tests I would want applied to me, and I will publish my own record in a form that permits them.
### Test 1 — Enacted or announced?

Mr. Martin's own framing demands this distinction, and item 7 violates it. **"$18 trillion in announced investment commitments"** is an announcement figure, not an investment figure. It is presented as nearly two-thirds of the American economy committed in a year and a half. Corporate announcements are marketing documents; they are routinely aspirational, frequently double-counted across press releases, sometimes restate spending already planned, and are conditioned on markets that have not happened yet. The honest version is: a large volume of announced intentions, of which some meaningful fraction will become steel and concrete, and we will know which in five years.

I apply here the same rule I applied when I declined to use the figure of 107 million illegal aliens: **a number too large to be plausible is a liability even when it favors you.** If I would not accept a wild figure from a source I distrust, I may not accept one from a source I like.
### Test 2 — Correlation or causation?

The crime figures are accurate. The explanation attached to them is not established. Violent crime has been falling since the 2022 spike — it dropped 4.5 percent in 2024, before this administration took office — and the decline in 2025 was broad across communities of every size, including small towns and nonmetropolitan counties where no National Guard was ever deployed. The FBI's own release notes that the statistics do not establish what caused the decline.

The honest claim is that the drop continued and accelerated on this President's watch, and that his policies may well have contributed. The claim in the list — that this is what happens when you put the Guard in blue cities — is an assertion of cause that the data cannot carry. **Continuation is worth claiming. It is not the same as authorship.**
### Test 3 — Counting actions or weighing effects?

"129 regulations repealed for every new one" counts documents. A regulation is not a unit of mass. One consequential rule can outweigh two hundred trivial ones in either direction, and the ratio tells you nothing about which kind was repealed. The same applies to "eight peace deals in eight months" — a claim that cannot be evaluated at all until someone says which eight conflicts, what was signed, by whom, and whether the fighting stopped. Some of those agreements may be substantial. A reader cannot know from a number.
### Test 4 — Durable or reversible? (The test conservatives should care about most)

This is the one I most want my readers to internalize, because the list mixes three entirely different classes of achievement as though they were equivalent.

- **Court rulings** are durable for a generation. *Trump v. CASA* on nationwide injunctions and *Trump v. Slaughter* on removal of executive officers will outlast this administration whatever happens in November.

- **Statutes** are durable until repealed, and repeal requires majorities. The Laken Riley Act and the One Big Beautiful Bill are real law.

- **Executive actions** evaporate at noon on the day a successor takes the oath. Deregulation by agency action, the DEI removals, the faith offices, the personnel rules, the energy permits — all of it can be undone with a pen and a signature, exactly as it was done.

A list that presents all twenty-five as "changes in the structure of the Republic" obscures the most important political fact of the decade: **most of what has been accomplished by executive action can be erased in a week, and a movement that mistakes reversible action for permanent achievement will wake up having built on sand.** If these things are good, the task is to convert them into statute and precedent. Saying so is not black-pilling. It is the only way to keep them.
### Test 5 — Compared to what?

A scorecard should be measured against the promises made, not against the opposition's record. Against the opposition, almost any list looks good. Against the promises, the picture is mixed, which brings me to the thing I cannot pass over.

## PART THREE: The one that touches my own paper

Two weeks ago I wrote about Greg Bovino's testimony that interior immigration enforcement had been halted. This list says the border was made a real border. **Both are true, and understanding why is worth more than either claim alone.**

Items 1 and 2 are about *the line* — crossings, catch-and-release, releases into the country, net migration. Those numbers are real and they are about the border. Mr. Bovino's complaint was about *the interior* — about the Border Patrol being pulled off interior operations after Minnesota and stationed on the line. A man can be entirely right that the border is sealed and entirely right that interior enforcement stopped, because those are two different operations.

So the list is not lying. But it is reporting the half of the ledger that reads well and is silent on the half that does not, and a citizen reading only this list would not learn that the interior operations ended, or that a thirty-year career officer says he was investigated and stripped of protection for saying so.

Two further observations on the immigration items, in the spirit of just weights:

**The departure figures are contested even among people who want them to be large.** Brookings puts 2025 removals at 310,000 to 315,000 against the administration's claim of over 600,000, and notes that 2024 removals were already around 285,000. DHS's claim of 1.9 million self-deportations is an extrapolation from survey data, and the Center for Migration Studies calls it a myth, estimating roughly 200,000 documented and actual self-departures. Negative net migration is solid. The specific totals are not, and I would not cite them from a podium.

**And here is the absence that tells you the most.** In a list of twenty-five immigration, economic, and law-enforcement achievements, there is not one word about mandatory E-Verify or the prosecution of employers who knowingly hire illegal labor. Not one. That is the single measure that would end the problem at its source, it requires no mass roundups, and it is missing from every scorecard produced by every administration of both parties for forty years. **Enforcement has fallen on the powerless and spared the people who wrote the checks.** Until that item appears on a list like this one, the border half of the ledger is a holding action, not a solution.

## PART FOUR: The passage I cannot let stand

Near the end, Mr. Martin turns to conservatives who are not satisfied and writes that this is called black-pilling, that it is a loser mindset, and that those who engage in it deserve to lose horribly, painfully, personally — and that they will, unless serious people step up to save them, which he calls pathetic but necessary.

I want to be careful here, because I understand the frustration underneath it. A man who has worked for a cause and watched allies sit on their hands has earned some heat. And he is right that the work of persuasion belongs to citizens rather than to officials, and right that turnout elections are won by ordinary people talking to their neighbors. I have said the same thing on this site.

But I will not adopt the method, and I would ask my readers not to.

**Contempt is not persuasion.** A soft answer turneth away wrath, and grievous words stir up anger (Proverbs 15:1). Telling a discouraged ally that he deserves to suffer has never once moved a man to the polls. It moves him to the couch, and it moves the watching independent somewhere worse.

**And the deeper problem is epistemic, not merely tonal.** A movement that treats doubt as a character defect has disabled its own error-correction. If noticing a failure gets a man called a loser, then the leader will only ever be told what is going well — and that is precisely the condition Mr. Bovino described when he spoke of "gate guards" controlling what reached the President. You cannot simultaneously complain that the man at the top is surrounded by people who filter bad news and demand that your own side stop delivering any.

Discernment is not disloyalty. The Bereans searched the Scriptures daily to see whether the things Paul told them were so, and the text calls them *more noble* for it (Acts 17:11). If that standard applies to an apostle, it applies to a press release.

So: share the list. Learn the items. Check them. And where an item does not hold up, say so *out loud among your own people*, because a movement that can correct itself will outlive one that cannot.

---

## PART FIVE: My position

**On scorekeeping.** I will publish my record in the form Mr. Martin proposes, and invite exactly the scrutiny I have applied here. Every claim will carry its source. Announcements will be labeled announcements. Where a number is contested, I will state the range and the dispute. Where a result may have causes other than my policy, I will say so. A President who tells the truth about his own record when the truth is inconvenient has given the country something more valuable than any single achievement: a government whose reports can be believed.

**On durability.** I would spend political capital converting executive actions into statutes, even at the cost of a slower list. A win that dies with an administration was a loan, not a victory.

**On honest measurement.** As I proposed in the immigration paper: publish the numbers quarterly, with methodology stated in full and the model open to audit by parties who disagree with one another. The remedy for a country that cannot agree on facts is not a better press office. It is measurement that a hostile party can check.

**And on the thing no list can reach.** Suppose every one of these twenty-five items is exactly as described. Suppose the economy booms, the border holds, the courts are reformed, and crime falls further still. A nation can have all of that and still be a people who do not love their neighbors, do not know God, do not raise their children, and do not keep their word. Policy is downstream of character. That is the whole premise of my candidacy, and it is why I neither despair at a bad list nor rest at a good one. The receipts matter. They are not the account.
