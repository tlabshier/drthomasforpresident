# 261031 - Wisdom in Disaster Preparation

# Will the World Go Dark for 90 Days?


## Separating the Signal from the Noise — and What a Righteous People Should Do About It


*Dr. Thomas — 31 October 2026*


---


For the past two weeks, my inbox has filled with the same question in a dozen forms. A video asks, *Will the world go dark for 90 days?* A blog asks why governments everywhere are suddenly telling citizens to become preppers, and suggests something big may be revealed after the midterm elections. Another warns of a “financial doomsday” as AI-driven cyberattacks hit banks from Seoul to Tokyo. A former FBI official tells his audience to get radios, food, and rain collection “today. Please.” Friends forward these to me and ask: *Do they know something we don’t?*


I want to answer that question carefully, because it deserves a careful answer. My conclusion, stated plainly at the outset, is this:


**No evidence shows any government expects the world, or America, to go dark for 90 days. The “90 days” in these stories comes from a Pentagon standard for military bases, not from a forecast. But the concern beneath the headlines is real. Our civilization has quietly made itself fragile — its food, water, money, and power now run through internet-connected controls that hostile actors are learning to attack, and AI is making them faster. The right response is neither panic nor dismissal. It is preparation at three levels — household, community, and nation — undertaken as an act of love for our neighbors.**


I stand on that position. Below I give my reasons, mark where the information is uncertain, and explain why the worry remains plausible even after the exaggerations are stripped away.


---


## 1. Where the “90 Days” Actually Comes From


The figure traces to a single news item. In late October, Fox News Digital reported on documents describing a Pentagon initiative called FORTRESS (Federal Operations for Resilience, Troop Resources, Energy, Supply, and Survivability). Under it, every major military installation in the continental United States is expected to sustain essential operations for at least 90 days without outside electricity, water, fuel, or communications. The plan calls for portable nuclear reactors, large battery banks, and microgrids so bases can disconnect from civilian power during a prolonged outage. The Pentagon’s spokesman summarized the thinking in three words: “Power is combat now.”


That is a *planning standard*, not a prediction. Militaries plan for the worst case; that is their job. A base commander in Wyoming observed that his base as a whole could not run for 90 days on its own, though its most important functions could. And one British outlet rightly noted that a plan to install new energy systems is not evidence that bases can already do it. FORTRESS tells us what the Department of Defense considers an intolerable vulnerability. It does not tell us that a 90-day blackout is scheduled.


Somewhere between the Pentagon’s documents and the YouTube thumbnails, “bases must be able to survive 90 days without the grid” became “the world will go dark for 90 days.” That is a change in kind, not degree.


## 2. What the Governments Are Actually Saying


The second pillar of the story is the claim that governments worldwide have *suddenly* and *simultaneously* begun urging citizens to stockpile. Here the facts are more ordinary than the commentary suggests.


**The United Kingdom.** On 22 October, the Cabinet Office launched “Be Ready,” the first phase of a three-year public campaign. It asks each household to keep 72 hours of supplies: about three liters of drinking water per person per day, food that needs no cooking or refrigeration, a battery- or wind-up radio, a torch, medicines, and cash. The government itself says the campaign builds on household-resilience guidance first published in May 2024 and brings Britain into line with France, Ireland, and the Netherlands, which already recommend the same three days. The European Commission recommended a 72-hour standard for all member states in March 2025. Britain’s new Defense Secretary, Wes Streeting, did promote the campaign and has separately warned that Britain must be ready to fight a war within roughly 1,000 days — but he also said he wanted people “alert but not alarmed.”


**Sweden.** Sweden’s civil-defense booklet, *If Crisis or War Comes*, has been mailed to every household since 2018 and was revised in 2024. It is not new.


**Japan.** Disaster Management Minister Goshi Hosono (the blog that circulated widely misspells his name) will issue a nationwide “emergency request” on 2 November — the day Japan’s new Disaster Management Agency opens. The request urges at least three days of food, water, and emergency toilet supplies, and preferably a week. The stated purpose is Japan’s well-known catalog of national-scale natural hazards: a Nankai Trough earthquake, a major quake beneath Tokyo, large quakes along the northern trenches, and a possible eruption of Mount Fuji. Japan has urged household stockpiling since the 2011 earthquake and tsunami killed about 19,000 people. The date is tied to an agency’s birthday, not to secret intelligence.


So the pattern is real — many governments are emphasizing preparedness this autumn — but the coordination is mostly explained by public, documented causes: the EU’s 2025 strategy, which Britain is now matching; Japan’s new agency; and a European security environment shaped by the war in Ukraine and by Russian sabotage campaigns. What is noticeably absent is any government asking citizens to prepare for 90 days. Every official figure is three to seven days.


## 3. The Mortality Figures


Several pieces in circulation cite a statistic that “90 percent of Americans would die” if the grid were down for a year. That figure deserves its context.


It originates with members of the Congressional EMP Commission, most prominently Dr. William Graham and Dr. Peter Pry, who testified that a nationwide blackout lasting about a year — such as might follow a high-altitude nuclear electromagnetic pulse or an extreme solar storm — could lead to the death of *up to* 90 percent of Americans through starvation, disease, and the collapse of the systems that feed and water dense populations. Others have offered lower estimates, around two-thirds.


Three cautions are in order. First, it is an upper bound for a *year-long, nationwide* outage, not an estimate for 90 days and not a description of any regional event. Second, it is a modeled judgment by advocates of grid hardening, not a measured fact; no such event has occurred in a modern society, and the figure is contested. Third — and this is the point I most want my readers to keep — *the figure’s uncertainty does not make the underlying logic wrong.* A nation of 340 million people is fed by trucks, refrigeration, pumps, and payment systems that all require electricity. Remove electricity long enough, and people will die in large numbers. The exact percentage is uncertain; the direction is not.


## 4. Why the Concern Remains Plausible


Having stripped away the exaggerations, I want to be equally honest about what remains, because a great deal remains.


**Attacks on physical systems through the internet are no longer theoretical.** On 12 October, attackers reached the refrigeration controls of supermarkets across France, forcing extended defrost cycles, disabling alarms, and in some reports driving temperatures as high as 50°C. The French finance minister told Parliament that roughly 700 of the country’s 10,000 supermarkets were affected. Food was destroyed, and some stores caught fire. Analysts reported spillover into Spain, Portugal, Poland, Germany, and Belgium. The controllers involved were, by the manufacturers’ own account, largely older models no longer receiving security updates and, in many cases, exposed directly to the public internet. In the same season, U.S. military commissaries suffered refrigeration outages now being investigated as a possible hack, and Minnesota and Michigan reported cyberattacks on state water systems.


**The attacks are getting faster.** Industry threat reports document a steep rise in intrusions by adversaries using AI tools, and an expert quoted by the Associated Press observed that such an attack on refrigeration controls is “not too difficult” to carry out. Whatever the exact numbers, the trend is that the skill required to cause physical harm through a keyboard is falling.


**The grid has been physically attacked before.** In December 2022, gunmen shot up two substations in Moore County, North Carolina, leaving about 45,000 customers without power, some for five days. No one has been convicted.


**The intuition of unnaturalness is sound.** Most people rightly sense that something is disordered about a society in which a grocery store’s freezers can be switched off from another continent, in which a family’s savings exist only as an entry in a networked database, and in which almost no household could feed itself for two weeks. That intuition is not paranoia. It is the recognition that convenience has been purchased at the price of resilience, and that the bill can come due all at once.


**Incentives point toward under-preparation.** Equipment makers profit by selling connected devices and by ending support for old ones; retailers save money by running cold chains through cheap remote controllers; utilities are rewarded for efficiency, not for redundancy. No one in that chain is paid to imagine the bad day. When the system’s incentives systematically discount a risk, citizens are right to discount the system’s reassurances.


**And the fear-sellers have incentives too.** I note, without accusing any particular writer, that much of the alarmist material arrives with affiliate links, survival-food sponsors, and book promotions. That does not make its warnings false. It does mean we should weigh them by their evidence rather than their volume.


## 5. The Moral Question


Readers of this site know the test I apply to every public question: *Is it righteous? If so, is it loving? Would I want it done to me?*


**Spreading fear beyond the evidence fails the first test.** To tell people the world will go dark for 90 days, when no one with knowledge has said so, misrepresents the proportion of the danger. Hyperbole is lying by proportion. It also fails the third test: no one wants to be frightened into panic-buying, and panic itself is a hazard — empty shelves in Britain followed within days of the Be Ready announcement.


**Dismissing the danger fails the second test.** A leader who tells people “nothing to see here” while freezers are being hacked and bases are being told to plan for 90 days without the grid is not loving his neighbor. He is sparing himself an awkward conversation at the neighbor’s expense.


**Preparing is love made practical.** The household that keeps two weeks of water and food is not merely protecting itself; it is getting out of the line at the shelter so the elderly widow down the street can be served first. The church that keeps a generator and a pantry becomes a place of refuge when the lights go out. A nation that hardens its grid protects the poor most of all, because the poor are least able to flee, stockpile, or buy their way through a crisis. Scripture commends the ant that *“provideth her meat in the summer”*** (Proverbs 6:8)**, and Joseph’s seven years of storage saved Egypt and his own family. Prudence is not faithlessness; it is stewardship.


## 6. What I Propose


My platform rests on *A New ERA* — Energy, Righteousness, and Accountability. Each speaks to this problem.


### For households: prepare beyond the official minimum


Three days is a floor, not a goal. I recommend that every household that can afford it work toward two weeks: water (about a gallon per person per day), food that needs no cooking, essential medicines, a battery- or crank-powered radio, light, a way to keep warm, cash in small bills, and paper copies of key documents. Do it steadily, not in a panic, and rotate what you store into ordinary meals. Then — and this is the part most guides omit — know your neighbors, especially the elderly and disabled, and decide in advance who will check on whom.


### For communities: make churches and towns resilience hubs


Congregations already know how to feed people. I urge every church to consider becoming a neighborhood resilience center: backup power, stored water, a pantry, a list of vulnerable members, and a plan for communicating when phones fail. Local governments should identify critical facilities — water pumping, sewage, hospitals, nursing homes — and ask, as the Pentagon now asks of its bases, *how long can this run on its own?*


### Energy: extend the FORTRESS principle to civilians


If our military judges it intolerable to depend on the civilian grid for 90 days, then the civilians who depend on that grid deserve the same seriousness. The technologies FORTRESS contemplates — small reactors, storage, and microgrids — should be extended to hospitals, water systems, and regional food distribution. I continue to advocate for thorium molten-salt reactors as the long-term solution and the foundation of an abundant, distributed, and resilient power supply. I also support hardening the large transformers the grid depends on against geomagnetic storms and electromagnetic pulse, and rebuilding domestic capacity to manufacture them, since a destroyed transformer that must be ordered from overseas can mean a blackout measured in months.


### Accountability: no critical control exposed to the open internet


The French supermarket attack succeeded because food-safety control systems were reachable from the public internet and ran software their makers had stopped supporting. That should be unacceptable for anything that keeps people alive. I propose that operators of critical systems — water, power, food cold chains, medical devices — be required to keep control networks off the public internet, and that manufacturers who sell internet-connected controllers for such uses bear liability when they abandon security support for devices still in service. Accountability means the party who profits from a risk bears its cost.


### Righteousness: truthful leadership


Finally, I ask leaders — including myself — to tell the public plainly what they know, what they suspect, and what they are preparing for, and why. Much of the speculation now circulating feeds on the sense that officials are withholding something. The cure is candor. A government that explains its reasons does not need to frighten its people, and a people who are told the truth do not need to be managed.


## 7. Conclusion


Will the world go dark for 90 days? Nothing in the public record says so, and I counsel my readers not to believe anyone who claims to know a date. But the questions behind the headline are serious ones. We have built a civilization that works beautifully until it doesn’t, and we have connected its vital organs to a network that our adversaries — and increasingly their machines — are learning to exploit. That is not a prophecy. It is a description.


The response I advocate is neither fear nor complacency, but stewardship: prepare your house, care for your neighbor, ask your community to plan, and ask your government to harden what keeps us alive. I hold this position open to a better argument. But I don’t think the better argument is that we should do nothing.
