# Legality Is Not Righteousness

### How tyranny parades as law — and why only a people under God can unmask it

Thomas Lee Abshier, ND — 27 August 2026

*Prompted by Michael Boldin's essay ["How Tyranny Parades as Law"](https://tenthamendmentcenter.com/2025/05/16/how-tyranny-parades-as-law/) at the Tenth Amendment Center (tenthamendmentcenter.com, May 16, 2025), which gathers the warnings of the founding generation and their teachers on this subject. Read it. What follows is not a summary of his piece but a continuation of it — because the founders diagnosed the disease and the diagnosis points to a remedy that constitutional mechanics alone cannot supply.*

---

## The oldest trick in the book

Jefferson summarized the problem in one sentence: law is often but the tyrant's will, and always so when it violates the rights of an individual.

Notice what he did *not* say. He did not say tyranny breaks the law. He said tyranny *becomes* the law. That is why most people never see tyranny coming. They are watching for the criminal who climbs through the window, while the real danger walks through the front door holding a signed and stamped document, accompanied by officials, announced in the press, and titled something noble.

The founding generation understood this because they had lived it. Every act that drove them to revolution was legal. The Stamp Act was legal. The Writs of Assistance — general warrants letting agents ransack any home for anything — were legal. The Intolerable Acts were named, by the government that passed them, the Coercive Acts, and even that honest label was legal. When the colonists objected, they were not called patriots. They were called lawbreakers. Which, by the statutes then in force, they were.

So the men who built this country did not make the mistake we make. They never confused *legal* with *right*. They knew these are two different words because they name two different things, and they knew that the space between those two words is precisely where tyranny lives.
## Why the mask works

The disguise succeeds for a reason that is not political but spiritual, and this is where I want to take the argument beyond what constitutional scholars usually do.

The word "law" carries moral weight. When we hear it, something in us stands at attention. Why? Because law, in the deep history of our civilization, was not originally the ruler's will. It was the ruler's *master*. The whole Western legal inheritance — from the Ten Commandments through the common law to the Declaration — rests on the premise that there is a Law above the law: a standard of right that no king wrote and no king can repeal, against which every human statute is measured and to which every human ruler will answer. The king is under the law, and the law is under God. Samuel Rutherford compressed it into two Latin words that helped ignite a revolution: *Lex Rex.* The law is king.

That is why "legal" *feels* like "righteous" to us. For centuries, the two were tethered. The statute borrowed its authority from the Standard above it.

Now watch what modern man has done. He has kept the word, kept the reverence, kept the courtrooms and the robes and the solemn language — and quietly cut the cord to the Standard. Law is no longer measured against the character of God or even against nature, and nature's God; it is whatever the sovereign enacts, and the sovereign is whoever currently holds power. The legal philosophers call this positivism. I call it the theft of borrowed glory. The statute now arrives wearing vestments it inherited from an altar it has abandoned, and the people, still trained by ten centuries of instinct, bow to the vestments.

That is the mask of legality, explained. Tyranny does not have to manufacture the costume. It found the costume hanging in the church's closet.
## The war over words

The old writers Boldin quotes saw that the decisive battle is fought in vocabulary — that a few loud words rule the majority, that the poison is always coated in something harmless, that oppression is sold under the sacred veil of the public good.

Scripture said it first, and said it as a curse: *"Woe unto them that call evil good, and good evil; that put darkness for light, and light for darkness"* **(Isaiah 5:20)**. The prophet is not describing a debating tactic. He is describing the final stage of a society's corruption — the stage at which the moral vocabulary itself has been captured, so that the conscience of the people, which can only think in the words it has been given, is turned against itself.

You know the modern catalog without my listing much of it. Surveillance is *safety*. Censorship is *protecting democracy*. Confiscation is *fairness*. Deliberately sowing confusion in children is *inclusion*. Debasing the currency — which quietly takes the stored labor of every working family — is *stimulus*. Each name is chosen so that opposing the thing sounds like opposing the virtue. Who could be against safety, fairness, protection? And so the citizen who resists is maneuvered, by vocabulary alone, into the position of the villain before a single argument has been made.

The defense against this is not cleverness. It is a discipline the Bible calls loving the truth — and it is costly, because the truth-teller must be willing to be called every ill name the machine can print. Paul warns of those who perish *"because they received not the love of the truth"* and are therefore given over to strong delusion **(2 Thessalonians 2:10–11)**. Note the order. The delusion is not the cause of the corruption; it is the penalty for preferring comfort to truth. A people that wants to be deceived will always find a government happy to oblige.
## A warning against party spirit

This weapon has been abused by Republicans too, or perhaps those who name themselves Republicans but do not legislate according to the platform of the party. The mask of legality is standard equipment for power, and every faction in American life has worn it. It is right to object when those who campaigned as conservatives betrayed the principles they supposedly stood for. Emergency powers stretched past their emergencies; surveillance programs blessed by secret courts; executive orders doing what Congress declined to do; civil asset forfeiture — the taking of property from citizens never convicted of anything. Such acts parade under color of law, that is, the color of God's law. Bastiat coined the phrase legal plunder in 1850, and it still applies.

If your test for tyranny is "which team did it," you have no test at all — you have a jersey. The only test that holds is the one Jefferson stated: does the act, whatever its name and whoever its author, violate the God-given rights of the individual? Apply that test evenly, or you are not a watchman; you are a partisan waiting for your turn at the costume closet.

And one caution more. The appeal to a higher law has itself been counterfeited. Kings claimed divine right; inquisitors burned men in God's name; every ambitious man who ever wanted to escape accountability has been tempted to claim Heaven's proxy. So let me be clear about what I am *not* proposing. I am not proposing that any man, myself included, be trusted as the infallible voice of the Law above the law. A theocracy of men claiming private revelation is just tyranny with better music. The higher Standard I appeal to is public, written, ancient, and testable — the revealed character of God in Scripture, reasoned about openly, argued over freely, imposed by persuasion and never by the sword. Until the Lord returns, we reason together. The moment anyone claims the Standard exempts him from being questioned, he has put on the mask himself.
## The unmasking disciplines

How does an ordinary citizen see through the costume? Not by expertise. By habits — and each is available to anyone.

**Judge the act, not the label.** Ask of every law what it *does* to a concrete human being's life, liberty, property, conscience, and family — never what it is named. The name is marketing; the mechanism is the truth. If you cannot describe what a bill does to one individual, you do not yet know what it is.

**Reverse the parties.** Whatever power is being created, imagine it wielded by the faction you most distrust — because it will be. Any power you would not hand your enemy, do not hand your friend.

**Watch for the exemptions.** When the authors of a rule exempt themselves from it — insider trading rules, mandates, security details for men who would disarm you — the rule is not law. It is a leash, and the exemption tells you who holds it.

**Distrust the emergency.** Nearly every durable expansion of power in the last century entered through a crisis and never left. Emergency is the costume's zipper. Whatever is granted "temporarily, because of the crisis" must carry its own death date, and a people serious about liberty holds funerals for expired powers.

**Keep your own vocabulary.** Refuse the renaming. Call taking *taking*, call censorship *censorship*, call a lie *a lie* — courteously, without rancor, and without exception. Trenchard was right that a few loud words rule the world; then let the remnant who love the truth keep the true words in circulation, at whatever social price. The first act of resistance is linguistic, and it is available to everyone, today, for free.

**And examine yourself first.** The mask works on us because something in us wants it to. Machiavelli's observation that men are satisfied with appearances is not a fact about *them* — it is a fact about the unregenerate heart, mine and yours. The man who will not face the truth about his own sins is in no condition to face the truth about his government's. Repentance is not a detour from civic vigilance. It is its training ground.
## The remedy the founders assumed

Here is the conclusion the constitutionalists reached. The founders built the finest set of parchment barriers ever devised — enumerated powers, separated branches, federalism, a bill of rights — and then told us plainly that none of it would hold by itself. John Adams: *"our Constitution was made only for a moral and religious people, and is wholly inadequate to the government of any other."*

Why inadequate? Because every barrier on paper must be enforced by men, and men who do not fear God will construe every ambiguity in favor of their own power — the Federal Farmer's warning — and a people who do not love truth will applaud them for it, so long as the costume is convincing. Structure without virtue is a lock on a door whose hinges have been removed.

Augustine said an unjust law is no law at all, and Martin Luther King Jr. stood on that sentence in a Birmingham jail to break laws that were legal and evil at once. But notice what the sentence requires: a standard of justice above the statute book, known and honored by enough of the people that the appeal to it lands. Strip a nation of that standard and King's argument becomes noise — and every future tyranny becomes unanswerable, because "it's legal" will be the final word, there being no court above it.

That is the true stake in whether America will return to God, and it is why this website exists. Not because Christians want their turn at the whip — the whole point is that no one can be trusted with the whip. But because liberty itself is a theological achievement. Rights that are gifts of the state can be revoked by the state, legally, with stamps, seals, and noble names. Rights endowed by the Creator stand above every legislature that ever convened. Remove the Creator from the public argument, and you have not secularized liberty; you have unhosted it, and it is only a matter of time — one crisis, one election, one emergency — before the costume closet opens again.

Law was given to man as a servant of righteousness. It becomes a tyrant the moment it becomes its own god. America's choice is not between religion and neutrality. It is between the Law above the law — and the tyrant's will, parading in its stolen robes.
