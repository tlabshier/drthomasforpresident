# 261031 - Wokism Part 8 - The Better Gospel

# The False Gods of Wokism


## Part 8: The Better Gospel — What a Righteous Nation Offers


by Thomas Lee Abshier, ND 11/1/2026


## Plain language summary


This series has examined the woke religion doctrine by doctrine: race, sex, speech, tolerance, crime, the border, and the schools. Under all its names, it worships one god: the self, with its desires and its grievances. Each of its doctrines tried to keep a Christian good, such as concern for the oppressed, compassion, freedom, mercy, or education, while discarding the God who gave it. Like cut flowers, those goods looked alive for a while and then withered. The answer is not a louder political movement. It is the gospel, stated plainly. God lives our lives with us, from the inside. He feels every choice we make. Our sins wound Him, and His holiness cannot abide them, so He entered His own creation as a man and paid for them Himself. Everyone who receives that gift is forgiven, given a new heart, and called a son of God rather than a slave. Such a person keeps God’s law freely, because he loves the One who lives it with him. A nation of such people needs very little government. The political shape of that gospel is what I call A New ERA: **Energy** (the motive of love for God and family), **Righteousness** (the standard of God’s character), and **Accountability** (kept as close to the individual as possible: conscience, family, church, community, and only then the state). When a people worship false gods, the nation suffers. When they turn back to the true God, He heals their land. This campaign is not finally a political strategy. It is a call to revival.


---


## One god under many names


When my friend Rick Kirchner posted his glossary of the left’s vocabulary, he called one entry “the woke religion.” In this series, I set out to test whether that was a fair description and to measure the worldview it named by its own coherence and by the standard of Scripture.


I believe the series has shown that the description is fair, and more than fair. The woke religion has doctrines, clergy, confessions, heresies, excommunications, and a vision of the end of history. And under all its many names, it worships a single god. Paul identified that god twenty centuries ago: people who *“changed the truth of God into a lie, and worshipped and served the creature more than the Creator”* (Romans 1:25).


The creature is the self. Look back at each doctrine:


- **In race** (Part 2), the self’s group decides its guilt or innocence.

- **In sex and gender** (Part 3), the self’s desires define the body.

- **In speech and institutions** (Part 4), the self’s faction decides what is true.

- **In tolerance** (Part 5), the self refuses to call any god true, and leaves the house empty for whatever moves in.

- **In crime and the border** (Part 6), the self’s sympathy decides whose suffering counts.

- **In the schools** (Part 7), the self is taught to be its own god from childhood.


And at the root (Part 1), the religion has a doctrine of sin with no redemption. It knows that wrongs persist, but it has no God to pay for them, so it passes the debt down by category forever and cannot forgive anyone.


## Cut flowers


Every doctrine of the woke religion began with something good. Concern for the oppressed is Biblical. Compassion for the suffering is Biblical. Freedom is Biblical. Mercy for the offender is Biblical. Welcoming the stranger is Biblical. Educating children is Biblical. The woke religion did not invent any of these. It inherited them from the Christian civilization it now despises.


But it tried to keep them while cutting them off from God, and a good cut off from God is like a cut flower. It looks alive in the vase for a while. Then it withers, because it has no root. Concern for the oppressed, cut off from God, becomes a permanent grievance with no forgiveness. Compassion becomes flattery. Freedom becomes self-worship. Mercy becomes the abandonment of the victim. Tolerance becomes indifference. Education becomes indoctrination in the self.


The answer is not to throw away the flowers. It is to plant them back in the soil they came from.


## The better gospel


Here is that soil, stated as plainly as I can state it. It is the truth at the center of this series and of my ministry, and it is the place where I believe the Christian faith, rightly understood, answers every doctrine of the woke religion at its root.


**God lives our lives with us, from the inside.** He does not merely watch us from a distance or dwell in us as a guest. *“In him we live, and move, and have our being”* (Acts 17:28). Nothing exists outside of God, so every human life, every experience, and every choice happens within His being. He feels it all: the joy of a faithful marriage, the grief of a murdered son’s mother, the shame of the liar, the courage of the man who tells the truth at a cost.


**His law is not arbitrary.** Because God lives our lives, His commandments are not rules a distant lawgiver made up for His own reasons. They describe what gives Him joy and what wounds Him. *“Against thee, thee only, have I sinned,”* David said (Psalm 51:4). Sin is not breaking a rule. It is wounding the One who loves us most, from the inside.


**His holiness cannot abide our sin.** God is light, and in Him is no darkness at all (1 John 1:5). His purity recoils from the evil that arises within His experience, walls it off, and judges it. Wrongdoing does not fade with time, and no good deed done later can cancel an earlier wrong. No creature can pay for its own sin.


**So God paid for it Himself.** The remedy had to come from inside creation, from one who was both innocent and able to die. The Creator became a man. He lived our life, felt our pain in His own flesh, and died unjustly, and His death paid a debt He did not owe. He rose, and He offers that payment to everyone who will receive it. (I have set out this argument step by step in *The Premise Change* and *Reconciliation of Man with the Father*, listed below.)


**He gives a new heart.** *“A new heart also will I give you, and a new spirit will I put within you”* (Ezekiel 36:26). In the fellowship I call this the one-trick pony: everything depends on the changed heart. A heart that has received God’s love is oriented to the highest morality, which is the person of Christ, and its governing rule becomes to love others as we have been loved.


**He calls us sons, not slaves.** *“Wherefore thou art no more a servant, but a son”* (Galatians 4:7). We keep His law not out of fear of a master, as Islam demands, and not as the arbitrary rules the woke religion rebels against, but freely, out of love for a Father who lives our lives with us and rejoices when we choose what is good.


That is the better gospel. Every doctrine of the woke religion is a distorted copy of some part of it.


## A glossary for a righteous nation


Rick Kirchner answered Rick Steves’s glossary with one of his own. I want to close the series the same way, by giving the woke vocabulary back its Christian meaning.


**Justice:** Giving every man what he is due for what he has done, with one measure for all (Leviticus 19:15). The victim’s wound is answered and the offender is offered a way home, as at the Cross.


**Equity:** What you have deposited. God’s love is offered to all without condition, but its fruit is received by choosing His way. Every cup of water given in His name is a deposit in the storehouse. There is no equity without a deposit.


**Inclusion:** Bringing people into God’s family, where there is neither Jew nor Greek, bond nor free, male nor female (Galatians 3:28).


**Diversity:** Giving the same love, God’s love, to all people in every place we go. The final picture of Scripture is every nation, kindred, people, and tongue standing together before the throne (Revelation 7:9).


**Privilege:** Stewardship. *“For unto whomsoever much is given, of him shall be much required”* (Luke 12:48). Advantage is not guilt to be confessed; it is a trust to be used for others.


**Identity:** Who I am in Christ. *“I am crucified with Christ: nevertheless I live; yet not I, but Christ liveth in me”* (Galatians 2:20). An identity secured by grace can hear itself criticized and lose nothing.


**Lived experience:** What God lives with every person. No one sees from every standpoint except the One who lives every life, which is why He alone can judge justly.


**Liberation:** Freedom from sin, not freedom to sin. *“Stand fast therefore in the liberty wherewith Christ hath made us free”* (Galatians 5:1).


**Tolerance:** Bearing with people you believe are wrong, protecting their freedom, and telling them the truth in love. Tolerance requires believing that some things are true.


**Safety:** A refuge in God, and a law that protects the innocent. Not protection from hearing ideas.


**The right side of history:** The side of the God who will judge it.


## The political shape of the gospel: A New ERA


I first ran for office in Oregon in 2003 under the slogan *A New ERA*: Energy, Righteousness, and Accountability. I have come back to that framework because it gives the gospel a political shape without turning it into a political program.


**Energy** is the motive. People need wind at their backs. A system works when it aligns with what actually moves people to act. Capitalism works because it rewards the self-interest of a man providing for his family. Communism fails because it demands saintly motivation it cannot produce. The deepest energy of all is love for the God who lives our lives, and the joy of giving Him a good experience of our choices. A people moved by that love will work, give, raise families, and care for their neighbors without being compelled.


**Righteousness** is the standard. Love alone is not enough. “Love your neighbor,” detached from a standard of right and wrong, becomes whatever feels good, and can justify anything. The standard is God’s character, revealed in Scripture, not human consensus. That is why the policy test I use is ordered: is it righteous; if so, is it loving; and would I want it done to me, measured by the self committed to righteousness, not by momentary appetite.


**Accountability** is the enforcement, and it belongs as close to the individual as possible. First conscience, then family, then church, then community, and only last the state. The closer accountability stays to the person, the healthier the society. A government that must enforce everything is the government of a people who have stopped governing themselves.


Notice how this answers the woke religion’s deepest error. The woke religion, having lost the changed heart, must enforce its creed from outside: through training, speech codes, loyalty statements, and the power of institutions. It has to, because it has nothing inside a man to work with. The gospel works from the inside out. A people with changed hearts need very little enforcement, because their own conscience, family, and church do most of the work.


## Why this is not a theocracy


A critic will say that this is a call for Christian rule, and that history shows where that leads. I take the objection seriously, because Christianity done wrong has done real harm.


My answer is the one I gave in *Transforming America*. God gave man free choice. He did not have to; He could have made beings that obeyed Him automatically. He made us able to refuse Him, because love that cannot refuse is not love. So a forced conversion is no conversion, and a government that tries to compel faith works against the purpose of creation itself. The brake on coercion is written into the center of the gospel.


That is why everything I have proposed in this series follows a few firm rules. The state may name sin, as a family names what falls short of its standard. It may protect the innocent and punish crimes that have victims. It must never watch a man’s private life, never compel him to believe, and never enforce worship. Prayer is invited, never required. The sacraments belong to the church and the family. The president leads by voice, as the Surgeon General did when he told the nation the truth about smoking and adult smoking fell by more than two-thirds without anyone being jailed.


A coercive “Christianity” is a counterfeit wearing the cross. It has no power to change a heart, and the changed heart is the only thing that has ever transformed a nation.


## Applying the test to the gospel itself


**Is it righteous?** Yes. It rests on the character of God, the only standard that does not contradict itself.


**Is it loving?** Yes. It offers every person, of every race, sex, faith, and history, the same thing: forgiveness for what he has actually done, healing for what was done to him, a new heart, and a place in God’s family.


**Would I want it done to me?** Yes. I would want to be told the truth, judged by what I have done rather than what I am, forgiven when I repent, and called a son of God rather than a slave or a category. That is what the gospel does. The woke religion cannot offer it, because it has no God to forgive.


## Why the stakes are real


When a people worship false gods, the nation suffers. That is the pattern of the entire Old Testament, and I believe it is the pattern of our time. The suffering does not usually come as fire from heaven. More often it comes as God letting a people have what they demanded, and letting them feel its weight. *“God gave them up”* (Romans 1:24, 26, 28). Families come apart, children grow up without fathers, trust collapses, and an empty house waits for whatever will move in.


But the same Scripture holds out the promise: *“If my people, which are called by my name, shall humble themselves, and pray, and seek my face, and turn from their wicked ways; then will I hear from heaven, and will forgive their sin, and will heal their land”* (2 Chronicles 7:14).


Notice to whom that promise is addressed: *my people*. Not the left, not the government, not the culture. Judgment begins at the house of God (1 Peter 4:17). The woke religion grew in the space the church left empty. Where we were silent about injustice, it claimed concern for the oppressed. Where we abused the freedom we have in Christ, it claimed freedom for itself. Where we stopped teaching our children, it taught them. The first repentance must be ours.


## Where the argument must be careful


No political victory can produce a changed heart. Laws can restrain evil and protect the innocent; they cannot make anyone love God. A Christian movement that puts its hope in winning elections has already made the woke religion’s mistake: trying to change people from the outside.


And the people drawn into the woke religion are not our enemies. Most of them are looking for justice, meaning, and belonging, and the church failed to show them where those are found. They are neighbors God lives and loves, and our task is to win them, not to defeat them.


Neither of these boundaries weakens the thesis. The woke religion is a false god. The gospel is the true one. And the nation must choose.


## What each of us can do


This series has proposed policies in each part, and I stand by them. But the most important things on this list do not require anyone to be elected.


- **Repent first.** Begin with your own heart and home. Ask whether a visitor could tell, from how you speak to your wife, raise your children, spend your money, and use your evenings, that God has done something in you.

- **Teach your children.** Teach them the moral law, and teach them why: God lives their lives and feels their choices. Do not leave their formation to anyone else.

- **Speak the truth plainly.** With reasons, without exaggeration, and with love. Do not be afraid of the argument.

- **Witness.** Tell your neighbors, including your Muslim neighbors and your woke neighbors, about the Father who lives their lives and has paid for their sins.

- **Serve close to home.** Run for your school board. Attend its meetings. Vote in every local election. The Christos Voting Network exists to help citizens hold every level of government accountable to a righteous standard.

- **Vote for righteousness.** Measure every candidate, of every party, by the same test: is what he proposes righteous, is it loving, and would you want it done to you?


## Where to push


- **Is the self really the god of the woke religion?** I have claimed that each of its doctrines makes the self, its desires or its group, the final authority. Show me a doctrine where it does not.

- **Can a nation be Christian without being coercive?** I say yes, if it names sin, protects the innocent, and never compels worship. Show me where that line has failed, and why.

- **Is “God lives us” the center of the gospel, or one emphasis among many?** I hold that it explains why God’s law is not arbitrary, why sin wounds Him, and why the Cross was necessary. Show me a better account of all three.


## A word of thanks


I am grateful to my friend Rick Kirchner, whose glossary started this series. He named the problem in a single entry. I have tried to examine it at length, and to offer what I believe is the only answer that can last. I hold these positions with conviction, and I hold them open to a better argument. If you think I am wrong, push where I have asked you to push. If you think I am right, begin with the first item on the list above.
