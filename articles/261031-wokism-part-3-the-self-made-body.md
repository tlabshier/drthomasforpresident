# 261031 - Wokism Part 3 - Sex and Gender

# The False Gods of Wokism


## Part 3: The Self-Made Body — Sex, Gender, and the God Who Lives Our Flesh


by Thomas Lee Abshier, ND 11/1/2026


## Plain language summary


Sexuality is where America divides. On theft and violence, nearly everyone agrees. On sex, the country splits, because the modern world experiences God’s sexual law as an arbitrary rule imposed by a distant lawgiver, and rebellion against it feels like freedom. The woke religion carries that rebellion to its end: the self, and its desires, define the body. A man can declare himself a woman, a child can be set on a path of hormones and surgery, and anyone who questions it is accused of harm. The Bible answers at the root. God lives our lives from the inside, including our bodies: “your body is the temple of the Holy Ghost… and ye are not your own” (1 Corinthians 6:19). His sexual law is not arbitrary. It describes what builds the families He designed, what gives Him joy, and what wounds Him. We keep it because we love Him. The nation should say this plainly, protect children from irreversible medical interventions, protect women’s sports and private spaces, and never compel anyone to affirm what he believes is false. It should not send police into anyone’s bedroom. Those who struggle with their sex or their desires are persons God lives and loves, and they deserve the truth told with compassion.


---


## Where the country divides


In *Transforming America*, Isak and I worked through every major area of public life looking for where Americans actually disagree. The answer surprised me by its simplicity. On stealing and killing, nearly everyone agrees, from the atheist to the priest. We don’t want people taking our things, and we don’t want people hurting us. The disagreement begins at sex.


That is where a society shows whether it intends to live by the law of God. When we get there, people say, “My body, my choice. We consented. It’s none of your business.” The same divide runs through abortion, marriage, pornography, the schools, and now the question of whether a man can become a woman.


I believe there is a reason the divide falls exactly here, and it is the key to this whole part. Most people experience God’s sexual law as a set of rules that a distant lawgiver made up, for reasons of His own, that forbid them to have what they want. If that were true, rebellion against those rules would look like liberation, and anyone who defended them would look like a jailer. That is exactly how the modern world sees the Christian sexual ethic.


But it is not true. And the woke religion’s doctrine of sex and gender is what happens when a culture builds on the assumption that it is.


## The doctrine stated fairly


The woke doctrine of sex and gender holds that:


- **Sex and gender are separate.** Sex is the body’s biology; gender is a person’s inner identity, which may or may not match the body.

- **Gender identity is authoritative.** When the body and the inner sense disagree, the inner sense is the truth about the person, and the body should be brought into line with it.

- **Affirmation is compassion.** To use a person’s chosen name and pronouns, and to support social and medical transition, is to love him. To question it is to harm him, possibly fatally.

- **Children can know.** A child who persistently identifies as the other sex should be affirmed, and, in some cases, should receive puberty blockers, cross-sex hormones, and surgery.

- **Sexual expression between consenting adults is beyond moral judgment.** Any sexual arrangement freely chosen is as good as any other, and the law must treat them as equal.


The steelman goes like this: “Gender dysphoria is real suffering. People who experience it have high rates of depression and suicide. When we affirm them, they feel seen; when we refuse, they feel erased. You may believe your religion, but you have no right to impose it on someone else’s body. What two adults do in private harms no one.”


That argument contains truth a Christian must hear. Gender dysphoria is real, and those who experience it suffer. Cruelty toward them is a sin. Many Christians have treated people who struggle with their sexuality as objects of contempt rather than as neighbors, and that failure helped this new religion win their hearts.


But the conclusion that follows from compassion is not the one the woke religion draws.


## The God who lives our flesh


In Part 1, I set out the truth at the center of the Christian faith as I understand it: God does not merely watch our lives or dwell in us as a guest. He lives our lives with us, from the inside. Every experience a man has, God has with him. *“In him we live, and move, and have our being”* (Acts 17:28).


That includes the body. Paul applied this truth directly to sex:


*“Know ye not that your bodies are the members of Christ? shall I then take the members of Christ, and make them the members of an harlot? God forbid… What? know ye not that your body is the temple of the Holy Ghost which is in you, which ye have of God, and ye are not your own? For ye are bought with a price: therefore glorify God in your body”* (1 Corinthians 6:15, 19–20).


*“Ye are not your own.”* That single sentence is the answer to the woke doctrine of the body. The body is not raw material for the self’s will. It is a gift, made by God, *“fearfully and wonderfully made”* (Psalm 139:14), and lived by God from the inside. What a man does with his body, God experiences with him.


This changes the meaning of the sexual law. God did not forbid fornication and adultery because He wanted to deny us pleasure. He designed sexual union to bond a man and a woman into one flesh (Genesis 2:24), to bring children into the world, and to bind the family that raises them. As I said in *Transforming America*, there is nothing in life that isn’t related to sex, because without sex there would be nothing to relate to: no family, no overcoming hard times together, no raising children the right way, no generation to follow.


When that union is honored, God lives the joy of it. When it is broken, by betrayal, by using another person’s body for a moment’s pleasure, by abandoning the children it produced, God lives that too. He feels the wound in the betrayed spouse, the emptiness in the one who was used, the loss in the child who grows up without a father. His holiness recoils from it. *“Against thee, thee only, have I sinned,”* David said after his adultery (Psalm 51:4). He understood whom his sin wounded first.


So the first and great commandment, to love God with all our heart, soul, mind, and strength, reaches into the body. We keep God’s sexual law not because we are victims of a God who made up arbitrary rules, but because we love Him, and our faithfulness gives joy to the One who lives it with us.


This is also why so many are turning elsewhere. People want a standard. They want to be told how to live. When the church stopped saying it plainly, some went looking for a religion that would. Islam offers a strict sexual code, and enforces it with dress, segregation, and punishment. It looks holy. But it is obedience to an external master, kept out of fear. The gospel offers something Islam cannot: a standard just as high, kept freely, out of love for a God who feels every choice we make. I will return to this in Part 5.


## Testing the doctrine


### 1. A body that is both raw material and sacred


Part 1 noted the first contradiction: gender is said to be a social construct, yet gender identity is treated as innate, fixed, and real enough to justify altering the body. Both cannot be true. If gender is constructed, then no child’s identity is fixed enough to justify irreversible treatment. If it is innate, then the claim that it is merely a social construct is false.


There is a deeper contradiction underneath. The doctrine treats the inner self as sacred, beyond question, while treating the body as raw material to be cut and chemically altered to match it. Why is the mind’s sense of itself more real than the body’s chromosomes, organs, and capacity to bear or beget children? The doctrine never says. It simply ranks the self’s will above the given body, which is what it means to make the self a god.


Christianity holds both together. The body and the soul are both made by God and lived by God. When they seem to disagree, the body is not the error to be corrected. The suffering is real and deserves care, but the cure is not to destroy the body to fit the feeling.


### 2. “Affirm or they will die”


The most powerful argument for medical transition of minors is that without it, distressed children will take their own lives. Parents are told to choose between a living son and a dead daughter.


This claim deserves to be taken seriously, and it has been. In 2024, England’s National Health Service published the Cass Review, the most thorough independent examination of pediatric gender medicine yet done. Dr. Hilary Cass found that the evidence for puberty blockers and cross-sex hormones in minors was remarkably weak, and that the claim that they reduce suicide was not supported by good evidence. England stopped routine prescription of puberty blockers to minors. Sweden and Finland had already moved toward caution. In 2025, in *United States v. Skrmetti*, the U.S. Supreme Court upheld Tennessee’s law prohibiting these treatments for minors.


When an intervention is irreversible and the evidence for its benefit is weak, the burden of proof belongs to those who intervene. A child cannot consent to the loss of his future fertility or to a lifetime of medical dependence. “Not proven harmful” is not reassurance; for an irreversible treatment given to a child, it is a warning.


### 3. Compassion that will not tell the truth


The doctrine defines compassion as affirmation: to agree with a person’s account of himself is to love him; to disagree is to harm him.


Scripture defines love differently. *“Thou shalt not hate thy brother in thine heart: thou shalt in any wise rebuke thy neighbour, and not suffer sin upon him”* (Leviticus 19:17). To flatter a neighbor in his error is not love. It is the easy way out for the one doing the flattering. A doctor who tells a patient what he wants to hear rather than what is true has failed his patient, however kindly he spoke.


Real compassion tells the truth and stays with the person while he hears it. It says: your suffering is real, God feels it with you, you are loved, and the answer is not to reject the body God gave you.


### 4. Freedom that compels


The doctrine speaks the language of freedom: my body, my choice, my identity. But it does not leave others free. Teachers are disciplined for declining to use pronouns they believe are false. Parents have been kept uninformed when their children were socially transitioned at school. Girls have been required to compete against males and to share locker rooms with them. Wedding photographers and website designers have been taken to court for declining to celebrate marriages their faith does not recognize.


A doctrine that demands everyone affirm it is not a doctrine of freedom. It is an established religion. As I wrote in *Transforming America*, when the state punishes a man for declining to take part in what he believes is sin, the state has become the enforcer of a secular religious creed. The Supreme Court recognized this in *303 Creative v. Elenis* (2023), holding that the state cannot compel a website designer to create messages celebrating a marriage she does not believe in.


### 5. Consent is not the only moral law


The doctrine holds that consent settles every sexual question. If both adults agree, no one is harmed.


But sex is never only about the two adults. It produces children, who did not consent to being raised without a father. It shapes the family, which is the foundation of the nation. It shapes the people who practice it, forming habits of commitment or of consumption. A culture that treats sex as a private transaction between consenting parties will produce exactly what ours has: fewer marriages, fewer children, more children growing up without both parents, and more loneliness.


And there is always a third party. God lives both persons. Consent between two people does not make an act good when the One who lives them both is wounded by it.


## Applying the test


**Is it righteous?** No. God made us male and female (Genesis 1:27). He designed sexual union for marriage between a man and a woman. A doctrine that treats the body as raw material for the will, and sex as a transaction beyond moral judgment, contradicts His design.


**Is it loving?** No. It sets children on irreversible paths on weak evidence. It calls flattery love and truth hatred. It compels the conscience of those who disagree.


**Would I want it done to me?** If I were a confused fourteen-year-old, I would want adults who loved me enough to protect my body until I was grown, not adults who handed me irreversible decisions. If I were a teacher, I would not want to be forced to say what I believe is false. If I were a girl, I would not want to compete against a male body. Measured by the self committed to righteousness, not by momentary desire, no one wants what this doctrine does.


## Why the concern is not overblown


A critic will say that transgender people are a tiny fraction of the population, and that conservatives have made them a target out of proportion to their numbers. I take the proportion question seriously, so here is my answer.


**The numbers have changed fast.** Gallup reports that nearly a quarter of Generation Z adults now identify as LGBTQ+, several times the rate of older generations. Referrals to gender clinics for adolescents, especially girls, rose steeply in many Western countries over the last fifteen years. Whatever the full explanation, a change that large and that fast among the young is not explained by biology alone. Culture shapes how young people understand their distress, and a culture that offers one explanation, loudly, will see more young people adopt it.


**It is paid for.** Pediatric gender medicine became an industry, with clinics, specialists, and lifelong prescriptions. When an industry’s revenue depends on a diagnosis, it is not neutral about that diagnosis.


**It reaches the foundation.** The question is not only about a small number of people. It is about what every child is taught about the body, about whether parents have authority over their own children, about whether words must mean what is true, and about whether the state may compel speech. Those questions reach every family in the country.


**It is spiritual.** Paul described a people who *“changed the truth of God into a lie, and worshipped and served the creature more than the Creator”* and who, as a consequence, were given over to dishonor *“their own bodies between themselves”* (Romans 1:24–25). The worship of the self leads to the reshaping of the body in the self’s image. That is not a political talking point. It is a spiritual pattern as old as idolatry.


## Where the argument must be careful


Gender dysphoria is real, and those who experience it are suffering. Some adults who have transitioned report that they are better off, and I do not claim to know every man’s heart. My argument is about the doctrine, about what we teach children, and about what the state may compel. It is not permission for contempt toward anyone.


Some of the data are uncertain. The causes of the rise in youth identification are debated, and the long-term outcomes of medical transition are not fully known, which is itself the problem when the treatment is irreversible and the patient is a child.


None of this weakens the thesis. God made us male and female, He lives our bodies with us, and a culture that makes the self its god will reshape the body to serve it.


## What I would do


The principles from *Transforming America* govern here: the nation should name what falls short of its standard and strengthen the family that sexuality exists to build. It must never watch, never compel, and never enforce belief. It should not send the police into anyone’s bedroom.


- **Protect children’s bodies.** No puberty blockers, cross-sex hormones, or surgery for gender transition before adulthood. The Supreme Court has confirmed in *Skrmetti* that states may enact this protection, and I would urge every state to do so.

- **Restore parents’ authority.** No school should socially transition a child without his parents’ knowledge, or teach gender identity as a doctrine. As I have argued elsewhere, school boards govern education, and voters should replace boards that do this.

- **Protect women and girls.** Women’s sports, locker rooms, shelters, and prisons should be separated by sex.

- **Protect conscience and speech.** No one should be compelled to use pronouns he believes are false, or to participate in a ceremony he believes is sin.

- **Lead by voice.** The president cannot change hearts by statute, but he can speak. In *Transforming America*, I pointed to the Surgeon General’s 1964 report on smoking: no one was jailed, but a nation told the truth with one voice, and adult smoking fell from about 42 percent to under 12 percent. The president can say plainly that marriage between a man and a woman is the foundation of the nation, that children need a mother and a father, and that sexual faithfulness is a gift to God and to the family.


## The better way


The woke religion promises freedom: be whoever you want, love whoever you want, do whatever you want with your body. It delivers a culture of lonely adults, fatherless children, and confused teenagers handed decisions no child should make.


The gospel promises something better. You are not your own; you were made, and you are loved. Your body is a gift, lived by God from the inside. Your faithfulness gives Him joy. Your sin wounds Him, and He has paid for it Himself, on the Cross. Whatever you have done with your body, and whatever confusion you have felt about it, you may come to Him, be forgiven, and be made whole.


That is the message the church must speak plainly, without contempt and without compromise. The young are looking for a standard. If we will not give them the true one, kept freely out of love, they will find a false one, either in the self-worship of the woke religion or in the forced obedience of Islam.


## Where to push


- **Is the body really given, or is it ours to shape?** I claim the body is God’s gift and God’s dwelling. Show me why the self’s inner sense should rank above it.

- **Is “God lives us” consistent with human freedom in sex?** I say God gives us freedom to choose and feels the consequences of our choice. Show me where that makes the sexual law arbitrary.

- **Can the state protect children without becoming a moral police?** I say yes: protect minors, protect conscience, and use voice rather than force with adults. Show me where that line fails.
