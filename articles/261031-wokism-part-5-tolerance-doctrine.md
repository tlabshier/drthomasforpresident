# 261031 - Wokism Part 5 - Tolerance Doctrine

# The False Gods of Wokism


## Part 5: The Empty House — The Tolerance Doctrine and the Crescent


by Thomas Lee Abshier, ND 11/1/2026


## Plain language summary


The woke religion teaches that every culture and every religion is equally valid, except Western Christianity, which it treats as uniquely guilty. A nation that believes this has no god of its own, and a house with no one living in it does not stay empty. Islam is a religion that also carries a political program: its canonical goal is a world under Islamic law. Most American Muslims are peaceful neighbors, and Muslims are only about one percent of the country. But Islam is patient, it grows fastest where a secular culture has stopped having children and stopped believing anything, and in some American cities it already governs. The left protects it as a favored minority even though Islam rejects nearly everything the left believes about sex. The deepest difference between Islam and Christianity is the one at the center of this series. The God of Islam is a master who stands outside man and commands submission. The God of the Bible lives our lives from the inside, became a man, felt our pain, paid for our sin, and calls us sons, not slaves. Islam offers a counterfeit holiness: a strict standard kept out of fear. The gospel offers the true one: the same high standard, kept freely, out of love for a God who feels every choice we make. The answer to Islam is not hatred of Muslims. It is honest law that distinguishes worship from sedition, and a church that lives the gospel well enough to offer Muslims something better. We are on a time clock, and the clock is measured in generations.


---


## The doctrine of tolerance


Every part of this series has examined a doctrine of the woke religion that replaces a truth of God with a human idol. This part examines the doctrine that holds the others together: tolerance, in its modern meaning.


Stated as fairly as I can, the tolerance doctrine holds that:


- **All cultures are equally valid.** No culture may judge another, and the belief that one’s own way is better is the root of colonialism and bigotry.

- **The West is uniquely guilty.** Western civilization, and especially Western Christianity, carries a special burden of guilt for colonialism, slavery, and war.

- **Minorities must be protected from criticism.** Criticism of a minority’s religion or culture, especially Islam, is a form of bigotry, which the left calls “Islamophobia.”

- **Immigration is a moral duty.** A wealthy nation that limits immigration is acting from fear and prejudice.


The steelman goes like this: “Muslims in America are citizens, neighbors, doctors, and shopkeepers. After September 11, many suffered threats and violence for the acts of strangers. The First Amendment protects every religion, not just yours. If you would want your faith protected in a Muslim country, you should protect theirs here.”


That argument contains truth, and a Christian must honor it. Muslims are our neighbors. Violence and contempt toward them are sins. Religious liberty is a principle we cannot claim for ourselves while denying it to others. I hold all of that.


But the tolerance doctrine goes much further than religious liberty. It forbids the nation to say that any god is true and any god is false. And a nation that cannot say which god is true has, in practice, no god at all.


## The empty house


Jesus told a parable about this: an unclean spirit leaves a man and wanders through dry places. Finding no rest, it returns and finds its former house *“empty, swept, and garnished.”* So it brings seven spirits worse than itself, and *“the last state of that man is worse than the first”* (Matthew 12:44–45).


A house swept clean of its old faith does not stay empty. That is the condition of the secular West. It has swept out the Christianity that built it, and it has decorated the rooms with tolerance, diversity, and inclusion. But tolerance is not a god. It cannot command loyalty, it cannot inspire a man to raise a family, and it cannot tell him what his life is for. A people who believe in nothing in particular will not defend anything in particular.


The house will not stay empty. The question is who moves in.


## What Islam is


In September, I examined an interview with Danny Burmawi, a Jordanian former Muslim who became a Christian and can no longer go home. His central point, which I judged correct without reservation, is that Islam is not only a religion. It is a comprehensive civilizational order that contains a religion. Its prayers, fasts, creed, and mosques are worship, and the First Amendment rightly protects them. But its program to replace the civil order with Islamic law, sharia, is a political program, and a republic may judge a man’s political allegiance even though it may not judge his worship.


Gad Saad, the evolutionary psychologist, made the same point on Joe Rogan’s program in July. Saad was born in Lebanon and fled its civil war. He was careful to say that not every Muslim, and not every Muslim leader, believes what he was describing. His subject was the canon: what the religion’s own texts and law teach. And the canon divides the world in two: the House of Islam, where Islamic law governs, and the House of War, everywhere else. Saad added that, on this reading of the law, a land once under Islamic rule belongs to Islam permanently, which is why some Islamists still speak of recovering Spain.


Saad’s evidence is history. Every one of the fifty-seven member states of the Organization of Islamic Cooperation once had no Muslims at all. Egypt was Coptic Christian. Syria was full of Christians. Lebanon, the country of Saad’s birth, had a Christian majority within his lifetime and is now majority Muslim. He noted that the change can take five years or five hundred. His point was not that America will live under sharia tomorrow morning. It was that if the pattern of a few American cities is repeated in dozens more over two or three centuries, the United States will not be the same country.


## Proportion


I have said many times that hyperbole is lying by proportion, so the size of the thing matters before we draw conclusions from it.


Muslims are about one percent of Americans. The Pew Research Center estimated 3.45 million in 2017 and projected roughly 8 million, a little over two percent, by 2050. In America, Islam gains about as many converts as it loses. On any near horizon, Muslims will not outnumber or outvote the nation as a whole.


But proportion cuts both ways. The national number is small, and the local numbers are not. In 2021, Hamtramck, Michigan, became the first American city with a city council made up entirely of Muslims. Dearborn, next door, has a Muslim mayor and a population in which Arab Americans are a majority. In 2025, New York City elected Zohran Mamdani, a Muslim and a democratic socialist, as its mayor.


The threat, stated honestly, is not an imminent national takeover. It is a long trajectory, plus local concentration now. Islam is patient. It grows where families have children, and the secular West has largely stopped having them. It gains influence where communities concentrate and vote together, and the empty house offers no resistance. Saad’s question is the right one: if you have the imagination to extrapolate over a few centuries, what kind of country will your descendants live in?


We are on a time clock. The clock is measured in generations, not in news cycles, and that is exactly why it is easy to ignore.


## The strange alliance


How does a movement of secular, sexually liberated progressives make common cause with a religion whose law punishes homosexuality, veils women, and forbids apostasy? Burmawi’s answer is a short history of the left. Marxism promised the workers a revolution. In America the workers became comfortable and declined to revolt. So the neo-Marxists substituted identity for class: race, sex, and sexual orientation became the new proletariat. Islam, being non-Western, once colonized, and opposed to the West, checks every box on the list of the oppressed.


The two movements agree on almost nothing except the enemy: the Christian West. They have, in effect, agreed to take it down first and decide later who gets the ruins. No one had to sign anything. In the fellowship we call this a de facto conspiracy: aligned interests producing aligned behavior without anyone coordinating. Paul named who stands at the head of such a column: *“For we wrestle not against flesh and blood, but against principalities, against powers, against the rulers of the darkness of this world”* (Ephesians 6:12). Two movements that both require the dethroning of the biblical God will march in the same direction.


The alliance shows its seams where Islam gains power. In 2023, Hamtramck’s all-Muslim council voted to ban the display of Pride flags on city property. The tolerance doctrine assumed that every minority would share its creed. Islam does not, and where it governs, it says so.


## The God who lives us, and the God who stands outside


Now I come to the center of this part, and of this series.


The God of Islam is one, utterly transcendent, and wholly other. Islamic theology insists that nothing is like Him, that He has no son, and that He never became a man. The proper relationship of a human being to this God is expressed in the word *Islam* itself: *submission*. The believer is called His *‘abd*, His servant or slave. God commands from outside; man obeys.


The God of the Bible is different at the root. As I have argued throughout this series, He does not merely watch our lives or command them from outside. He lives them with us, from the inside. *“In him we live, and move, and have our being”* (Acts 17:28). He feels our joys and our sorrows, our righteousness and our sin. And because He lives our lives, He did what the God of Islam is said never to do: He became one of us. The Son entered His own creation, felt our pain in His own flesh, and died to pay for the sin that His holiness could not abide. (I have set out that argument step by step in *The Premise Change* and *Reconciliation of Man with the Father*, listed below.)


Because of that, the relationship changes. *“Henceforth I call you not servants… but I have called you friends”* (John 15:15). *“Wherefore thou art no more a servant, but a son”* (Galatians 4:7). *“Ye have received the Spirit of adoption, whereby we cry, Abba, Father”* (Romans 8:15).


This is why Islam is a counterfeit holiness. In my October essay *Why Are Christians Leaving for Islam?*, I looked at why some Westerners convert. They are not fleeing holiness; they are looking for it. Islam offers a rhythm of prayer, a standard stated out loud, a role for men, a community that notices, and a faith that claims the whole of life. Every item on that list belonged to the church first, and the church let them lapse.


But the discipline rests on something different, and that is the whole difference. Islam is, as I put it in September, Old Testament religion with a sharper stick and more supervision. It offers a strict standard, enforced from outside, kept out of fear of a master. It looks holy. It comes with slavery.


The gospel offers the same high standard, kept from inside, out of love for a Father who feels every choice we make. We keep His law because our faithfulness gives Him joy and our sin wounds Him. *“Stand fast therefore in the liberty wherewith Christ hath made us free, and be not entangled again with the yoke of bondage”* (Galatians 5:1). The Christian is free, and he uses his freedom to love. Islam cannot sell that. It is sold next door.


## Testing the tolerance doctrine


### 1. Tolerance that cannot judge is indifference


Real tolerance means bearing with people you believe are wrong, and protecting their freedom to be wrong. It requires a belief that some things are true and others false. The woke doctrine abolishes that belief. It forbids anyone to say that one religion is truer than another. That is not tolerance. It is indifference, and indifference cannot defend anything, including itself.


### 2. All cultures are equal, except one


The doctrine says no culture may judge another, then judges Western Christian civilization more harshly than any other. Colonialism, slavery, and conquest are treated as uniquely Western sins, although Islam conquered from Spain to India and ran a slave trade for over a thousand years. The doctrine is not relativism. It is relativism for everyone except the West.


### 3. A program protected like a race


The word “Islamophobia” does something clever. It treats criticism of a set of ideas as if it were hatred of a group of people. Burmawi drew the distinction well: antisemitism is hatred of Jews, not criticism of Judaism. No one calls it antisemitic to criticize the Talmud. But “Islamophobia,” as its advocates use it, means criticism of Islam, not hatred of Muslims. Laws against it are blasphemy laws by another door.


And notice why the charge lands so hard. For a devout Muslim, Islam is his identity, so criticism of the idea is experienced as an attack on the self. The Christian’s identity is not his ideology. It is Christ: *“I am crucified with Christ: nevertheless I live; yet not I, but Christ liveth in me”* (Galatians 2:20). A man whose worth is secured by grace can hear his religion criticized and lose nothing. A man whose worth is secured by adherence cannot. That is one more thing the gospel offers the Muslim that his own faith cannot: ground to stand on that no criticism can take away.


### 4. The alliance with its opposite


The tolerance doctrine depends on the belief that every minority shares the progressive creed. Islam is the clearest proof that this is false. A coalition that champions sexual liberation and also shields a religion that forbids it is not held together by a shared belief. It is held together by a shared enemy.


## Applying the test


**Is it righteous?** No. The tolerance doctrine forbids a nation to say which God is true. A nation that will not name the true God will not keep His law.


**Is it loving?** No. It is not loving to Muslims, who are left in a religion of fear without ever hearing the gospel clearly, because Christians have been taught that witnessing is intolerance. It is not loving to apostates, who are abandoned. And it is not loving to our grandchildren, who will inherit whatever moves into the empty house.


**Would I want it done to me?** If I were a Muslim seeking God, I would want a Christian to tell me plainly about the Father who lives my life and calls me His son, not to stay silent out of politeness. If I were a Muslim woman in a community that enforced the veil, or a Muslim who wanted to leave his faith, I would want a nation that protected my freedom, not one that protected the program.


## Why the concern is not overblown


A critic will say that this is fearmongering about a peaceful minority. I have tried to keep the proportion honest, so here is why I still hold the concern.


**The canon says what it says.** The expansionist doctrine is not my invention or Saad’s. It is in the classical law of the religion, and the history of fifty-seven nations bears it out. That not every Muslim believes it does not change what the canon teaches.


**History shows the pattern.** Christian Egypt, Christian Syria, and within living memory Christian Lebanon became Muslim. In none of them did it happen in a single generation. In all of them, it happened.


**Demographics favor the believer.** A people who have children inherit the future. A secular culture that has stopped believing has also, largely, stopped having children. That is arithmetic, not prejudice.


**The vacuum is real.** A nation that has emptied itself of faith offers no counter-story. Islam does not have to defeat Christianity in America. It only has to wait for the church to finish abandoning it.


## Where the argument must be careful


Most American Muslims are peaceful, law-abiding citizens, and many came here for the freedom Islamic countries denied them. My argument is against a political program and a false god, not against persons. Muslims are neighbors and captives to rescue, not enemies to defeat. *“Love your enemies,”* Jesus said, and most Muslims are not even our enemies.


I must also be careful about the remedy. Government theology is a sword that turns. The state may not judge a man’s worship, and it must never become the instrument of a religious test for belief. The proper line is between worship and sedition: between the mosque and the program to replace the Constitution with sharia.


These boundaries do not weaken the thesis. Islam is a counterfeit holiness, the tolerance doctrine has left the house empty, and the church must fill it with the true one.


## What I would do


These are positions I have argued before, in the fellowship and on this site, held as advocacy with reasons and open to a better argument:


- **Distinguish worship from sedition.** Protect the prayer, the fast, the creed, and the mosque, and protect conversion in either direction. Do not protect the political program to replace the Constitution with Islamic law.

- **Enforce the laws we already have.** The naturalization oath requires an applicant to renounce foreign allegiance and support the Constitution, and federal law (8 U.S.C. §1424) already bars naturalizing anyone who advocates a totalitarian form of government in the United States. Apply these honestly. The fairest form, and the form most likely to stand in court, is a set of constitutional commitments asked of every applicant of every faith, judged by conduct and advocacy rather than by creed.

- **One law for all.** No religious law may displace the civil and constitutional rights of any American, including the rights of women and of those who leave their faith.

- **No blasphemy laws in any costume.** Oppose every law, regulation, or school mandate that treats criticism of a religion, including Islam, as a punishable offense.

- **Shelter the apostate.** A Muslim who becomes a Christian or leaves Islam may face threats from his own family and community. The nation should protect him as it would protect anyone threatened for his beliefs.

- **Lead by voice.** The president should say plainly that America was built by a Christian people on the moral law of the Bible, that every faith is free to worship here, and that no faith is free to replace the Constitution.


## What the church must do


Law can lock the door. It cannot fill an empty house. As I wrote in October, a lock on the door is worth having, but it is not worth much on a house where no one is living.


**Judgment begins at the house of God.** We in the West have abused the freedom we have in Christ. We used our liberty *“for an occasion to the flesh”* (Galatians 5:13): for pornography, for broken marriages, for consumer comfort, and for a faith that asked nothing of us. When the young man who wants a standard walks into our churches and finds none, he goes looking elsewhere. If we will not turn back to God now, freely, we may one day be forced into a submission we did not choose. It is better to turn now.


**Witness openly.** The answer to Islam is not the sword. It is the force of the gospel, taught and lived. We must tell our Muslim neighbors plainly, with love, that the God they fear as a distant master is a Father who lives their lives with them, who became a man and felt their pain, and who calls them sons. Former Muslims like Danny Burmawi are proof that Christ is still drawing Muslims out of Islam, often at great cost.


**Be the example.** Muslims will not believe that freedom in Christ produces holiness unless they see it. Our marriages, our sons, our money, and our evenings must show that something has been done to us that was not done to our neighbors. Our love for Christ must be greater than our anger at Islam. The goal is not fewer Muslims. It is more disciples of Jesus.


## Where to push


- **Is it fair to judge Islam by its canon when most Muslims do not follow it?** I say a religion’s canon governs its future, because reformers and radicals both return to it. Show me a case where the canon was permanently set aside.

- **Can the state distinguish worship from sedition without becoming a judge of religion?** I say yes, by judging conduct and advocacy, not creed. Show me where that line fails.

- **Is “God lives us” really absent from Islam?** I claim Islamic theology forbids God to become a man or to dwell in His creation as Father. If Islam teaches an indwelling God, show me where.
