# 261116 - Nazi Takeover of Germany

# How the Nazis Really Took Over Germany:


### Why the Truth Is a Better Warning Than the Meme

*By Thomas Lee Abshier, ND | 16 November 2026*

A post has been going around on social media under the title "This is how the Nazis took over Germany." Beside a swastika flag it lists ten things the Nazis supposedly did:


 	- They banned guns.

 	- They got rid of God.

 	- Banned church services.

 	- Burned Bibles and books in the streets.

 	- Tore down statues.

 	- Burned the flag.

 	- Got rid of any and all history they did not agree with.

 	- Got rid of the public police so only the rich could afford it.

 	- Put their country in a state of fear.

 	- Created domestic terrorists.


My position is this: as history, the post is mostly wrong, and in several places it gets things backward. But the alarm it is trying to sound is real. Tyranny does disarm the people it plans to persecute. It does try to capture the church. It does erase inconvenient memory. And it rules through fear and manufactured enemies. I want to keep that warning and correct the history, because a warning built on false history fails the moment someone checks it. I also think the true history carries a sharper warning than the meme does.

## The ten claims, one by one

**1. "They banned guns."** For the German public generally, this is false. For the people the Nazis meant to destroy, it is true. Germany's strict gun laws came from the Weimar Republic: a near-total ban after the First World War, followed by a permit-and-registration law in 1928. The Nazi Weapons Act of 1938 replaced the 1928 law and *loosened* it for citizens considered loyal. Party members, hunters, and officials were exempted from many requirements. Jews were treated the opposite way. Starting in 1936 the Gestapo ordered police not to issue them firearm permits. On November 11, 1938, the day after Kristallnacht, a regulation banned Jews from owning firearms, ammunition, and even knives and clubs. The Nazis did not need a new law to disarm their political opponents. The 1928 law already gave police discretion over permits, and the registration records showed where the guns were. So the honest summary is: *they armed their friends and disarmed their targets.*

**2. "They got rid of God."** False as stated, though the truth is no comfort. Point 24 of the 1920 Nazi Party platform endorsed "positive Christianity," and most German Christians welcomed Hitler's rise in 1933. The regime did not try to abolish God. It tried to replace Him with a counterfeit shaped by the state. It backed the "German Christians" movement, which sought a single nazified Reich Church and a Bible stripped of its Jewish roots. Christians who refused were persecuted. The Confessing Church declared in the Barmen Declaration that its allegiance was to Christ and Scripture, not to the Führer. Pastor Martin Niemöller spent seven years in concentration camps. Dietrich Bonhoeffer was executed. Many historians believe the Nazis intended to eradicate traditional Christianity after winning the war. This was more dangerous than open atheism, because the counterfeit kept the vocabulary of the faith.

**3. "Banned church services."** Mostly false. Catholic and Protestant churches held services throughout the Nazi years. Smaller groups were hit hard: Jehovah's Witnesses were banned in 1933, and the Salvation Army disappeared from Germany. Jewish worship was destroyed outright, as the next point shows.

**4. ****"Burned Bibles and books in the streets."** The book burnings were real. On May 10, 1933, students in university towns across Germany burned works the regime called "un-German," including those of Heine, Marx, Freud, and Einstein. Christian Bibles were not a target of those fires. But on Kristallnacht, November 9–10, 1938, the Nazis burned more than 1,400 synagogues and desecrated or set fire to Torah scrolls. The Torah is the Law of Moses, the first five books of our own Bible. So the claim is true in a way its author may not have intended.

**5. "Tore down statues."** True in kind, but aimed the other way from how the meme implies. The Nazis removed monuments to people their ideology called alien. In November 1936 a Nazi official in Leipzig had the statue of Felix Mendelssohn torn down while the mayor was out of the country. His stated reason was that Mendelssohn was a Jew. Mendelssohn had been baptized a Lutheran and was the man who revived Bach's music. The Nazis also purged "degenerate" art from museums. Meanwhile they built monuments of their own, including the Wagner monument whose foundation stone was laid in Leipzig in 1934.

**6. ****"Burned the flag."** False. The Nazis did not burn the flag. They replaced it. Soon after Hitler became chancellor, the black-red-gold flag of the democratic republic was dropped. In March 1933 the old imperial black-white-red flag was restored as co-national flag alongside the swastika banner, and in September 1935 the swastika flag became the sole national flag. The Nazis were the party that *protected* flags. A Weimar-era law against flag desecration, passed in December 1932, was extended in 1933 and 1935 to cover the swastika flag.

**7. ****"Got rid of any and all history they did not agree with."** Substantially true. School curricula were rewritten around racial history. Jewish contributions to German culture were erased: Mendelssohn's music was banned along with his statue. The past was remade to serve the party.

**8. "Got rid of the public police so only the rich could afford it."** False, and the reverse of what happened. The Nazis captured the police and expanded them. In February 1933, Hermann Göring, then Prussian Interior Minister, ordered Prussian police to cooperate with Nazi paramilitary groups. Days later the regime began deputizing SA men, SS men, and Stahlhelm veterans as auxiliary police. In Prussia alone, about 50,000 armed paramilitaries patrolled alongside regular officers, beating and arresting political opponents. Göring created the Gestapo in April 1933, and by 1936 all German police were centralized under Heinrich Himmler. Most ordinary policemen kept their jobs and served the new state. Germany ended up with more police, not fewer.

**9. ****"Put their country in a state of fear."** True. On February 27, 1933, the Reichstag building burned. The next day President Hindenburg signed the Reichstag Fire Decree, which suspended civil liberties and expanded police power. It was supposed to be an emergency measure, but it stayed in force until the regime fell in 1945.

**10. ****"Created domestic terrorists."** Substantially true. The Nazis presented the Reichstag fire as the opening shot of a communist revolution and used it to round up Communists and Social Democrats. A young Dutch communist, Marinus van der Lubbe, was convicted of setting the fire. Historians still debate whether he acted alone or whether Nazi agents were involved. Either way, the regime created a terrorist enemy in the public mind and used it to justify everything that followed.

**The tally:** three claims are substantially true (fear, manufactured enemies, rewritten history). Three are half-truths that the meme bends out of shape (guns, books, statues). Four are false or backward (God, church services, the flag, the police).

## How they actually took power

The post's title promises to explain how the Nazis took over Germany, but it skips the actual mechanism. The record is plain:


 	- **January 30, 1933:** President Hindenburg appointed Hitler chancellor through a backroom deal among conservative elites. Hitler did not hold a majority, and the elites expected to control him.

 	- **February 1933:** Göring Nazified the Prussian police, and party paramilitaries were deputized.

 	- **February 27–28:** The Reichstag burned, and the emergency decree suspended civil rights.

 	- **March 5:** In an election held under intimidation, the Nazis won about 44 percent.

 	- **March 23:** The Reichstag passed the Enabling Act, which let Hitler's cabinet make law without parliament. It passed with Communist deputies arrested and Social Democrats harassed, and the Nazis' coalition partners and the Catholic Center Party voted for it.

 	- **May 2:** Trade unions were dissolved and their leaders arrested.

 	- **July 1933:** The Nazis were the only legal party. That same month the regime signed a Concordat with the Vatican promising to respect the Church's autonomy, a promise it soon broke.


Hitler came to power through the law, then used an emergency to suspend the law, and then had a legislature vote its own power away. Many respectable people helped him because they believed he would restore order, faith, and national pride. He came wrapped in the old flag and in the language of "positive Christianity." He pledged law and order and then deputized his street fighters as police. That is the real warning in this history: tyranny can arrive looking like patriotism and piety.

## The question under the question

I understand why people share this post. It is not really about 1933. It reads Germany through America's recent fights: statues pulled down in 2020, flags burned at protests, calls to "defund the police," pressure on gun owners, hostility toward faith in public life. The reader senses that these things are signs of something dangerous, and he reaches for the worst example in modern history to say so.

Part of that instinct is sound. Disarming the disfavored, capturing or silencing the church, erasing memory, and governing through fear are real marks of tyranny, and they are worth watching for. But those marks do not belong to one party. In Germany the tyrants carried the flag, used Christian language, and wore the police badge. If we teach ourselves that tyranny always looks like our opponents, we will not recognize it when it looks like us.

## Why the concern remains plausible

I am not dismissing the worry behind the post. The strongest form of it rests on solid ground:


 	- **Registration and discretion are tools of disarmament.** Attorney Stephen Halbrook's research, collected in *Gun Control in the Third Reich*, documents how the Nazis used the 1928 registration records and the police's power to grant or deny permits to identify and disarm political opponents and Jews, years before the 1938 ban. The Gestapo also shut down independent gun clubs. Mainstream historians and fact-checkers reply that Jews were under one percent of the population and could not have stopped the Nazi state even if armed. They point to the Warsaw Ghetto Uprising of 1943, which was heroic but ended in massacre. Both sides grant the central fact: a government that keeps a list of gun owners and decides who counts as "reliable" can disarm whomever it chooses without passing a new law.

 	- **Capturing the church is a pattern, not an accident.** The Nazis did not need to ban Christianity. They needed a church that blessed the state. Most German Protestants and Catholics went along, and the Confessing Church was a minority. A faith that will not tell the government *no* has already been captured.

 	- **Emergency powers do not expire on their own.** The Reichstag Fire Decree was presented as temporary and lasted twelve years. Any free people should be suspicious when an attack is used to justify a permanent expansion of state power.


These are the lessons worth carrying from Germany. They point to registration lists, compliant churches, and emergency decrees, which matter far more than torn-down statues or burned flags.

## The moral point

I hold that political speech must pass the same test as any other act: is it righteous, is it loving, and would I want it done to me? A meme that gets history backward fails the first test before it reaches the others. *"Thou shalt not bear false witness against thy neighbour"* **(Exodus 20:16)**. That commandment covers our opponents too. False history against the other side is still false witness, even if the other side has done real wrong.

It also defeats its own purpose. *"He that is first in his own cause seemeth just; but his neighbour cometh and searcheth him"* **(Proverbs 18:17)**. The first person who checks the post against the record will dismiss its true points along with its false ones, and the real warning about disarmament, church capture, and rule by fear gets thrown out with the rest.

So before sharing a post like this, I ask: Is it true? Can I show where it comes from? Would I want my own side's history told this carelessly? If we want our neighbors to take the danger of tyranny seriously, we have to show them the danger as it actually happened. The true history is the stronger warning. I hold these conclusions open to a better argument and better evidence, and I would welcome both.

## Sources and their limits

Most of my sourcing comes from the mainstream historical record: the United States Holocaust Memorial Museum, Britannica, and standard reference histories. On the gun question I read both sides: Bernard Harcourt's statutory comparison of the 1928 and 1938 laws (*Fordham Law Review*), PolitiFact's fact-checks, and Stephen Halbrook's work for the Independent Institute, which presents the strongest case that Nazi gun control was a tool of repression. Harcourt and Halbrook largely agree on what the statutes said. They disagree about how much it mattered. I have not read the German statutes in the original myself, so I am relying on their translations.

Three questions remain open. Who set the Reichstag fire is still disputed. Historians disagree about how far the regime meant to go against the churches after the war. And the counterfactual of an armed Jewish population cannot be tested. The mainstream record could understate the role of gun registration in the early repression of political opponents. Halbrook's archival work suggests it has, and that is a reason to treat his findings as leads worth following. Readers who want to judge for themselves should start with the sources below.
