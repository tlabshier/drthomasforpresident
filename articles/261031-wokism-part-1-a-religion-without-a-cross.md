# 261031 - Wokism Part 1 - A Religion Without a Cross

# The False Gods of Wokism


## Part 1: A Religion Without a Cross


by Thomas Lee Abshier, ND 10/31/2026


## Plain language summary


Wokism is a religion. It has a doctrine of sin, rituals of confession, heretics, excommunication, and a vision of the end of history. It is not a new religion so much as a Christian heresy: it took real Christian concerns, such as care for the oppressed, the stranger, and the poor, and cut them off from God. Without God, it has no one to forgive, so it has a doctrine of sin with no path to redemption. It blames people for the groups they were born into, so some carry guilt they can never repent of, while others are excused from repenting of the wrongs they actually did. The Christian alternative begins with a truth most Christians have never heard stated plainly: God does not merely watch our lives; He lives them with us, from the inside, and feels every choice we make. His laws are not arbitrary rules. They describe what gives Him joy and what wounds Him, and we keep them because we love Him. When a nation worships false gods, it suffers. This series examines the god that much of America now worships, measures it against the true God, and offers the better way.


---


## Why this series


My friend Rick Kirchner recently posted a reply on Facebook to a glossary published by Rick Steves, the travel writer, in which Steves defined the vocabulary of American conservatives in a way that left conservatives only one role to play: fascists in waiting. Rick Kirchner returned the favor, term by term. Then he did something more useful. He wrote a second glossary describing the vocabulary of the left itself: institutional capture, orthodoxy, the schoolhouse, the gender takeover, the border, the justice system, and, at the center of it all, “the woke religion.”


I want to take that last entry seriously, because I think it is the key to all the others. Rick named it in a sentence. This series will examine it at length.


My thesis is this: **Wokism is a religion, and its god is a false god. When a people worship a false god, the nation suffers the consequences.** That is not a figure of speech. It is the pattern of the entire Old Testament, and it is the pattern of history. A nation is held together by what it worships, and the character of its god becomes the character of its laws, its schools, its courts, and its families.


I hold this position with conviction, and I hold it open to a better argument. I will state the strongest case the other side would make, and then my answer to it. At the end of this part, I will name where a critic should push if he thinks I am wrong.


## What I mean by “Wokism”


No real person is a pure type. When I describe “the Leftist,” as I did in my 2025 essay *The False Religion of Leftism*, I am describing a coherent set of beliefs, not a census of Democrats. Many people hold some of these beliefs loosely, and many vote for the left for other reasons entirely: a union, a family tradition, a single issue. This series is about the creed, not the voter.


The creed, stated as fairly as I can state it, holds that:


- **Society is a structure of oppressor and oppressed groups.** Power is the master key that explains history, institutions, and relationships. Race, sex, sexual identity, and other categories determine where a person stands in that structure.

- **A person is primarily a member of his group.** His moral standing, his knowledge, and his responsibilities follow from his position in the hierarchy of privilege.

- **Justice means equity.** Equal outcomes between groups, not equal treatment of persons, are the measure of a just society. Where group outcomes differ, discrimination is presumed to be the cause.

- **Knowledge is relative to standpoint.** The “lived experience” of the oppressed carries an authority that argument and evidence from outside the group cannot overrule.

- **Self-defined identity, especially sexual identity, is sacred.** To question it is to do violence to the person.

- **Language constructs reality,** so speech must be governed. Words that wound the oppressed are a form of harm, and the community must police them.


These ideas descend from Marxist critical theory, by way of critical legal studies, critical race theory, intersectionality, and queer theory. In a single generation, they moved from graduate seminars into human resources departments, medical schools, corporate training, and kindergarten curricula.


## Where the gold came from


An idol is made of real gold. That is why people bow to it.


The gold in this idol is Christian. Concern for the oppressed is not a Marxist invention. *“Thou shalt neither vex a stranger, nor oppress him: for ye were strangers in the land of Egypt”* (Exodus 22:21). *“Learn to do well; seek judgment, relieve the oppressed, judge the fatherless, plead for the widow”* (Isaiah 1:17). America’s history contains real sins: slavery, the betrayal of treaties, Jim Crow. A Christian who denies these things has not read the record honestly, and a Christian who feels nothing about them has not read his Bible.


The strongest case a sincere woke believer would make is this: “Christians talked about loving their neighbor for centuries while they owned their neighbors, segregated them, and excluded them. We are doing what the church failed to do. You call it a religion because you do not like being judged by your own standards.”


That charge contains enough truth to sting, and the church should feel the sting. Judgment begins at the house of God (1 Peter 4:17). Where the church was silent about injustice, it gave this new religion room to grow.


But here is the answer. The woke believer has kept the Christian moral intuition while throwing away the God who gave it. That is the definition of a heresy: a real truth torn out of the whole it belongs to, and made into a whole of its own. Concern for the oppressed, cut off from God, does not stay what it was. It becomes something else, and the rest of this essay shows what.


## The foundation: God lives us


Before I can show what Wokism lost, I have to state what the Christian faith actually offers. I believe there is a truth at its center that most Christians have never heard stated plainly.


**God does not merely watch our lives, or dwell in us as a guest. He lives our lives with us, from the inside.** Every experience a man has, God has with him. *“In him we live, and move, and have our being”* (Acts 17:28). *“By him all things consist”* (Colossians 1:17). Nothing exists outside God, so every act of every person, good and evil, happens within His being, and He feels it.


This is not the “all is one” of Eastern religion. In that view, good and evil dissolve into an indifferent whole. The God of the Bible does the opposite. He is light, and in Him is no darkness at all (1 John 1:5). His holiness recoils from every evil that arises within His experience, walls it off, and judges it. Scripture shows us a God who feels: He was *“grieved at his heart”* by the corruption before the Flood (Genesis 6:6). *“In all their affliction he was afflicted”* (Isaiah 63:9). We are warned to *“grieve not the holy Spirit of God”* (Ephesians 4:30). When Saul persecuted the church, Jesus asked him, *“why persecutest thou me?”* (Acts 9:4). After his sin with Bathsheba, David said to God, *“Against thee, thee only, have I sinned”* (Psalm 51:4). He had wronged Uriah and Bathsheba, but he understood whom the sin wounded first.


This changes the meaning of God’s law. If God were a distant lawgiver who made up rules for His own reasons, then His commandments, especially the ones that restrain our appetites, would be arbitrary, and we would be their victims. Rebellion against such a God would look like liberation. That is exactly how the modern world sees the Christian sexual ethic, and that is why sexuality has become the line that divides the country.


But God’s law is not arbitrary. It describes what gives Him joy and what wounds Him, because He lives what we do. The first and great commandment is to love the Lord thy God with all thy heart, soul, mind, and strength. We keep His commandments because we love Him, and because our righteous choices give joy to the One who lives them with us. Sin is not breaking a rule. It is wounding the One who loves us most, from the inside.


This is also why the Cross was necessary. I have set out that argument step by step elsewhere (see *The Premise Change* handout and *Reconciliation of Man with the Father*, listed below). In brief: God’s love requires free creatures who can genuinely refuse Him. His holiness cannot tolerate the evil they choose. Wrongdoing does not fade on its own, and no creature can pay for its own sin. So the remedy had to come from inside creation, from the Creator Himself, innocent and yet able to die. The Cross is not an arbitrary transaction. It is the only bridge between a holiness that cannot abide darkness and a love that requires free creatures who can choose it.


Hold that picture in mind. It is the standard by which I will measure the woke religion in every part of this series.


## The anatomy of a religion


Rick Kirchner mapped the parallels well, and others have mapped them before him. John McWhorter, a linguist at Columbia and no conservative, argued in *Woke Racism* (2021) that the antiracism of writers like Ibram X. Kendi and Robin DiAngelo functions as a religion, and he called its adherents “the Elect.” Gad Saad, in *The Parasitic Mind* (2020), described these beliefs as “idea pathogens” that disable a culture’s capacity to reason.


The structure is plain once you see it:


- **Original sin:** privilege, inherited by birth into a group.

- **Confession:** acknowledging one’s privilege, in public, in the approved words.

- **Clergy:** diversity officers and trainers, who interpret the doctrine for institutions.

- **Heresy:** dissent, labeled as harm, “violence,” or a “dog whistle.”

- **Excommunication:** cancellation, the loss of job, platform, and reputation.

- **Sacraments and liturgy:** land acknowledgments, pronoun declarations, required trainings.

- **Eschatology:** “the right side of history,” the promised end in which the oppressor class is dethroned and equity reigns.


None of this would matter if it were simply a private faith, freely chosen. It matters because it is administered by the institutions that govern public life, and because it is not neutral. It is a god with a character, and the laws it produces reflect that character.


## Testing the creed: does it hold together?


A true religion must be coherent, because its God is one. Truth does not contradict itself. Here the woke religion fails, and its failures are not at the edges. They are in its foundation.


### 1. A doctrine of sin with no redemption


This is the deepest failure, and it follows directly from the absence of God.


The woke religion has rediscovered something true: wrongdoing does not simply fade. Slavery and segregation left consequences that persist. In the argument for the Cross, this is the premise that moral disorder is conserved; later good cannot cancel earlier wrong. On that point, the woke and the Christian agree.


But the Christian story continues: since no creature can pay for its own sin, God Himself entered creation and paid it. The woke story has no next step. It has the debt, and no one to pay it. So the debt is passed down, by group, from generation to generation, and it can never be discharged. Guilt attaches to people who were born into a category, and no confession, training, or reparation ever settles the account. The work is never finished.


That is why the woke religion is so joyless and so punitive. A fifteen-year-old social media post can end a career, and no apology suffices. Forgiveness requires someone who was wronged and who is willing to absorb the cost. In the Christian faith, that Someone is God, who lives every injury from the inside and bore it on the Cross. Take God away, and there is no one left to forgive.


### 2. Guilt moved from deeds to categories


Because the woke religion assigns guilt by group rather than by act, it breaks the link between sin and repentance in both directions.


Some people carry guilt they cannot repent of, because they never chose it. A man cannot repent of being born white, or male, or into a prosperous family. He can only confess it, again and again.


Others are excused from repentance for sins they did choose. When a member of an “oppressed” group commits a wrong, the creed explains it by the system, and personal repentance is crowded out. Even the sins Scripture locates in the individual heart, envy, wrath, and slander, can become virtues when aimed at the approved targets.


Scripture rejects both errors in a single verse: *“The son shall not bear the iniquity of the father, neither shall the father bear the iniquity of the son: the righteousness of the righteous shall be upon him, and the wickedness of the wicked shall be upon him”* (Ezekiel 18:20). Guilt belongs to the one who did the deed. Every man is called to repent of what he has done, and every man who repents can be forgiven. That restores both justice and mercy, which the woke religion has split apart.


In light of the foundation above, the loss is even greater. Repentance is not only a legal settlement. It is turning away from wounding God. A creed that excuses some people from repentance excuses them from caring what their acts do to the One who lives them.


### 3. Lived experience, and the One who lives every life


The woke religion holds that the lived experience of the oppressed carries a special authority. Taken in its strongest form, it is right about something: no man fully understands another’s suffering from the outside.


But follow that premise to its end. If knowledge depends on standpoint, then no one sees the whole, and every group’s truth is sealed off from every other’s. The result is not justice. It is tribal warfare, in which each group’s experience is sovereign and no judge can stand over them.


The Christian answer is that there is One who does see from inside every life. God lives the experience of the slave and of the master, of the victim and of the offender, of the oppressed and of the oppressor. He alone judges from every standpoint at once, which is why He alone can judge justly. The woke religion has correctly sensed that judgment requires knowing a life from the inside. It has simply forgotten the only One who does.


### 4. The contradictions at the center


The creed contains contradictions that its adherents must not examine:


- **Gender is said to be a social construct, yet gender identity is treated as innate,** fixed and real enough to justify medical intervention on children. Both cannot be true.

- **All knowledge is said to depend on one’s standpoint, yet the creed’s own moral claims are presented as universal** and beyond question. Relativism is applied to everyone’s beliefs except its own.

- **Discrimination is opposed by discrimination.** Ibram X. Kendi wrote in *How to Be an Antiracist* (2019) that *“the only remedy to past discrimination is present discrimination.”* That is a candid statement of the creed, and it answers injustice with more of the same.

- **Tolerance is enforced by exclusion.** As I wrote in 2025, the Leftist seeks to impose tolerance in the name of tolerance, and persecutes Christians for violating his most sacred principle.


A God whose nature is one does not contradict Himself. A god assembled from human desires will contradict himself wherever two desires collide.


### 5. The individual disappears


The final failure is the loss of the person. When a man is first a member of a category, he becomes a representative of his group rather than a soul. His words are weighed by his skin and his sex, not by their truth. His guilt or innocence is assigned before he has done anything at all.


The Christian doctrine is the direct alternative: each person is made in the image of God, known by Him from the inside, judged as an individual, and redeemed as an individual. *“There is neither Jew nor Greek, there is neither bond nor free, there is neither male nor female: for ye are all one in Christ Jesus”* (Galatians 3:28). That verse does not deny our differences. It denies that they determine our standing before God.


## Applying the test


The question I ask of every policy is ordered: (1) Is it righteous? (2) If so, is it loving? (3) Would I want it done to me, measured not by momentary appetite but by the self committed to righteousness?


Apply it to the core doctrine of group guilt.


**Is it righteous?** No. God judges deeds, not ancestry (Ezekiel 18:20). A doctrine that assigns guilt by birth contradicts the character of the Judge.


**Is it loving?** No. It teaches children to see their classmates as oppressors or victims before they see them as persons, and it offers no forgiveness to anyone.


**Would I want it done to me?** No one, of any group, would want to be judged by the color of his skin. That is the dream Martin Luther King Jr. preached, and the woke religion has inverted it.


## Why the concern is not overblown


A critic will say that this is a culture-war panic over a few campus slogans. I disagree, for three reasons.


**It is institutional, not marginal.** These ideas are not confined to activists. They have been written into corporate training, university hiring through diversity statements, medical school curricula, and K–12 instruction. A creed taught to children for most of their waking hours does not stay in the seminar room.


**It is paid for.** A large industry of consultants, trainers, and administrators earns its living from the doctrine. When an institution’s budget depends on a belief, it is not neutral about that belief. That does not make every claim false, but it means the burden of proof belongs to those who profit from the intervention.


**It acts on the spirit, not only the mind.** Paul described this pattern twenty centuries ago: people who *“changed the truth of God into a lie, and worshipped and served the creature more than the Creator”* (Romans 1:25). A people who make the self and its desires sacred will build laws in the image of that god. A people who teach their children that their neighbors are enemies by birth will reap division. That is not a statistical prediction. It is how spiritual causes work.


## Where the argument must be careful


Honesty requires marking the limits of what I have claimed. Not everyone who uses this vocabulary is a devotee; many repeat it because their employer requires it, or because they have never heard another account. Racial injustice in America’s past is real, and some disparities today have causes worth examining case by case. My charge is against the god, not against the people who have been drawn to it. Most of them are seeking justice, and the church failed to show them where justice is found.


None of these qualifications weakens the thesis. The creed is a religion, it is incoherent, and it cannot forgive. Its sincere followers deserve better than an idol.


## The remedy


The answer to a false religion is not silence and not hatred. It is the true one, lived well.


That begins with the church. If Christians had lived the gospel of a God who feels every injustice, the woke religion would have had nothing to steal. Where we were silent, we must repent. Where we have abused the freedom we have in Christ, we must turn back.


Then we must say plainly what the woke religion cannot: God lives every life. He feels the pain of the oppressed, and He feels the sin of the oppressor. He has paid for that sin Himself. Every person, of every group, may come to Him, repent of what he has actually done, be forgiven, and be made new. That is the only justice that ends in reconciliation rather than revenge.


The parts that follow apply this test to each article of the woke creed: race, sex and gender, institutions and speech, the doctrine of tolerance and the rise of Islam, law and the border, the schools, and finally the better gospel that a righteous nation can offer in its place.


## Where to push


If you think I am wrong, these are the places to press:


- **Is Wokism really a religion, or only a political movement that borrows religious language?** What would distinguish the two?

- **Can a secular framework provide real forgiveness** without a God who absorbs the cost? If so, what does it look like, and why has it not appeared?

- **Is “God lives us” Biblical, or a borrowing from Eastern thought?** I claim it is Scripture’s plain teaching, kept distinct from monism by God’s holy recoil from evil. Show me where that fails.

- **Can group disparities be addressed justly without group guilt?** I say yes, by judging deeds and helping persons. Show me a case where that fails.
