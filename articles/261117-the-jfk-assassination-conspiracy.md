# 261117 - The JFK Assassination Conspiracy

# The Kennedy Assassination: The Truth Deserves the Truth


*By Thomas Lee Abshier, ND | 17 November 2026*


A friend recently shared a Facebook post that is circulating across many feeds. It announces that "the truth is finally out." According to the post, declassified documents released on January 30, 2025, prove that John F. Kennedy was killed in a military-intelligence coup. It says the files reveal a CIA death squad called "Operation Execute," a Federal Reserve "hit memo," and an audio tape in which Kennedy says, "If I fall, let history know I tried to warn them." It says Kennedy had signed "the Tonkin Act" to pull our troops out of Vietnam, that Lyndon Johnson rescinded it the day after Dallas, and that Lady Bird Johnson owned the company that made the munitions for the war.


I have never believed the lone-gunman account was the whole story, and I still don't. I stand on the side of those who believe the American people were not told the truth about Dallas, and that the Central Intelligence Agency, at a minimum, hid what it knew. The record supports that position more strongly today than at any time since 1963.


That is exactly why I am writing this. Much of what the post says is not true, and some of it appears to have been invented. A true cause carried by false evidence does not stay a true cause for long in the public mind. The first person who checks one claim and finds it fabricated will throw out the rest, including the parts that are real. If we care about the truth of the Kennedy assassination, we owe it the truth.


## Why this matters morally


The Ninth Commandment is not a rule about courtrooms only: *"Thou shalt not bear false witness against thy neighbour."* **(Exodus 20:16)** When we pass along an accusation without checking it, we become witnesses to it. If the accusation is false, we have borne false witness, even if we did not invent it. If it is true but carried by fabricated evidence, we have made it easier for the guilty to escape, because the defenders of the official story can point to the fabrication and dismiss everything else.


Scripture also warns against judging before hearing the whole matter: *"He that answereth a matter before he heareth it, it is folly and shame unto him."* **(Proverbs 18:13)** And it tells us how to test any case: *"He that is first in his own cause seemeth just; but his neighbour cometh and searcheth him."* **(Proverbs 18:17)** The post is the first to plead its cause. Here is what happens when we search it.


## What the post gets wrong


**The release date.** No JFK files were released on January 30, 2025. President Trump signed Executive Order 14176, ordering full release, on January 23, 2025. The National Archives released the files on March 18, 2025: about 2,200 records, roughly 80,000 pages. Most of those pages had been public before, with redactions; what was new was the removal of the redactions. Later in 2025, a House Oversight task force pried loose additional CIA records, which I discuss below.


**"Operation Execute," the Federal Reserve "hit memo," and the Kennedy tape.** I can find no trace of any of these in the National Archives collection. The earliest versions I can find are Substack posts that describe them in nearly the same words as the Facebook post, including a Fed memo labeled "Directive Alpha." None of those posts cite a record number, which every document in the JFK collection has. The fact-checking staff at *The Dispatch* found no evidence the Fed memo existed. I cannot prove a negative, but the burden of proof is on whoever claims a document exists, and no one has produced one. As for the quotation, I have found no recording, transcript, or archive reference for it anywhere except the posts that repeat it. Until someone produces the tape, I treat the quotation as invented.


**"The Tonkin Act."** Kennedy never signed a Tonkin act of any kind. The Gulf of Tonkin Resolution passed Congress in August 1964, nine months after Kennedy's death, and it did the opposite of what the post claims: it gave President Johnson broad authority to escalate the war. The primary record on this is not in dispute.


However, a real document underlies the confusion. On October 11, 1963, Kennedy approved National Security Action Memorandum 263, which ordered the withdrawal of about 1,000 of the roughly 17,000 American military advisers in Vietnam by the end of 1963, as part of a plan to bring most of them home by the end of 1965. Four days after Dallas, on November 26, Johnson signed NSAM 273. Its text says the withdrawal objectives "remain as stated" in Kennedy's October announcement, yet within months the policy was moving the other way. Historians still argue over whether Kennedy truly meant to leave Vietnam (Fredrik Logevall and Lawrence Freedman are skeptical; James Galbraith, John Newman, and the late Fletcher Prouty argue he did) and over whether NSAM 273 quietly reversed his course. That is a legitimate, documented question. The "Tonkin Act" story replaces it with a fiction.


**Executive Order 11110 and "sound money."** Kennedy signed EO 11110 on June 4, 1963. It delegated to the Secretary of the Treasury the authority to manage silver certificates, and its purpose was to make it easier to retire them so that Federal Reserve notes could replace them, under a law Kennedy himself had signed that same day. It did not challenge the Federal Reserve. Before the 1960 election, Kennedy said publicly that he did not advocate any change in the Federal Reserve System. The order's text is public, and it contradicts the claim.


**Lady Bird Johnson and Brown & Root.** Lady Bird's fortune was in broadcasting: she owned the Austin radio and television station KTBC. Brown & Root belonged to the brothers Herman and George Brown, and it was Halliburton that bought it, in 1962. The connection to Johnson was nonetheless real and close. The Browns were among Lyndon Johnson's most important financial backers from his first congressional race in 1937, and Johnson helped them win federal contracts. In August 1965, Brown & Root joined the RMK-BRJ consortium, which built most of the bases, ports, and airfields the United States used in South Vietnam. Brown & Root built infrastructure; it did not make munitions. The truth here is a story of political patronage and war profits, which is serious enough without inventing ownership by the First Lady.


## What the record actually shows


Here the post understates the case. The documented record is more troubling than most Americans know.


**The CIA lied to every official investigation about a man it called "Howard."** In 1963, a CIA psychological-warfare officer in Miami named George Joannides ran and financed an anti-Castro Cuban student group, the DRE. That group had direct contact with Lee Harvey Oswald in New Orleans in the summer of 1963, three months before the assassination. The DRE's members knew their CIA case officer as "Howard." The CIA told the Warren Commission in 1964 and the House Select Committee on Assassinations in 1978 that no such officer existed. Then, in 1978, the agency brought Joannides out of retirement and made him its liaison to that same House committee, without telling the committee who he was. In 1981 the CIA gave him a career commendation medal, citing in part his work as liaison to the committee. All of this was confirmed by CIA records released in July 2025 after a House Oversight task force pressed the agency. Dan Hardway, a former committee investigator, testified in May 2025 that Joannides was running an operation to undermine the congressional probe. That is not a theory. It is the agency's own paper trail.


**The CIA was watching Oswald earlier and more closely than it admitted.** The March 2025 release added technical detail about the agency's surveillance of Oswald in Mexico City, where he visited the Soviet and Cuban embassies weeks before the assassination. The officer then in charge of covert action in Mexico City was David Atlee Phillips, whose name the post mentions. Cuban exile leader Antonio Veciana later said he saw Oswald meeting with his CIA handler, "Maurice Bishop," whom many researchers believe was Phillips. Phillips denied it, and Veciana's account changed over the years. I treat it as a lead, not a proof.


**A congressional committee concluded there was probably a conspiracy.** In 1979, the House Select Committee on Assassinations concluded that Kennedy "was probably assassinated as a result of a conspiracy," though it could not identify the other gunman or the extent of the plot. That conclusion rested mainly on acoustic analysis of a police radio recording, which a National Academy of Sciences panel rejected in 1982. Researchers still dispute the acoustics on both sides. But the fact that Congress's own investigation broke with the Warren Commission remains a matter of record.


**The names in the post have real, if limited, documentary trails.**


 	- *E. Howard Hunt.* Before his death in 2007, the former CIA officer and Watergate burglar told his son, Saint John Hunt, a story naming Lyndon Johnson at the top of a plot and implicating CIA officers Cord Meyer, David Atlee Phillips, William Harvey, and others. *Rolling Stone* published the account in 2007. Hunt had a long record of deception, and he never repeated the claim under oath. It is testimony from a participant in the intelligence world, and it should be weighed as such: a lead to be followed, not a verdict.

 	- *George H. W. Bush.* A real FBI memo, written by J. Edgar Hoover on November 29, 1963, says the Bureau briefed "Mr. George Bush of the Central Intelligence Agency" about the reaction of Cuban exiles in Miami. It came to light in *The Nation* in 1988. Bush denied it was he, and the CIA said the reference was to a different man, George William Bush, a junior watch officer. The CIA's explanation is the testimony of an interested party, and the memo is genuine. But nothing in it links Bush to the assassination itself; it concerns a briefing after the fact.

 	- *Billie Sol Estes and the "Mac Wallace" tapes.* Recently, a grandson of the convicted Texas swindler Billie Sol Estes released recordings in which Estes and Johnson aide Cliff Carter allegedly discuss Johnson hiring Malcolm "Mac" Wallace to kill Kennedy. Glenn Beck's team had an expert examine the recordings and found them convincing. No independent laboratory or archive has authenticated them, and Estes had every reason to trade in secrets. They are firsthand claims, and I treat them as leads.


## The question under the question


Behind the Facebook post is a deeper question than "who fired the shots." It is this: **Do the permanent agencies of the federal government answer to the people's elected representatives, or do they protect themselves at the expense of the truth?**


On that question, the record gives a clear answer for at least one agency in at least one case. The CIA deceived the Warren Commission, deceived the House Select Committee, assigned a man with a hidden connection to the case to manage that committee's access to its records, rewarded him, and fought for decades to keep the evidence secret. Whatever happened in Dealey Plaza, an agency of the United States government obstructed the investigation of a president's murder. That is not paranoia. It is the documentary record, and it is reason enough for every citizen to demand full disclosure of what remains withheld.


## Why the suspicion of conspiracy remains plausible


I want to be honest about why so many Americans, myself among them, have never accepted the official account:


 	- **The intuitive sense that the story does not fit.** A drifting ex-Marine who defected to the Soviet Union, returned with a Russian wife, mingled with both pro-Castro and anti-Castro circles, and was watched by the CIA in Mexico City, then killed the president and was himself killed two days later in a police basement before he could stand trial. Each piece can be explained. Together, they strain belief.

 	- **Motive and money.** Kennedy had fired CIA Director Allen Dulles after the Bay of Pigs. He was signaling a reduced commitment in Vietnam. A war that eventually cost hundreds of billions of dollars made fortunes for contractors, including Johnson's closest backers. Motive is not proof, but a detective who ignores motive is not doing his job.

 	- **The pattern of concealment.** Innocent institutions do not usually lie to Congress for sixty years. The CIA's behavior around Joannides, Mexico City, and the withheld records is the strongest reason to suspect the agency had something to hide, whether that was involvement, foreknowledge, or embarrassment at having been close to Oswald and failing to stop him.

 	- **The link between the system and the behavior.** An intelligence agency that operates in secret, funds itself partly outside public view, and is accountable to almost no one will tend, over time, to protect itself first. That is not a slander against the many honorable people who have served in it. It is a statement about what unaccountable power does to institutions run by fallen men.


## On the comparison with President Trump


The post closes by comparing Kennedy with President Trump: two men, it says, targeted by the same "Deep State." Whether that comparison holds must be judged on its own evidence: the documented conduct of agencies and officials toward President Trump, which deserves its own careful examination. What I will say is that borrowing invented evidence from the Kennedy case to make that comparison weakens it. If the comparison is true, it does not need fabricated tapes and memos, and if we use them, we hand every opponent of the argument an easy reason to dismiss it.


## What I ask of my fellow citizens


First, before you share a post like this one, check one claim. Choose the most specific one, a date, a document, a quotation, and look for its source. If it has none, say so.


Second, don't let debunking false claims persuade you that the whole question is closed. It is not. The Joannides record alone justifies a renewed demand that every remaining JFK record, including the withheld IRS files and any CIA material still held back, be released in full.


Third, hold to the truth even when a falsehood would serve your side. *"Lying lips are abomination to the LORD: but they that deal truly are his delight."* **(Proverbs 12:22)** The case for accountability in our intelligence agencies is strong. It is strongest when it rests on evidence alone.
