## Where We Stand


Eight months ago, on 28 February 2026, the United States and Israel began an air war against Iran that killed its Supreme Leader and much of its military command. Iran answered by striking American bases across the Gulf and choking the Strait of Hormuz, and for a time tried to charge a toll for passage through it. We have since seen a failed ceasefire, a failed preliminary agreement, and a naval blockade that remains in place. More than fifteen American service members are dead and some eight hundred wounded. Thousands of Iranians are dead. Oil sits above $100 a barrel; diesel has passed $6 a gallon.


The blockade has done one thing well. Before the war, China bought between 80 and 90 percent of Iran’s oil, and that oil was the regime’s chief source of cash. By early summer, tanker trackers reported that Iranian crude had all but stopped leaving the Gulf. The mullahs’ main revenue has been cut off—for now.


On 3 November, the voters handed Congress to the Democrats—the House and the Senate as well. Officials in Tehran read the result as Americans rejecting the war. On the eve of the vote, American forces struck Iranian positions near the Strait after the Revolutionary Guard fired on a U.S. aircraft. Qatar and Pakistan are carrying a new interim proposal; Iran says Hormuz reopens only on its own conditions. The President has threatened major strikes after the election, and Israel’s prime minister is urging him on.


The morning after the vote, the Vice President wrote on X that the war was the reason his party lost, and challenged anyone who thought the war was worth the Senate to make that argument. Asked about the post, the President agreed that the war had pushed up the price of gasoline and cost his party votes—and said he would do it again, because Iran cannot be allowed a nuclear weapon. When the second-highest officer in the government publicly asks whether the cause was worth its price, the question of our will is no longer only a question about the voters. It is being asked inside the administration that must carry the war.


The election does not change the question. It changes who must answer it. The new Congress takes its seats in January, and any authorization of force now passes through a Democratic majority. The regime is betting that we will trade anything for cheaper fuel and peace. It may be right. Our military is strong, but our will is in question, and this paper addresses the question of our will to fight to ensure that the regime of mullahs does not have nuclear weapons with which to terrorize and hold the world hostage to their demands.


## My Position in Brief


My stand: the war in Iran is righteous. We face an enemy willing to blackmail the world with nuclear weapons. We have been criticized as a pawn of Israel. Whatever our initial motivation or the pressures upon us, I believe the fight is righteous for two reasons: first, to free an oppressed people; and second, to deny a terrorist regime the weapon of world terror. America can win this war, but it will be won or lost first in America. It cannot be won with blood and bullets alone. It must be won by transforming hearts, and the first transformation must be in the hearts of the American people; the second is in the hearts of the mullahs and people of Iran. That transformation is the foundation of victory; without it, no success in the Gulf will last.


The prerequisite—the step before the first step in winning the war in Iran—is winning the war raging in the heart of America. As a nation, we have turned our backs on the Gospel and Lordship of Jesus Christ. Every ailment of the nation—poverty, disease, homelessness, war, addiction, divorce, abortion, gender confusion, factions, and social unrest—has its root in that rebellion, and will begin to heal when we bow our knees to the Lord of the Universe. The cure is a nation that has turned back to God. That nation speaks the Gospel of Christ in public without fear, embarrassment, or apology; counts righteousness as a national good; bears the cost of justice and righteousness when there is no quick relief; and speaks with conviction that the God we worship is the True God. Only such a people can hold a line for as long as an enemy resists surrender.


But we need not wait until the national soul is healed and sanctified before we take our first step in freeing the world of Iranian-sponsored terror and the threat of a nuclear Iran. We can act today with the tools and consent we have. We are already engaged in the first step: hold the blockade, find and destroy the regime’s nuclear and missile work as it appears, refuse any truce that lets the Mullahs rebuild their forces, take away the oil weapon, and carry the Gospel to the Persian people by every means we have.


The first step buys time. It does not end the war. The regime cannot be bargained into goodness, cannot be bombed into a new heart, and cannot be invaded at a price we are willing to pay. But if we hold the pressure and keep finding their work, we can keep Iran from a bomb. That delay buys time while hearts change. The hearts that must change are both theirs and ours; one without the other will not be sufficient to effect the cure the world needs.


This paper sets out the path we must walk on three fronts:


 	- halting the development of the bomb in Iran;

 	- fighting the evil and rebellion against God that has invaded and held the heart of America; and

 	- waging the offensive against the evil that has possessed the hearts of Iran’s leaders, and freeing the people of Iran from the ideology that has made them submit to a god who is not the God of the Bible. The true God is love and mercy, justice and righteousness, and He gives liberty in Christ.


## Why the Battle Begins at Home


It would be easy to see the Iran war as a foreign problem about nuclear weapons and terrorism. I see it as a spiritual problem with a physical symptom. The people of Iran are under the domination of an unGodly spirit; its leaders, as the ministers of this spirit, oppress the people according to its nature. We can look over there and see clearly the oppression and unGodliness of the regime, which clothes itself in the vestments of piety while it commits the most unholy acts against its people and exports its false religion worldwide by force, deception, and seduction, and prevents exit once in its capture.


It would be easy to say that the cure is simply to kill the men who rule Iran, but we risk being judged by our own words. Jesus warned us: *“Why do you see the speck that is in your brother’s eye, but do not notice the log that is in your own eye?… First take the log out of your own eye, and then you will see clearly to take the speck out of your brother’s eye”* **(Matthew 7:3, 5)**. We have clearly seen their sin—the terror, lies, and murder they perpetrate—but we have practiced the national sins of lust, greed, gluttony, and pride. Their scripture condemns our sins, as evidenced by a scripture or tradition that covers women in shapeless garments to keep from inflaming lust. The gesture does not change the heart, but it allows a believable pretense of holiness and spiritual superiority, letting those of their religion feel holier-than-thou by comparison. And so the words and testimony of the American Christian are discarded as the meaningless words of a hypocrite.


Thus, to be an effective witness, we must sanctify our nation. We have no moral standing in their eyes (rightfully) to condemn their hypocrisy unless we are examples of the holiness we call the leaders of Iran to embrace. The path to victory is clear: we must bow the knee and accept Jesus as the Lord of our individual and common lives. This means obeying His Law and resisting the temptations of sin. As a free people, we are free to do anything that is good, lovely, right, and of good report – we are not free to do evil.


The ships, planes, and bombs are tools of carnal warfare. The spiritual weapons are will, patience, and the willingness to sacrifice for righteousness. Those are moral goods, and they should be taught openly in public schools, used as points of argument in courtrooms and legislative chambers, and used as guides by the executive branch that administers the law. We have spent generations teaching the young that morality is a private decision. We now suffer the attack of an enemy that claims a form of godliness but is blind to the truth and goodness of the God of the Bible. God will judge such hypocritical righteousness as filthy rags—and Scripture says the same of ours: *“all our righteous deeds are like a polluted garment”* **(Isaiah 64:6)**. In the meantime, we suffer under the threat of terror attacks by people who call us the Great Satan. And if we measure the behavior of some among us against that charge, we must admit that we have fallen far from the high calling of Godliness. To be an effective witness, we must repent, change, turn, and worship the True God. When we do, our witness will convict, and our testimony will convince. Our spiritual purity should be the primary tool we use in tearing down satanic strongholds.


The Apostle Paul said: *“For the weapons of our warfare are not of the flesh but have divine power to destroy strongholds”* **(2 Corinthians 10:4)**. The tools of the flesh—sanctions, strikes, blockades, reserves, production—are real, and we must use them. But they are small. They can destroy a centrifuge hall. They cannot destroy the belief that commands the hall to be rebuilt. The motivation to keep fighting evil is easier to maintain when service to the God of creation, and His approval, is the primary reward.


That is why the most important front in this war runs through American homes and churches. A family paying $6 for diesel is paying the price of Hormuz. Whether that family will keep paying it, and keep supporting the men who stand watch in the Gulf, depends less on the next strike than on what that family believes is worth sacrificing for.


Iran is not the only danger of this kind. Advanced artificial intelligence, engineered plagues, and the slow demographic retreat of the civilization that carries the Bible are each on the horizon. Laws and technical controls can slow each. None stays solved while the people who must uphold those laws treat righteousness as optional. Iran is simply the nearest case—close enough to see, costly enough to feel, and urgent enough that delay is dangerous. Waiting for a greater danger to make the need for repentance obvious may be waiting until it is too late for anything but a miracle. Wise action while the threat is on the horizon is the better course.


The promise and the order are both in Scripture: *“If my people who are called by my name humble themselves, and pray and seek my face and turn from their wicked ways, then I will hear from heaven and will forgive their sin and heal their land”* **(2 Chronicles 7:14)**. Humility, prayer, and repentance come first; healing follows repentance and righteousness.


## The Hard Question


Can a regime that preaches martyrdom and dreams of imposing its faith on the world be negotiated with? I see no scenario in which the mullahs willingly give up the knowledge and the will to build a bomb. If we leave them in power, we may be leaving another North Korea in place—but one armed with a more aggressive, expansionist theology. Such an outcome would terrorize the world for generations. And the likeliest result of any agreement is that they sign it, wait, and rebuild.


When people tell us what they believe and what they will do, we should believe them. Islamic tradition has a word for a truce made from weakness: hudna, a temporary cessation of fighting that may be ended when strength returns. Its model is often taken to be the Treaty of Hudaybiyyah of 628, which Muhammad made with the Meccans and which was set aside two years later when his forces marched on Mecca. In 1994, months after signing the Oslo Accords, Yasser Arafat told a mosque audience in Johannesburg that his agreement with Israel was like Hudaybiyyah. Shia teaching adds taqiyya, the permitted concealment of one’s true belief under threat. Not every Muslim reads these traditions as a license for deceit, but the men who rule Iran have given us every reason to believe they do.


While the men who send others to die as suicide bombers are probably not as eager to die themselves, that is no reason to trust them. Their doctrine of world domination tells us what moves them: the survival of their rule, so that they may fight another day and propagate their eschatology and the rule of their god over all mankind. A truce with such men is useful only if its terms make that other day impossible. And because no one can see into their hearts, this paper does not rest our safety on the hope that they will always prefer survival to martyrdom. It rests our safety on preventing the bomb.


## What History Teaches


**Japan.** No modern regime was more devoted to a god-man than imperial Japan. Its soldiers fought to the last man on Iwo Jima and Okinawa; its pilots flew kamikaze missions. Yet Japan’s home islands were never invaded. The Emperor chose surrender in August 1945, after the blockade that was starving the country, the firebombing of its cities, the two atomic bombs, and the Soviet declaration of war—and he chose it against much of his general staff, whose officers attempted a coup to stop his surrender broadcast and failed. The bombs and the Soviet entry broke the deadlock; historians still debate their relative weight, but the decision came before any American soldier set foot on the home islands. What came after was different. Under seven years of occupation, the Emperor renounced his divinity, State Shinto was dismantled, and the nation was remade. Two lessons follow. At the top, the regime’s survival was the deciding question. And the end of the fighting was not the same as the change of the nation’s heart.


**Iran, 1988.** After eight years of war with Iraq and human-wave attacks in which boys were sent across minefields, Ayatollah Khomeini accepted a United Nations ceasefire. He said that accepting it was more deadly to him than drinking poison. The founder of the Islamic Republic chose the survival of his regime over continuing the holy war. But survival was not repentance. Within a few years, Iran was secretly building a nuclear weapons program. The 1988 ceasefire was a truce to rebuild.


**Iran, 1953 and 1979.** In 1953, the United States and Britain helped overthrow Iran’s elected prime minister and restore the Shah; our own government has since acknowledged its part. For twenty-five years the Shah ruled with a secret police and a secular modernization that offered his people prosperity for some and fear for many, but no hope for the soul. In 1979 the people threw off that yoke and took up the far heavier one of the mullahs, who concealed their ambitions until they held power.


We were wrong to impose a secular ruler on Iran, and we are paying the price of that arrogance: when the people threw him off, the mullahs imposed on them the worship of a false god. We should right our wrong. We cannot impose Godly government on them either, because such a government must be chosen freely/willfully/purposefully if it is to last. So I propose that we witness to them, let them see the good fruit of our testimony, and pay to repair the civilian infrastructure we damaged in our attacks. They must choose to worship Jesus as Lord, but we should never cease praising His name, telling of His good works, and showing them the good life they could have in His service.


That history holds one more lesson. On 11 February 1979, the Shah’s army declared itself neutral, and his regime fell within hours. The men with the guns were ordinary citizens—unaffiliated, unradicalized, loyal only to a degree—and when enough of the people turned against the Shah, so did they.


**North Korea.** Since its first nuclear test in 2006, North Korea has threatened the world, starved its people, and remained in power. It has never used a nuclear weapon, because its rulers want to keep ruling. A nuclear hermit kingdom is a terrible outcome, though so far a deterrable one. But deterrence does not change hearts; it only means one force is larger than the other. It may keep the peace for generations while millions live and die in bondage. And Iran is not North Korea: Pyongyang’s creed is the keeping of its own throne, while Tehran’s is the spread of its faith over the world. What has deterred one cannot be assumed to deter the other.


The secular tools of war, surveillance, and deterrence are small beside the weapons of spiritual warfare. We are in a battle of good against evil. The belt of truth, the breastplate of righteousness, the gospel of peace, the shield of faith, the helmet of salvation, and the sword of the Spirit, which is the word of God **(Ephesians 6:14–17)**, are the weapons we should help the victims of this regime take up against their captors. That may be a long war. It is also the only war that ends with the enemy transformed, not merely contained.


## The Fork


These lessons bring us to a choice that our leaders must make.


If our aim is limited to keeping the uranium out of a bomb, reopening Hormuz, and constraining the regime, the tools of economic pressure and verified inspection can maintain that status quo. The limiting factor is the American people’s perseverance in sustaining that pressure. During that time, the country’s infrastructure should not be targeted, and the regime’s war machine should be pressed without pause.


If our aim is removing the regime by force, the honest answer is the Japanese answer: total defeat and occupation. Iran has about ninety million people, twice Iraq’s population, and nearly four times its territory, much of it mountainous. Iraq required more than 150,000 American troops and years of insurgency. Removing the Islamic Republic by force would require a far larger war, millions under arms, and years of governing a hostile country afterward. If that is the aim, a president should say so, ask Congress to declare war, mobilize the nation, and tell the American people the price.


What we are doing now is the worst of both. An air war with regime-change ambitions and no stated end is costly enough to convince Tehran that only a bomb will save it, and not decisive enough to remove the men who would build it.


Many who supported the February campaign now call the months since the ceasefire a low-intensity quagmire: a blockade, sanctions, and occasional strikes that let Iran rebuild while we wait. Of the present course, they are right. The holding action I propose below is not the present course in better words. It differs in four ways. Strikes on the nuclear and missile programs are a standing condition, not an occasional reply timed to news cycles and elections. There is one condition for ending them—total access—not a shifting list of demands that invites bargaining. Congress votes the red lines in advance, so Tehran knows where force begins and cannot simply wait out one President’s patience. And the uranium is accounted for first, before any relief. A blockade without those terms is a siege without a purpose. With them, it is a vise with one way out.


As I wrote in August, no sustained war should be fought without a vote of Congress that states the objective in one sentence, the conditions for ending it, and a sunset date. Jesus told us to count the cost: *“For which of you, desiring to build a tower, does not first sit down and count the cost, whether he has enough to complete it?”* **(Luke 14:28)**. We did not count the cost before 28 February. But the same passage shows the righteous course for a king who finds he cannot win at the price he is willing to pay: *“while the other is yet a great way off, he sends a delegation and asks for terms of peace”* **(Luke 14:32)**. Counting the cost is not only a warning against beginning; it is a guide for what to do once we have begun.


## The Objective in One Sentence


Iran shall not build a nuclear weapon or hold the world’s commerce hostage, and America will keep the pressure, the watch, and its witness to the Gospel until the Iranian people are free and the uranium is removed.


## The Plan, Part One: The Line We Hold Abroad


What follows can be done now, with the authority and the forces we already have. It is a holding action. Its purpose is to keep the regime from turning time into a bomb while the deeper work proceeds.


### 1. Press the war machine, spare the people


A blockade raises the price of bread. Sanctions empty pharmacies. Those things fall on the people, and the men who rule Iran have shown that the suffering of their people does not move them. To a ruler who believes he is building God’s dominion over the earth, the hungry are paving stones laid down under his chariot. He will spend them. The boys sent across the minefields in the 1980s are the proof.


What moves them is the survival of their rule and the spread of their doctrine. So the pressure must be aimed where the regime actually lives.


**Keep the blockade, defend shipping, answer every attack.** Answer every attack on our forces or on commercial vessels firmly and immediately.


**Cut the money, not the bread.** Aim sanctions and financial disruption at the Revolutionary Guard (the ideologically committed), its documented commercial empire, its weapons and nuclear programs, and the specific officials and businessmen who run or knowingly finance those networks. Wealth alone is not guilt, and kinship alone is not complicity. A successful Iranian merchant is not a target because he is rich; a financier who launders money for the missile program is a target because of what he does. Use informants, cyber operations, and interdiction of weapons and dual-use goods as the daily instruments of pressure.


**Strike the program, and strike it again.** Strike nuclear and missile sites when intelligence confirms the target and the harm to bystanders is low. When they rebuild, strike again. Not a burst of bombing timed to an election, and not a pause because a news cycle has moved on: a standing condition, for as long as the regime refuses the one condition below. Every strike must still weigh the value of the target against the lives around it; we do not level a building to reach one man.


**Spare what keeps the people alive.** No strikes on power grids, water, or desalination. Keep a humanitarian channel for food and medicine open, so every Iranian can see who is starving him. If we have struck civilian life-support wrongly, we should say so and rebuild it.


### 2. Find the bomb before it is built


Here is the weakest link in the holding action. Our forces can destroy what they can find. The danger is what they cannot find.


No one outside Iran knows where its roughly 440 kilograms of uranium enriched to 60 percent now lie—enough for about ten weapons if refined further, unseen by inspectors since June 2025, much of it reportedly under the rubble of bombed sites. The U.S. envoy Steve Witkoff says Iran’s own negotiators told him, days before the war, that they held about 460 kilograms and knew it could make eleven bombs; Iran’s foreign minister denies saying so. Whichever figure is nearer the truth, the danger is the same. Refining that material to weapons grade does not require a great industrial plant; it could be done in a hall small enough to hide. History warns us. An Iranian opposition group, not Western intelligence, exposed the enrichment plant at Natanz in 2002. The plant at Fordow was built inside a mountain and kept secret for years before the United States, Britain, and France revealed it in 2009. A regime that hid whole plants can hide a room.


So the holding action rests on three things, in this order:


 	- **Eyes inside.** The best intelligence on a hidden program comes from people inside it. Most will never leave Iran, and we should not ask them to. A scientist who stays at his desk and quietly tells us what he sees does more good than one who flees. Secure, encrypted channels—satellite internet, protected messaging—let such men reach us without meeting in a dark alley. We should build those channels, publicize their existence, and protect them.

 	- **A door for those who must leave.** For the few who can get out with what they know, we offer asylum, protection, support, and a public promise that those who have not committed atrocities will have a place in a free Iran. This is what America did with German scientists after World War II. It will be a trickle, not a flood; a police state sees to that. One defector who knows where the uranium is can still do more damage to the program than a bomb.

 	- **Total access.** In the end, nothing replaces inspectors with unfettered access to go anywhere. That is why the condition below is not one demand among many. It is the only demand that answers the detection problem.


### 3. One condition, and only one


The regime will offer truces. It has offered them throughout this war, and each has been a pause to rearm—what its own tradition calls a hudna. So the terms must be simple enough that no truce can hide inside them.


One condition ends the campaign of strikes: total access. Every site, every tunnel, every warehouse, every floorboard that could hold a centrifuge or a missile line, open to inspectors at any hour, permanently, with cameras, sensors, and satellites watching and no sunset clause. And the 60 percent uranium dug out, counted, and shipped out of the country under international custody, as we airlifted weapons-grade uranium out of Kazakhstan in 1994 and shipped Libya’s nuclear equipment to Oak Ridge in 2004.


The offer to Tehran can be stated in a sentence: Let us in, let us dig out the uranium, and the bombs stop falling on your heads.


Everything else—the blockade’s relief, the release of prisoners including imprisoned Christians, free passage through Hormuz—follows in phases, each tied to a verified act and snapped back the day a term is broken. The first phase is the accounting of the uranium, because it is the danger that cannot wait. Not “trust and verify.” Distrust, and verify forever.


I do not expect the regime to accept these terms, because they forbid the very rebuilding its truces are offered to allow. For the Iranians to accept such terms would be near capitulation without regime change. But these are the only terms acceptable to us. Until the mullahs accept them, the pressure continues.


### 4. If they accept, keep our word


If they do accept, we must keep our word. Israel kept its treaty with the Gibeonites even though it was obtained by deception, because it had sworn before the Lord **(Joshua 9:15–20)**. The man who may dwell on God’s holy hill is the one *“who swears to his own hurt and does not change”* **(Psalm 15:4)**. A regime that lies must face a nation whose word holds. Keeping our word does not mean trusting theirs. It means that when we strike, it is because they broke the terms, and the whole world can see it.


### 5. Draw the red lines now, and let Congress vote on them


Rather than drifting toward war week by week, the President should ask Congress, now, for a narrow authorization: if Iran expels inspectors, moves its highly enriched uranium, enriches toward weapons grade, or attacks American forces, the United States may strike its nuclear and missile programs and the Guard units responsible. Anything larger requires a further vote. This honors the Constitution, tells Tehran exactly where the lines are, and replaces the pattern that has twice taught Iran that negotiating does not protect it.


I am under no illusion about the new Congress. Many in its majority want the war ended on almost any terms, and the Vice President’s post has given them cover from within the President’s own party. We saw where “out on any terms” leads in Afghanistan in 2021, when we abandoned Bagram Air Base and the Taliban took it within days. But the red lines are not a vote for endless war. They are a vote that says where war begins. A member who will not vote for the war should still be asked whether he will let Iran move its uranium without consequence. Let him answer in public. My campaign speaks first to those who will follow me; this request is addressed to whoever holds the votes.


### 6. Make the bomb worthless to them—as a backstop, not the plan


Everything above is designed to prevent a bomb, not to live with one, and that order matters. Deterrence works only on men who prefer their own survival to their cause. The conduct of Iran’s rulers—Khomeini’s “poison” in 1988, the bargaining throughout this war—suggests that at the top they do. But that cannot be proven in advance, and their theology honors martyrdom. A nation should not stake its cities on a judgment about the inner life of its enemy. So prevention is the plan, and deterrence is the net beneath it.


If, despite all this, the mullahs someday get a weapon, it must buy them nothing. The United States should declare now that any use of a nuclear weapon by Iran, or any transfer of nuclear material to a proxy, will be treated as an attack by the Iranian state and will end the regime. We should extend that guarantee to Israel and the Gulf states, so that no neighbor races to build its own bomb, and invest in nuclear forensics so that no regime can hide behind a terrorist.


### 7. Take away the oil weapon


The regime holds two oil weapons. One is its own oil, which pays for its missiles. The other is the Strait of Hormuz, through which the world’s oil passes and which it can choke at will.


**Its own oil.** The blockade has cut off the regime’s main revenue. The danger now is a relief deal that quietly reopens the pipeline to China, which bought most of Iran’s crude before the war—much of it relabeled as coming from other countries to evade sanctions. No oil relief should come before the uranium is accounted for. When relief does come, the secondary sanctions on the refiners, tankers, and traders who carry disguised Iranian crude must stay in force until the final phase is verified.


**The strait.** Making Hormuz irrelevant has many steps, and the people should be told the timeline:


 	- **Now to three years:** Expand and defend the Saudi and Emirati pipelines that already carry oil around the strait—knowing that they are fixed, known targets that drones, missiles, and saboteurs can reach, so that they soften a shock but cannot carry the full load. Refill the Strategic Petroleum Reserve. Accelerate permitting and production in Alaska, Canada, and elsewhere outside the Gulf. Require the Asian nations that buy most of the Gulf’s oil—China, India, Japan, and South Korea—to share the cost of guarding it. Never accept an Iranian toll on the strait.

 	- **Three to eight years:** New production outside the Gulf comes on line. Because oil is priced on one world market, this softens the price pressure but cannot free us without a massive production increase. The United States was a net exporter before this war, and gasoline still rose above four dollars a gallon, because oil prices are set by world supply.

 	- **Now to ten years:** Conventional nuclear plants built at full speed, and thorium molten-salt reactors brought from design to operation.

 	- **Ten to twenty years:** Nuclear power carried into transportation, through electric vehicles and through using the high heat of molten-salt reactors to make hydrogen and synthetic fuels.


Meanwhile, we will pay high fuel prices for several years. If we are honest about the method, follow the timeline, and report our progress truthfully, the nation can bear the trial, because it will see hope fulfilled as each project goes from conception to completion. Abundant energy is the one victory over the mullahs that requires neither confrontation nor agreement.


### 8. China and Israel


China is the regime’s banker and its shield at the United Nations. Its leaders have called our war on Iran illegitimate while their refineries took Iranian crude at a discount. We should warn any nation that finances the regime’s weapons by buying its smuggled oil that it will face consequences, including in its trade with us.


Israel is our ally and stands closest to the danger. It will act on its own judgment and its own clock, as any nation under threat of annihilation would. We should coordinate closely, share what we know, and be honest with our own people: this is not just Israel’s war that we have joined. A nuclear Iran threatens American cities, American sailors, and the world’s commerce. It is our war too.


## The Plan, Part Two: The Gospel to Persia


### 9. Speak Christ openly, and let Persians carry the word


The Gospel of Christ is the one message the regime has no lasting defense against. That victory may already be under way. Iran is widely reported to have one of the world’s fastest-growing underground churches. Estimates range from a few hundred thousand to over a million believers, and they have grown without help from Washington, through house churches, satellite broadcasts, and the regime’s own cruelty. Large majorities of Iranians tell pollsters they want an end to the Islamic Republic.


Yet the church is fast-growing and still small—a few believers among ninety million—and fragile. Any organized resistance or open sharing of faith is met with harshness, and these young sprouts of faith could be quickly crushed. I support the church, but left to its own strength alone, the Gospel will be suppressed unless God brings a massive, supernatural spread of it in the hearts of the masses. Over the centuries, where Christians have answered Islamic conquest with silence, Islam has expanded, and Christianity has eroded. Something very different from what has happened in the past must now be done. What must change is not the Gospel, and not its weapons—the Gospel compels no one—but the strength behind it: the full voice of a nation speaking it, and the full resources of a nation carrying it.


I hold that the United States may speak the Gospel as a nation; I set out the argument in Part Three. But how we speak must pass the test of love, and the first neighbor to consider is the Iranian convert. The regime already charges converts with acting for foreign powers. If every Bible that reaches Tehran carries an American government seal, we hand the interrogator his charge sheet.


So I propose a division of labor:


 	- **The President speaks.** The President of the United States should say, openly and often, that Jesus Christ is the hope of the Persian people as He is of ours. No more silence, no more embarrassment.

 	- **The nation builds the roads.** Our government should fund and defend the means by which the word travels: satellite internet terminals in Iranian hands, anti-censorship tools, Persian-language broadcasting through a renewed Radio Free America, and the capacity to carry Scripture in by high-altitude balloon and airdrop, as leaflets were dropped over Germany and Japan in the Second World War and as balloons have carried Bibles into North Korea.

 	- **Persian believers carry the word.** The content should come from the Persian-language Christian broadcasters and ministries who already reach millions of Iranians, and above all from Iranian believers themselves. We should ask them what they need and what endangers them, and let their answers govern how we help. Digital Scripture, on cards and drives small enough to hide, protects the recipient. We must not make the persecutor’s work easier.


When the message of Christ is everywhere, talk of Him becomes ordinary and harder for the authorities to prosecute.


### 10. How to speak to a Muslim heart


How we speak matters as much as whether we speak. I propose that we begin by honoring what Islam got right.


Islam arose as a revolt against the idols of Arabia—against stones, spirits, and the many gods of the Kaaba. The Quran’s sharpest warfare is aimed at the pagans. In that, Muhammad turned his people in the right direction: toward one God who has no image. Scripture calls the powers men worshipped in place of Him *“weak and beggarly elemental spirits”* **(Galatians 4:9)**. The early teaching of the Meccan period—care for the poor and the orphan, prayer, modesty, sexual purity—reads largely like the moral law of the Torah.


Then, in Medina, the revelations changed. Permission to fight, to rule, and to take more wives came as each need arose; Muhammad’s own wife Aisha is reported to have remarked that his Lord seemed quick to grant his wishes. And by the doctrine many Islamic jurists hold—that later verses supersede earlier ones—Medinan law of domination came to govern how Meccan mercy is read.


So our message to the Muslim world can be this: You were right to turn from the idols. You were right to seek the one God. Keep remembering Him five times a day if you wish; remembering God is holy. But the God of Abraham does not need your hands washed five times to accept your heart. He asks for a clean heart, and He offers to make it clean. He came among us as a man, knows our sorrows, and carries what we cannot. *“Come to me, all who labor and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest… For my yoke is easy, and my burden is light”* **(Matthew 11:28, 30)**. You may still call Him by the name your language uses. What changes is not His name but the teaching about Him: the God of Medina whose prophet must not be questioned, or the God of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob who came in Christ.


And we can invite every Muslim to judge by fruit, as Jesus told us to: *“You will recognize them by their fruits”* **(Matthew 7:16)**. We pray *“your will be done, on earth as it is in heaven”* **(Matthew 6:10)**. Then look at the earth each faith builds. Where the law of the regime is enforced by morality police and the gallows, the rulers live in luxury and tell the poor that their reward is in paradise; the young man without work or a wife is sent to jihad with a promise of heaven. Ask the simplest question: would you choose to be that young man? The rulers have already answered it with their own lives—they live as though the paradise they promise is not worth waiting for. Ask, too, why so many in the Muslim world long to move to the West. They are moving toward the fruit of the God of Isaac, not of Ishmael—fruit we ourselves have squandered, but fruit nonetheless.


This is not the mullahs’ coerced religion turned around. A state that enforces a moral code with police and executions compels conscience. A nation that offers the Gospel to anyone willing to hear compels no one.


### 11. Speak to the soldiers who are not yet the regime’s


The Revolutionary Guard is chosen and indoctrinated for loyalty. Iran’s regular army, the Artesh, rests far more on young conscripts—sons of ordinary families who did not choose the mullahs and did not choose their service. In 1979, the regular army declared itself neutral and let a regime fall. Our broadcasts and our witness should speak directly to those young men: we have no quarrel with you; the men who command the Guard are not your masters by right; rather, the God who made you and knows your name is your rightful Lord. When enough of the people turn, the men with the guns will turn with them. That happened once in Iran. It can happen again, this time toward freedom.


## The Plan, Part Three: The Battle at Home


### 12. Recover the freedom to speak


For nearly eighty years—since Everson v. Board of Education in 1947—Americans have been taught that the Constitution forbids our government to speak of Christ. That teaching is false, and it has done more to disarm and harm us in this war than any Iranian missile or concession.


The text forbids Congress to make a law respecting an establishment of religion, or prohibiting the free exercise thereof. It forbids a national church and the coercion of conscience. It does not command public silence about God in the government. The men who wrote it did not intend or read it that way. Congress opened its sessions with paid chaplains from the first. And in 1803, under President Jefferson—the very man whose phrase “a wall of separation” Everson made famous—the Senate ratified a treaty with the Kaskaskia nation in which the United States paid $100 a year for seven years toward the support of a Catholic priest and $300 toward building a church. Historians debate what that treaty proves: the tribe was already Catholic, and the payment was part of a land settlement with a sovereign nation, not a mission sent to convert it. But it proves at the least that the founding generation did not think federal money and the Christian faith must never touch, least of all in our dealings with other nations.


The law itself has begun to return to the founders’ reading. In Kennedy v. Bremerton School District (2022), the Supreme Court set aside the test it had used for fifty years and held that the Establishment Clause must be read by reference to historical practices and understandings. The prison that holds us now is not mainly in the courts. It is in our minds: the fear of being called a bigot, a zealot, a crusader, for saying in public what we believe in private. That fear has chilled the speech of a free people. This campaign aims to thaw that chill, and to replace the stigma attached to the Bible with a love of holiness and the blessings God gives a land because of that holiness.


Why is this right, and not only lawful? Because liberty that cannot be spoken is not liberty, and a nation that will not name the source of its freedom cannot offer that freedom to anyone else. Love of neighbor does not keep its best news to itself. The Persian believer in prison is asking us to stop being silent. We cannot answer him until we stop being silent at home.


### 13. Become the nation we are calling them to be


We cannot persuade Iranians that the faith of the West is better than the religion of the mullahs if we do not live it. A people who have watched their rulers force a counterfeit morality on them will not be won by a nation that has abandoned morality altogether—by our pornography, our broken marriages, our disregard for the sanctity of women’s bodies, our leaders growing rich, and the poor being sent to fight in their wars. We cannot give the Iranian people another example of leadership devoted to itself.


So the foreign policy and the domestic policy are one policy. *“Righteousness exalts a nation, but sin is a reproach to any people”* **(Proverbs 14:34)**. We must repent as a nation and live what we preach. That repentance begins in each house: the flesh made to wait upon the Spirit, appetite satisfied in the time and place that please God, the long reward of a peaceful and loving life chosen over quick pleasure. It is a hard sell, because its cost is obvious and its reward comes over time. It is also the only foundation strong enough to carry a nation through a long war.


America’s righteousness is not a separate subject from the war. It is what makes our witness credible and our endurance possible. Energy, Righteousness, and Accountability to God—who knows our hearts and how we have loved our fellow man—the E, R, and A of The New ERA Vision, will defeat the mullahs over a generation. At some point the mullahs will find that their faith has no ground left to grow in.


### 14. How we will know the work is advancing


We cannot count a change of heart directly. We can count its fruit. These are the signs we should watch and report:


 	- **At home:** citizens writing their representatives about this plan; Christians in public life willing to speak their faith without apology; candidates at every level who will vote the red lines; the steady return of public prayer and witness to places from which it was driven.

 	- **In the Gulf:** the uranium accounted for; inspectors on the ground; sites found before they are finished; Hormuz open without tolls.

 	- **In Iran:** satellite terminals and secure channels in use; the growth of house churches, as Persian ministries report it; survey evidence, such as the GAMAAN studies, of Iranians turning from the regime’s religion; the first quiet signs of conscripts and officials who will no longer serve it.


If the signs at home do not move, the holding action abroad will shrink with them. Then the plan contracts to argument, to the channels that private believers can keep open, and to prayer. That would not prove the plan wrong. It would prove the warning right.


## Why Now: A War for the Nation’s Attention


I have spent years looking for the place where a long argument about God, morality, and government touches what ordinary people feel today. This is it. A family paying $6 for diesel is paying the price of Hormuz, and Hormuz is a weapon only because the world runs on Gulf oil. That price ties to Iran, to Islam, to tyranny, to uranium, and to the question of whether bombing again after the election would achieve what bombing before it did not. The answer runs straight through the letters of the platform I have carried since 2004: E for abundant energy, so that no regime can hold our economy hostage; R for righteousness, so that our witness is worth hearing and our will is strong enough to last; and A for the accountability, before God and man, of those who serve and of those who violate their trust.


The plan should be on the desks of the President, the Secretary of War, the Joint Chiefs, and the leaders of the incoming Congress. But a letter sent to them by one citizen reaches a staffer and the wastebasket. Influence in a republic comes from the people behind an idea. So this is the campaign:


**Speak it plainly, in my own name and face.** I will present this paper myself, on video, illustrated so that viewers can see the strategy rather than only hear it, and post it wherever Americans watch.


**A letter campaign with one message: Look in your inbox.** Citizens write their senators and representatives, the President, and the Secretary of War, asking them to read this plan. Each reader sends it to his circle, and they to theirs. When enough people point to the same document, those in power read it. If the followers lead, the leaders will follow.


**Supply lines before the first volley.** A single viral post is a gesture; a campaign must be sustained. The site must carry the supporting body of work—who I am, what I stand for, the full platform, and the record of my life told by me rather than by my opponents—so that others can carry the argument if I cannot carry it myself.


I will not pretend this message is safe to say. It names a regime that has plotted murder on American soil, and it calls Muhammad a false prophet. It may bring ridicule, attempts to discredit me, or worse. It may put those who work with me at risk, and I have told them so; each must make his own choice. Jesus told us to count the cost before building a tower or going to war, and that applies as much to speaking as to fighting. I have counted it. I will speak in my own name, because a message that asks a nation to stake lives on it must have a real man standing behind it.


## Why Not the Nuclear Option


Some say that if we used nuclear weapons, Iran would capitulate as Japan did. It might. The bombs of August 1945 helped bring Japan’s surrender before any invasion. But the first nuclear strike since 1945, against a country that does not have the bomb, killing hundreds of thousands of men, women, and children who did not choose their rulers, cannot pass any moral test I accept. It would teach every tyrant on earth the lesson Tehran already suspects: that only a bomb of their own makes them safe. And even in Japan, the bombs ended the fighting; they did not change the nation’s heart. That came afterward, under occupation, and after an all-out war ending in unconditional surrender; without that, we will not occupy Iran. A nuclear strike would give us a surrender, perhaps, and a people who hated us for a century.


## The Moral Test


**Is it righteous?** Restraining the commanders and financiers of a regime that fires on sailors, hangs its own people, and pursues a bomb is the restraint of evildoers that Scripture assigns to civil authority: *“he does not bear the sword in vain. For he is the servant of God, an avenger who carries out God’s wrath on the wrongdoer”* **(Romans 13:4)**. Offering a door to every man who will leave is mercy. Targeting a man for his wealth or his family name, or striking the water and power that keep children alive, is not righteous, and this plan forbids both. Proclaiming Christ to a captive people is righteous. Breaking faith, or deliberately destroying civilian life-support, is not.


**Is it loving?** Love restrains the predator and refuses to punish the prisoner for his jailer’s crimes. It does not withhold the gospel from a people dying without it, and it does not lie to its own citizens about the price of fuel or the length of the war. Love does not flatter, either: *“You shall not hate your brother in your heart, but you shall reason frankly with your neighbor”* **(Leviticus 19:17)**. Telling America to repent is an act of love, not contempt. Love for our neighbor includes the Gulf sailor, the Israeli family under missile fire, the Iranian believer in prison, the young Iranian conscript, and the American family paying for fuel.


**Would I want it done to me?** If I were an Iranian father under this regime, I would want the world to make my rulers’ grip so uncertain that their guards began to slip away; to give me a safe way to tell the truth I know; to spare my water and light; and to tell me the truth about God by the hands of my own countrymen, so that my family was not marked as spies for receiving it. I would not want my city destroyed to reach men I never chose. And if I were an American, I would want a leader honest enough to tell me that the war cannot be won without my own repentance.


## The Options, and My Choice


My judgment is this: the mullahs want to survive in order to spread Islam. They are willing to enter a deceitful truce, pretending to be at peace so they can rebuild. Their conduct after 1988, their bargaining throughout this war, and North Korea’s example all point the same way. Peace is not their goal, but they will stop for a while if they think they can live to fight another day. Both regimes rest on domination: in North Korea, the keeping of it; in Iran, its spread, the domination of Islam over the whole world. Without a change of heart, without surrendering their hearts to Jesus as Lord, any negotiation is likely to give them nothing but time to rebuild. Without a change of heart, the renunciation of the false god of Islam, I think the most likely course is that Iran will try covertly to move its uranium toward a weapon.


That leaves three options.


 	- **A war declared by Congress,** fought to the end of Iran’s unconditional surrender, with its full cost counted and told to the people. We must not drift into that war without a vote and without a plan.

 	- **A truce on terms that make rebuilding a nuclear weapon capability impossible,** with total, permanent access to inspection, and the uranium removed. The regime is unlikely to accept it, because it forbids their real goal. It would be the near equivalent of capitulation without regime change.

 	- **The long war:** the holding action abroad—blockade, standing strikes on the program as it appears, money cut off, eyes inside, the oil weapon taken away—while the Gospel goes to Persia and the battle for the American heart is fought at home.


I choose the third, with the door to the second always open and the red lines of the first option voted in advance. We continue the long war until the mullahs accept the terms of the second, and if they cross the red lines, force is already authorized. We do not drift into the full war of the first without the vote, the mobilization, and the honest accounting it demands. And we do not pretend that the holding action can last forever on a nation’s fading patience. It lasts as long as the American people are willing to sustain it, which is why the battle at home to transform hearts is not an afterthought to this plan. It is the plan’s foundation.


## Why the Concern Remains Real


The uranium is unaccounted for. The inspectors report no sign that Iran has rebuilt its damaged sites, but the absence of visible rebuilding is not proof of absence; the regime has hidden whole plants before. It has lied about undeclared sites and about the purpose of its enrichment, and its own traditions give it doctrines for doing so. It has new incentives to build a bomb, and some in its parliament urge leaving the nonproliferation treaty.


Its missiles reach farther than it admitted. On 20 March, according to American and British officials, it fired two intermediate-range ballistic missiles at the joint U.S.-British base on Diego Garcia, about 4,000 kilometers away—twice the range to which Iran had claimed to limit its missiles, and roughly the distance from Iran to central Europe. Neither reached the base: one failed in flight and the other was intercepted by an American warship. Tehran denies firing them and calls the episode an Israeli false flag. But a regime reaching for that range is not building only for its neighbors.


It kills its own people, sponsors terror abroad, and has plotted murder on our soil. Its ideology contains an apocalyptic strand, and no one can promise that strand will never capture a desperate leadership. Tehran is already reading our election, and our Vice President’s words after it, as signs that we will tire first. A regime that believes time is on its side will wait us out unless we change what time costs it—and unless we become a people who do not tire.


## The Strongest Objections


**From those who want escalation now:** a nuclear Iran would be permanent, and the regime is weaker today than it has been in decades. Every month of waiting lets it rebuild missiles, which it reportedly has begun doing, and perhaps recover its uranium. The months since the ceasefire, they say, have become a quagmire in which Iran rebuilds while we wait; had we resumed full combat, the Strait would be open and prices falling. A hard war now, they argue, is the lesser evil compared with a nuclear-armed theocracy later. A campaign for the nation’s heart is a generational hope; the uranium is a present danger. This argument holds out hope that one overwhelming air campaign, while the regime is weak, would end the threat.


The sharpest form of this argument holds that a nuclear Iran cannot be deterred at all. Men who seek martyrdom do not fear a counterstrike; a regime with that creed and an arsenal of thousands of missiles would use a bomb against Israel, Saudi Arabia, or the United States. To believe otherwise, they say, is to impose Western values on a vengeful theocracy.


**From those who want restraint:** the war has failed by its own measures. They argue that the Gulf states should broker a face-saving end, that the blockade itself prolongs the attacks on shipping, and that a Christian message carried with American support will confirm the regime’s claim that this is a crusade, endanger Iranian converts, and divide Americans who do not share the faith. Repeated strikes, they add, invite retaliation and can harden the regime rather than weaken it. And now they can point to the Vice President himself, who asks whether the war was worth the Senate.


**From those who doubt the foundation:** a change of the nation’s heart cannot be measured, cannot be scheduled, and may never come. To rest a war strategy on it, they say, is to substitute hope for policy—and the centrifuges run on a shorter clock than revivals.


**From Muslims who will object to how I speak of Islam:** many devout Muslims read the Medinan verses in their historical setting and live peaceably, and scholars dispute how far abrogation reaches. I accept that, and I do not speak in universal terms of the believer in the street as I speak of the men who rule Iran. But I will not soften the claim at the center: Jesus is the true and rightful Lord of the universe and every heart, and the fruit of the regime’s religion is visible to anyone who looks.


My answers:


 	- **To the hawks:** escalation has been tried twice without achieving its aims; bombs do not dig uranium out of rubble, and only access does. No overwhelming air campaign has ever brought unconditional surrender (remember the shock and awe of the Iraq war). Only a ground invasion, boots on the ground, and a sustained siege will produce unconditional surrender. Even then, victory by arms does not win the hearts of the vanquished; an occupation with a sustained, systematic, and true program of education, cultural re-valuing, and replacement of the old spiritual allegiance will change and demolish the worldview that sustained the imperialistic or apocalyptic ideology and theology. If the will of the American people will not sustain that invasion to unconditional surrender and the transformation of hearts to its end, any halfway measure is a gesture, not a strategy. On the quagmire, I agree with the diagnosis and not the cure. The present drift does let Iran rebuild; that is why the holding action I propose is built on standing strikes, one condition, voted red lines, and uranium first, rather than on occasional raids and shifting demands. On deterrence, I share the hawks’ conclusion and differ on the evidence. At every hard test, the men at the top have chosen the survival of their rule: in 1988 Khomeini drank his “poison” rather than lose the state, and the martyrdom the regime preaches has mostly been martyrdom for others. But I will not stake American and Israeli cities on that reading of their hearts. That is exactly why this plan rests on prevention—total access and the uranium removed—and treats deterrence only as the net beneath it.

 	- **To the restraint school:** every truce so far has produced only a pause for rearmament, and a face-saving end on Tehran’s terms would buy us cheap oil for a while as they build bombs and become a permanent nuclear theocracy. That is unacceptable; such a threat has no place in the kingdom of heaven on earth. The Vice President’s question deserves a straight answer: yes, keeping a bomb from this regime is worth more than a Senate majority, as the President himself has said. Elections are won and lost every two years; a nuclear theocracy would be with us for generations. As for the crusade charge, the Gospel is offered, not imposed; balloon drops of Bibles and Radio Free America offer information that every soul should have available as an option. Persian believers, not soldiers, will speak and share their testimony and encouragement as they can and support those who show interest in following Christ as Lord; Iranian Christians themselves have asked the world to stop being silent.

 	- **To those who doubt the foundation of heart transformation and sanctification:** I do not rest the near-term defense on revival. The holding action is designed to be done now, with the tools we have. I rest its endurance on revival, because nothing else has ever carried a free people through a long war. The clock is real. That is why the work at home must begin now, not after this or a greater danger is upon us.


I hold my position because the regime cannot be bargained into goodness, cannot be bombed into a new heart, and cannot be invaded at a price we are willing to pay. The program of nuclear armament can be held back while its rulers fear for the one thing they love – the worldwide expansion of Islam. Iran’s people are offered the one thing they lack, the Gospel, the knowledge and opportunity to leave a religion of submission under threat of the sword, and instead submit to the way of God who asks for our heart and love. If we sanctify our hearts as a nation, we can see this work through to its end – the establishment of His Kingdom on Earth as it is in Heaven.


I hold this as a workable plan, and remain open to a better argument.


## Areas of Uncertainty


 	- Whether the regime’s leaders are fundamentally deterrable cannot be proven in advance. Their conduct in 1988 and in this war suggests they prize the survival of their rule; their theology honors martyrdom, and some among them may prize it more. I hold that any treaty they sign will be made in bad faith, and I do not rest the plan on deterrence. The terms I propose are designed for exactly that: they do not depend on good faith, only on inspectors’ eyes and our resolve.

 	- No one outside Iran knows where its highly enriched uranium is or how much is recoverable. This is the great unknown, and the weakest point of the holding action. The figures themselves are disputed: the IAEA’s last accounting was about 440 kilograms; the U.S. envoy reports Iran’s negotiators claimed about 460; Iran denies the account. All three are interested parties to some degree.

 	- We cannot know in advance whether our intelligence can find a small hidden enrichment or weapons effort in time. Past programs were exposed late.

 	- Whether secure channels can bring out insider knowledge at any useful scale, without exposing the men who use them, is untested at the level this plan requires.

 	- The true range of Iran’s missiles is uncertain. The Diego Garcia launch rests on the testimony of American, British, and Israeli officials; Iran denies it, and neither missile reached its target.

 	- Every strike carries risk to bystanders that must be weighed honestly, one strike at a time.

 	- Economic pressure hits the vulnerable hardest. The leadership lives in luxury while the people suffer, yet allowing trade gives the mullahs money for bombs and missiles. Pressure must be aimed at the Guard and its financiers as precisely as we can manage, while keeping food and medicine flowing.

 	- Estimates of Iran’s Christian population vary widely, because believers there must hide their faith.

 	- Whether China and other importers will share the burden of Hormuz, or stop buying disguised Iranian oil once the blockade eases, is uncertain. So is how quickly bypass pipelines can be expanded and defended in wartime. Drilling and refining capacity must be authorized and implemented without bureaucratic delay.

 	- How quickly American hearts can turn, and whether that turning will carry into votes, budgets, and Congress’s patience, cannot be scheduled. Revivals in our history have sometimes changed the nation’s laws and sometimes stayed within the churches.

 	- How far the administration itself will hold its course after the Vice President’s break with the war is unknown.

 	- Historians of the Founding disagree about what treaties like the Kaskaskia agreement prove about the Establishment Clause’s original meaning.

 	- Casualty figures differ between the Iranian government, human-rights groups, and U.S. officials.

 	- As of this date, the new Congress’s stance on the war remains unknown.

# Winning the Long War with Iran — Outline and Summary (V.4, 14 November 2026)


A summary of Dr. Thomas’s position paper for the Sunday Fellowship and the mailing list. Every point made in the full paper is covered here in brief.


## The Thesis in One Paragraph


The war with Iran is righteous: it aims to free an oppressed people and to deny a terrorist regime the power of nuclear blackmail. But it will be won or lost first in America—in the hearts of the American people—and only then in the Gulf. Our military is strong; our will is in question, now even inside the administration. We cannot win it with blood and bullets alone. So the plan has three fronts. Abroad, we hold the line with the tools we have today, to keep Iran from building a bomb. At home, we fight for the nation’s heart, because only a people who have turned back to God will have the endurance to hold that line, and the moral standing to be heard. And in Iran, we carry the Gospel to the Persian people, the one message the regime cannot answer.


## I. Where We Stand


 	- The U.S. and Israel went to war with Iran on 28 February 2026, killing its Supreme Leader and much of its military command.

 	- Iran struck U.S. bases, choked the Strait of Hormuz, and tried to charge a toll for passage.

 	- A ceasefire and a preliminary agreement have failed; a U.S. naval blockade remains.

 	- More than 15 Americans dead, some 800 wounded; thousands of Iranians dead. Oil above $100; diesel above $6.

 	- The blockade has worked on one front: China bought 80–90% of Iran’s oil before the war, and that oil has almost stopped moving. The regime’s main income is cut off—for now.

 	- On 3 November, voters gave the Democrats both the House and the Senate. Tehran read this as Americans tiring of the war.

 	- The next morning the Vice President blamed the war for the losses and asked whether it was worth the Senate. The President agreed it cost votes, and said he would do it again.

 	- The point: our military is strong, but our will is in question—now inside the administration, not only among the voters—and the regime is betting on that.


## II. The Position in Brief


 	- The stand: the war is righteous—to free an oppressed people and to deny a terrorist regime the bomb—whatever our initial motives or Israel’s pressure.

 	- The step before the first step: win the war in America’s heart. The nation has turned its back on Christ; poverty, disease, addiction, divorce, abortion, gender confusion, and social unrest all have their root in that rebellion, and will begin to heal when we bow our knees to God and follow His ways.

 	- The cure: a nation turned back to God—speaking Christ in public without fear or apology, counting righteousness a national good, bearing long costs.

 	- But we need not wait: we act today—hold the blockade, destroy the nuclear program as it appears, refuse any truce that lets Iran rebuild, take away the oil weapon, carry the Gospel to Persia.

 	- The first step buys time; if we hold the pressure and keep finding their work, we can keep Iran from a bomb. Only changed hearts—theirs and ours—end the war.

 	- Three fronts: (1) halt the bomb; (2) fight the rebellion against God in America; (3) free Iran’s people from a false god.


## III. Why the Battle Begins at Home


 	- The Iran war is a spiritual problem with a physical symptom: an ungodly spirit rules Iran through its leaders, who clothe oppression in the robes of piety.

 	- We risk being judged by our own words. Before removing the speck from their eye, we must take the log from ours **(Matthew 7:3–5)**. Their scripture condemns our lust, greed, gluttony, and pride, so they dismiss the American Christian as a hypocrite.

 	- We have no moral standing unless we live the holiness we call them to. Our purity is our primary weapon against satanic strongholds.

 	- The spiritual weapons—will, patience, sacrifice for righteousness—are moral goods that should be taught openly in schools, argued in courts and legislatures, and used to guide the executive.

 	- Our own righteousness without God is *“a polluted garment”* **(Isaiah 64:6)**; we must repent, turn, and worship the True God. Then our witness will convict, and our testimony will convince.

 	- Sanctions and bombs are “weapons of the flesh”; they can destroy a building but not the belief that orders it rebuilt **(2 Corinthians 10:4)**.

 	- Whether America sustains the pressure depends on what families believe is worth sacrificing for.

 	- AI, engineered plagues, and the decline of Bible-carrying civilization are similar dangers. Iran is the nearest; waiting for a bigger danger means waiting until only a miracle will do.

 	- Scripture’s order: humble ourselves, pray, repent—then God heals the land **(2 Chronicles 7:14)**.


## IV. The Hard Question: Can This Regime Be Trusted?


 	- No. The mullahs will not give up the knowledge or the will to build a bomb—a North Korea with a more aggressive, expansionist theology.

 	- Hudna: a temporary truce made from weakness, broken when strength returns (Treaty of Hudaybiyyah, 628). Arafat compared Oslo to it in 1994.

 	- Taqiyya: Shia permission to conceal one’s beliefs under threat.

 	- Not all Muslims read these as licenses to deceive—but Iran’s rulers have given every reason to think they do.

 	- Their doctrine of world domination tells us what moves them: survival of their rule, to fight another day. Any truce must make that day impossible.

 	- Because no one can see into their hearts, our safety rests on preventing the bomb, not on hoping they always prefer survival to martyrdom.


## V. What History Teaches


 	- Japan, 1945: Surrendered before any invasion, after blockade, firebombing, two atomic bombs, and the Soviet declaration of war—against much of its general staff. The nation’s heart changed only afterward, under seven years of occupation.

 	- Iran, 1988: Khomeini accepted a ceasefire “worse than poison”—then Iran secretly built a nuclear program. A truce to rebuild.

 	- Iran, 1953 and 1979: America helped overthrow Iran’s elected prime minister and restore the Shah. When the people threw off his secular rule, the mullahs imposed a false god.

 	- We were wrong and should right the wrong: not by imposing godly government (it must be chosen to last), but by witnessing, showing the good fruit of our faith, and paying to repair the civilian infrastructure we damaged.

 	- The army turned in 1979: the Shah fell within hours of his ordinary, unradicalized army declaring itself neutral.

 	- North Korea: a nuclear tyranny can survive for decades; deterrence changes no hearts. And Iran’s creed is expansion, not only survival—what deters Pyongyang cannot be assumed to deter Tehran.

 	- The deeper weapons are spiritual—the armor of God **(Ephesians 6:14–17)**.


## VI. The Fork


 	- Limited aim (no bomb, open Hormuz, regime constrained): achievable with economic pressure and verified inspection—limited by Americans’ perseverance.

 	- Regime change by force: total defeat and occupation of a country of 90 million. If that is the aim, the President must ask Congress to declare war.

 	- What we are doing now is the worst of both.

 	- Critics are right that the present drift is a quagmire that lets Iran rebuild. The holding action differs in four ways: standing strikes, not occasional ones; one condition, total access; red lines voted in advance; uranium first. “A vise with one way out.”

 	- Count the cost; a king who cannot win should seek terms **(Luke 14:28–32)**.


## VII. The Objective in One Sentence


Iran shall not build a nuclear weapon or hold the world’s commerce hostage, and America will keep the pressure, the watch, and its witness to the Gospel until the Iranian people are free.


## VIII. Part One — The Line We Hold Abroad (What We Can Do Now)


**Press the war machine, spare the people.**


 	- Keep the blockade, defend shipping, answer every attack immediately.

 	- Sanction the Revolutionary Guard and the specific men who run or fund its weapons—not people who are merely wealthy or related to officials.

 	- Strike nuclear and missile sites when found; strike again when rebuilt.

 	- Never strike water, power, or food supplies. Admit and repair wrongful harm.


**Find the bomb before it is built — the weakest link.**


 	- About 440 kg of near-weapons-grade uranium (~10 bombs’ worth) is missing, much of it under rubble. The U.S. envoy says Iran’s negotiators claimed 460 kg, enough for 11; Iran denies it.

 	- Iran has hidden whole plants before (Natanz, 2002; Fordow, inside a mountain, 2009).

 	- Best hope: insiders reporting through secure channels; asylum for the few who can escape; above all, inspectors with total access.


**One condition, and only one.** Total, permanent access, and the uranium shipped out. “Let us in, let us dig out the uranium, and the bombs stop falling on your heads.” All other relief comes in verified steps, uranium first. “Distrust, and verify forever.”


**If they accept, keep our word** **(Joshua 9:15–20; Psalm 15:4)**.


**Congress should vote clear red lines.** We saw in Afghanistan in 2021—Bagram abandoned and seized by the Taliban within days—where “out on any terms” leads. Members who oppose the war should still say publicly whether Iran may move its uranium unchallenged.


**Make the bomb worthless—as a backstop, not the plan.** Prevention comes first, because no one can prove the regime is deterrable. If prevention fails: any Iranian nuclear use, or handoff to terrorists, ends the regime; extend the guarantee to Israel and the Gulf states.


**Take away the oil weapon.**


 	- No oil relief until the uranium is accounted for; keep sanctions on smuggled Iranian oil.

 	- Bypass pipelines help but are easy targets.

 	- Timeline: reserves and non-Gulf oil (0–3 years); new production (3–8 years); nuclear and molten-salt reactors (to 10 years); nuclear-powered transport and synthetic fuels (10–20 years).

 	- Fuel will cost more for years. Report progress honestly so the nation sees hope being fulfilled.


**China and Israel.** Nations that buy Iran’s smuggled oil should face consequences, including in trade. Israel will act on its own clock; this is our war too.


## IX. Part Two — The Gospel to Persia


**Speak Christ openly, and let Persians carry the word.**


 	- Iran’s underground church is among the world’s fastest-growing—but still small among 90 million, and fragile. Left to its own strength, it could be crushed.

 	- Where Christians have answered Islamic conquest with silence, Islam has spread. What must change is not the Gospel but the strength behind it: the full voice and resources of a nation. The Gospel compels no one.

 	- The President speaks: openly naming Christ as Iran’s hope for prosperity, peace, and happiness in society, life, business, and international relations.

 	- The nation builds the roads: satellite internet, anti-censorship tools, Radio Free America, Scripture by balloon.

 	- Persian believers carry the words: the strongest witness is those in the trust circle: family and friends.


**How to speak to a Muslim heart.** Honor Islam’s turn from idols to one God; note the change at Medina; offer the God who asks for a clean heart and makes it clean **(Matthew 11:28–30)**; judge by fruit **(Matthew 7:16)**.


**Speak to the conscripts.** Iran’s regular army is largely made up of draftees, not the hand-picked Guard. In 1979, the army let a regime fall.


## X. Part Three — The Battle at Home


**Recover the freedom to speak.**


 	- Since Everson (1947), Americans have been taught the Constitution forbids government to speak of Christ. It does not.

 	- The First Amendment forbids a national church and coerced conscience, not public faith. Congress opened with paid chaplains; in 1803 the Senate ratified a treaty paying for a priest and a church for the (already Catholic) Kaskaskia nation.

 	- Kennedy v. Bremerton (2022) now reads the clause through founding-era history.

 	- The real prison is fear. This campaign aims to thaw that chill and replace the stigma of the Bible with a love of holiness.


**Become the nation we are calling them to be.** We cannot win Iranians to a faith we do not live. *“Righteousness exalts a nation”* **(Proverbs 14:34)**. Energy, Righteousness, and Accountability to God—the E, R, and A of The New ERA Vision—will defeat the mullahs over a generation.


**How we will know it is working.** At home: letters, open witness, red-line votes. In the Gulf: uranium found, inspectors in, Hormuz open. In Iran: secure channels used, churches growing, conscripts refusing to serve. If nothing moves at home, the line abroad shrinks.


## XI. Why Now, and the Campaign


 	- Six-dollar diesel ties ordinary life to Iran, Islam, tyranny, and uranium.

 	- The answer is the platform carried since 2004: Energy, Righteousness, and Accountability before God and man.

 	- The campaign: Dr. Thomas presents the plan on video in his own name; citizens send it to Congress, the President, and the Secretary of War (“Look in your inbox”), and to their own circles. “If the followers lead, the leaders will follow.”

 	- The message carries risk; he has counted the cost and speaks in his own name.


## XII. Why Not Use Nuclear Weapons?


 	- It might force surrender, but killing hundreds of thousands who never chose their rulers fails every moral test, and teaches every tyrant that only his own bomb is safe.

 	- Even in Japan, the bombs ended the fighting; occupation changed the nation. We will not occupy Iran.


## XIII. The Moral Test


 	- **Is it righteous?** Restraining evildoers is civil authority’s God-given work **(Romans 13:4)**. Targeting people for wealth or family, or bombing civilian lifelines, is not.

 	- **Is it loving?** Love restrains the predator, spares the prisoner, does not withhold the Gospel, and tells its own people the truth—including the call to repent **(Leviticus 19:17)**.

 	- **Would I want it done to me?** An Iranian father would want his rulers weakened, a safe way to tell the truth, his water and light spared, and his own countrymen to bring the Gospel. An American would want a leader honest enough to call him to repentance.


## XIV. The Options and the Choice


 	- A full war declared by Congress, with its cost counted.

 	- A truce on terms that make rebuilding impossible (unlikely to be accepted).

 	- The long war — hold the line abroad with standing strikes, carry the Gospel to Persia, fight for the nation’s heart at home.


Dr. Thomas chooses the third, with the door to the second always open and the first’s red lines voted in advance. The battle at home underpins the plan.


## XV. Why the Concern Remains Real


 	- The uranium is missing; Iran has hidden plants and lied before.

 	- It has new reasons to build a bomb and talks of leaving the nonproliferation treaty.

 	- Its missiles reach farther than it admitted: U.S. and British officials say it fired at Diego Garcia, ~4,000 km away, twice its claimed limit (neither missile hit; Iran denies firing).

 	- It kills its own people, sponsors terror, has plotted murder on U.S. soil, and has an apocalyptic strand in its ideology.

 	- It believes time is on its side, and reads our election and the Vice President’s words as proof.


## XVI. The Strongest Objections — and the Answers


 	- **Hawks:** Strike hard now; the drift is a quagmire; a nuclear Iran can’t be deterred because its rulers seek martyrdom. Answer: Escalation has failed twice; bombs can’t dig uranium out of rubble; a campaign Americans won’t sustain is a gesture. The drift is a quagmire—so the holding action is built differently. On deterrence: the rulers have chosen survival at every hard test, but the plan doesn’t bet on it; it rests on prevention.

 	- **Restraint:** End the war; the Gospel looks like a crusade; even the Vice President asks if it was worth the Senate. Answer: Every truce has let Iran rearm; keeping a bomb from this regime is worth more than a majority; the Gospel is offered, carried by Persian believers, and requested by Iranian Christians.

 	- **Skeptics:** A change of heart can’t be scheduled. Answer: The near-term defense doesn’t depend on revival; its endurance does—so the work at home starts now.

 	- **Muslim objectors:** Many Muslims live peaceably. Answer: Agreed—but Jesus is Lord, and the regime’s fruit is plain.


The position is held open to a better argument.

Whether Iran’s leaders can be deterred (the plan does not depend on it); where the uranium is, and how much (IAEA, U.S. envoy, and Iran disagree); whether hidden work can be found in time; whether insiders can report safely; the true range of Iran’s missiles; risk to bystanders; harm of sanctions to ordinary people; the size of Iran’s church; whether China will stop buying smuggled oil; how fast pipelines can be expanded; how quickly American hearts will turn and whether that will reach Congress; how far the administration will hold its course after the Vice President’s break; scholarly disagreement over the Kaskaskia treaty; conflicting casualty figures; the new Congress’s stance.

Mainstream news and research, commentary from the right (Spivak, RealClearPolitics), IAEA reports, tanker-tracking data, U.S. and British officials’ accounts of Diego Garcia and Iran’s denial, the envoy’s account of pre-war talks and Iran’s denial, standard histories, church surveys, the 1803 treaty and its critics, and the two Supreme Court cases. Government statements from all sides are treated as interested testimony. Reporting from inside Iran is limited by censorship, and Western reporting leans on government sources; either could hide evidence that would change the picture.
