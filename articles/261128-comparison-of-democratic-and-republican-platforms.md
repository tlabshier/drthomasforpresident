# 261128 - Comparison of Democratic and Republican Platforms

# Judging the Parties by What They Would Build


### A Plank-by-Plank Reading of the 2024 Platforms on Life, God, the Family, and Charity

Thomas Lee Abshier, ND | 28 November 2026

In an earlier essay, "The Enemy of My Enemy," I argued that a movement must be judged by what it would build, not by whom it opposes. That principle applies to political parties. A party's platform is its own statement of what it would build if given power. It is the fairest place to judge a party, because it is the party's own words, adopted by its own delegates.

This paper applies that test to the two major parties' 2024 platforms, the most recent ones adopted. (Each party writes a new platform every four years, in presidential election years.) I look at four questions that I believe matter more than any others for a nation's moral health: the protection of human life, the place of God in public life, the structure of sex, marriage, and family, and the difference between charity and compulsion.

I will state my conclusion at the outset. On all four questions, I find the Democratic platform committed to positions I believe are opposed to God's standards, to be enforced by the power of government. I find the Republican platform better on all four, but retreating on two of them. I will show my reasons plank by plank, in the platforms' own terms, so that a reader who disagrees with me can see exactly where and why.

A companion paper, "Come Out of Her," draws the practical conclusion for Christians who call themselves Democrats. This paper lays the foundation it stands on.

## What the Party Is and Is Not

**I do not call the Democratic Party a Communist party.** Its platform calls for expanded government programs, such as a higher federal minimum wage and an expanded Child Tax Credit, within a market economy. It does not call for the state to own the means of production, and its leaders have publicly rejected the agenda of the Democratic Socialists of America. But direction matters more than labels. The DSA program calls for democratic social ownership of major industries; the difference between voting the chains on and seizing power by revolution is a difference of method, not of destination. And the party's voters are moving that way: Gallup reports that Democrats now rate socialism and free enterprise equally, at 65% positive each. I believe the party's direction expands the state's control over economic and moral life, and I will argue that.

**I do not call it a Godless party.** According to the Public Religion Research Institute, the Democratic Party remains majority religious and majority Christian. Many of its members are believers, and many vote as they do because of their faith.

**But a name is an endorsement.** A man who registers, campaigns, or introduces himself as a Democrat lends his name and his vote count to the whole platform, not only to the parts he likes. So while I do not judge the faith of the people in the party, I do hold them accountable for the platform their name strengthens. That is a description of what the platform says, not a verdict on any person's soul. God alone judges souls; my task is to describe what each party has written and to warn.

## Life

**What the Democratic platform says.** The 2024 Democratic platform pledges: *"With a Democratic Congress, we will pass national legislation to make Roe the law of the land again… We will repeal the Hyde Amendment."* The Hyde Amendment is the long-standing rule that bars federal tax dollars from paying for abortion. The platform also promises to protect access to abortion pills.

**What the Republican platform says.** The 2024 Republican platform states that the Fourteenth Amendment guarantees that no person can be denied life or liberty without due process, and that the states are therefore free to pass laws protecting those rights. It opposes late-term abortion and supports access to birth control and IVF. For the first time in four decades, it does not call for a national law protecting unborn life, and it removed the explicit "right to life" plank. The 2016 platform had affirmed that "the unborn child has a fundamental right to life which cannot be infringed." Pro-life leaders split over the change: some called it a betrayal, while others counted the Fourteenth Amendment reference as a gain.

**A shared cause.** The Satanic Temple, a federally recognized tax-exempt religious group, has created what it calls a "Satanic Abortion Ritual" and has sued Texas, Indiana, and Idaho, claiming abortion restrictions burden its rite. The group is distinct from the Church of Satan and says it does not believe in a literal devil. I do not claim the Democratic Party is a front for it. I claim what the documents show: a self-declared Satanist body treats abortion as a sacrament, and the Democratic platform fights for the same practice.

**My judgment.** I hold that a human life begins at conception and is known by God before it is born: *"Before I formed thee in the belly I knew thee; and before thou camest forth out of the womb I sanctified thee"* **(Jeremiah 1:5)**. The ancient name for killing children for the parents' advantage was Molech: *"And thou shalt not let any of thy seed pass through the fire to Molech, neither shalt thou profane the name of thy God: I am the LORD"* **(Leviticus 18:21)**. Israel's children were sacrificed for prosperity and favor; ours are sacrificed for convenience and career.

On that understanding, the Democratic platform proposes to establish the legal killing of unborn children as national law and to make every taxpayer help pay for it. That fails every test I apply. It is not righteous, because it takes innocent life. It is not loving, because it abandons the most defenseless neighbor. And no one would want it done to himself; every person reading this was once the unborn child whose life the law would leave unprotected.

The Republican platform does not propose this, but it has stepped back from defending the unborn at the national level. I count that as a retreat, and I say so, because a principle that excuses my own side is no principle.

## God and Religious Liberty

**What the Democratic platform says.** The 2024 Democratic platform pledges to protect the free exercise of religion and to maintain the separation of church and state. It promises to "continue to honor both religious freedom and other civil rights, not put them at war with one another." Reporting on the draft found that it contained no mention of "God," "Lord," or "Almighty." In 2012, the party removed God from its platform and then restored the reference by a contested voice vote on the convention floor. The 2020 platform referred to "God-given potential."

**What the Republican platform says.** The 2024 Republican platform pledges to champion the "First Amendment Right to Pray and Read the Bible in school."

**My judgment.** The absence of a word is not the heart of the matter. Platforms are policy documents, and a party can honor God without naming Him, or name Him without honoring Him. A voice vote that grudgingly restores God's name is not as bold a confession as it appears.

The heart of the matter is what happens when religious conscience and newly declared civil rights conflict. The Democratic platform's promise not to put religious freedom and "other civil rights" at war has been read by religion reporters and Christian critics alike as a commitment not to let religious conscience override anti-discrimination law. Combined with its support for the Equality Act, discussed below, that means a Christian school, hospital, adoption agency, or business could be compelled by law to act against its faith. I regard that as the government taking up the sword against conscience.

The First Amendment forbids Congress to make a law "respecting an establishment of religion." It was written to keep the federal government from imposing a national church, not to remove the Bible and prayer from public life. The founders understood rights as gifts of the Creator, not grants of the state. A government that treats conscience as subordinate to its own declarations has placed itself above the source of the rights it claims to protect: *"Righteousness exalteth a nation: but sin is a reproach to any people"* **(Proverbs 14:34)**.

## Sex, Marriage, and the Family

**What the Democratic platform says.** The 2024 Democratic platform pledges to pass the Equality Act, which would add sexual orientation and gender identity to federal anti-discrimination law in housing, public accommodations, education, credit, jury service, and federal programs. It pledges to protect "marriage equality in federal law." It states that Democrats will "vigorously oppose state and federal bans on gender-affirming health care" and calls for insurance coverage of "all medically necessary care for gender transition."

**What the Republican platform says.** The 2024 Republican platform pledges to "keep men out of women's sports, ban Taxpayer funding for sex change surgeries, and stop Taxpayer-funded Schools from promoting gender transition," and to restore protections for women and girls under Title IX. It promises to restore parental rights and to support universal school choice, including education savings accounts for homeschooling. It promises to "promote a culture that values the sanctity of marriage." But it dropped long-standing language defending marriage as the union of one man and one woman, and its condemnation of the Supreme Court's *Obergefell* decision. Social conservatives fought to keep that language and lost.

**My judgment.** I hold, with historic Christian teaching, that sexual union belongs within the marriage of one man and one woman, and that conduct outside it calls for repentance rather than celebration. I hold that the body is God's design, not raw material to be remade, and that a child's body is not a field for experiment. I hold that parents, not the state, bear primary responsibility for raising their children. On those convictions, the Democratic platform proposes to use the full power of federal law to promote and enforce a view of sex and family that I believe is contrary to God's design, and to compel dissenters to participate.

I am not judging any person's worth before God. Every person bears His image, and every person, including me, needs His mercy. I am judging what government would promote and enforce. A government may protect every citizen from violence and abuse; it may not compel citizens to affirm what their consciences reject, and it should not use public money to promote what it ought to discourage.

The Republican platform resists the Democratic platform on gender and on parental rights. On marriage, it has quietly withdrawn. I count that as a second retreat.

## Charity and the Reach of Government

**What the platforms say.** The 2024 Democratic platform calls for expanding federal programs, among them a higher federal minimum wage and an expanded Child Tax Credit, paid for by higher taxes on the wealthy and on corporations. The 2024 Republican platform promises not to cut Social Security or Medicare, a break from the party's long-held stance on entitlement reform, and otherwise emphasizes tax cuts, deregulation, and work. Both parties now defend the large programs most Americans have paid into. The difference is direction: one party seeks to widen what government provides; the other, at least on paper, to narrow it.

**My judgment.** My objection is not to taxes. Scripture is clear that government is owed its due: *"For for this cause pay ye tribute also: for they are God's ministers, attending continually upon this very thing. Render therefore to all their dues: tribute to whom tribute is due; custom to whom custom; fear to whom fear; honour to whom honour"* **(Romans 13:6–7)**. A government that keeps order, defends the border, and punishes evil has a rightful claim on my money.

My objection is to compelled giving presented as compassion. Charity is love, and love must be free: *"Every man according as he purposeth in his heart, so let him give; not grudgingly, or of necessity: for God loveth a cheerful giver"* **(2 Corinthians 9:7)**. When the state takes from one man to give to another who could provide for himself, no one has been charitable. The giver was coerced, and the receiver learns to expect rather than to serve.

God's law did command care for the poor. The gleaning law told the farmer to leave the corners of his field *"for the poor and stranger"* **(Leviticus 19:9–10)**, and the third-year tithe fed the widow and the fatherless **(Deuteronomy 14:28–29)**. But those were duties before God, carried out by the giver's own hand, and the text attaches no civil penalty to the gleaning law. The poor gathered with their own labor. That pattern honors both the giver's freedom and the receiver's dignity.

Our welfare system has too often done the reverse. Aid to Families with Dependent Children, created by the 1935 Social Security Act, tied a mother's aid to the absence of a father, and in 1968 the Supreme Court, in *King v. Smith*, struck down state rules that cut off aid when a man lived in the home. Charles Murray argued in *Losing Ground* (1984) that welfare's incentives drove much of the collapse of the family among the poor; other economists found the effect smaller. I side with Murray: a system that pays a woman more when the father is gone teaches a community that fathers are optional.

Most food-stamp recipients are children, the elderly, or the disabled, and many working-age adults on these programs work low-wage jobs. The problem I see is narrower, and in my years of practice I saw it often: able-bodied adults who have settled into being cared for. A young woman with cerebral palsy, who can barely speak and must be pushed to her post, hands out samples at our local warehouse store every day and brightens everyone's morning. A system that pays people not to serve robs them of the dignity she has. *"For even when we were with you, this we commanded you, that if any would not work, neither should he eat"* **(2 Thessalonians 3:10)**.

## The Long March and the Long Game

I have watched the culture move a great distance from the moral and legal world of the 1950s in which I was born. That movement has not been accidental. Incrementalism is a deliberate strategy: propose a large change, accept a smaller one, and advance again the next session. The Fabian socialists of the late nineteenth century made gradualism their method, and a phrase associated with the German student left of the late 1960s, "the long march through the institutions," describes the same patience applied to universities, media, courts, and churches.

The same patience is now aimed at the rules by which power is won and kept:


 	- **Who may vote.** The SAVE America Act, requiring proof of citizenship to register, passed the House 218–213 in February 2026. In the Senate every Democrat voted against it, joined by four Republicans, and it died.

 	- **How a president is chosen.** Eighteen states and the District of Columbia, all led by Democrats, have joined the National Popular Vote compact, pledging 222 of the 270 electoral votes it needs. The Electoral College makes a president build support across many states; the compact would let the most populous states and largest cities decide.

 	- **What the Constitution means.** The jurisprudence the party's justices favor treats the Constitution as "living," letting nine judges update its meaning to fit the culture without the amendment process the Constitution itself requires.


Each can be defended on its own terms, and Democrats defend them. Taken together, they point one way: more power at the center and fewer limits on how it is gained.

But incrementalism is a tool, not a creed, and it works for whoever uses it with patience and conviction. The clearest recent proof is the pro-life movement. For nearly fifty years after *Roe v. Wade*, pro-life Americans refused to treat abortion as settled. They passed limited laws, built institutions, changed hearts, and shaped the courts, one step at a time, until the Supreme Court overturned *Roe* in *Dobbs v. Jackson Women's Health Organization* on 24 June 2022.

That is a hopeful lesson. A trend that took decades to build can be reversed by people who are steady, principled, and unwilling to quit. It is also a warning. The Republican retreats of 2024 show that the drift can move a party that claims to resist it. The remedy is not a better slogan. It is leadership by voice, changing hearts first, so that the law can follow.

## Applying the Test to My Own Side

In "The Enemy of My Enemy," I argued that judging a movement by its principles binds my own side as tightly as the other. If a name is an endorsement, that rule binds every registered Republican too.

In my judgment, the Republican platform is better than the Democratic platform on all four questions I have examined, but it is not faithful on all of them. It has stepped back from defending unborn life at the national level, and it has stepped back from defending marriage. The Republican platform does not promote abortion and same-sex marriage the way the Democratic platform does, but it has stopped opposing them at the national level. I regard that as a bargain: silence on sin in exchange for votes. A bargain that trades righteousness for earthly gain always turns bad eventually, and a victory won that way may prove a Pyrrhic one, producing little good fruit.

There is also a long record of Republican leaders quietly allowing what they publicly oppose. In 2014 Senator Ted Cruz described leadership's plan on a debt-ceiling vote: let the measure pass on 51 votes so every Republican could vote no and tell his constituents he opposed it. In 2015 he counted nineteen votes in ten months that passed with a majority of Democrats and a minority of Republicans. A few defections, timed well, are enough to let the drift continue while each member's record stays clean.

The prophet Amos is my model here. He began by indicting the surrounding nations, Damascus, Gaza, Tyre, Edom, Ammon, and Moab. Then he turned on God's own people: *"For three transgressions of Judah, and for four, I will not turn away the punishment thereof; because they have despised the law of the LORD"* **(Amos 2:4)**. Then he turned on Israel, at greater length still. *"For the time is come that judgment must begin at the house of God"* **(1 Peter 4:17)**.

So I say to Republicans: restore the plank for life and the plank for marriage. A voter who cares about these questions should say so to the party he usually supports, not only to the one he opposes.

## The Strongest Case Against My Reading

A Christian who votes Democratic would answer me with arguments.

**"The prophets demanded justice for the poor."** They did. Amos thundered *"But let judgment run down as waters, and righteousness as a mighty stream"* **(Amos 5:24)**, and Jesus said *"Inasmuch as ye have done it unto one of the least of these my brethren, ye have done it unto me"* **(Matthew 25:40)**. My answer: the command is to the believer's own hands and purse. Nothing in it licenses taking from my neighbor by force and calling it my righteousness. And the least of these includes the child in the womb.

**"Many Democrats oppose abortion."** True. In Pew's January 2026 survey, 84% of Democrats said abortion should be legal in all or most cases, which means roughly one in six did not. About half of Democrats who oppose abortion are Black or Hispanic, and they are far more religious than other Democrats. My answer: your conscience is right, and your name is in the wrong place. The platform counts your membership as support.

**"Voting is choosing among imperfect options, not endorsing every plank."** That is a fair point about a ballot, which is one choice between two names. A party label is different: it is a standing declaration of allegiance that the party counts, cites, and spends.

**"Platforms are aspirational; few voters read them, and conduct matters more than words."** All of that is partly true. But platforms show direction, and over time a party's officeholders tend to move toward them.

**"I register as a Democrat to vote in its primaries."** In many states that is the only way to vote in a party's primary. If that is your reason, say so openly: "I am a registered Democrat to elect Godly men as candidates." That is honest, it is legal, and I welcome it. What I will not ask anyone to do is swear to something false. The contest for a nation's soul is won by open truth: *"But have renounced the hidden things of dishonesty, not walking in craftiness… but by manifestation of the truth commending ourselves to every man's conscience in the sight of God"* **(2 Corinthians 4:2)**.

## Why This Concern Remains Serious

The concern remains serious for four reasons.


 	- **Platforms show direction.** They tell us what a party's most committed members want. The Democratic platform has grown more explicit with each cycle, from "safe, legal, and rare" to taxpayer-funded abortion written into national law. Movement in one direction, year after year, is the strongest evidence that a spirit is driving it, not merely a set of policy preferences.

 	- **These planks are proposals for law.** Law is enforced by the sword of government, and what government enforces, it teaches. A generation raised under a law learns to regard it as right.

 	- **The questions are not secondary.** Whether the unborn are protected, whether conscience is free, whether the family is honored, and whether charity remains love rather than compulsion are questions on which, I believe, the health of a nation rests.

 	- **Silence is not neutral.** *"But if the watchman see the sword come, and blow not the trumpet, and the people be not warned… his blood will I require at the watchman's hand"* **(Ezekiel 33:6)**. My neighbors are walking toward judgment.


I stand on the side of protecting unborn life, freedom of conscience, the family as God designed it, and charity freely given. On those questions I find the Democratic platform opposed and the Republican platform wavering. The areas of real uncertainty are how faithfully each party's officeholders follow its platform, how much of each platform reflects its voters rather than its activists, and how courts would apply laws like the Equality Act in practice.

## What I Am Asking

To every Christian who calls himself a Democrat: read your party's platform, and ask whether you can put your name to it before God. If you cannot, take your name off it, or stay only to vote in the primaries and say openly why. To every Republican: tell your party to restore the planks for life and marriage. To all of us: give with our own hands, and claim the promise: *"If my people, which are called by my name, shall humble themselves, and pray, and seek my face, and turn from their wicked ways; then will I hear from heaven, and will forgive their sin, and will heal their land"* **(2 Chronicles 7:14)**.

I am not asking anyone to hate his neighbor. *"For we wrestle not against flesh and blood, but against principalities, against powers, against the rulers of the darkness of this world, against spiritual wickedness in high places"* **(Ephesians 6:12)**. I am not judging anyone's soul; God will weigh what each man knew and what he did with it. My task is the watchman's, to make sure no one can say he was not told.

I hold my position open to a better argument. But I believe a nation is judged by what it chooses to protect, and that our choices on these questions will shape what we become.
