# 261105 - 2026 Betrayal by the Establishment Republicans

# Sabotage, Surrender, or Both?


### An answer to Scott Lively’s postmortem on the 2026 midterms


Thomas Lee Abshier, ND — 5 November 2026


*Occasioned by Scott Lively, [“’26 Election Postmortem: Revenge of the Purple Uniparty,”](https://claude.ai/chat/LINK-TO-LIVELY-ARTICLE) published on his Substack on 5 November 2026. Pastor Lively has been in the front lines of Christian political engagement for thirty years, including two campaigns for Governor of Massachusetts. He argues that the midterm loss was engineered from inside the Republican Party. That charge deserves a serious answer.*


---


## Plain language summary


Two days ago, the Democrats won both houses of Congress. Scott Lively argues that ordinary voters did not reject the Trump agenda. He says the loss was the work of the “Purple Uniparty,” a globalist class that runs the top of both parties, and that establishment Republicans deliberately sabotaged their own party to hurt the President. He points to Senator John Cornyn congratulating the Democrat who won his Texas seat before the race was called, to Majority Leader John Thune refusing to set aside the filibuster to pass the SAVE America Act, and to a media campaign that blamed Israel for the Iran war and turned part of the conservative base against Trump. Behind it all, he sees George Soros’s method of steering events by seeding false narratives.


This perspective adds granularity to my answer to Steven Yates which I wrote yesterday. In it I said that we did not have a Republican majority; we had a majority named Republican that too often legislated like Democrats. The record on the SAVE America Act proves it. Blaming Israel for a decision made by an American President is wrong, and the hatred of the Jews that it feeds is evil. A governing class runs across both parties.


I would soften Pastor Lively’s declaration of these men as (“evil-hearted”), only because we cannot know a man’s heart. I can see such a plan come from an evil heart. I see consistency with a hypothesis, and I let God judge. He claims a coordinated plan, but the record only shows acts. Again, the appearance is only of conspiracy which implies intent and adjudicated/evidence of collusion. On this point I do not have enough data to do more than postulate an actual meeting of men who coordinated, colluded and conspired to commit the documented acts. The problem with documenting conspiracy, is that such collusion is inherently clandestine, so the absence of evidence is not evidence of absense. Having said that, I have no difficulty in declaring the appearance of conspiracy.


Having quibbled enough about words, I believe the acts Dr. Lively identifies are enough to condemn. He also leaves out the President’s own decisions: the war was begun without a vote of Congress, and voters punished that.


On another point, he compares the voters who left Trump to mothers who kill their children. This is true in the following sense: 1) if Trump is committed to legislating for good, but 2) had not yet done it because the Senate was controlled by RINOs (Democrats claiming to be Republicans), and 3) taking out his anger against Trump by voting for men who are committed to killing babies in the womb, then 4) this is almost the literal effect of voting for Democrats. I think his analogy is remarkable apt.


The remedy is not continue vote for men who stand for Godliness, and vote out those who have shown themselves to be turncoats, traitors, haters of God, and allies in the fight against God’s way and law. The remedy for Trump’s inaction in legislating his promises into action was to judge every House and Senate candidate in the midterm by his votes. Our job, as voters, as contributors to the national conversation, was to continue to lobby that Trump and the Senate/House write those promises into law. The problem was the RINOs who blocked legislation, not in his promise. To the extent Trump denied he made promises, like, “No  new Mideast Wars.” and “Any one who supports pursuing Epstein is not a friend of mine, and I don’t want his support.” Trump is fully culpable for breaking faith.


What was need is a president who stands behind God’s way, takes stands for God’s way in legislative, judicial, and executive action. We can see from the vote,  people choosing Democrats when the Republicans and Trump did not fulfill their promist, is a people without a moral center. Thus, voting is a symptom of a deeper problem. The remedy is to turn the hearts of the people back to God, so that they would never under any circumstance vote for a man who stands for legislating directly against God’s way.

## PART ONE: What Pastor Lively claims


His argument has five parts.


- **The loss was an inside job.** The midterm wipeout was not a spontaneous verdict of ordinary voters but the latest operation of a bipartisan elite that has held the upper decks of both parties since the first Bush administration and has never accepted the populist interruption that began in 2016.

- **John Cornyn is the emblem.** Senator Cornyn, who lost his primary to Ken Paxton, congratulated the Democrat James Talarico before the Texas race was called and offered to help with his transition. Pastor Lively reads this as gloating by a man who had undermined the Trump-endorsed nominee all year.

- **John Thune is the co-conspirator.** The Majority Leader treated the filibuster as untouchable and told the White House the votes for the SAVE America Act weren’t there, protecting the status quo both wings of the Uniparty prefer.

- **Israel was made the scapegoat.** The Iran war was the largest single cause of MAGA defections, but what drove them was a narrative that cast the war as Israel’s war rather than a necessary confrontation with what he calls the “Islamo-Marxist axis.” This activated antisemitism on both the right and the left.

- **Soros wrote the playbook.** George Soros’s theory of reflexivity, set out in *The Alchemy of Finance* (1987), holds that biased perceptions reshape the reality they describe. Pastor Lively argues that the same method, applied politically, produced the “Purple Revolution” of 2016 to 2020, the “2020 coup,” and now the 2026 result.


---


## PART TWO: Where he is right


**The Senate record on the SAVE America Act is damning.** The House passed the bill with every Republican vote. In the Senate, which Republicans controlled 53 to 47, it never passed. When Republicans tried to attach it to a filibuster-proof budget package, Senators Susan Collins, Lisa Murkowski, Mitch McConnell, and Thom Tillis voted with every Democrat to block it, and the amendment failed 48 to 50. They did it more than once. Senator Thune, for his part, said publicly that Republicans were not going to get rid of the legislative filibuster, and he declined to hold the Senate in session through August to force the issue. Proof of citizenship to vote in federal elections is supported by a large majority of Americans. A party that cannot pass it while holding the House, the Senate, and the White House has not been defeated by its opponents. It has declined to use the power it was given.


**The filibuster is sacred only when the people’s priorities are at stake.** Pastor Lively is right to call it a sacred cow, and the history shows why. The Senate abolished the filibuster for most nominations in 2013 and for Supreme Court nominations in 2017. In 2025, the current Republican majority changed the rules again so that it could confirm nominees in batches. When the governing class wants something, the rules bend. When the voters want proof of citizenship at the ballot box, the rules become holy. That is not principle. It is preference wearing the robes of principle.


**The Cornyn post happened.** On election night, before the Associated Press had called the race, Senator Cornyn posted that “the voters have spoken,” congratulated Mr. Talarico on his election, and offered to help with his transition. Senator Cornyn had pledged at his concession in May to support the Republican ticket, and conservative critics say he did little to keep that pledge. A man who could not find time to campaign for his party’s nominee but found time to welcome his opponent before the votes were counted has told us where his heart was.


**Blaming Israel for an American decision is wrong.** The war with Iran was launched on 28 February by the United States alongside Israel. The American President made the decision to go to war. The American Congress decided not to vote on it. Israel did neither. Yet a poll taken in March found that a majority of voters, and even a plurality of Republicans, believed the war benefited Israel more than America. Whatever one thinks of the war, assigning responsibility for an American choice to a foreign ally lets our own leaders escape accountability. And where that accusation becomes hatred of the Jewish people, it is not a political opinion. It is an evil that has been used against the Jews in every century, and Christians should have no part in it.


**Soros is a fair subject of concern.** The theory of reflexivity is in his book, and he has said plainly that he seeks to shape events, not merely to profit from them. His foundations and political committees have spent enormous sums to advance their causes in America and abroad, and some of the groups behind the “color revolutions” of Eastern Europe received his money. A man who funds both the narrative and the movement that acts on it should expect to be watched.

## PART THREE: Where he overstates


*He that is first in his own cause seemeth just; but his neighbour cometh and searcheth him* **(Proverbs 18:17)**. I want to search Pastor Lively’s case as a friend would, because a case that cannot survive a friend’s search will not survive an opponent’s.


**He claims to know hearts, and he claims a plan he does not show.** His subtitle calls these men “evil-hearted” and says they “deliberately” sabotaged their party to hurt Trump. His text calls their acts “coordinated.” But the evidence he offers is of acts, not of coordination or motive. And there are simpler explanations for each act. Senator Thune has defended the 60-vote rule consistently since March, on the stated ground that Republicans will want it back when they are in the minority. Senator Collins represents Maine, which flipped to the Democrats on Tuesday. Senator Tillis announced his retirement and has opposed Trump on several fronts. Senator McConnell has defended the filibuster for his whole career. Senator Cornyn lost his seat after the President endorsed his opponent a week before the runoff, and days later he posted the fable of the scorpion that stings the frog carrying it across the river. That is the bitterness of a wounded man, which is ordinary, sinful, and human. It is not proof of a conspiracy.


*Judge not according to the appearance, but judge righteous judgment* **(John 7:24)**. Righteous judgment judges acts by evidence and leaves the heart to God. I make the same judgment of these senators that I made of the Democratic Party yesterday: I judge the stand, not the soul. And the acts are enough. Whether four senators blocked the SAVE America Act by plan, by principle, by fear of their voters, or by resentment of the President, the effect on our elections is the same, and the remedy is the same: replace them with men who will vote as they promised. We do not need to prove a cabal to justify that. Asserting a cabal we cannot prove gives every opponent an easy way to dismiss the part of the argument that is true.


**Senator Cornyn did not decide Texas.** Mr. Talarico won by more than a million votes in one tally. Mr. Paxton himself conceded and said he would pray for him. Senator Ted Cruz, no friend of the establishment, called Mr. Talarico to congratulate him and called the results a “blue tsunami.” The words of Senator Cornyn’s post are the words of an ordinary concession; it is the timing and the contrast with his treatment of Mr. Paxton that offend. Reading “gloating triumphalism” into the text goes beyond the text. Pastor Lively also leaves out the other half of the Texas story. Senate Republicans spent millions to stop Mr. Paxton in the primary because they believed his legal and personal scandals would put the seat at risk. The President chose him anyway. Perhaps the establishment’s warning was self-serving. But the warning came true, and an honest postmortem must say so.


**He removes the President from his own story.** Pastor Lively agrees that the Iran war was the largest single factor in the loss. But the war was not begun by Thune or Cornyn. It was begun by the President, without the authorization of Congress that Article I requires, and it has lasted eight months instead of the weeks that were promised. By October, nearly seven in ten Americans told pollsters the war had not been worth fighting, and voters who opposed it favored Democratic candidates by a wide margin. That is not a narrative seeded by Soros. It is a judgment made by citizens who paid for the war at the pump and the grocery store and were never asked through their representatives. I hold that Iran is a standing threat, and I have set out my plan to win the long war. But I have also said since the summer that the decision belongs to Congress. A movement that cannot admit its own leader’s errors cannot correct them.


**His language about the voters is out of proportion.** He likens the conservatives who abandoned Trump over the war to women who kill their own children because they hate the father. I understand the point he is reaching for: that a voter can destroy what he loves to punish someone he resents. But the comparison is grossly out of proportion. A voter who cast a ballot in protest of a war begun without a vote of Congress has committed no crime and has some constitutional reason on his side. Exaggeration is a form of lying by proportion, and contempt is not persuasion. *A soft answer turneth away wrath: but grievous words stir up anger* **(Proverbs 15:1)**. The same holds for the nickname he gives Mr. Talarico. I criticized Steven Yates yesterday for name-calling cabinet officers, and I apply the same standard here. Mr. Talarico’s positions on abortion and sexuality defy God’s law, and I will say so plainly. The mocking name adds nothing to that case.


**He states as settled what he has not shown.** He refers to “the 2020 coup” and the “installation” of the Biden administration as established fact. I will not try to settle the question of 2020 in this paper. My point is narrower: his argument about 2026 does not need it, and asserting it without evidence asks the reader to accept the weakest claim in order to reach the strongest. The same caution applies to a commenter on his post who suggests that fraud may explain this year’s result. The President has accepted the result, the margins were very large, and I know of no firsthand report of fraud on that scale. If such reports come forward, they should be treated as leads and investigated. Until then, the right response to the risk of fraud is the one Pastor Lively himself names: pass the SAVE America Act.

## PART FOUR: The question under the question


Pastor Lively’s real question is not about Cornyn, Thune, or Soros. It is this: **Is the Republican establishment a true opposition to the Democrats, or a wing of one governing class that treats the voters’ wishes as something to be managed?**


**I stand on the side of the second answer.** I believe there is a governing class whose interests run across both parties, that it was in place long before 2016, and that it regards populist and Christian movements as interruptions to be contained. Pastor Lively has seen it from inside the Massachusetts Republican Party. I have seen it in the platform retreat of 2024 and in the record of the past two years.


The areas of uncertainty are these. “Coordinated” and “single controlling strata” claim more than the evidence shows. What the record shows is shared interests, shared donors, shared habits, and a shared dislike of disruption. That is enough to produce the same results as a plan, without anyone needing to give orders. It is also harder to fight, because there is no headquarters to expose. Second, some of what looks like sabotage is ordinary political weakness: senators from states that lean Democratic, members afraid of losing their seats, and leaders who prize the institution over the agenda. Weakness and betrayal produce the same votes but call for different responses; the weak can sometimes be stiffened, while the faithless must be replaced.


**Why the concern remains plausible** despite those uncertainties:


- **The money runs both ways.** Many of the same corporate and financial donors fund both parties’ leadership, and they expect returns from whoever wins. No conspiracy is needed to explain why the leaders of both parties protect the same interests.

- **The pattern holds across forty years.** Amnesty without enforcement in 1986, tolerance of illegal labor for decades, the bank rescues of 2008, wars begun without declarations, and now an election-integrity bill blocked by the majority’s own members. In each case, the costs fell on ordinary citizens, and the benefits went to those with access.

- **The rules bend in one direction.** The filibuster was set aside for nominations three times when the governing class wanted it set aside, and was held sacred when the voters wanted proof of citizenship.

- **The voters sense it.** Tuesday’s result is itself evidence. Voters in Texas and Kansas did not suddenly embrace the Democratic platform. Many of them concluded that the party they had elected would not do what it had promised, and they removed it. Pastor Lively calls that sabotage by the Uniparty. I call it the voters’ verdict on the Uniparty. Both descriptions point to the same disease.


*No man can serve two masters* **(Matthew 6:24)**. A legislator who campaigns for the voters and legislates for the donors has chosen his master. That is the heart of the Uniparty problem, and it is a moral problem before it is a political one.

## PART FIVE: What follows


Pastor Lively closes by predicting that the governing class will go on treating every nationalist interruption as a temporary anomaly. He may be right. But a diagnosis is only useful if it leads to a cure. Here is what I propose.


- **Judge every candidate by his votes, not his label.** The four senators who blocked the SAVE America Act were all elected as Republicans. Labels told voters nothing; the roll calls told them everything. Every Christian voter, and every group such as the Christos Voting Network, should keep a record of how each member actually voted on the questions that matter, and publish it.

- **Demand that promises be written into law.** *But let your communication be, Yea, yea; Nay, nay: for whatsoever is more than these cometh of evil* **(Matthew 5:37)**. Ask every candidate in the 2028 primaries three questions, and ask for answers in writing: Will you vote for the SAVE America Act? Will you vote to set aside the filibuster to pass it if necessary? Will you insist that Congress vote on any war before American forces are committed to it? A man who will not answer plainly has answered.

- **Primary the faithless; strengthen the weak.** Where a member has broken faith with his voters, replace him in the primary. Where a member is merely afraid, give him a reason to be braver: letters, calls, and visible support from his own district. Most members of Congress fear their voters more than their leaders, when the voters make themselves heard.

- **Restore the war power to Congress.** The surest defense against the charge that this was “Israel’s war” is for the American people’s representatives to own the decision. Let Congress vote on the Iran war in January, with its objectives and limits stated. If Iran is the threat I believe it to be, make the case to Congress and win the vote. A war fought with the consent of the governed cannot be blamed on a foreign ally.

- **Win the voters back with reasons, not contempt.** The conservatives who left the Republican ticket this year are not our enemies. Many of them are our brothers and sisters in Christ who were angry at a war without a vote and prices without relief. We will not win them back by comparing them to murderers. We will win them back by telling them the truth, admitting our side’s errors, and offering a better plan.

- **Seek righteousness first.** Pastor Lively is right that spiritual forces are at work in our politics. But the answer to spiritual warfare is repentance, not a better enemies list. *If my people, which are called by my name, shall humble themselves, and pray, and seek my face, and turn from their wicked ways; then will I hear from heaven, and will forgive their sin, and will heal their land* **(2 Chronicles 7:14)**. The promise is conditioned on God’s people turning, not on our opponents being exposed. That is the R in The New ERA Vision, and it is where every other reform must begin.


---


## Conclusion


Pastor Lively has named a real disease. The Republican majority of the past two years did not use the power the voters gave it, and some of its members actively blocked what its voters wanted. A governing class that spans both parties has shaped American policy for decades, and its money and habits explain much of what happened this week.


But a diagnosis must be proportionate to the evidence, or it will not be believed by those who most need to hear it. The record shows betrayal, weakness, and resentment by identifiable men, and those can be answered at the ballot box. It does not show a single hidden hand that makes our votes meaningless. If it did, there would be nothing left to do. There is a great deal left to do.


The loss on Tuesday was sabotage in part, surrender in part, and a judgment by the voters in part. We can answer all three: by replacing those who sabotaged, by stiffening those who surrendered, and by giving the voters a party, and a people, worth voting for. That is why I run.

## Sources and their limits


The factual claims in this paper rest mainly on the mainstream record: national and Texas news coverage of the election results and of Senator Cornyn’s election-night post, coverage of the Texas primary runoff, reporting on the Senate votes on the SAVE America Act, and national polling on the Iran war. That record is not neutral, and I have tried to read it accordingly. Accounts of Senator Cornyn’s conduct in the general election come mostly from conservative critics and from his own statements, both of which are interested testimony; I have not found a full record of what he did or did not do for Mr. Paxton between May and November. The March poll on whether the war benefited Israel was sponsored by organizations critical of Israel, and its wording may have shaped its answers; I use it only to show that the view was widespread, not to measure it precisely. Senators’ motives are, by nature, not in the public record; I have offered the reasons they have given publicly and noted that those reasons are their own testimony. On Mr. Soros, the size of his political spending is documented, but the degree to which it directs events rather than amplifies them is a matter of judgment. Where press or government curation could have hidden evidence, I have tried to say so, and I have labeled a claim unproven rather than false where the evidence is simply absent.
