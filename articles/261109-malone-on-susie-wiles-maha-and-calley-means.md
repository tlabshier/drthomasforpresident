# 261109 - Malone on Susie Wiles, MAHA, and Calley Means

# Inside the Pattern, Not Above It:


### Reading Malone on Calley Means and the MAHA Capture


Nov 9, 2026 · @Thomas Lee Abshier, ND


## Where I Stand


On November 9, 2026, Drs. Robert Malone and Jill Glasspool Malone published an essay on their Substack, *Malone News*, titled [“Did Susie Wiles Really Block MAHA?”](https://substack.com/home/post/p-224188281) Their answer, and mine, is: partly. And the part that is true is being used to shield the people who actually hold the pens.


I stand with the Malones’ central finding. The lobbying capture that Calley Means described on *The Joe Rogan Experience* and *Face the Nation* is real, and much of it is visible in public filings. But the frame he wraps it in, a heroic Secretary and a heroic President kept in the dark by a corrupt staff, does not survive contact with the public record. Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. defended the glyphosate order in his own words, thanked Susie Wiles on her way out, and stood behind Heidi Overton’s nomination to lead the FDA. Reporting from last May indicates that the President himself rebuked his own FDA commissioner for moving too slowly on flavored vapes. The men at the top are inside the pattern, not above it.


There are real areas of uncertainty. Much of what Means says rests on private conversations that no one else has confirmed. At least one of his specific numbers appears to be overstated. And no one outside the administration can see the motives behind Kennedy’s choices, whether they reflect industry capture, political calculation, or sincere agreement. Those uncertainties must be acknowledged, but they do not change where the signatures are.


The question under the question, the one every voter who joined the Make America Healthy Again (MAHA) movement is really asking, is this: *Was the movement betrayed, and by whom?* My answer is that a system betrayed it: industry money buys access to the people who decide policy, and the system runs through the top of the administration, not merely around it.


## What Means Alleged and What Malone Argues


Calley Means co-founded the wellness-payments company TrueMed, introduced Kennedy to the Trump 2024 campaign, and led MAHA implementation inside the White House for about twenty-one months. He resigned on October 22, 2026, twelve days before the midterms. On Rogan (released November 6) and on *Face the Nation* (November 8), he gave the fullest insider account yet of why.


As the Malones summarize it, Means aimed at four groups:


- **Chief of Staff Susie Wiles and her deputies.** He says Wiles pulled the proposed federal definition of ultra-processed food after lobbyist calls, including from Coca-Cola and Mondelez, and told him directly that she needed sugar-industry money for the midterms. He points to the soaring revenue of Continental Strategy, the lobbying firm where her daughter works, and of Ballard Partners, her former firm.

- **Pollster Tony Fabrizio,** whose firm’s published client list includes Pfizer, PhRMA, tobacco companies, and Pepsi, while he served as the political check on cabinet health policy. Fabrizio denies the allegations.

- **Staffers Walker Barrett, James Blair, Chris LaCivita, and Richard Walters,** whom he says drove decisions on glyphosate, kratom, fruit-flavored vapes, and the food definition on behalf of named industries.

- **FDA nominee Heidi Overton,** whom he accuses of blocking food and psychedelic policy, driving the reversal on Moderna’s mRNA flu vaccine, writing the kratom (7-OH) rule, and working with the Justice Department on the brief urging the Supreme Court to take up the glyphosate liability case.


The Malones take these allegations seriously and weigh them on their merits. But they make three further points. First, Means is not a neutral witness: his TrueMed holdings drew conflict-of-interest questions from Rep. Jake Auchincloss, Sen. Adam Schiff, and Dr. Mary Talley Bowden, and he helped build the apparatus he now condemns. Second, his interview tour does three jobs at once. It assigns the failures of the first two years to named staff; it positions Kennedy and Trump for a second-half “reset”; and it repositions Means himself as the base’s reformer. Third, the protective frame around Kennedy and Trump is, in their word, false.


They draw on Toby Rogers, the political-economy scholar who writes *uTobian*, who reduced the Means account to five steps: the President and Secretary are heroes; staff bamboozles them; they stay unaware despite daily news coverage; when they find out they are furious and do nothing; and the fix is a constitutional amendment Means never spells out. Rogers notes that Rogan never asked the obvious question: why were none of these alleged betrayals met with firings?


The Malones close with a list of actions Kennedy could take by his own signature, without Congress: amend the COVID-19 PREP Act liability declaration, withdraw the Moderna mRNA flu-shot license, withdraw the Overton nomination, reopen the ultra-processed-food definition, and adopt the vaccine-program reforms the Informed Consent Action Network (ICAN) listed in June. They add three structural reforms for Congress: sworn, machine-readable disclosure of the private clients of anyone advising the cabinet on health policy; a five-year rather than one-year lobbying ban for senior appointees; and publication of the Office of Management and Budget’s interagency review record for every major health order and FDA rule within thirty days.


## What the Record Confirms, and What It Corrects


I grade evidence by its kind, not by the outlet that carries it. A signed document or a man’s own published words outrank a secondhand account. An official denial is testimony from an interested party. A firsthand report from an insider is a lead to be checked, not a finding.


**What primary evidence confirms.**


- **The glyphosate order.** On February 18, 2026, the President signed an executive order invoking the Defense Production Act to secure domestic production of elemental phosphorus and glyphosate-based herbicides. CNBC reported that it came one day after Bayer proposed paying $7.25 billion to settle the Roundup cancer lawsuits. The following morning, Kennedy told CNBC that the order “puts America first where it matters most,” in defense readiness and the food supply, and he followed with a long post on X supporting it. The Associated Press noted that Kennedy had said as recently as January that he believes glyphosate causes cancer. This is the Secretary’s own voice, on the record, within a day of the signing.

- **The food definition.** HHS sent a proposed ultra-processed-food definition for review in August. Semafor reported on October 26 that the fight over that definition contributed to Means’s resignation and that he was frustrated by the lack of progress on its release. That corroborates the shape of his account, though not the private conversation he describes with Wiles.

- **Wiles’s background.** Federal disclosures filed before she became chief of staff showed she lobbied for the tobacco company Swisher International while running the Trump campaign. Her lobbying career is undisputed.

- **The flavored vapes.** FDA Commissioner Marty Makary resigned on May 12, 2026. According to The Wall Street Journal and Reuters, the President had rebuked him earlier that month for not approving flavored vapes and nicotine products quickly enough, and the FDA approved them days later. The New York Times reported that Makary told people close to him he could not in good conscience approve flavored vapes because of their appeal to young people.

- **Means’s conflicts.** Auchincloss and Schiff wrote to Wiles and Kennedy in late 2025 asking whether Means had violated conflict-of-interest law while holding his TrueMed stake. The White House said at the time that Means complied with all ethics requirements and played no role in health-savings-account policy.


The vape episode deserves emphasis because it goes further than the Malones go. If the reporting is accurate, the pressure to approve fruit-flavored vapes did not come only from staff offices; it came from the President. That is precisely the fact that the Means frame cannot hold.


**What the record corrects.**


- **The timing of Wiles’s exit.** Reporting indicates that Wiles submitted her resignation on November 5, and the President announced her departure on Friday, November 6 (the Malones say Thursday), the same day the Rogan interview was released. Means’s interview did not cause her exit. The President praised her lavishly and moved her to his political operation, which is not the language of a man who has just discovered a betrayal.

- **The kratom figure.** Means said the 7-OH kratom tolerance was relaxed twenty-fold, and the Malones repeat it. One legal analysis of the published thresholds calculates roughly a twofold change. Until someone reconciles the numbers against the rule itself, the “twenty times” figure should not be repeated as fact.

- **What remains unverified.** I could not independently confirm Wiles’s reported remark about sugar money, her text to Means, the Republika Srpska trip, Makary’s statement to CBS naming Big Tobacco, or the market move in packaged-food stocks on the day Means left. I relied on the Malones’ account for the Continental Strategy and Ballard Partners revenue figures, the Reynolds American contribution to MAGA, Inc., and Fabrizio’s client list. These are leads and, in several cases, public filings anyone can check. They are not yet findings.


**The official answer.** A White House spokeswoman responded by pointing to the revised dietary guidelines, the push to remove artificial ingredients, investment in regenerative farming, and continued Trump-Kennedy cooperation. That is interested-party testimony. It is also partly true: the 2025-2030 Dietary Guidelines did advise limiting highly processed foods, a real reversal of the prior advisory committee’s position. The administration has delivered some things. The question is what it traded away to deliver them, and who decided.


## The Overton Test and the Signature Test


The strongest part of the Malones’ essay is what Rogers called the Overton test, and I agree it is the right place to press.


Heidi Overton was deputy director of the White House Domestic Policy Council, the office that coordinated MAHA policy across HHS, FDA, CDC, and USDA. On August 19, 2026, the President nominated her to lead the FDA, calling her a “ROCKSTAR” in his administration. By the Malones’ account, Kennedy called her the best person for the job after one of the toughest decisions of his tenure. On November 8, Means told *Face the Nation* that she lacks the character or integrity to be commissioner.


Both judgments cannot be right. Either Means is wrong about Overton, in which case much of his account of the interagency process falls with her; or he is right, in which case the President and the Secretary chose her with full knowledge of the policies she had shaped. A Secretary who has been overridden does not nominate the person who overrode him. The overridden-Secretary thesis cannot carry this episode.


The second test is simpler. Call it the signature test. Responsibility follows authority. A chief of staff controls access and schedules; a cabinet secretary signs rules, licenses, declarations, and nominations. The Malones list actions that require only Kennedy’s signature, from amending the PREP Act declaration under 42 U.S.C. section 247d-6d(b) to reopening the food-definition process. ICAN’s June 30 letter listed twenty vaccine-program reforms in the same category. None has happened. When a man holds the pen and does not use it, the explanation cannot be that someone else stopped him.


This is not a charge of bad faith. I do not know Kennedy’s heart, and the record is consistent with political calculation or sincere agreement as well as with capture. But the moral principle does not depend on motive. *“For unto whomsoever much is given, of him shall be much required”* **(Luke 12:48)**. The Secretary of Health and Human Services has been given great authority. His commitment to the movement that put him there shows in what he signs.


## Why the Concern Remains Plausible


Some readers will say that every claim here is either unproven or explainable, and that the honest posture is to wait. I disagree. Several lines of reasoning, taken together, make the core concern more plausible than not, even before Congress subpoenas a single document.


**The direction never varies.** Glyphosate, flavored vapes, kratom, the food definition, the mRNA flu vaccine: in each case where MAHA policy met a concentrated industry interest, the outcome moved toward the industry. One reversal can be prudence. Five in the same direction is a pattern, and patterns have causes.


**The money is on the table.** The lobbying firms tied to the chief of staff’s network posted historic revenue, according to filings the Malones cite. A tobacco company’s affiliate made a seven-figure contribution to the President’s super PAC before the administration eased its vape and kratom posture. None of that proves a quid pro quo, and the Supreme Court has made such proof very hard in *McDonnell v. United States*. But the absence of a prosecutable bribe is not the absence of influence. Access that costs money is still for sale.


**The framing is unnatural.** An herbicide is declared a matter of national defense the day after its manufacturer offers billions to settle cancer lawsuits. A Secretary who built a career suing that manufacturer, and who said in January that the chemical causes cancer, defends the order within a day. People sense when an explanation does not fit the facts, and that intuition deserves a hearing even when it cannot yet be documented.


**The witness knows the playbook.** Means once consulted for Coca-Cola, by his own account, helping defeat sugar taxes. He is not neutral, and he has reasons to shape the story. But a former industry consultant is exactly the person who would recognize industry tactics from the inside. His bias argues for checking his claims, not for dismissing them.


**The revolving door is documented.** The Malones cite Courtney Knupp, the National Security Council staffer they say drove the glyphosate order and then became CEO of the United Soybean Board. Whatever the particulars, the movement of people from policy offices into the industries they regulated is a structural feature of Washington, and it does not need a conspiracy to work.


Scripture names the mechanism precisely: *“And thou shalt take no gift: for the gift blindeth the wise, and perverteth the words of the righteous”* **(Exodus 23:8)**. The warning is not addressed to villains. It is addressed to the wise and the righteous, because a gift does its work on good men without their noticing.


## What a Physician Wants from Washington, and from Us


I write as a naturopathic physician, and I believe most chronic disease in this country is diet-induced. From that vantage point, the most consequential item in this entire story is the one that sounds the most technical: the federal definition of ultra-processed food.


A definition is not a ban. But once it exists, it can be written into school-lunch standards, SNAP eligibility, labeling, procurement, and research priorities. It is the hinge on which the rest of a food-policy reform turns. That is exactly why, if Means is right, the beverage and snack industries fought it hardest. The first thing I would ask of the Secretary is the simplest: publish the definition the agency already drafted and sent for review.


The Malones’ structural reforms are sound, and I support all three. Sworn disclosure of the clients of anyone who advises the cabinet on health policy would close the gap through which consultants shape policy without registering as lobbyists. A five-year revolving-door ban would make the trade of public office for private payoff slower and costlier. Publishing the interagency review records would let the public test Means’s account against the administrative record, which is the only evidence that can settle it.


But I would add a word of realism. My mentor, Dr. Norman Easley, used to say that getting people to change their diet is like getting someone to change their religion. Washington follows the American plate more than it leads it. The food industry lobbies so hard because our appetites make its products profitable. A federal definition will help, and capture must be exposed and ended. But no family needs to wait for a federal definition to stop buying what the definition would describe. The deepest reform is a change of heart and mind at the kitchen table, and that one is in our hands today.


## Sources and Their Limits


This essay leans on the mainstream record: wire services, national newspapers, trade press, and congressional letters. That record has its own curation. It reports what officials say and what reporters can confirm, and it is slow to report what neither side wants known. Where evidence could be hiding, it is most likely in the interagency review files for the glyphosate order and the kratom rule, which are not public, and in the internal communications of the offices Means named. I worked from the Malones’ text for the Rogan and *Face the Nation* quotations, the Toby Rogers posts, the FEC and LegiStorm figures, and Kennedy’s post nominating Overton; I did not pull those primary records myself. The White House’s response and Means’s own account are both testimony from interested parties.
