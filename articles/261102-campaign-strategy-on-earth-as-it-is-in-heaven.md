# 261102 - Campaign Strategy - On Earth as it is in Heaven

# On Earth as It Is in Heaven


*The strategy for winning the country for Christ

The campaign itself is the first act of leadership*


Nov 2, 2026 · @Thomas Lee Abshier, ND


I believe the purpose of a human life is not any single achievement but the journey itself, taken in fellowship with God and with one another, received day by day. I believe that answer matters more now than at any time in history, because machines are beginning to out-think us at the very work we once thought made us human. And I believe the way to win this country for Christ is not by force or by law imposed from the top, but by showing Americans a way of life so clearly righteous and so plainly good to live that they choose it for themselves. This campaign is my attempt to do that in public. It is an argument, and I hold it open to a better one.


## The question under the question


On 6 October 2026, OpenAI posted 722 mathematical manuscripts written by an unreleased AI model. The company says the model was handed roughly 4,000 open problems, and the papers claim results on hundreds of them, some of which mathematicians had worked on for decades. Many of those claims are not yet verified, and checking a single proof can take months. But even if only a fraction stand, something has changed.


Mathematicians are the most specialized thinkers we have. Many have given twenty years to a single problem. Now a machine has produced work on hundreds of problems at once, and the people best placed to judge it are asking, in public, what their place is. Young mathematicians have described it with grief and anger.


The question is not new. Some voices in the environmental movement have long treated human beings as a burden the earth would be better without. What is new is that the question is now pressed on everyone. No field of human work will be untouched: not law, not medicine, not art, not ministry, and not politics.


This touched me. I developed much of the platform on this site in conversation with AI. I give a topic, the machine drafts, and I read, correct, and decide. For a while I wondered whether that made the work less mine, or less worth offering. The mathematics release settled it for me. If the same tools can do in a night what the finest mathematicians could not do in a lifetime, then there is no virtue in pretending I worked alone, and no shame in the tools. What remains for a human being to do is the part no machine can do for us: to judge what is righteous, to choose it, to live it, and to find out whether it is good to live.


So the question under the question is not “Can machines think?” It is “What are people for?” A political platform that cannot answer that has nothing to say about the world now arriving. My campaign manager, Joelle, put it to me directly: until the platform answers the question of purpose, no one can judge whether it is good.


## Beauty that would not hold


My answer begins with three visions I had as a young man, during the handful of times I took psychedelics. I tell them because they taught me something I have found true ever since, not because I commend the means.


In the **first**, I saw a rainbow of a beauty I cannot describe. I was drawn toward it, and as I moved closer, it grew more beautiful, then began to fade in the direction I was going. Another spot, off to the side, looked even more beautiful, so I went toward it, and it too brightened and faded. I chased it faster and faster, and I never reached the end. I would collapse in exhaustion, and the vision would begin again. It went on for hours. Its beauty was what made it horrible; I believe it was the worst experience of my life.


In the **second**, I saw a bird of paradise in a pot beside a staircase, the most beautiful flower I had ever seen. It began to droop, and I gave it more love and attention, and it revived. Then it drooped again, and revived less, and at last it collapsed into black decay and ran down the stairs.


In the **third**, I saw a temple like the Parthenon, solid and magnificent. A crack formed, then another. A stone fell, a beam, a column. The ground opened, and the whole of it was drawn down into the earth.


I have come to read these as one lesson. Each was a picture of life lived in the flesh: the soul glimpses a beauty that belongs to the Kingdom of Heaven and tries to seize it all now, in this world. It cannot be done. Anything we choose as the purpose of life, any mission, achievement, pleasure, or monument, will rise, become ordinary, and pass away. There is nothing under the sun we can point to and say, “This, held forever, is the point.”


Scripture said it long before I saw it: *“Hast thou found honey? eat so much as is sufficient for thee, lest thou be filled therewith, and vomit it”* **(Proverbs 25:16)**. When Israel tried to gather more manna than the day required, it bred worms and stank (Exodus 16:20). I was trying to gather all the manna at once, and it rotted.


## The purpose of life: the journey, taken together


If no single thing can be the purpose, what is? I believe the answer is the journey itself, and that the journey has meaning because we take it together.


Each season of life brings its own problem to solve and its own beauty. Trials and victories, defeats, fear, hope, disappointment, and desire will come. When one frontier is won, another appears. That is not a curse. Jesus said, *“In my Father’s house are many mansions”* **(John 14:2)**. There is always another room. The task is not to reach the last one but to receive today’s portion with gratitude: *“Give us this day our daily bread”* **(Matthew 6:11)**, and *“Sufficient unto the day is the evil thereof”* **(Matthew 6:34)**. The beauty of the day is sufficient unto it as well. We do not need to swallow the whole world in one gulp. We need to calibrate our souls, by the Holy Spirit, to receive enough for today and leave a little hunger for tomorrow.


And the journey alone would be meaningless. Climbing a mountain no one else will ever hear about, solving a problem no one shares: so what? What gives the journey its worth is fellowship: caring about another person’s road, working beside him, celebrating victories together and mourning defeats together. *“Two are better than one… For if they fall, the one will lift up his fellow: but woe to him that is alone when he falleth”* **(Ecclesiastes 4:9–10)**. Isak, who works with me, likes the line his old pastor used: fellowship is two fellows in a ship. We are all on one ship, this planet, and if we ever leave it, we will be on a larger one, still under the same God and the same Spirit.


Joelle offered a saying from the Jewish tradition that holds both halves of the human condition. A man carries a note in each pocket. One reads, “For my sake the world was created.” The other reads, “I am but dust and ashes.” He takes out whichever he needs. Both are true. AI pushes hard on the second note, and that is not all bad; pride needed humbling. But the first note is just as true, and a civilization that forgets it will despair.


AI will change the journey. I do not know what it will look like. The Industrial Revolution emptied some cities and filled others, and Detroit rose, fell, and is finding its way again. Everything has its season **(Ecclesiastes 3:1)**. What I am confident of is that the journey will not end, and that its meaning will not change: walking with God, and with one another, toward what is good.


## Winning the country for Christ: the strategy


I see no benefit in aiming low. The goal is the one Jesus taught us to pray for: *“Thy kingdom come. Thy will be done in earth, as it is in heaven”* **(Matthew 6:10)**. That is the aim of this campaign.


It does not mean a state church, or faith enforced by law, or a ruler who decides what citizens must believe. Two phrases I return to explain why: “No king but King Jesus,” and “Every man a philosopher king.” If Christ is King, no president is, and the citizen who governs himself under God needs no master in Washington. A nation is won for Christ the way a person is: by being shown the truth, freely choosing it, and finding it good to live.


So the measure of every policy I propose is a set of questions asked in order. Is it righteous — does it accord with what God has established? If so, is it loving toward my neighbor? And would I want it done to me, feeling his situation in my own body, mind, and heart, judged not by my appetite of the moment but by my soul’s eternal good? A policy that fails the first question is not rescued by passing the others.


The strategy that follows has four parts.


- **Answer the question of purpose first.** People will not follow a program if they cannot see the point. That is why this essay begins where it does. In the world AI is bringing, the Christian account of the person — made in God’s image, called to love, walking a road with others — is not a private comfort. It is the answer the culture is now asking for.

- **Show the life, not only the argument.** Every position on this site is meant to describe a way of living, and each one has a look and feel. Our work now is to make that visible: in stories, video, plays and pictures that let a person see what it would be like to live in a country ordered this way, and to ask whether he would want to. Machines can draft the ideas. Whether they are good to live in is a human question, and only people can answer it.

- **Show that it fits together.** The articles here, now more than nine hundred across my sites, are not a pile of opinions. They are meant to be one picture, seen from many angles: medicine, education, energy, law, family, faith. We are building maps that let a visitor see the whole and how each part connects, and the same maps will become the backbone of a school. What a person can see whole, he can judge whole.

- **Ground it in a true account of reality.** For forty years I have worked on Conscious Point Physics, an attempt to explain the physical world from first principles that also leave room for, and point to, the mind of God. I am working now on the structure of the photon, and beyond it on dark matter and on unifying the forces of physics, the problem Einstein was still working on when he died. This is research in progress, and it will matter to this campaign only if it turns out to be true. If it does, it will show that faith and science were never at war, and that would strengthen everything else on this site.


None of this is a fear campaign. It is an invitation: here is a picture of the good life, here is why I believe it is righteous, here is how it fits together, and here is how it would feel to live it. Come and see.


## The campaign itself as an act of leadership


The president’s chief power over the life of the nation is his voice. He cannot make a family whole, a school board wise, or a church faithful. He can name what is righteous, give his reasons, urge citizens to act on them, and show by his own conduct that he means it. That kind of leadership does not wait for an election. It can begin now, and this campaign is where I am beginning it.


That shapes how the campaign is run.


- **In the open about method.** I have told you how this platform was developed, with AI as a drafting partner and with me reading, correcting, and deciding. A country about to live with these tools needs to see someone use them honestly and keep the human judgment where it belongs.

- **In fellowship.** This work is not done alone. It grows out of weekly conversations with a group of friends who meet on Sundays to ask how life could and should be lived, and out of daily work with the younger people who run this campaign with me. If the purpose of life is the journey taken together, a campaign that claims it should be such a journey.

- **Without money.** I take no donations. No one who reads this site is being sold to; I only ask for your attention and your judgment.

- **Open to a better argument.** Every position here is a stand I hold, with my reasons. None of it is offered as final authority. If you have a better argument, I want it, and I will change my mind.

- **Patient.** We will not swallow the whole world in a gulp. We will offer what is ready, a day’s portion at a time, and keep building.


The Kingdom of Heaven will not be finished on earth by any campaign. But a country can turn toward it, one household, one school board and one conscience at a time. My part is to set the picture before you as clearly and as honestly as I can, and to live it as well as I can while I do. Yours is to look at it and decide. Either way, we are on the ship together.
