# 261023 - Naming the Good and Evil of Religions and Political Parties

# No King but King Jesus: Calling Good and Evil by Their Names


*Dr. Thomas Abshier — from a conversation with Isak Gutierrez, October 23, 2026*


## The Problem with a Faith That Does Not Touch the Ground


Christianity is the most important worldview a man can hold, but it is not a private sentiment. If Jesus is Lord, He is Lord of the public square as well as the prayer closet, and what we believe about Him must connect with how we vote, how we legislate, how we do business, and what we call good and evil in our common life. A faith that confesses Christ on Sunday and then, the rest of the week, advocates for the very things He calls sin has not yet understood how the kingdom of heaven is to be established on earth.


In this paper I take three positions. First, a Christian cannot consistently stand for the platform of a party that has made the killing of unborn children, the surgical and chemical alteration of the sexes, and the redefinition of marriage central planks of its program. Second, Islam is a false religion, and by the standard Scripture gives us, its revelation came from a spirit opposed to Christ. Third, both of these judgments belong to the realm of persuasion, not the realm of the sword. Government has no business punishing a man for holding a false religion. Society, the Church, and every Christian citizen have every business telling him the truth.


I hold these positions open to a better argument.


## The Test I Apply


For every policy and every public stand, I ask three questions in order:


- **Is it righteous?** Does it conform to what God has established as right?

- **If so, is it loving?** Does it serve my neighbor’s real good?

- **Would I want it done to me?** Measure it not by my momentary appetite, but by the self committed to righteousness, with the soul’s eternal perspective in view.


Humoring a neighbor in his error is not love. *“Thou shalt not hate thy brother in thine heart: thou shalt in any wise rebuke thy neighbour, and not suffer sin upon him”* **(Leviticus 19:17)**. If my neighbor is following a path that leads away from God, the loving act is to tell him, plainly and without contempt. I would want the same done for me.


## Christian and Democrat: Two Claims That Do Not Fit in One Sentence


If a man stands for the Democratic Party platform and also calls Jesus Lord, he affirms two things that cannot both be held.


The 2024 Democratic platform calls for restoring and enshrining a legal right to abortion, pledges to repeal the Hyde Amendment so that federal tax dollars can pay for abortion, promises to protect “gender-affirming care” and to oppose state bans on it, including for minors, pledges to pass the Equality Act, which would make sexual orientation and gender identity protected classes under federal civil-rights law, and commits the party to codifying same-sex marriage in federal law.


Each of these positions inverts something Scripture plainly teaches. God forms the child in the womb **(Psalm 139:13–16)** and forbids the shedding of innocent blood **(Proverbs 6:16–17)**. He made man male and female **(Genesis 1:27; Matthew 19:4)**. He defined marriage as the union of one man and one woman **(Genesis 2:24; Matthew 19:5–6)**. A platform that sets out to protect and fund the opposite of each of these is not neutral toward God’s order. It is organized against it.


To say “Jesus is Lord” and then to work for that program is to say, in effect, “Everything the Lord of the universe intends to establish in His creation is wrong, and we will establish the opposite.” Jesus addressed this directly: *“Not every one that saith unto me, Lord, Lord, shall enter into the kingdom of heaven; but he that doeth the will of my Father”* **(Matthew 7:21)**. Those who called Him Lord while working iniquity heard Him say, *“I never knew you”* **(Matthew 7:23)**.


**Where the language needs care.** A registered Democrat is not necessarily a man who has embraced every plank. Some vote the party out of family habit, union loyalty, or sincere concern for the poor, without ever weighing the platform against Scripture. My claim is about standing for the platform, not about the hidden state of any particular voter’s soul, which only God sees. But the qualification does not dissolve the stand. Once a Christian sees what the platform actually advances, he must choose between the platform and his Lord. He cannot keep both.


## Islam: A Revelation That Denies the Son


### What Christians are told to test


Scripture gives us a direct test for any spirit claiming to bring a new revelation: *“Every spirit that confesseth that Jesus Christ is come in the flesh is of God: and every spirit that confesseth not that Jesus Christ is come in the flesh is not of God: and this is that spirit of antichrist”* **(1 John 4:2–3)**. Paul adds, *“Though we, or an angel from heaven, preach any other gospel unto you than that which we have preached unto you, let him be accursed”* **(Galatians 1:8)**, and he warns that Satan himself masquerades as an angel of light **(2 Corinthians 11:14)**.


The Quran presents its message as delivered to Muhammad by the angel Jibril, the Arabic name for Gabriel. That message denies that Jesus is the Son of God (Quran 4:171; 112:1–4) and denies that He was crucified (Quran 4:157). If there is no crucifixion, there is no atonement, and if there is no atonement, there is no gospel. By the test the apostles gave us, a spirit that brings that message is not of God. I therefore conclude that the being Muhammad encountered was not the Gabriel of Luke 1 but a deceiving spirit presenting itself as an angel of light. The same judgment applies to the angel Moroni of Mormonism, who likewise arrived with a “fuller” revelation that superseded the one sealed in Christ.


This is a theological judgment, and I make it as a Christian. A Muslim will reject it. But it is not an insult; it is the plain consequence of believing that Jesus Christ is who He said He was.


### What the doctrine itself prescribes


I want to separate what the Islamic texts teach from what some Muslim cultures practice. Female genital cutting, for example, is not commanded in the Quran; it is a cultural practice found in some Muslim and some non-Muslim societies. I do not lay it at the Quran’s door.


Other things, however, are in the texts:


- **Subjugation of Christians and Jews.** Quran 9:29 commands fighting the “People of the Book” until they pay the *jizya*, a tax, “and feel themselves subdued.” This became the *dhimmi* system that governed Christians and Jews under Islamic rule for centuries: protected, but taxed and legally subordinate. Pagans were treated more harshly still.

- **Death for leaving.** The Quran itself does not prescribe an earthly penalty for apostasy, and some Muslims cite “there is no compulsion in religion” (2:256). But the hadith in Sahih al-Bukhari records Muhammad saying, “Whoever changes his religion, kill him,” and the classical schools of Islamic law adopted that penalty. A number of Muslim-majority countries still criminalize apostasy today.

- **A revelation that served its messenger.** When a verse arrived granting Muhammad special latitude in his marriages, his wife Aisha remarked that his Lord seemed quick to fulfill his wishes (Sahih al-Bukhari, Book of Tafsir, on Quran 33:51). Muslims themselves preserve the record.


Now picture a spirit designing a religion to spread and to hold its converts. It would bring enough truth to make the message credible. It would tax and subject the adherents of the true faith so conversion becomes the path to equality. It would make leaving the faith a capital offense, so that once a man speaks the creed he cannot easily walk away. It would also keep the messenger’s own desires satisfied. Set that design beside what God revealed in Christ, and ask which one looks like the work of the Creator of the universe.


### Side by side


The comparison is stark. The God of the Bible says,* “Greater love hath no man than this, that a man lay down his life for his friends”* **(John 15:13)**, and He did exactly that: He came into His own creation and died for those who were His enemies. Christianity is self-sacrifice for the sake of the other.


Islam, at the point where it differs from Christianity, asks the believer to bring the unbeliever into submission. The Christian lays down his life; the doctrine of the jizya asks the Christian to lay down his dignity. One gives; the other takes. When we evaluate religions, we are entitled to compare them by their fruits and by what they ask of their followers toward their neighbors.


### Taqiyya and the plausibility of strategic weakness


*Taqiyya* is commonly understood as the principle of pretending to be weak and peaceful until one is strong enough to take over. Strictly, taqiyya is the doctrine, rooted in Quran 16:106 and 3:28 and developed mainly in Shia Islam, that permits a believer to conceal his faith under threat of persecution. It is not, in its formal definition, a general license to deceive non-Muslims for advantage, and most Muslim immigrants and refugees are exactly what they appear to be: people seeking safety and work.


But my concern is not imaginary. Islamic jurisprudence does permit deception in war, and the Islamist movement, which is distinct from ordinary Muslim believers, has at times stated a long-game strategy openly. The Muslim Brotherhood’s 1991 “Explanatory Memorandum,” entered into evidence in the 2008 Holy Land Foundation terrorism-financing trial, described its work in North America as a “civilization-jihadist process” aimed at undermining Western civilization from within. Whether that memo represented a realistic plan or one activist’s ambition is debated. The fact that it was written at all suggests that people with the means to pursue it have contemplated a strategy of patient growth followed by dominance. It is reasonable to watch for it.


## The Good in a False Religion, and the Good People Within It


The Quran contains much that is good: prayer, almsgiving, honesty in trade, care for orphans. I acknowledge this freely. But those things are good only because they are godly, and the godly portions were already in the Bible. A copy that takes the truth and bends it toward another master does not become a true revelation because it contains true sentences.


Consider an illustration I made up for the purpose. Suppose a religion taught everything Christians teach: love your neighbor, tell the truth, care for the poor. And suppose that once a year, on Halloween, its members went out and sacrificed a random person to Satan. We would not say, “Look at all the good they do; the sacrifice is just part of their tradition.” We would commend every good thing, demand that the sacrifice stop, and name the spirit that commanded it. The good parts do not sanctify the evil part. The evil part pollutes the whole.


At the same time, most Muslims are good people. They were born where they were born, raised as they were taught, and many of them sincerely seek God. To say the religion is false is not to say that every Muslim is an enemy. Isak put it well: profiling every member of a faith is dangerous, especially for those caught in the middle by the misfortune of birthplace and political climate. The leadership’s goals are not necessarily the people’s goals. Ordinary Muslims are carried along by the system they were born into, much as Americans pay taxes for wars they did not choose.


### Who rises to the top


Why, then, do movements built on good people so often become instruments of domination? Because power selects for a particular kind of person. Psychologists describe a “dark triad” of traits: Machiavellianism, the love of power and manipulation; psychopathy, the absence of feeling for what others feel; and narcissism, the love of self above all. A man with all three sees other people not as persons but as objects to use. Give such a man a religion that already authorizes the submission of others, and he has both a vehicle and a justification. He can rise to the top and direct sincere followers toward his own ends.


The pattern repeats across history: use people until they are no longer useful, then discard them. Stalin, once in power, executed the Old Bolsheviks who had brought him there and purged his officer corps, including Marshal Tukhachevsky, in 1937. Those who rose in their place saw the lesson and became completely subservient. Scripture records the same pattern in King Saul, who turned his spear on David, the commander who had won his battles, out of fear that David would overtake him. This use-and-discard of allies is a characteristic mark of the demonic. It stands in exact opposition to the Shepherd who lays down His life for the sheep.


## Two Chessboards: Where the Sword Belongs and Where It Does Not


How should a Christian nation respond to a false religion? Not with the sword.


Picture two chessboards stacked one above the other, connected by trapdoors. On the upper board, the board of society, we live our ordinary lives. Here we argue, persuade, rebuke, praise, and shame. Here a man may hold a false religion, and here we may tell him plainly that it is false, try to win him away from it, and refuse to call it equal to the truth. This is the realm of the Church, the family, the press, and every citizen.


The lower board is the realm of the state, the realm of the sword. A man drops through the trapdoor onto that board when he does something the state must punish: murder, theft, fraud, assault. On that board the questions change. How much premeditation was there? What mitigating circumstances? Was he competent to know right from wrong? What punishment fits, and what will restore him? The state’s board should aim at rehabilitation, so that a man who has truly turned to God and become an asset to society can be lifted back onto the upper board.


Holding a false belief does not drop a man through the trapdoor. Government should not prosecute anyone for being a Muslim, a Mormon, a Hindu, or an atheist. When a religion’s adherents act on its worst doctrines, by attacking apostates, by coercing conversion, or by plotting violence, they fall onto the state’s board, and the state must act. But the belief itself is answered on the upper board, by truth spoken in love.


The hard cases are the ones where we argue about which board an act belongs on. Abortion is the clearest example. A late-term abortion and the killing of a newborn differ by a few inches of birth canal, yet one is called “my body, my rights” and the other murder. The line our law currently draws is not moral; it is convenient. I hold that the unborn child belongs on the state’s board, under the same protection as every other human being, and that society’s board, through the culture of sexual responsibility it either builds or erodes, must do most of the work long before any woman faces that choice.


## Why Tolerance Became a Lie


Isak observed that he grew up hearing conservative talk radio warn about Islam and eventually wrote it off as fear-based propaganda, the kind both parties use. Feeling enlightened, he set aside the real question: is this religion right or wrong? That is the trap. Recognizing that propaganda exists does not settle what is true. It only obliges us to check our sources and evaluate the claims.


America has become multicultural in a way that means, in practice, that we may not judge any culture or creed. We have built an entire vocabulary for honoring every path: “your truth,” “inclusion,” “tolerance.” Under that vocabulary, we are asked to accept a little abortion, a little sexual confusion, a little worship of a god who is not the Father of Jesus Christ, because objecting would be unkind.


But every instance of these things diminishes the kingdom of heaven to the extent that we take part in it. God does not say, “It is fine to have a little of another kingdom that is dedicated to breaking down mine.” Some things are simply neutral to the kingdom; a house built on the corner neither serves nor opposes it. But a creed that teaches the subjugation of Christians, or a policy that ends the life of a child, is not neutral. To call these equal to the truth is to soft-pedal evil and call it charity.


I would rather make a whole culture wrong, where it is wrong, than leave my neighbor in a lie. I will honor every true and godly thing in that culture as fully as anything that came from God’s own mouth. But I will call out the parts that oppose His kingdom by name.


## Why the Concern Remains Plausible


Some readers will say I am painting with too broad a brush. Let me state the uncertainties, then explain why they do not change my conclusion.


The uncertainties are real. Muslims are not a monolith; Sufi, Ahmadi, reformist, and secular Muslims reject the coercive readings I have described. The Islamist movement and ordinary Muslim believers are not the same thing. Taqiyya’s formal meaning is narrower than its popular use. Many Democrats have never read their platform.


But the concern remains plausible for reasons that do not depend on the worst-case reading:


- **The texts are there.** The jizya verse and the apostasy hadith are not fringe documents; they are in the canonical sources, and classical Islamic law has been built on them for over a thousand years. A reformer must argue against his own tradition to set them aside.

- **The behavior follows the doctrine.** Where Islamic law has governed, Christians and Jews have lived as a taxed and subordinate class, and apostates have faced death or prison. Doctrine shapes law, and law shapes behavior.

- **Power seeks a vehicle.** The dark-triad personalities who rise in every movement will use whatever authorization the doctrine provides. A religion whose texts authorize submission gives them more to work with than one whose founder washed His disciples’ feet.

- **Platforms are written by the committed.** A party platform is not a random sample of its voters; it records what the party’s most committed members intend to accomplish. When that intent opposes God’s order on life, sex, and marriage, the Christian voter is enlisting in it whether he reads the document or not.

- **Our intuition is not wrong.** Most people still sense that something is unnatural about altering a child’s sex, about ending a life a few inches before birth, about a revelation that always seemed to suit its messenger. That sense is the law written on the heart (Romans 2:15), and it deserves to be heard.


## Putting On the Yoke


This campaign is not meant to punish anyone. It is to enroll people into the kingdom of God: to invite them to put on the yoke of Christ.


A yoke does not let you walk wherever you please. When you are yoked, you go the direction your partner goes. Taking Christ’s yoke means walking His way with Him. It costs something. You will not sleep with whomever you want. If you have already joined yourself to someone, the question is no longer whether you are compatible but how you will become happy in the choice you made. You will forgive before the sun goes down instead of nursing yesterday’s anger. In business, you will tell the buyer the truth about your product, its strengths and its weaknesses, so that he can choose as you would want to choose in his place.


Why would anyone take on such a yoke? Because it is the yoke of the One who loved us enough to die for us, and because, as He promised, it is easy and its burden is light compared with the chains of every alternative master **(Matthew 11:28–30**). It all comes down to two commandments: love God, and love your neighbor as yourself.


In a sense, we are selling God. And a good salesman offers an honest side-by-side comparison. Here is Christ: self-sacrificial love, forgiveness, and the dignity of every person made in God’s image. Here is the alternative: submission imposed on others, a revelation that served its messenger, and death for the one who tries to leave. Here is a political program that protects the child, honors marriage, and accepts the body God gave us; and here is one that funds the opposite. Let every man see the comparison clearly and choose.


That is the heart of the Vision for a New ERA: Energy, Righteousness, and Accountability. Righteousness is not one value among many in a multicultural marketplace. God established it, and there is no king but King Jesus.
