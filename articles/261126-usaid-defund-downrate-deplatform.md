# 261126 - USAID - Defund, Downrate, Deplatform

# Who Decides What Is True?


### The USAID Files and the Government That Wanted to Grade Its Own Homework

**Thomas Lee Abshier, ND** | 26 November 2026


---


On 24 November 2026, the administration turned the old USAID website into an archive titled the "USAID Files": 248 internal emails, memos, briefings, chat logs, and videos, dated from 2013 through 2024, most of them from the Biden years. The commentary site *Not the Bee* led its coverage with a clip from an internal USAID webinar. In it, a senior advisor for human rights, technology, and democracy explains the appeal of adding fact-checking to existing aid programs. The goal, she says, is to "debunk information in a way that aligns with the objectives of government partners."

I want to take that sentence seriously, because it answers a question many Americans have been asking for six years: *Was our government paying people to decide what the public may believe, and calling it "fact-checking"?*

I stand on the side of yes. The files show that a federal agency, using taxpayer money, built machinery to watch speech, rate it, flag it to the platforms, and steer advertisers away from it. They show that the people running that machinery defined "disinformation" as something other than falsehood. And they show that those people knew the public would object, and worked to keep it quiet. What the files do not show, so far, is a USAID request to remove an American's post. I will be careful about that line, and I will explain why I do not think it rescues the program.

## What the Files Show

The most careful independent reading I have found is by Bradford Karony, who went through all 248 files. His summary, together with what other reporters have pulled out, gives this picture.

**Monitors and flaggers.** In August 2022, an Internews senior advisor wrote to a USAID official about YouTube's "trusted flagger" program: "We have social media monitors already operating in about 20 countries." He added that he was fairly sure USAID was funding staff to flag issues to the platforms. That is one man's belief, not a payment record, but it describes the arrangement plainly. In May 2022, USAID emailed Google a list of candidate partners for the trusted-flagger program. Meta appears in 138 of the files, Google and YouTube in 127, and Twitter in 44. The Twitter trail stops after Elon Musk bought the company.

**The "three verbs."** In July 2022, a USAID deputy assistant administrator asked the Global Disinformation Index (GDI) for a collaboration paper covering ten countries. GDI's concept note proposed three actions against "high-risk" media: *defund, downrank, and deplatform.* To their credit, USAID officials pushed back on the draft. One called it vague, and another replied, "This is not what we need right now at all." Their objection was that the proposal was poorly made. They did not object to the aim.

**"Disinformation is not true or false."** On 5 January 2022, GDI co-founder Daniel Rogers trained USAID staff in a webinar titled "Information Disorder and Its Threat to Democracy." His example of an "adversarial narrative" was a Breitbart page collecting local news reports of crimes committed by illegal aliens. He did not say the stories were false. His charge was that putting true stories together formed a misleading narrative. In the same talk, he credited newsroom training for the fact that outlets did not give the Hunter Biden laptop story "oxygen," and he called the story "weirdly fake." We now know the laptop was real.

**Lawyers who saw the line.** The agency's own counsel told staff that the work "must be viewpoint neutral." A 2024 guidance told them to avoid anything that could reasonably be read as pressuring the social-media companies. In April 2023, counsel warned that one proposal looked like an end run around Congress and the regulatory process. In 2024, senior counsel warned that repeated pointed questions to Meta about its Trusted Partner Program could be seen as coercive. By one email's account, USAID then stopped pressing.

**Secrecy.** The talking points advised staff to write only what would pass "the Washington Post test," and when in doubt, to "pick up the phone." In October 2022, a staffer was told to keep a new Information Resilience Council "extremely non-public." After press coverage of the Election Integrity Partnership, a senior official wrote that they needed to "get through the midterms without any leaks."

**Foreign targets.** The removal requests, monitors, and lists in the files concern foreign countries: Libya, Sudan, Guatemala, Sri Lanka, Georgia, Ukraine, Moldova, Belarus. Some of those requests were plainly defensible. One email chain asks Facebook to deal with threats made against staff of a USAID program in Sudan. Protecting aid workers from people who threaten them is not censorship.

## The Defense, Stated Fairly

Samantha Power, the former Administrator, called the release a reckless, partisan disclosure of cherry-picked files, and said the work was aimed at foreign disinformation. Her defense deserves to be stated at full strength:


 	- Hostile governments such as Russia, China, and Iran run real influence operations, and some of them incite violence.

 	- USAID's mandate was foreign, and the 2024 appropriations law confined this funding to countering foreign efforts abroad.

 	- Counsel did restrain staff, and the files contain no request against an American speaker.

 	- The administration that published the files is an interested party. It chose what to release, and its landing page says plainly that it meant to show the agency had been "weaponized."


I accept points 1 and 4 without reservation. Point 4 cuts in both directions, though. Mrs. Power is an interested party too, and her statement is a denial from the person whose program is under review. That is testimony to be weighed. It is not evidence that settles the matter.

Points 2 and 3 are where the defense fails. They answer a narrower question than the one the files raise.

## The Question Under the Question

The public never asked only "Did USAID delete my Facebook post?" It asked something deeper: *Has my government come to believe that its job is to manage what people think, so that public belief lines up with official goals?* The files answer that question, and the answer does not depend on which country the monitors were sitting in.

Look again at the sentence that opened this essay. A fact-checker's only loyalty is to the facts. Once his work is measured by whether it "aligns with the objectives of government partners," he is no longer checking facts. He is doing public relations with a badge. Now set beside that the training that told USAID staff a set of *true* crime reports could count as "disinformation," because of the narrative it formed. Together, the two documents describe a system in which truth is not the test. The test is whether a story helps or hurts the people in power.

Scripture has a name for calling a true report false because it is inconvenient: *"Thou shalt not bear false witness against thy neighbour"* **(Exodus 20:16)**. And it has a warning for a whole society that learns to do it: *"Woe unto them that call evil good, and good evil; that put darkness for light, and light for darkness"* **(Isaiah 5:20)**. A state that hires others to label true things false has broken the ninth commandment, whatever it pays them and whatever the reason.

## Why "It Was Only Foreign" Does Not Rescue It

**First, it fails the moral test I apply to every policy.** Is it righteous? Is it loving? Would I want it done to me?

It is not righteous, because it subordinates truth to policy. It is not loving toward the farmer in Moldova or the voter in Guatemala, whose information was being shaped by a foreign government without his knowledge. To him, the American sponsor is invisible and the "independent" fact-check looks like independence. And I would not want it done to me. If the Chinese government paid monitors in my hometown to flag my posts to the platforms, and trained them to treat my true reports as "adversarial narratives," Americans would rightly call it an attack on our self-government. We are not entitled to do to other nations what we would call an attack if it were done to us. *"All things whatsoever ye would that men should do to you, do ye even so to them"* **(Matthew 7:12)**.

**Second, a weapon built abroad does not stay abroad.** The same years and the same vocabulary produced a domestic version. In 2022, the Department of Homeland Security launched a "Disinformation Governance Board," and public outcry closed it within months. In the *Murthy v.* *Missouri* litigation, the lower courts found that the White House and the FBI had likely pressured platforms over Americans' speech. The Supreme Court sent that case back on standing without ruling on whether the pressure was lawful. In 2024, Mark Zuckerberg wrote to Congress that the administration had repeatedly pressured Meta over COVID content, and that he regretted demoting the laptop story after FBI warnings. The USAID Files do not prove that USAID ran the domestic effort. They do show the same theory of "information integrity," the same partners, the same trusted-flagger channels, and the same instinct to keep the work out of sight. A man who builds a gun and trains himself to use it has not promised never to point it at home.

**Third, the lawyers' warnings condemn the program.** Counsel kept telling staff to stay viewpoint neutral, to avoid anything that looked like pressure, and to avoid an end run around Congress. Nobody gives that advice to people who are nowhere near the line. Pair those warnings with the advice to pass "the Washington Post test," to pick up the phone, and to get through the midterms without leaks, and you have a picture of people who knew the public would not consent. A government that has to hide what it is doing from the people who pay for it has already answered the question of whether it should be doing it.

## Why the Concern Remains Plausible

Some of the strongest claims circulating this week go beyond the documents, and I will name them below. But the underlying worry, that the American government was engaged in managing speech, does not rest on any single viral clip. Several things make it plausible:


 	- **The structure is unnatural.** In a free country, the government is supposed to be the thing the press checks. A system in which the government funds the checkers, introduces them to the platforms, and judges their work by its own objectives turns that relationship upside down. Most people sense this immediately, before they have read a single memo, and they are right.

 	- **The incentives all point one way.** Grants for "countering disinformation" grow when the threat looks larger. The organizations that rate outlets are paid by those who want the ratings. GDI's exclusion list reportedly placed dozens of American conservative outlets among the "riskiest," the same outlets that criticized the people funding the system. Nobody has to conspire for this to happen. The money does the steering.

 	- **The doctrine leads to the behavior.** Once "disinformation" means a narrative that undermines trust in institutions, rather than a falsehood, every critic of an institution becomes a suspect. The behavior people fear follows from the definition the trainers taught.

 	- **Each piece came out only when it was forced into the open.** The Disinformation Governance Board, the Election Integrity Partnership, and now these files all became public through outcry, litigation, or a change of administration. They did not come from voluntary disclosure. A pattern like that earns suspicion.


## Where the Coverage Outruns the Evidence

I agree with the *Not the Bee* piece on the central point, and I recommend it. But on a few claims it goes further than the files, and I would rather win this argument on solid ground:


 	- The files, as reviewed so far, contain **no USAID request to remove or demote content by an American or an American outlet.** The headline's suggestion that the files confirm "all the censorship stuff you thought" overstates what has been documented about domestic speech, though not what has been documented about intent and method.

 	- The claim that South America swung right *because* USAID was defunded is a correlation with many other causes. These files do not establish it.

 	- The accusations that USAID was a party slush fund or a CIA laundering tool are old and may have merit, but **these files are not the proof of them.**

 	- The link to the Cyber Threat Intelligence League is offered as a question, and it should stay a question until someone produces a document.

 	- I could not independently confirm the date or setting of the opening clip. Its words match the rest of the record, but readers should treat it as reported, not verified.


None of these cautions weakens the thesis. The documented facts are enough.

## What I Would Do

I offer these as positions I hold and will argue for, and I am open to a better argument.


 	- **No federal money to rate, flag, or downrank lawful speech, at home or abroad.** The government may speak in its own name, openly, as loudly as it likes. It may not pay third parties to suppress others' speech. I would ask Congress to write that rule into law for every agency, including State, DHS, and the intelligence community. I would enforce it by executive order until Congress acts.

 	- **Every government contact with a platform about content goes on the public record.** If an official asks a company to act on a post, the request, its reason, and the response should be logged and published within a set period. Exceptions should be narrow: credible threats of violence and crimes against children. The "pick up the phone" culture would end.

 	- **A true report is never "disinformation."** No federal training, grant, or contract may use a definition of disinformation that includes accurate information. That one rule would have stopped the Breitbart example cold.

 	- **Finish the accounting.** Release the rest, including the files that make the current administration's allies look bad, along with a complete index so that anyone can see what was withheld. Transparency that only cuts one way is just another narrative.

 	- **Protect real people from real threats.** Threats against aid workers and incitement to violence are crimes, and the platforms should handle them as crimes. That work does not need a "disinformation" bureaucracy, and it should not be used to justify one.


The deeper remedy is not a better government fact-checker. It is a people who check facts themselves. Scripture places that duty on each of us: *"Prove all things; hold fast that which is good"* **(1 Thessalonians 5:21)**. A free citizen does not need his government to pre-chew the news. He needs the truth, all of it, and the liberty to weigh it. *"And ye shall know the truth, and the truth shall make you free"* **(John 8:32)**. A government that fears its people knowing the truth has forgotten whom it serves.
