# 261112 - Social Media and Medical Censorship

# Who Decides What You May Hear?


### Dr. McCullough, Joe Rogan, and the Censorship Campaign

*By Thomas Lee Abshier, ND  | 12 November 2026*


---


On November 4, 2026, Dr. Peter McCullough sat down again with Joe Rogan, nearly five years after his first appearance on December 13, 2021. In the opening minute Rogan told him, "You were the first guy that got me canceled, I think, about COVID." Later in the conversation Rogan said the White House had called Spotify and called his management.

I want to use this reunion to take a stand on the question it raises. The question is not whether Dr. McCullough was right about every drug, every statistic, and every mechanism. He was not, and I will say where. The question is whether the government of the United States has any business deciding which doctors the American people may hear. My answer is no. I stand on the side of open medical speech, and I hold that the federal pressure campaign of 2021 and 2022 was wrong in principle, unwise in practice, and should be made unlawful by statute so that no future administration can repeat it.

I will lay out the record, the moral reasoning, what I would do as president, and the places where the evidence is uncertain or the language is slippery.


---


## What Happened

The record is not a matter of rumor. Much of it comes from the officials and companies themselves.

**The Facebook pressure.** In July 2021, then–Press Secretary Jen Psaki said publicly that the White House was flagging posts for Facebook and that Facebook needed to remove "harmful, violative posts" faster. President Biden said that month that social media platforms were "killing people." In August 2024, Mark Zuckerberg wrote to the House Judiciary Committee that in 2021 senior Biden administration officials, including from the White House, "repeatedly pressured our teams for months to censor certain COVID-19 content, including humor and satire," and expressed frustration when the company did not comply. He wrote: "I believe the government pressure was wrong, and I regret we were not more outspoken about it."

**The Spotify pressure.** Dr. McCullough's episode (#1747) aired December 13, 2021. YouTube removed the copy posted on his own channel. Dr. Robert Malone's episode (#1757) followed on December 31. In January 2022, 270 scientists, professors, and health workers sent Spotify an open letter demanding a misinformation policy, aimed chiefly at the Malone episode. Neil Young and other musicians pulled their music. Spotify added content advisories. On February 1, 2022, Psaki was asked whether that was enough. She called it "a positive step" and said "more can be done," and that the White House wanted every major platform to keep "doing more."

**The calls.** Rogan has said more than once that Spotify got calls from the top. In June 2025 he said Spotify received calls from "two former presidents." In the November 2026 episode he said Spotify got calls from the president and his management got calls from the White House. Spotify has not confirmed or denied this publicly. I treat Rogan's account as firsthand testimony from the man on the receiving end. He is in a position to know what his management was told. It deserves to be investigated, not dismissed. But it is not yet documented.

**The courts.** In *Missouri v.** Biden*, a federal district court and then the Fifth Circuit found that federal officials had likely coerced or significantly encouraged platforms to suppress protected speech. In June 2024 the Supreme Court, in *Murthy v.* *Missouri*, reversed 6–3 on standing. It did not reach the First Amendment question. The majority held that the plaintiffs had not shown that a specific removal could be traced to a specific government demand, and said some of the lower court's factual findings appeared "clearly erroneous." Justice Alito, joined by Justices Thomas and Gorsuch, dissented and said the plaintiffs were likely to prove that the executive branch coerced Facebook into censoring speech.

So the highest court never ruled that the pressure was lawful. It ruled that these particular plaintiffs could not sue. That is a door left open, not a verdict of innocence.

**The cost to the man.** Dr. McCullough left Baylor Scott & White in early 2021 and lost faculty posts, editorships, and, after a long appeal, both of his board certifications. The American Board of Internal Medicine recommended revocation in October 2022, saying he had "provided false or inaccurate medical information to the public," and the revocation was final by January 2025. A paper he co-authored on vaccine myocarditis was withdrawn by Elsevier in 2021. Another, on mRNA vaccines, was retracted by *Cureus* in 2024. He disputes each of these and has published his rebuttals.

## The Question Under the Question

When people argue about Dr. McCullough, they usually argue about ivermectin, spike protein, or myocarditis rates. Those are real questions. But I think the millions who shared clips of his interview were reaching for something deeper. They were asking: *Am I allowed to hear the other side?** Am I allowed to decide for myself?*

That is the question under the question. It does not depend on McCullough being right. It depends on whether a free people may weigh evidence about their own bodies, or whether that weighing must be done for them by officials who will not show their work.

Scripture gives the principle plainly: *"He that is first in his own cause seemeth just; but his neighbour cometh and searcheth him."* **(Proverbs 18:17)** The first speaker always sounds right until he is cross-examined. A public health establishment that is never cross-examined will always sound right, and it will be wrong more often than it knows.


---


## Two Questions, Not One

I want to keep two questions separate, because blending them is how both sides mislead.

**First question: Was the censorship wrong?** Yes. That answer does not wait on the second question.

**Second question: Was Dr. McCullough right?** On some points, yes, or at least closer to right than his censors. On one major point, ivermectin, the question is still open, though the official record says it is closed. On a few others, his sources do not hold up.

Where the dissenters were vindicated, or the official line was overconfident:


 	- **Lab origin.** In 2020 and 2021, the suggestion that the virus came from a Wuhan laboratory was treated as a conspiracy theory and suppressed on major platforms. Today the FBI, the Department of Energy, and (since January 2025) the CIA judge a lab-related origin "most likely." They hold that view with low to moderate confidence and do not agree on which lab. It is not proven. But it was never disinformation.

 	- **Transmission.** The public was told vaccination would stop the spread. By August 2021 the CDC director herself acknowledged the vaccines could not prevent transmission in breakthrough cases. Mandates nevertheless continued on the theory that the unvaccinated endangered others.

 	- **Myocarditis.** In June 2021 the FDA added a myocarditis warning to the mRNA vaccine fact sheets, especially for young men. Raising that risk was not misinformation. It was a label the regulator itself adopted.

 	- **Remdesivir.** In November 2020 the WHO issued a conditional recommendation against remdesivir for hospitalized patients, finding no meaningful effect on mortality in its Solidarity trial, while U.S. hospitals continued to use it as the standard of care. Reasonable doctors differed. That is the point: it was a live question, not settled science.

 	- **Hospital incentives.** The CARES Act gave hospitals a 20% Medicare add-on payment for inpatients diagnosed with COVID-19. Pointing out that payment structures shape behavior is not a conspiracy theory. It is how economics works.


Where the question is still open:


 	- **Ivermectin and early treatment.** I prescribed ivermectin during the pandemic. In some patients I saw it work dramatically. In others it did not. The difference I saw was timing: it worked best when it was started early. I also know physicians who treated very sick patients with a protocol broader than ivermectin alone, and their recovery rates were far better than the severity of the disease would have predicted. That is clinical observation, not a randomized trial. It is not proof. But it is not nothing, either. It is firsthand testimony from doctors who were in the room with the patients.The official record says the large randomized trials — TOGETHER in Brazil, ACTIV-6 in the United States, COVID-OUT, and PRINCIPLE in the United Kingdom — found no clinically meaningful benefit for outpatients. I take those trials seriously. But they did not test what Dr. McCullough and doctors like me were actually doing. They tested ivermectin by itself, not the sequential, multidrug protocol he published in 2020. They enrolled patients up to a week after symptoms began, when the window for an antiviral to matter may already be closing. Several enrolled mostly vaccinated, lower-risk people, among whom so few were hospitalized that the trials could barely test whether the drug kept anyone out of the hospital. Even the TOGETHER trial could not rule out a modest benefit. I do not know whether those design choices were careless, constrained, or chosen to produce an answer. I do know that a trial built that way cannot settle the question its headlines claimed to settle.

So I will not tell the patients who recovered, or the doctors who treated them, that they imagined it. Nor will I claim the matter is proven the other way. The honest statement is this: the trials that were run do not support ivermectin as a stand-alone treatment given late. Whether early, combined treatment saves lives is a question the medical establishment never properly tested. The censorship campaign made sure the doctors raising it could not be heard while it was being decided.


Where his sources do not hold up:


 	- **Retracted and withdrawn papers.** The athlete cardiac-arrest figures and some VAERS-based estimates he has cited rest on sources that do not meet the standard of evidence he himself demands when he cites papers by first author. The journals' decisions to retract are interested-party judgments, and I do not treat them as final. But the underlying data problems they named are real enough that I would not build a case on those papers.


So Rogan's line, "It was your misinformation that all turned out to be true," goes too far. Not all of it did. But enough did — on the origin, on transmission, on myocarditis, on the narrowness of permitted debate — that the censors forfeited any claim to be the referees of truth. If the referee is wrong about the biggest calls of the game, he cannot be trusted to decide who gets to play.

## Why Censorship Was Wrong Even Where the Censors Were Right

I test every policy by three questions, in order. Is it righteous? If so, is it loving? And would I want it done to me?

**Is it righteous?** Righteousness begins with what God has established, not with what is convenient for the state. God gave every man a conscience and a duty to *"prove all things; hold fast that which is good."* **(1 Thessalonians 5:21)** A man cannot prove what he is forbidden to hear. When government reaches through private companies to silence a licensed physician speaking about his own clinical experience, it usurps the conscience of every citizen who would have weighed that physician's words. It also violates the plain intent of the First Amendment, which forbids Congress — and by fair extension the whole federal government — from abridging the freedom of speech. Doing indirectly, by phone call and public shaming, what it may not do directly is not a loophole. It is the same wrong with better deniability.

**Is it loving?** The defenders of the pressure campaign will say it was loving: people were dying, and false information could kill them. I take that seriously. Some people did take bad advice, and some were harmed. But love that treats adults as children who cannot be trusted with an argument is not love. It is management. A loving government answers bad information with better information, published openly, with the data shown. Dr. McCullough asked in February 2022 why officials who called him a misinformer did not simply invite him to review his data. That was the loving path, and it was not taken.

**Would I want it done to me?** *"Therefore all things whatsoever ye would that men should do to you, do ye even so to them."* **(Matthew 7:12)** I am a naturopathic physician. My views on diet and chronic disease are outside the mainstream. If the federal government decided my articles were "misinformation" and pressed the platforms to bury them, I would consider that a grave wrong — not because I am certain I am right on every point, but because I have a right to make my case and my neighbor has a right to judge it. I cannot claim that right for myself and deny it to Dr. McCullough, or to the doctors who disagree with him. The rule has to cut both ways or it is not a rule.

The censorship campaign fails all three tests. It was not righteous, because it seized a judgment that belongs to the citizen's conscience. It was not loving, because it replaced persuasion with suppression. And no official who carried it out would want it done to himself.

## What I Would Do

I offer these as positions I hold and will defend, open to a better argument.

**1. Put the rule in statute, not just in an executive order.** On January 20, 2025, President Trump signed Executive Order 14149, "Restoring Freedom of Speech and Ending Federal Censorship," which forbids federal employees from using federal resources to abridge protected speech and directs an investigation of the prior four years. I support it. But an executive order can be revoked with a pen on the next inauguration day. I would urge Congress to pass, and I would sign, a law that forbids any federal officer from requesting, urging, or pressuring a private platform to remove, demote, or label lawful speech, with a civil cause of action for any citizen whose speech is suppressed as a result. The *Murthy* decision showed that without such a law, a citizen can almost never prove standing. The law should fix that.

**2. Require full disclosure of every contact.** Any communication between a federal official and a platform about specific content or users should be logged and published within thirty days. Sunlight is the cure for the quiet phone call. If an official believes a post is dangerous, let him say so publicly, in his own name, and let the public judge his reasoning.

**3. Answer speech with speech.** In the next health emergency, federal agencies should publish their underlying data, their uncertainty, and the arguments against their own recommendations. They should hold open debates with credentialed dissenters. A public health establishment that will not debate has already admitted it cannot win the argument.

**4. Use the president's voice on the private gatekeepers.** Medical licensing is a state matter, and certification boards like the ABIM are private bodies. The federal government should not run them. But a president can and should say plainly that a board which strips credentials for public speech — rather than for harm to a patient in the clinic — is confusing orthodoxy with competence. I would urge state legislatures and medical boards to adopt that distinction.

## Why the Concern Remains Plausible

Some will say the censorship worry is overblown: the Supreme Court found no proven link, the platforms made their own choices, and the officials were only "encouraging." I want to explain why I do not find that reassuring.

First, there is the intuitive sense that something was unnatural. Nations that cannot agree on borders, currency, or trade agreed almost overnight on lockdowns, mask rules, and a single permitted treatment narrative. Real science is messy and slow. Uniformity that fast suggests coordination, or fear, or both — not discovery.

Second, the money was enormous. Vaccine makers earned tens of billions of dollars. Hospitals were paid more for COVID diagnoses. Platforms depended on government goodwill for their regulatory futures. When those incentives all point the same way, honest people can drift into the same conclusion without ever conspiring.

Third, the link between doctrine and behavior is visible. The phrase "misinformation" was turned from a description into a verdict. Once a claim wore that label, no further argument was required to suppress it. That machinery did not disappear when the pandemic ended. It is waiting for the next emergency.

Fourth, the people who carried it out have not had to answer for it. Rogan asked whether anyone ever apologized. Except for Mr. Zuckerberg's letter, the answer is essentially no.

## Notes on the Source Article

*These notes concern the Focal Points newsletter that prompted this essay. They are corrections or clarifications, not criticisms of its central concern, which I share.*


 	- **Board certification.** The newsletter describes Dr. McCullough as "board-certified." The ABIM revoked both his internal medicine and cardiology certifications; the revocation was final by January 2025 after his appeal. He remains licensed to practice in Texas. The accurate description is that he was board-certified for decades and was stripped of those certifications in a disciplinary action he disputes.

 	- **Who "got Rogan canceled."** The organized backlash in January 2022 — the 270-signer letter and Neil Young's departure — centered on Dr. Malone's December 31 episode (#1757), not Dr. McCullough's (#1747). Dr. McCullough's episode came first and his YouTube copy was removed, but the two episodes together drove the controversy.

 	- **"The most widely used treatment protocol in the world."** I could find no data that measures this. The 2020 *American Journal of Medicine* paper was widely read and cited. Whether it was the most widely used protocol is unknown.

 	- **"Since the virus came out of the Wuhan Institute of Virology."** That is the leading hypothesis in U.S. intelligence, but it is held with low to moderate confidence, and the Energy Department favors a different Wuhan lab. It is a strong probability, not an established fact.

 	- **The White House calls to Spotify.** These rest on Rogan's account. They are credible and worth investigating, but not yet documented in the public record.

## Sources and Their Limits

My sourcing leans heavily on the mainstream record: court opinions, government documents, wire services, medical journals, and fact-checkers. Some of those sources were themselves participants in the controversy. The journals that retracted Dr. McCullough's papers, the board that revoked his certifications, the agencies that issued the guidance, and the platforms that removed content are all interested parties, and their judgments about their own conduct should be read as testimony, not as final verdicts.

Where curation could have hidden evidence: content removed in 2021 and 2022 is now hard to recover, so the full range of what was suppressed is not knowable from public archives. Internal government–platform communications are known only through litigation discovery, congressional subpoenas, and voluntary disclosures like Mr. Zuckerberg's; much remains private. Clinical trials of repurposed drugs were funded and designed largely within the same establishment whose recommendations were in dispute. I have tried to account for this by stating the strongest case for the dissenting side alongside the trial results, and by treating firsthand reports like Rogan's as leads rather than either proof or noise.
