# The Millstone:


### What Our Schools Teach Children About Sex and Gender


Oct 27, 2026 · @Thomas Abshier


## Plain language summary


More than 500 school districts use a sexuality curriculum called Rights, Respect, Responsibility (the “3Rs”). It begins in kindergarten. Over the grades, it teaches children that a person’s body may not match who he “really” is, that gender is a matter of self-identification, and, by high school, how to use condoms and what rights minors have to abortion. Parents in many districts must find out about it and opt their children out; if they do nothing, the child is taught.


I hold that this is an abomination, and I do not use the word loosely. My philosophy, teaching, and platform are that God is not a distant observer of our lives. He experiences every life from the inside, including every child in every classroom. When a child is taught that his body is a suggestion rather than a gift, God experiences that confusion within the child. Jesus said that whoever causes one of these little ones to stumble would be better off drowned with a millstone around his neck. A nation that authorizes this teaching is placing that millstone around its own neck.


My position is not that parents should be allowed to opt out of this teaching, or even that they should have to opt in. It is that no public school should teach any child that gender is optional or changeable, and that no taxpayer dollar should pay for it. Education belongs to the local community, and every school board should control what its schools teach. In my administration, I will speak against this teaching wherever it appears, urge every community to vote out school board members who advocate it, and direct the Department of Education to withdraw federal funds and support from any school that teaches it. Sexual purity and the recognition of God-given sex and its roles will be among the clearest marks of my campaign and my administration.


*Provenance: Claude drafted this essay in my voice at my request on 27 October 2026, after checking an Alliance Defending Freedom fundraising email against the curriculum publisher’s own lesson summaries. I’ve marked points where I am taking a position that’s still open to a better argument.*


## How this came to my attention


An email from Alliance Defending Freedom (ADF), sent through a paid sponsorship, described a K–12 sex-education curriculum grade by grade and asked readers to sign a petition. Fundraising emails are written to alarm, so before writing anything I had the claims checked against the curriculum’s own documents, published by its author, Advocates for Youth.


The result: the email is substantially true. In two places it slants the evidence (noted below), and in several places it understates what the curriculum contains. I base this paper on the publisher’s own words, not ADF’s description, because the publisher’s words are enough.


## What the curriculum teaches


The following is drawn from Advocates for Youth’s published lesson summaries for *Rights, Respect, Responsibility*.


**Kindergarten.** A lesson on families, a lesson in which children place labels for body parts on a body diagram, and a lesson on personal space and unwanted touch.


**First grade.** A gender-roles lesson built around the picture book *My Princess Boy*, followed by discussion of how to treat people who behave differently than we expect. A reproduction lesson with a worksheet titled “Can This Make Babies?” (ADF’s email implied this worksheet was sexual. It is not; it asks children which living and nonliving things reproduce.)


**Second grade.** An anatomy lesson teaching children the names of sexual and reproductive body parts, framed around body parts that “mostly” girls have and “mostly” boys have, and distinguishing people by their “sex assigned at birth.”


**Fourth and fifth grades.** Puberty. A fifth-grade anatomy lesson in which teachers are encouraged to tell ten-year-olds that “sometimes people can have reproductive parts that don’t match who they are.” A lesson defining sexual orientation, with anonymous questions collected from the children.


**Sixth grade.** A lesson on gender stereotypes, a lesson on romantic relationships, and the “Sex Ed Sleuth” lesson, in which small groups evaluate online sexual-health websites. (ADF’s email said this research is done unsupervised. The publisher says the homework is done with a parent. Critics in California have argued that the recommended sites link onward to graphic material; that claim deserves examination but is not established here.)


**Seventh grade.** Anatomy of persons “assigned female at birth” and “assigned male at birth.” A lesson defining sexual orientation and gender identity. A role-play in which students script a couple communicating about a sexual situation, with guidance for the teacher on responding to “homophobia” if two boys act out the scenario.


**Eighth grade.** Students assess their school’s climate for LGBTQ students and propose how the school should improve it. Birth control. A condom-use sequence demonstrated by the teacher.


**Ninth grade and above.** Gender identity, cisgender and transgender defined, with homework from the television show *I Am Jazz*. Students practice putting a condom on a penis model or a banana in pairs, then are assigned to go to a store and attempt to buy condoms. A lesson on the options for unintended pregnancy, including abortion. In twelfth grade, students research minors’ rights in their state, including the right to an abortion. Students in tenth grade prepare a lobbying campaign to their principal on the school’s climate for LGBTQ students.


Advocates for Youth reports that the curriculum is used in more than 500 school districts and reaches more than five million students. The publisher states that it intends the curriculum to be used in its entirety, while allowing districts to select lessons. Some districts adopt it with opt-out procedures that require parents to know the lessons exist and take action to remove their child.


The curriculum contains some sound material. Teaching a five-year-old that no adult may touch him in secret, and that he must tell a trusted adult if one does, is good. Lessons on bullying and on friendship are good. But a poisoned well is not made safe by the clean water poured into it. The sound lessons could be taught without the doctrine, and the doctrine is the point of the curriculum. Its own teacher’s guide states that it is designed to be inclusive of all gender identities and that the concept is explored across every grade level.


## Why this is not a minor matter: God experiences the life of the child


Most Christian teaching pictures each of us as a separate creature standing before God, trying to please Him from the outside. That picture is true, but it is half the picture. The other half is central to everything I teach, in physics, in psychology, and in politics.


God is not a spectator reading a report on His creation. He is the One whose continuous attention sustains every creature in every moment, and so He knows each life *as that creature lives it*. I call this His experiential indwelling. In plain words: God experiences your life from inside you. We are points of observation within the One God, distinct from Him, yet never outside Him. The contemplative traditions of the East have long seen that all is one; the Christian tradition has long seen that God is holy and that we stand before Him as creatures. Both are true, and the truth of each gives the other its weight. Because God indwells each of us, every act we do and every act done to us occurs within His own experience.


Consider what that means in a second-grade classroom.


A seven-year-old boy is sitting at his desk. The teacher, kind and well-intentioned, tells him that body parts like his are what *mostly* boys have. Three years later, she tells him that sometimes a person’s body does not match who he is. By seventh grade, he is defining gender identity on a worksheet. God is in that boy. God experiences the boy’s trust in his teacher, his acceptance of what the adult in authority has told him, and the seed of doubt planted in him about the most basic fact of his own existence: that God made him male, and that his body is the evidence.


God does not experience that as a neutral lesson. He experiences it as a lie told to a child He loves, inside the child. Scripture is not ambiguous about how God receives this. *So God created man in his own image, in the image of God created he him; male and female created he them* (Genesis 1:27). To teach a child that this is optional is to teach him to doubt the first thing God said about him.


## The millstone


Jesus spoke more severely about this offense than about almost any other:

>
*But whoso shall offend one of these little ones which believe in me, it were better for him that a millstone were hanged about his neck, and that he were drowned in the depth of the sea.* (Matthew 18:6)


>
*And whosoever shall offend one of these little ones that believe in me, it is better for him that a millstone were hanged about his neck, and he were cast into the sea.* (Mark 9:42)


The Greek word translated “offend,” *skandalizō*, means to place a stumbling block, to cause to fall. It does not mean to hurt someone’s feelings. It means to trip a child on the path to God, so that he grows up walking the wrong way.


That is what this curriculum does. A child who is taught, gradually and kindly, from kindergarten forward, that sex is assigned rather than given, that his body may be mistaken, and that the right response to confusion is affirmation rather than correction, is being bent. He is being shaped to grow up revering what God calls an abomination and treating the reality God created as an oppression to be overcome. The teacher who delivers the lesson stands, whether he knows it or not, in the place of the one who sets the stumbling block. That is the position the school board and the state have put him in.


And this is a matter for nations, not only for individuals. God deals with peoples as well as persons. *Righteousness exalteth a nation: but sin is a reproach to any people* (Proverbs 14:34). A nation that authorizes, funds, and requires this teaching is not merely tolerating a private sin; it is officially offending the little ones in God’s own experience of their lives. That is not a small provocation. It is a declaration of war against the Almighty, and the outcome of that war is not in doubt. I do not want my country to go there.


## Compassion is not the same thing as affirmation


The defenders of this curriculum will say it teaches tolerance and protects vulnerable children. Some children do struggle with confusion about their sex, and some adults struggle with same-sex attraction. They deserve compassion, patience, and the company of people who love them. I say that without reservation.


But compassion is not affirmation of the confusion. *Thou shalt not hate thy brother in thine heart: thou shalt in any wise rebuke thy neighbour, and not suffer sin upon him* (Leviticus 19:17). Humoring a neighbor in his error is not love. A physician who tells a patient with a dangerous illness that he is perfectly well is not being kind. A teacher who tells a confused boy that his confusion may be his truest self is not being kind either.


Here the difference between the two pictures of God becomes stark. If God is outside the child, looking on, then perhaps a little ambiguity in the curriculum is a small matter between the child and his Maker. But if God is inside the child, experiencing the confusion as it is planted, then the soft, tolerant, benign version of this teaching is not neutral ground. It is the ground of the evil one, dressed in the appearance of fair-mindedness. To stand there and call it compassion is to become an ally of the one who wants the child lost.


## The test applied


I evaluate every public policy by three questions, in order.


**1. Is it righteous?** Is it consistent with what God has established? God established that man is created male and female and that a child is to be raised in the knowledge of the Lord by his father and mother (Deuteronomy 6:6–7; Ephesians 6:4). A curriculum that teaches children that sex is assigned at birth and may be mistaken, and that places the state between a child and his parents on matters of sexuality, is not righteous. The analysis could stop here.


**2. If so, is it loving?** It does not reach this question. But even its defenders’ best case, the protection of confused children from bullying, can be met without teaching every other child that his body may be lying to him.


**3. Would I want it done to me?** Would I want, as a child, to be taught by the adults I trusted that my body might be a mistake? Would I want, as a father, to discover that my second-grader had been taught this unless I had found and signed a form? No man who has felt his own child’s trust would want this done to him or to his child.


## What I will do


These are positions I take and hold open to a better argument.


### Not a consent question


Many reformers ask that parents be allowed to opt their children out of gender instruction, or that schools require parents to opt in. Those are improvements over the present, but they concede the essential point: that it is a proper use of public money and public authority to teach some children that gender is optional, so long as the paperwork is in order. I do not concede it. A lie does not become acceptable because a parent signed a form. The question is not who consents to the teaching; it is whether the public should pay for it at all. It should not.


### 1. Education belongs to the community


Education should be local. Every school board should control what its schools teach, and every school board answers to the community that elects it. That is where this will finally be decided, and it is where it should be decided. I will encourage every community in America to examine what its schools teach about sex and gender, and to vote out every school board member who advocates teaching children that gender is optional or changeable. Parents and neighbors who attend board meetings, read the lessons, and stand for office are doing the most important civic work of this generation.


### 2. The voice of the President


A President cannot write a curriculum, and should not. But he decides what he lends his voice to. In my administration I will speak out against any school that teaches any child that gender is optional or changeable. I will not lend my voice, my office, or my approval to that teaching in any form. The office of the President is a pulpit, and I will use it to say plainly what God has made plain: He created man male and female, and a child’s body is the gift that tells him which he is.


### 3. No federal money for gender confusion


I will direct the Department of Education to withdraw federal funds, aid, and support from any school that teaches gender confusion, and I will ask Congress to write that condition into law so that it does not depend on who holds the office. No federal grant shall fund an organization that produces or promotes curricula teaching gender identity to minors. Advocates for Youth has reportedly received tens of millions of dollars from the Centers for Disease Control since 1995.


This is not federal control of curriculum; it is the opposite. The federal government will not tell a local school what to teach. It will decline to pay for what it judges to be harm to children. A community that wishes to teach gender confusion with its own money will answer to its own voters, and to God. It will not do it with money taken from the rest of the nation.


### 4. Protect the teacher’s conscience


No teacher shall be disciplined for refusing to teach that a child’s sex is assigned or that his body may be mistaken. The teacher should never be put in the place of the one who sets the stumbling block.


### 5. Honor the parents’ authority


In 2025 the Supreme Court held, six to three in *Mahmoud v. Taylor*, that Maryland parents had a constitutional right to withdraw their children from lessons using LGBTQ-themed storybooks that burdened their religious exercise. Where a school continues such teaching in defiance of the community’s will, every parent should have that protection without having to sue for it. And the material that remains properly part of health education, anatomy with correct names, puberty, and protection from abuse, should be posted publicly in full and taught only with the parent’s knowledge.


### 6. Support parents who teach their own children


The surest protection is a family that teaches its children itself. Christos Homeschool exists for this reason, and public policy should make it easier, not harder, for parents to choose it.


### A mark of this campaign


Sexual purity, and the recognition of the sexes and their roles as God made them, will be one of the most prominent and identifiable marks of my campaign and my administration. I do not raise this issue reluctantly or as a concession to one constituency. I raise it because a nation that teaches its children to doubt how God made them has lost its way at the root, and every other reform I propose depends on recovering it.


## Why the concern stands


The data on outcomes are not settled, and I will not pretend they are. No study I know of isolates the effect of this specific curriculum on how children come to identify. But the concern is not a guess, and it does not wait on a study.


First, the intuitive sense of wrongness that nearly every parent feels on reading these lessons is not bigotry. It is the conscience recognizing what God wrote on the heart (Romans 2:15). A curriculum that must be adopted over parents’ objections and taught under opt-out rules is a curriculum whose authors know how parents would vote.


Second, the authors are not neutral educators. Advocates for Youth describes its own work as training thousands of young activists each year to lead in the progressive movement. A curriculum written by an advocacy organization teaches its advocacy.


Third, the pattern of identification has shifted sharply in the generation that grew up under this kind of instruction. Gallup found that the share of American adults identifying as LGBTQ has more than doubled since 2012, driven largely by adults under thirty, and nearly one in four adults in Generation Z now identifies that way. Correlation is not proof of cause. But when a doctrine is taught to children from kindergarten forward, and the children grow up believing it in numbers no previous generation approached, the burden of proof falls on those who say the teaching had nothing to do with it.


I stand on the side of the children and their parents, and against this curriculum. The areas of uncertainty are the exact causal weight of any one curriculum and the variation in how districts adapt it. Neither changes what the curriculum teaches, nor what God experiences when a child is taught it.


## Conclusion


The question is not whether we are kind. It is whether we will keep faith with the children God has placed in our care and in whom He lives. We are not permitted to plant doubt about God’s creation in a child and call it education. The millstone is not my threat. It is Christ’s warning, and a warning given in time is an act of love. A nation that heeds it can still turn back.

### Editorial notes


- **Lesson summaries date from 2018.** Advocates for Youth published a 2024 teacher’s guide; some lesson wording may have changed. Before publication, check the current lessons at 3rs.org, especially the second- and fifth-grade anatomy lessons quoted here.

- **“More than 500 districts, more than 5 million students”** is from Advocates for Youth’s own IRS Form 990 (program year April 2023 – March 2024), as summarized by a nonprofit-data site. ADF’s “13 states” figure was not verified.

- **Federal funding.** The figure of $26 million from the CDC since 1995 is reported by the Washington Free Beacon. Confirm against USAspending.gov before stating a number in print; the text currently says “tens of millions” and “reportedly.”

- **Gallup figures.** About 9% of U.S. adults identified as LGBTQ+ in Gallup’s 2025 data, more than double 2012. The Gen Z figure (about 23%) is from Gallup’s February 2025 report on 2024 data; confirm before publication.

- **Federal funding withdrawal: legal path.** The General Education Provisions Act (20 U.S.C. §1232a) bars the Department of Education from exercising “direction, supervision, or control” over curriculum. A funding cutoff tied to curriculum content will be challenged under that statute and under the Spending Clause rule that conditions on federal money must be stated unambiguously by Congress in advance (*Pennhurst*, 1981; *South Dakota v. Dole*, 1987). That is why the text asks Congress to write the condition into law. Executive Order 14190, “Ending Radical Indoctrination in K-12 Schooling” (Jan. 29, 2025), already directed agencies to plan funding measures against gender-ideology instruction; check the current state of its implementation and litigation before publication, and decide whether to cite it as precedent.

- **Mahmoud v. Taylor** was decided June 27, 2025, 6–3, Justice Alito writing. It granted a preliminary injunction and rested on the Free Exercise Clause, so the right it recognizes is tied to religious objection.

- **“Not pantheism.”** Earlier writing (Christos Civitas Code, ch. 3) states that creator and creature remain distinct; the formulation here (“points of observation within the One God, distinct from Him, yet never outside Him”) is meant to hold both sides. Revise if you want the Eastern parallel stated more strongly.
