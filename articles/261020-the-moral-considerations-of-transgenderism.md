# 261020 - The Moral Considerations of Transgenderism

# The Arithmetic of Murder: What the Zizians Teach About Justice and Righteousness


*A position paper from Dr. Thomas for President, responding to Tucker Carlson’s October 19, 2026 interview with Andy Ngo about his book* The Zizians: Inside a Trans Death Cult.


---


## Why This Story Deserves a Position


Most crime stories don’t warrant a presidential position. This one does, for two reasons.


First, it is a moral parable written in blood. A small group of brilliant young people, trained to reason about morality as arithmetic, followed the arithmetic to murder and believed they had done nothing wrong. That is the end of a road many in our culture are walking more slowly.


Second, it exposes ordinary failures in our justice system that have nothing to do with ideology and everything to do with whether we protect innocent people. An old man was nearly killed, waited more than two years for a trial that never came, and was murdered before he could testify. That should not happen in any American courtroom, and we can prevent it.


I’ll start with the facts, then the moral lesson, then the policy.


## What Happened


The group outsiders call the “Zizians” formed around Jack LaSota, known as “Ziz,” a computer-science prodigy from Fairbanks, Alaska, who came to the San Francisco Bay Area in the mid-2010s to join the “rationalist” and “effective altruist” communities. Those communities, centered on internet forums and Bay Area nonprofits, try to reason toward the greatest good through logic and calculation. Many of their members are preoccupied with the risk that artificial intelligence will destroy humanity.


LaSota built his own doctrine on top of theirs. In his writings, he taught that each hemisphere of the brain contains a separate person, that each may be “good” or “evil,” and that a select few, including himself, were good on both sides and called to save the world. Followers were encouraged to separate their hemispheres through prolonged sleep deprivation. LaSota, a biological male, transitioned after arriving in California and taught that men who identify as women possess special cognitive gifts for this mission. The group lived on old boats and then in box trucks, outside the economy and outside ordinary society.


Authorities have linked the group to six deaths; Ngo counts eight by including two former members who took their own lives. The chain of events, as charged by prosecutors and reported by the press:


- **November 13, 2022, Vallejo, California.** Curtis Lind, a 79-year-old landlord who had befriended the group and let them live on his land, sought to evict them after years without rent. Days before the eviction deadline, he was struck on the head, run through the chest with a sword, and lost an eye. He shot two attackers, killing one (Emma Borhanian) and wounding another (Alexander Leatham). Prosecutors charged Leatham and Suri Dao with attempted murder and, under California’s provocative-act doctrine, with Borhanian’s death. LaSota, who had earlier faked his own drowning, was found at the scene, was not held, and disappeared.

- **December 31, 2022, Chester Heights, Pennsylvania.** Richard and Rita Zajko, parents of member Michelle Zajko, were shot dead in their home hours after Rita texted her estranged daughter trying to reconcile. Michelle Zajko was charged with their murder in June 2026; the district attorney said she did not act alone but named no one else.

- **January 17, 2025, Vallejo.** Curtis Lind, now 82 and still waiting to testify, was ambushed outside his property and killed with a blade. Prosecutors allege Maximilian Snyder lay in wait for him to keep him from testifying. Snyder has pleaded not guilty.

- **January 20, 2025, near the Canadian border in Vermont.** Border Patrol Agent David Maland was shot and killed during a traffic stop. One occupant, Ophelia Bauckholt, was killed in the exchange; the other, Teresa Youngblut, was wounded and faces federal charges. Youngblut had applied for a marriage license with Snyder. Zajko is charged with supplying the gun.

- **February 16, 2025, western Maryland.** LaSota, Zajko, and Daniel Blank were found living in box trucks on a snowbound property and arrested on trespass, gun, and drug charges. LaSota also faces a federal charge of illegal gun possession by a fugitive. He has not been charged in any death.


As of this writing, nearly four years after Curtis Lind was impaled, no one has been tried for it. One defendant has been found incompetent to stand trial; the other has disrupted hearings and attempted escape. The only living witness to the attack, the victim himself, is dead.


Every person named above is presumed innocent of any charge not yet proved. That presumption is not a technicality. It is part of what makes justice just. But the presumption of innocence protects the accused at trial, not a reason the trial should never happen.


## The Moral Lesson: Calculation Without Righteousness


The most important sentence in the interview is Ngo’s report that when the leaders were arrested, they kept saying they had done nothing wrong. I believe they meant it.


Effective altruism, at its best, asks a fair question: how can I do the most good with what I have? Most people in that movement give money to fight malaria and would never harm anyone. But the underlying method treats morality as a sum. Every being is a unit of suffering or happiness; the right act is the one that produces the best total. Within that method, no act is wrong in itself. Only acts whose numbers come out badly are wrong. If the arithmetic says that one man stands in the way of saving billions, whether billions of people or, as some in these circles seriously argue, billions of farmed shrimp, the arithmetic says to remove him.


The Zizians did not betray that logic. They obeyed it without the restraint that ordinary decency and fear of consequences give everyone else. That is why the story matters beyond its body count. It shows what a brilliant mind does with ethics that has no floor.


Righteousness is the floor. “Thou shalt not kill” is not one weight among others to balance against projected benefits. It is a boundary drawn by the One who made both the killer and the victim, and it is not ours to recalculate. This is why I hold that every public act must pass three questions in order:


- **Is it righteous?** Does it stay within what God has established as right?

- **If so, is it loving?** Does it actually serve my neighbor’s good?

- **Would I want it done to me?** Not to my momentary appetite, but to the self committed to righteousness.


The Zizians began with the second question and never asked the first. They loved “humanity” and “sentient beings” in the abstract, and so felt free to kill a particular old man who had given them a place to live. Love without righteousness becomes a license. Righteousness is what keeps love from becoming murder.


There is a second thread. LaSota wrote that he regarded his own male body as an alien appendage and that puberty was the death of part of his soul. He then taught followers to split their own minds by depriving themselves of sleep until they broke. A doctrine that begins in contempt for the body God gave you does not stay confined to your own body. I say this with care: I do not claim that people who identify as transgender share LaSota’s beliefs or his violence. I claim something narrower and, I think, undeniable: a worldview that treats the created body as an enemy and other persons as numbers will, given enough time and enough isolation, treat other persons’ bodies as obstacles.


Finally, there is an irony worth noting for readers of this site’s series on artificial intelligence. These were people who feared that a powerful intelligence without aligned values would destroy humanity. They then became small, powerful intelligences without aligned values. The danger was never intelligence. It was intelligence severed from righteousness.


## Where I Part Company With the Interview


I agree with much of what Andy Ngo reported, and I respect the risk he has taken to report it. But this site holds to a rule of proportion, and two parts of the interview don’t meet it.


**The claim of a surge in “trans violence.”** Ngo describes rising violence by transgender people, and on Carlson’s broadcast that claim was stated without numbers. The databases that track mass shootings do not support it. The Violence Prevention Project, which tracks mass killings in public places, lists one transgender perpetrator among roughly two hundred since 1966. The Gun Violence Archive, using a broader definition, lists a handful among thousands of incidents since 2013. One gun-rights research group, the Crime Prevention Research Center, using its own narrower slice of recent years, finds transgender shooters overrepresented relative to their small share of the population. The honest summary is that the cases are very rare, the definitions disagree, and the question is not settled. Ngo himself said in the interview that not enough research has been done. A claim that cannot yet be measured should not be stated as a trend.


Ngo’s strongest point here is one I accept: we cannot measure this well because many agencies and news outlets record suspects only by their chosen names and pronouns, so the data needed to answer the question is never collected. That is an argument for better records, which I propose below. It is not proof of the trend.


**Collective blame.** A cult is responsible for its own actions. The law of God has always held each person accountable for his own sin: *“The son shall not bear the iniquity of the father, neither shall the father bear the iniquity of the son”* **(Ezekiel 18:20)**. The same principle forbids punishing a whole class of people for the acts of a few who share a trait with them. If my neighbor’s son joined a violent cult that used my faith’s vocabulary, I would not want every Christian in America held answerable for him. The third question, “Would I want it done to me?”, settles this one quickly.


I also decline Mr. Carlson’s suggestion that cross-sex hormones explain the violence. They may contribute to mood and aggression in some people; some evidence supports that, and it deserves serious study. But a man who changes his body chemistry and a man who calculates that a landlord may be killed are making two different decisions. The second is the moral decision, and the law punishes it.


## What I Would Do


### 1. Preserve the testimony of vulnerable witnesses at once


Curtis Lind’s testimony died with him. It did not have to. California law already permits a *conditional examination*: sworn, cross-examined testimony taken before trial and preserved for use if the witness later cannot appear. It applies to witnesses who are elderly, ill, or in danger. Lind was all three, and a California court used exactly this procedure for one of Lind’s friends in this same case. A California court should have done the same for Lind in 2023.


*Position:* In every violent felony case where the victim or a key witness is over 65, seriously injured, or credibly threatened, prosecutors should be required to seek a recorded, cross-examined deposition within ninety days of charging, and federal grants to state prosecutors should be conditioned on that practice. The accused keeps his constitutional right to confront the witness, because the cross-examination happens. The witness keeps his voice even if he is killed.


Separately, our law already holds that a defendant who kills a witness to silence him forfeits his right to object to that witness’s earlier statements. Prosecutors should use that doctrine fully whenever the evidence supports it.


*Is it righteous?* Yes: “A false witness shall not be unpunished” (Proverbs 19:5), and the true witness must be heard. *Is it loving?* Yes, to the victim, and even to the accused, who is better served by a prompt trial than by years of limbo. *Would I want it done to me?* If I were the old man with a sword wound in my chest, I would want my testimony taken the week I could speak.


### 2. A speedy trial belongs to the victim too


The Sixth Amendment guarantees the accused a speedy trial. It does not forbid one when the accused prefers delay. Delay has become a defense strategy, and in this case it succeeded in the worst possible way.


*Position:* Victims in violent felony cases should have an enforceable right to a trial within a set period, with continuances beyond it granted only on written findings of necessity. Competency proceedings, which stalled this case for months, should run on an expedited schedule with a firm deadline. Federal victims’ rights law already gives victims a right to proceedings free from unreasonable delay; I would strengthen it with a remedy, and encourage states to match it.


### 3. Hold the people you detain


LaSota had an outstanding warrant when police found him at the scene of a sword attack and a fatal shooting. No one ran it. He feigned unconsciousness at the station, was sent to a hospital without a guard, and walked out. He was free for more than two years, during which three more people were killed by people around him.


*Position:* Anyone detained at the scene of a homicide or attempted homicide must be checked for warrants before release, and anyone transported to a hospital from police custody in such a case must remain under guard until released by an officer who has made that check. This is not a new law so much as a minimum standard of competence, and federal law-enforcement grants should require it.


### 4. Public records must tell the truth


A government record exists so that the public, the courts, and other police agencies can know who is who. When a booking record lists only a chosen name and a self-declared sex, it can hide a suspect’s prior record, mislead the public about a dangerous person at large, and, as noted above, make it impossible to study crime honestly.


*Position:* Booking and court records should carry the person’s legal name, every known alias, and sex as recorded at birth, alongside any name or identity the person asserts. Nothing in this forbids a person from living under the name he chooses. It forbids the government from keeping a false or incomplete public record. “Divers weights are an abomination unto the LORD” (Proverbs 20:23); a record that conceals the truth is a false weight.


On booking photographs, I hold a mixed view and welcome a better argument. A photograph released before conviction can follow an innocent man for life, and I would not want that done to me. But when a suspect in a violent felony is at large, or when the public needs to recognize a person charged with violence, the photograph should be released. I would draw the line there.


The press is free and must stay free. I would not compel any newspaper’s style. But I would remind journalists that their first duty is to tell readers what happened. When a naming convention hides a fact the reader needs, such as the sex of a suspect described in a public-safety alert, the convention should yield to the truth.


### 5. Study the question honestly


*Position:* Federal crime statistics should record sex at birth and declared gender identity as separate fields, so the question of whether any group is over- or underrepresented in violent crime can be answered by evidence instead of by whoever shouts loudest. And the National Institutes of Health should fund independent research on the psychiatric and behavioral effects of long-term cross-sex hormone use, including mood and aggression, with the results published whatever they show. A question this consequential should not be left to activists on either side.


### 6. Punish acts, never identities


*Position:* I will oppose any proposal that removes a right from a class of Americans because some members of that class have committed crimes. Charge the person who pulled the trigger, the person who supplied the gun, and the person who planned the killing. Charge them fully and try them promptly. That is justice. Punishing their neighbors is not.


## Conclusion


The Zizians show what happens when the brightest minds of a generation are taught that morality is arithmetic, that the body is negotiable, and that a select few may decide who lives. They also show what happens when a justice system moves so slowly that a victim dies waiting to be heard.


The answer to the first is righteousness: the conviction that some things are wrong whatever the projected sum, because God has said so. The answer to the second is ordinary, practical justice: preserve the testimony, hold the trial, guard the prisoner, keep honest records, and judge each man by his own acts.


Curtis Lind opened his land to strangers. He deserved better from them, and he deserved better from us. These positions are my best judgment of how to give the next Curtis Lind what he was owed. I hold them open to anyone with a better argument.
