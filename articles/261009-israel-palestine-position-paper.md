# 261009 - Israel-Palestine Position Paper

# Two Neighbors, One Land: A Position on Israel and the Palestinians


*Dr. Thomas for President — Position Paper*: *Companion to “The Land Twice Promised: An Honest History of the Israeli–Palestinian Conflict” | 09 October 2026*


---


## The Moral Foundation


Every policy on this conflict should face one test before any other: would a neighbor on the receiving end feel loved by it? I mean love in its plain sense — willing the other’s good, telling him the truth, and refusing to do him harm I would not accept done to me.


This conflict has two sets of neighbors. One is the Jewish people, who have been hunted across two thousand years and three continents, who returned to the land of their fathers, and who were murdered in their homes on October 7, 2023, by men who filmed it with pride. The other is the Palestinian Arab people, many of whom lost their homes in 1948 and 1967, millions of whom have lived for generations without a state, a vote over the power that rules them, or the security of knowing their house will still stand next year.


A policy that loves one of these neighbors by lying about the other fails the test. So does a policy that sounds even-handed by pretending the two sides have behaved identically. They have not. One side’s recognized leadership repeatedly accepted partition; the other’s repeatedly rejected it and chose war. One side’s government, whatever its sins, is answerable to courts, a free press, and elections; the other’s ruler in Gaza is a movement whose founding charter called for killing Jews. Moral proportion requires saying so. It also requires saying that Palestinians who did none of these things have suffered for them, and that Israel has wronged Palestinians in ways that cannot be explained away as self-defense.


As a Christian, I add one conviction of my own. Scripture records God’s promise to Abraham: “I will bless them that bless thee, and curse him that curseth thee” (Genesis 12:3). I take that seriously, and I believe America has been blessed in part by its friendship with the Jewish people. But blessing Israel is not the same as approving every act of an Israeli government, and it never licenses cruelty toward the Arab neighbor, who is equally made in the image of God. The Lord who gave the land also commanded, “The stranger that dwelleth with you shall be unto you as one born among you, and thou shalt love him as thyself” (Leviticus 19:34).


The positions below follow from these principles. I state them as my position, with my reasons, and I hold each one open to a better argument.


---


## 1. Israel’s Existence Is Not Negotiable


The first question in this conflict is whether a Jewish state may exist at all. Every other question — borders, settlements, refugees, Jerusalem — assumes the answer is yes. A movement that answers no has nothing to negotiate except the timing of Israel’s destruction.


My position: the United States will treat Israel’s existence and its right to defend its citizens as settled. We will not fund, recognize, or legitimize any body that seeks Israel’s elimination, whether by arms, by demography, or by the “right of return” used as a weapon. American security cooperation with Israel will continue.


**Why:** The Jewish people needed a refuge in 1939 and the world closed its doors. Six million died. A people that has learned what happens when it depends on the goodwill of others may rightly insist on defending itself. Loving the Jewish neighbor means not asking him to bet his children’s lives on his enemies’ promises.


## 2. No Reward for October 7


Hamas planned and carried out the mass murder, rape, and kidnapping of civilians. No settlement that leaves Hamas armed and governing in Gaza can be called peace; it would teach every armed movement in the world that atrocity pays.


My position: the United States will hold to the disarmament of Hamas as a condition of Gaza’s reconstruction, will support completing the deployment of the international stabilization force under the October 2025 ceasefire plan, and will not allow reconstruction funds to pass through Hamas or any body it controls.


**Why:** Rewarding murder wrongs its victims a second time, and it wrongs the next set of victims by inviting the next attack. It also wrongs the people of Gaza, who have lived under Hamas’s rule and paid the price of its wars.


## 3. Gaza’s Civilians Are Our Neighbors Too


More than two million people live in Gaza, about half of them children. The war has destroyed most of their homes, and famine was confirmed in 2025. The ceasefire has reduced but not ended the killing.


My position: the United States will press for full and secure humanitarian access, will lead a reconstruction effort with Gulf and European partners as Hamas is disarmed, and will apply American law — including the existing Leahy Law, which bars aid to units that commit gross human rights violations — equally to every recipient of American arms. We will continue to support Israel’s defense, and we will expect American weapons to be used within the laws of war.


**Why:** A Palestinian child in Gaza did not choose Hamas and did not plan October 7. If I would not accept my own grandchildren being made to pay for the crimes of men they never met, I cannot ask it of him. Holding a friend to the law is a form of friendship, not a betrayal of it; it is also what our own law already requires.


## 4. End the Inheritance of Refugee Status


Seventy-eight years after 1948, the number of registered Palestinian refugees has grown from about 700,000 to nearly six million, because refugee status under the UN agency UNRWA passes to descendants indefinitely. No other refugee population in the world is treated this way. The 850,000 Jews who fled or were expelled from Arab lands in the same era were absorbed and resettled; their grandchildren are not refugees.


My position: the United States will work to transfer UNRWA’s education, health, and relief services to host governments and to the Palestinian Authority over a defined period, will press Lebanon and other host states to grant Palestinians born there the rights to work, own property, and become citizens, and will support an international compensation fund for property lost by refugees on both sides of the 1948 era — Arab and Jewish alike.


**Why:** Keeping people in camps for four generations to preserve a political claim is cruelty disguised as solidarity. A Palestinian born in a Lebanese camp deserves a passport, a profession, and a future, not a key to a house that no longer exists and a grievance in its place. Loving him means freeing him from a role assigned to him by politicians. Compensating losses honestly, on both sides, is how neighbors settle accounts that cannot be undone.


## 5. Self-Government Earned by Renouncing Violence


Palestinians, like any people, have a legitimate desire to govern themselves. But a state is a power to make war as well as to make laws. A Palestinian state that became a base for attacks on Israel would be a disaster for both peoples.


My position: the United States supports Palestinian self-government that grows as it proves itself — in security, in the rule of law, and in the open renunciation of violence against Israel. American aid to the Palestinian Authority will remain conditioned on ending payments to the families of terrorists (as the Taylor Force Act of 2018 already requires) and on removing incitement from schools and official media. The final form of Palestinian self-government — a state, a confederation with Jordan, or some other arrangement — is for the parties to negotiate, not for Washington to impose.


**Why:** The test of renunciation is fair because it asks of Palestinian leaders only what we ask of any neighbor: that he stop threatening to kill the family next door. It is also respectful, because it treats Palestinians as moral agents capable of choosing peace rather than as permanent victims incapable of responsibility.


## 6. One Standard of Justice in the West Bank


About three million Palestinians and over half a million Israeli settlers live in the West Bank (Judea and Samaria). Palestinian terrorism against Israelis there is real and deadly. So is violence by some Israeli settlers against Palestinian villagers, including arson, beatings, and the driving of families off their land.


My position: the United States will not dictate final borders; those belong to direct negotiation between the parties. But we will insist on one standard of justice. Violence against Jews in the West Bank must be punished, and so must violence against Arabs. American policy will support Israel’s prosecution of terrorists, and we will apply visa bans and sanctions to individuals of any nationality who commit violence against civilians there, as prior administrations have done.


**Why:** “Ye shall have one manner of law, as well for the stranger, as for one of your own country” (Leviticus 24:22). A Jewish family in Hebron and an Arab family near Nablus each deserve to sleep without fear of a neighbor’s torch. A justice that protects only one of them is not justice.


## 7. Widen the Circle of Peace


Every lasting gain for peace in this conflict has come through Arab states recognizing Israel: Egypt in 1979, Jordan in 1994, and the Abraham Accords nations beginning in 2020. Each treaty has held.


My position: the United States will make normalization between Israel and Saudi Arabia, and with other Arab and Muslim states, a priority of its diplomacy, and will ask those states in return to invest in Palestinian prosperity and to support the disarmament of Hamas.


**Why:** Peace is built neighbor by neighbor. The more Arab nations treat Israel as a neighbor, the less any armed movement can claim to speak for the region, and the more resources and protection Palestinians gain from friends who also want peace.


## 8. Honesty at Home


The conflict has come home. Jewish Americans have been assaulted, synagogues attacked, and Jewish students driven from campuses. Some voices on the left excuse Hamas; some voices on the right revive the old lie that Jews secretly control the nation.


My position: the federal government will enforce civil rights law to protect Jewish Americans, and every American, from harassment and violence on campuses and in public life, while protecting free speech, including speech critical of Israeli policy. Criticizing a government is free speech; threatening or excluding a person because he is a Jew is not.


**Why:** The test is whether a Jewish neighbor hearing our public discourse would feel he is among people who wish him well — and whether a Palestinian-American neighbor would feel the same. Hatred of either is a sin against the neighbor and a corrosion of the republic.


---


## Conclusion


The honest history of this conflict leaves no room for either side’s mythology, and an honest policy should leave none either. America should stand with Israel’s right to exist without apology. America should also refuse to treat Palestinians as acceptable losses or as permanent pawns. These are not competing commitments. They are the same commitment — love of neighbor — applied to two neighbors who share a small and holy land.


This is my position, and these are my reasons. If someone shows me a better way to love both peoples, I will take it.
