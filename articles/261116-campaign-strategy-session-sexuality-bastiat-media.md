## The three "nuclear" issues and sexuality as the hinge

Thomas named Islam, abortion, and homosexuality/LGBT/trans as the three issues that have neutered Christianity, because Christians are afraid to say anything about them; all three must be confronted. Of the three, sexuality hits people hardest. Islam reads as intellectual or metaphysical, framed as freedom of religion; sexuality hits "below the belt."

**Why sexuality is the hinge.** Everyone applauds preaching against lying, stealing and murder, and against false testimony before a jury. Say that a person may not sleep with whomever he wants, and the objection comes at once: this is personal. Thomas sees sexuality as the telltale God placed between a person who follows what is good and one who is willing to please Him. The harm is a mark on the soul, and its effect on society shows only statistically, across large populations over long periods: follow God's law and society goes one way, depart from it and it diverges.

**Aimed at the church, meant for the nation.** Thomas said the message is realistically heard first by Christians, many of whom will oppose it. He cited the promise to *"my people, which are called by my name"* **(2 Chronicles 7:14)**: the call is to believers, not pagans. Yet the goal is national repentance and national holiness, and he invoked the warning *"I never knew you: depart from me"* **(Matthew 7:23)**.


 	- **Susan's point (Sunday meeting).** Taking the Lord's name in vain may mean calling yourself a Christian without living it. Thomas had never heard the two connected; Joelle agreed fully. Thomas's example: someone who claims Christ while living with a boyfriend, having had an abortion, and supporting LGBT causes.

 	- **Joelle's experience.** She described her own early encounter, after coming to Christ around 23, with a professing Christian whose life did not match her outspoken faith, and how confusing that was.

 	- **Christians opposing abortion limits.** Joelle described two of the kindest Christian women she knows who strongly oppose Idaho's abortion law, believing they are defending women's rights against patriarchal control. Thomas called this putting Moloch on the altar: sacrificing children for convenience.

 	- **Why the church is silent.** Christians who are not living the teaching cannot in good conscience hold themselves up as the example. Speaking the standard convicts the speaker, so the church self-censors.


**What the law should and should not reach.** Joelle asked, setting homosexuality aside, whether the law has anything to say about sexuality between a man and a woman. Thomas said no: he has consciously placed that in the realm of the church and conscience. He sees three realms: what the law punishes with the sword of the state; morally neutral choices ("cherry, vanilla or chocolate"); and sexuality in the middle, where secular and religious meet.

Thomas grounded the distinction in Scripture. The Old Testament prescribed death for adultery, homosexuality and bestiality, while seduction of an unmarried woman led to marriage. Jesus said *"He that is without sin among you, let him first cast a stone at her"* **(John 8:7)** and *"go, and sin no more"* **(John 8:11)**. God's standard is clear and excludes these acts from His kingdom, but judgment under spiritual law is God's; the believer's job is to bring people to God so they choose godliness.

**Sex and death.** Thomas noted that the two are tightly linked in people's minds, perhaps as the polarity of birth and death. He has not explored it and called it an open puzzle.

**Past experience: Oregon Measure 9 (1992).** Thomas recounted his part in the Measure 9 campaign in Portland, which would have declared homosexuality abnormal in the Oregon Constitution and kept it out of school teaching. He believes it catalyzed militant gay activism. When the Oregon naturopathic association took a side, he wrote to every member of the profession urging neutrality; the firestorm was unmatched before or since. His licensing board reviewed it and found he came right up to the line but did not cross it, saying free speech is robustly defended. He told Margo that going public nationally will generate far more controversy.

**Existing writing.** Thomas has written extensively on homosexuality, but less on godly sexuality, fidelity and chastity in general. *Transforming America* has a chapter on it, blended with other topics and not hard-hitting enough; he expects to rework it. He thinks sexual conduct inside the church differs little from outside it, which may be the bigger issue. Joelle said that matches what she sees.

## Libertarianism

Thomas is firmly anti-libertarian: he sees it as having no standard of good and evil, every man his own law so long as there is consent and no aggression, and so as anti-biblical. Joelle said she had long been closest to libertarianism, belongs to no party, but concluded years ago that it is a pipe dream because it requires changing everyone's mindset.


 	- Thomas spent 10 to 15 years debating his friend John Howard, who was present at the libertarian founding in Orange County in 1980, found it unworkable, and became an anarcho-capitalist: no government at all, with each person contracting for his own police and law.

 	- Joelle cited the New Hampshire town where libertarians tried to put this into practice as evidence it does not work. She doubts it would work even if everyone were fully responsible, because people need a deciding voice and look to leaders.

 	- Joelle said that only in the last few years has she come to see authority as God-given and not bad, which is why she finds Thomas's philosophy so interesting.


## Accountability and the voting network

Thomas said the Accountability pillar of the New ERA Vision (Energy, Righteousness, Accountability) is undeveloped, and Joelle's question about the Lindsay Clancy case opened it up. He coined the acronym for his 2004 Senate run, and Accountability was the A that fit; Joelle said it bears on every subject.

**When is a person responsible?** Joelle observed that even an agreed murder becomes excusable once there is a reason, and the same logic is used for abortion. Thomas generalized it: a bad mood, depression, a named condition or a claim of victimhood is offered as the excuse, yet the harm to the other person still counts. Thomas checked during the call and has not written on the Clancy case; he agreed it needs addressing.

**The voter's accountability.** People use their lack of influence as an excuse: one vote makes no difference. Thomas's answer is multiplication: if each person tells two, and they each tell two, the chain reaches about 127 million voters in 27 generations. Non-voters who stay home share responsibility when the other side takes power and, in his words, a host of satanic legislation follows. From medicine he added that people do not act until they are in pain, whether visiting the doctor or going to the polls.

**The voting network.** Bill Sizemore, who ran for Oregon governor several times, told Thomas during his Senate run that any candidate who gets on the ballot draws about 25% of the vote; everything beyond that must be earned. Thomas's idea is to connect that 25% into a voting network:


 	- Small, interconnected groups modeled on the Sunday group.

 	- They discuss issues, take stands, write letters, send faxes, post on social media, and get friends out to vote.

 	- Their grounding: sexuality, Islam, and Bastiat's *The Law*, plus concrete things to do.

 	- Thomas sees that network as the nucleus that could transform America.


Joelle asked for more on how this would be described to people, especially the sexuality part: why it matters, how it affects the soul, and why it has anything to do with law, which she called a revolutionary idea today. Thomas took that on as a project.

**Platform list.** Thomas had Claude go through the full repository of his essays and list every platform plank. It ran for pages, in his words a full remake of society.

## Media plan

The plan is a ladder of formats for each major topic, so a viewer can go as deep as he wants. Thomas wrote 23 essays for the president site last week alone, so he cannot narrate everything himself, and every essay runs well past short-form length (typically 3,000 words, ranging from 2,000 to 6,000).


 	- **Trailer.** A short video, about three minutes, giving a topic overview and pointing to the deeper material. Joelle's idea, which Thomas endorsed.

 	- **Long-form essay.** The existing written paper.

 	- **Produced video.** The standard format of a speaker cutting away to illustrations and back.

 	- **Audiobook.** Kindle and Amazon/Audible audiobooks with a synthesized voice, integrating the full topic. Thomas is considering producing these book after book.


**Cadence and distribution.** Personal videos only for the big topics. Thomas notices about one each week that stands out; a short video on why it matters can lead into the longer video and the article. Joelle: post everywhere (Instagram, Reels, Facebook and the rest) and cut the same content into shorter clips.

**Candidate video topics:** Islam; abortion; homosexuality; godly sexuality and fidelity; Bastiat's *The Law*. Each may be its own video or part of a series.

**AI video and voice.** Thomas saw Julia McCoy, an AI consultant, presenting through a realistic AI clone of herself and thought it worth using for some videos.


 	- Joelle reported, from a meeting with YouTube contacts, that YouTube deprioritizes realistic-looking AI video, which would kill organic reach.

 	- Clearly AI visuals (an animated version of Thomas, a realistic sketch) avoid that and do not mislead. Viewers who feel fooled start questioning everything.

 	- She had asked about audio: AI voices and music do not trigger the penalty; only realistic AI visuals do.

 	- Thomas agreed: nothing that diminishes reach. He noted that being controversial enough may get the campaign above the noise.


**Studio.** Thomas has a helper assembling cabinets as part of getting set up for the video work: two done, a third nearly done, one more to go.
