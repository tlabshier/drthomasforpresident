# 261110 – Vance, the Midterms, and the Scapegoat

# The Goat Carries Our Own Sins


### On the midterm rout, the vice president’s diagnosis, and the duty to confess before we accuse


*An essay occasioned by the New York Times opinion column “JD Vance Trots Out an Old Scapegoat” (10 November 2026)*


Thomas Lee Abshier, ND — 10 November 2026


**Source article:** [Opinion | JD Vance Trots Out an Old Scapegoat — The New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/11/10/opinion/vance-trump-midterms-israel.html)


---


## Where I stand


I will state my position first, so the reader can test the rest of this essay against it.


Blaming “our relationship to Israel” for the Republican defeat of 3 November is a scapegoat explanation. It fails the evidence, and before it fails the evidence, it fails the moral test I apply to every public act: *Is it righteous? If so, is it loving? Would I want it done to me?* It moves the weight of American decisions onto a foreign people, and, given the long history of this particular move, onto Jews in that nation and in this one, when the record points to choices made in Washington.


I hold this position open to a better argument. If the vice president’s pollster publishes data showing that the Israel question moved a meaningful share of 2024 Trump voters, I will weigh it and say so. I will also say where I think Mr. Vance touched something real, and where the column criticizing him goes further than the evidence. But the center holds: a party that lost because of its own choices must first confess its own choices.


---


## What happened, and what was said


On 3 November, Democrats took both houses of Congress. They flipped Senate seats in Iowa, Kansas, Maine, Ohio, North Carolina, South Carolina, and Texas. Kansas elected its first Democratic senator since 1932, and Iowa its first since 2008. Rural Iowa moved toward Democrats, who took three of that state’s four House seats.


The next morning, Vice President Vance posted a long postmortem on X. “The reason we lost is the war,” he wrote. He reported that a pollster he called one of the smartest people he knows had told him why 2024 Trump voters were angry and planning to vote Democratic. There were four reasons: end the war in Iran, focus on the cost of living, put America First, and “change our relationship to Israel.” Donald Trump Jr. endorsed the post without reservation.


The Times column answering him makes three claims. First, that the fourth item is an implied accusation against Jews. Second, that most Republicans still regard Israel favorably, so abandoning that position would alienate the party’s own base. Third, that a better explanation comes from the analyst Sean Trende: nearly every decision of the past two years alienated some part of Trump’s coalition. Tariffs hit business owners and farmers. Immigration raids frightened Hispanic communities that had swung to Trump. Gifts and profits flowed to the President’s family. There was disorder at the Pentagon, a sharp turn against old allies, and a national debt still climbing.


---


## What the voters said


Here I go to the best primary evidence available, which is the voters’ own testimony in the exit surveys. Exit polls are imperfect, but they are the voters speaking, and they are large.


In the NBC exit poll, the share of voters calling an issue “crucial” to their vote ran as follows: government corruption, 55 percent; cost of living, 49 percent; immigration enforcement, 36 percent; the Iran war, 34 percent. Two-thirds of voters said Trump’s tariffs hurt the economy. Asked for the single most important issue, voters ranked foreign policy fifth of six. The AP’s survey of more than 100,000 voters found about six in ten saying the U.S. was wrong to take military action against Iran, but only about a third calling the war crucial to their vote. One conservative analyst compiled roughly fifty polls and surveys and found none in which Iran was voters’ top issue.


As far as I could find, no major exit poll asked about Israel at all. The absence of a question is not proof of zero effect. But if the alliance with Israel had driven defections on the scale Mr. Vance implies, I would expect it to surface somewhere in surveys of this size, in the open-ended answers, the cross-tabs, or the commentary of the pollsters who ran them. It did not.


Against this, Mr. Vance offers one unnamed pollster’s private report. I treat that as a lead, not a finding. We do not know the sample, the question wording, or whether “change our relationship to Israel” was the voters’ phrase or the pollster’s summary.


Even the war, which did weigh on voters, did most of its damage at the gas pump. The President himself conceded that gasoline prices hurt his party. A war’s cost at the pump is the result of an American policy, decided and conducted by Americans.


So on the evidence I conclude: the column’s diagnosis, a broad betrayal of a broad coalition, fits the exit polls far better than the vice president’s does.


---


## The question under the question


Still, a serious man asks what a claim is reaching for, not only whether its particulars hold. What is the line about Israel reaching for?


I believe it is reaching for a question many younger conservatives now carry: *Did America go to war for someone else?* That is a fair question for a sovereign people to ask, and it is not foolish. The evidence behind it is not imaginary:


- **The opinion shift is real.** Pew found in March that 57 percent of Republicans under fifty viewed Israel unfavorably, against roughly a quarter of those over fifty. Gallup found Republican sympathy for Israelis down ten points since 2024, to its lowest level since 2004. On this point, the column overstates its case when it says Republicans stand about where they stood under Reagan and Bush. That is true of older Republicans. It is not true of the party’s next generation, and Mr. Vance is speaking to them.

- **Foreign pressure has been real.** In June, Mr. Vance publicly criticized members of Israel’s government for opposing the President’s efforts to end the war. He has also pointed to a *Time* report that an Israeli government–funded effort paid American conservative influencers to denounce the U.S.–Iran ceasefire. If that report is accurate, Americans have every right to object.


So here is my answer to the question underneath. A foreign government, any foreign government, friend or foe, working to steer American public opinion should be named, disclosed, and limited by law. I would apply the same rule to Israel, to Qatar and its gifted 747, to China, and to every other foreign hand. That is guarding our own door.


But guarding our own door is a different act from blaming the neighbor for what happened inside our own house. The war was launched by an American president under American authority. According to Israel’s own press, Israel has largely not taken part in strikes on Iran since April. The threatened tariffs on Canadian potash and the retaliation against Kansas sorghum were not decided in Jerusalem. Neither were the immigration raids, the crypto profits, nor the President’s remark in May, while gas stood at $4.50 a gallon, that “I don’t think about Americans’ financial situation.”


The first recorded blame-shift in Scripture has the same shape:


*“And the man said, The woman whom thou gavest to be with me, she gave me of the tree, and I did eat.”* **(Genesis 3:12)**


Adam was not wrong that Eve had handed him the fruit. He was wrong about whose hand had taken it.


---


## The moral test


I run every policy and every public act through three questions, in order.


**1. Is it righteous?** Righteous judgment puts responsibility where it lies. It does not move a man’s guilt onto his father, his son, or his neighbor.


*“The soul that sinneth, it shall die. The son shall not bear the iniquity of the father, neither shall the father bear the iniquity of the son: the righteousness of the righteous shall be upon him, and the wickedness of the wicked shall be upon him.”* **(Ezekiel 18:20)**


Assigning an American electoral defeat to a foreign ally, on the strength of one unpublished poll, is weighing with a false balance.


*“Divers weights are an abomination unto the LORD; and a false balance is not good.”* **(Proverbs 20:23)**


**2. Is it loving?** To millions of American Jews, and to the millions of American Christians who love Israel, the message received is plain: *you are why we lost.* That sets neighbor against neighbor inside the coalition the vice president says he wants to rebuild. And history teaches that when leaders explain a nation’s failure by pointing toward the Jews, what follows has never stayed inside the boundaries of policy debate.


**3. Would I want it done to me?** Suppose my own campaign failed, and a rival explained the failure not by my platform but by my church or my friends. I would recognize the move at once. Its purpose is to relieve the speaker of repentance.


The act fails all three questions. That judgment does not depend on Mr. Vance’s motive. It depends on what the act does.


---


## The scapegoat in its first meaning


The column’s title uses the word “scapegoat,” and the word deserves its original meaning, because that meaning is the remedy.


*“And Aaron shall lay both his hands upon the head of the live goat, and confess over him all the iniquities of the children of Israel, and all their transgressions in all their sins, putting them upon the head of the goat, and shall send him away by the hand of a fit man into the wilderness.”* **(Leviticus 16:21)**


Notice what the high priest did. He *confessed*, and he confessed the sins of his own people, not the sins of the nations around them. The goat of Leviticus carried confessed sin. The political scapegoat carries unconfessed sin by naming someone else. One is the beginning of atonement. The other is the avoidance of it.


The Republican Party’s way back does not run through a new enemy. It runs through a plain confession of the record: tariffs that punished farmers and small business; a war whose costs landed on working families while its end kept slipping; enforcement that fell hard on peaceable people at immigration courts and in their homes; and the steady mixing of public office with private gain. This is the Accountability in the New ERA Vision. A movement that can name its own failures can recover from them. A movement that cannot will keep finding someone new to blame.


---


## On motive, and on the column’s tone


I do not know Mr. Vance’s heart. The column concludes that the move is “true to type.” I cannot see that far, and I do not think the columnist can either.


The record is mixed. Mr. Vance has said publicly that he respects the Jewish religion. He has defended the alliance in America First terms and rejected the claim that Israel controls American policy. He also stood before a Turning Point audience in October 2025 and let an antisemitic question about Judaism pass without rebuke, which drew sharp criticism from conservatives at the time. Taken together, I read this as a man trying to hold a divided coalition without paying the price of correcting either side of it. That is a failure of leadership. It is not proof of hatred.


I also cannot adopt the column’s method. It describes the vice president and the Secretary of War in schoolyard insults, and it quotes a farmer calling the President a moron. In my September paper “A Just Weight,” I wrote that contempt is not persuasion. I hold to that when the contempt is aimed at people I am criticizing too.


*“A soft answer turneth away wrath: but grievous words stir up anger.”* **(Proverbs 15:1)**


The column is also selective in its own way. It treats Mr. Trende’s diagnosis as the whole story, and it passes over the real generational shift that gives Mr. Vance his audience. Even so, on the question of why Republicans lost, the column is closer to the evidence than the vice president is, and I give it that credit.


---


## Why the concern remains plausible


Some will say I am reading too much into one bullet point in one post. I take the objection seriously, but I believe the concern stands, for four reasons.


**The selection.** As *Axios* observed, the post named the war and inflation but left out the tariffs, the corruption controversies, and the White House construction projects. Those are the failures that implicate the administration in which Mr. Vance serves. A diagnosis that leaves out every item at home and adds one item abroad has the shape of a scapegoat, whatever the intent.


**The incentive.** The vice president is positioning for 2028, as nearly every account of the post noted. A story that pins the defeat on a war he was reported to have doubted, and on a foreign ally, while sparing the President and himself, is convenient. Convenience does not prove a claim false, but it is a reason to demand the data.


**The permission.** Rhetoric at the top of a movement sets the bounds of what the movement’s fringe believes it may say. When a vice president links an election loss to Israel without evidence, the fringe does not hear a nuanced point about alliance terms. It hears permission. I do not claim this one post will cause harm. I claim that this is how such permission is given.


**The disproportion.** A 78-year alliance, a question that appears on no exit poll, and a single line in a list. Something carrying that much historical weight should not be made to carry an election’s blame on that little evidence. The disproportion itself is the warning sign.


---


## What I would do


**Confess the record.** I urge every Republican leader to own the decisions voters actually named: corruption, prices, tariffs, enforcement excess, and the conduct of the war. That is where repentance starts.


**One rule for all foreign influence.** Require full public disclosure of any advocacy funded by a foreign government, including paid social-media influencers, and refuse gifts of state like the Qatari aircraft. The rule applies to friend and foe alike, Israel included. Guard the door. Do not blame the neighbor.


**Debate the alliance on its merits.** Questions about military aid, war aims, and the terms of the U.S.–Israel relationship are legitimate. They should be argued openly as policy, with reasons, and not slipped in as an excuse for an election.


**Decide the Iran war on its warrant, not its polls.** I have written separately on how I believe that war must be won. Whether to continue, change course, or conclude it must rest on the warrant of defense against attack and the threat of attack, not on what was popular on 3 November.


**Retract or publish.** I call on the vice president either to withdraw the fourth item or to publish the data behind it, so that it can be examined. That is the standard I asked of a friendly scorecard in September, and it is the standard I ask here.


*“For every man shall bear his own burden.”* **(Galatians 6:5)**

## Sources and their limits


This essay leans heavily on the mainstream record: major newspapers, wire services, network exit polls, and established pollsters. I graded evidence by kind rather than by outlet. Exit surveys are the voters’ own testimony and were treated as primary. Statements by the vice president, the President, and advocacy groups on every side were treated as interested-party testimony.


The limits I am aware of:


- **Exit polls have known weaknesses.** Sampling, weighting, and question wording can all shift results. None that I found asked about Israel directly, so they cannot prove that the issue moved no votes. They can only show that it did not register in the questions that were asked.

- **The vice president’s pollster is unpublished.** If that data is released, it could change my assessment, and I would revise this essay accordingly.

- **The report of Israeli-funded influencer payments reached me secondhand,** through the *Jerusalem Post*‘s account of the vice president’s remarks citing *Time*. I have not examined the underlying reporting.

- **I deliberately read dissenting readings of the same data.** A left-leaning anti-war outlet (Scheerpost) reads the exits as a rejection of both the war and U.S. backing for Israel. A conservative outlet (Townhall) reads them as economy-first. I found the economy-and-corruption reading better supported by the numbers, but both are interesting readings.

- **Curation risk.** Search engines and major outlets select what reaches a reader. Evidence for the vice president’s claim may exist that the mainstream record has not carried. I have looked for it and not found it, and I state that as the limit of my looking rather than as a final verdict.
