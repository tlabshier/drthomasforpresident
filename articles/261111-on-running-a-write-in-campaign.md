# 261111 - Running a Personal Witness, Write in Campaign

# Why I Will Not Be a Spoiler

*By Thomas Lee Abshier, ND | 11 November 2026*

I am running a self-funded, write-in campaign. I am committed to the campaign and to speaking what I believe is a vision for what could truly make America good and Godly. I am doing this to win. But if the support isn't there to win, I will step aside rather than split the vote and hand the election to a candidate I strongly oppose; on election day, I will cast my own vote for the candidate I support. Here is my reasoning, and I welcome a better argument.

## The moral test

I judge every public act by one question: would my neighbor feel loved by it? A campaign is a public act. If my candidacy draws just enough votes from a good candidate who could win to elect someone who would harm the nation, I have not served my neighbors. Many people believe Ross Perot's 1992 run did exactly that, helping elect Bill Clinton by drawing votes from George H. W. Bush.

Whether Perot actually changed the outcome is still argued. Some analyses of the exit polls found his voters would have split roughly evenly between the two major candidates. But the danger is real in any close race, and the principle does not depend on settling that one case.

## Why make a statement at all

I am not running because I trust the electorate's moral judgment as it stands. I am running because I do not. A democracy left to itself tends to act like a mob, following whoever is loudest or best funded. The Founders knew this, which is why they built a republic with restraints rather than a pure democracy.

John Adams, then President, put the condition plainly in a letter of 11 October 1798 to the officers of the First Brigade of the Third Division of the Massachusetts Militia: *"We have no Government armed with Power capable of contending with human Passions unbridled by morality and Religion. … Our Constitution was made only for a moral and religious People. It is wholly inadequate to the government of any other."*

I hope that by election day there will have been a moral transformation in this nation. But I do not want to leave the outcome to chance. I want to make a statement: to set out, in plain view, what a government grounded in God's Word would do, so that it is clear whom a moral voter should support.

A campaign can change the country without winning. No third party has won the presidency since the Republicans in 1860, yet the major parties have often adopted what a strong outside movement proved the people wanted. That influence comes from the movement's size before the election, not from splitting the vote on election day. So I will build the network in the open, and if it is not large enough to win, I will step aside and press its platform on the party I belong to.

That party is the Republican Party. I support its platform, except for the 2024 changes that weakened its stance on marriage and abortion. The 2024 platform dropped the call for a national abortion ban and removed the language defining marriage as between one man and one woman. I am not starting a third party. My intent is to offer a new foundation on which to base government, which I believe was the original intent of the Founders: a government based on God's Word, the Bible, solving our problems according to Biblical principles.

## Why I am running at all

I see the current government as captured by money in both parties. Money bends judgment. Scripture said it long ago: *"And thou shalt take no gift: for the gift blindeth the wise, and perverteth the words of the righteous"* **(Exodus 23:8)**. We get the best government money can buy, which is to say a government that serves its buyers. Government is a service, not a business.

Everyone senses that something must change. Very few can say what it should change to. That is the question I work on every day, one position paper at a time: what would a righteous society actually look like, and how would we get there from here? The platform is my campaign brochure, my lawn sign, and my media posts. I am the face and the person behind it, but the ideas are what I am running on.

## No donations, no obligations

I am not accepting contributions. I am paying for this campaign myself so that I owe nothing to anyone. When it comes time to decide, no donor will have a claim on my judgment.

## The test: one tells two

The way I intend to win, if I can, is the oldest method. If each person who agrees with what I am saying tells two others, and each of them tells two more, the doubling reaches the scale of a national election within about twenty-seven steps. Joe Biden received about 81 million votes in 2020. That is the order of the network we would need.

So we will decide on evidence, not hope. As we approach the election, if that network exists, we go forward. If it does not, we divert our efforts toward influencing those on the ballot who have a chance to win. I will say so plainly and early.

## The ideas outlive the candidate

If we stand down, nothing is lost. We will turn our full effort to persuading whoever holds office to enact what we propose. It is the same policy with a different face. A movement that depends on one man is finite. An idea that can live beyond the man and catch fire in other people can change the nation, the world, and the course of history.

That is what I am trying to build: not a personal career, but a well-reasoned account of how a free people can govern themselves righteously, which others can carry forward long after I am gone. I invite you to read it, test it, argue with it, and if it persuades you, tell two people.
