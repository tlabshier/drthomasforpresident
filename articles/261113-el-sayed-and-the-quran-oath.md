# 261113 - El-Sayed and the Qur'an Oath

# The Book, the Oath, and the Trust of Office:


### On Senator-elect El-Sayed

*By Thomas Lee Abshier, ND | 13 November 2026*

On January 3, 2027, Abdul El-Sayed of Michigan will be sworn in as the first Muslim elected to the United States Senate. He has said he will place his hand on a Quran, perhaps Thomas Jefferson's copy, which Keith Ellison used in 2007, or an old Quran held by the University of Michigan. Some of my fellow conservatives have reacted as though the book were the scandal. I do not think it is. There is a real concern here, but it is not the one the headlines point to, and it is larger than one senator.

My position has three parts, and I hold all three.

First, **the state must not bar him.** He has every right to take his oath on the Quran, and no government may apply a religious test to his office.

Second, **his public record has earned scrutiny** before he is handed the nation's secrets, and that scrutiny should be of his allegiance, not his prayers.

Third, and this is the point the first two are in service of: **his election is a symptom of a deeper illness.** We have welcomed a foreign god into our camp and paid it deference, and most of the people who did so do not know that is what they did. I count Islam, as a political and civilizational program, among the four existential threats to our republic: 1) artificial superintelligence, 2) engineered bioweapons, 3) Islam, and 4) the capture and corruption of government by corporations. I argued this at length in *The Militant Islamic Threat* (2019), in "The Revolutionary and the Devotee" (Renaissance Ministries, September 2026), and in Chapter 14 of *Transforming America*; and I set out its foreign-policy front in "Winning the Long War with Iran." Here I want to show how the three parts fit together, because they are usually set against each other, and they belong together.


---


## 1. The book is not the problem

Article VI of the Constitution says that officers of the United States "shall be bound by Oath or Affirmation, to support this Constitution; but no religious Test shall ever be required as a Qualification to any Office or public Trust." No law requires a Bible. The swearing-in itself uses no book at all; the book appears at the ceremonial photograph afterward. Franklin Pierce affirmed instead of swearing. Theodore Roosevelt took no oath on the Bible.

I am a Christian, and I believe the Bible is the Word of God. Swearing the oath of office on the Bible is a symbolic act representing the oath taker's responsibility before the God of the Bible to uphold the oath. El-Sayed's refusal to place his hand on the Bible and swear to uphold the Constitution tacitly admits that he does not recognize the authority of, or hold himself accountable to, the God of the Bible and its revealed standards. Rather, El-Sayed swearing an oath with his hand on the Qur'an silently testifies to his accountability to the god of the Qur'an, who has commanded its followers to subdue all lands for him, put Christians and Jews under the jizya, and kill the pagans. Since Mr. El-Sayed holds the Quran sacred, he has told us that he is loyal to the program and god of the Quran.

There are those who say that requiring him to swear on a Bible would teach him and every onlooker that our oaths are theater and use the words of Jesus to justify why he should not be required to use the Bible: *"But let your communication be, Yea, yea; Nay, nay: for whatsoever is more than these cometh of evil."* **(Matthew 5:37)**

The argument is that this clarifies his choice: by putting his hand on the Quran, he tells the nation plainly which God he answers to. This is true, and perhaps we should use this as our indicator of the man's loyalty. A wise people would make that distinction and see that this was a man with divided loyalties at best, and perhaps (even likely) a hidden loyalty to another crown. Perhaps we should make it easy for him to lie, allow anyone to swear on anything they want, and thus let their colors be seen without any show of resistance or indicator that he has identified himself as an invader. Perhaps we should allow everyone to swear on anything they consider holy, and make a public exposure of their true loyalties by taking an oath, pretending to be loyal to the country and Constitution.

If we did this, we would then have an easy cultural cue that the oath was false and that this was, in fact, a covert conqueror, a 5th column, a Trojan horse, disguising himself as a loyal citizen sworn to uphold the Constitution and the God underlying the laws of the Republic. But a man could quickly recognize that this was a tag, and if he feared a well-informed public who recognized the signs of other loyalty, he would not do so; instead, he would lie if he feared he would be opposed if his loyalties were exposed. The fact that El-Sayed has brazenly identified his god, and his loyalty to the program of the Qur'an, indicates that we have already psychologically accepted the invader as a harmless, diverse-belief, that has been demonized, or fears the title of Islamophobe and wants to virtue signal that he is tolerant. The lack of outrage is already symptomatic of our submission to Islam. We have been neutered as a society. His telegraphed oath on the Qur'an was a test of the waters to see if the public had been sufficiently accommodated to Islam in public office in America. The result was yes. Even my writing-assistant AI, which knows my stance, took this position, which suggests this thought/feeling/attitude has permeated the general zeitgeist of the common mind. We have already lost our hot and hard acute immune response against the outrage of those who are sworn to protect and defend the Constitution being sworn in on a symbol of another god and its cultural norms.  We have already lost the national will, the discrimination, and the courage required to oppose an invader whom we are allowing to direct the legislation of law enforced by the sword of the State. Yes, a man who cannot put his hand on the Bible and swear to be accountable to the table would have hidden that, but the fact that we are letting such a man be sworn in as a Senator, and make laws that govern us is an outrage, and there is none being expressed. I do not want our nation to be judged by God and suffer under the curse of being subject to the god of Islam. America needs to recognize the symbols that reveal men's intentions. If people hide their inner thoughts, that is good; at least they are not speaking them openly as though loyalty to another god is an acceptable standard for America. If we don't have the obvious clue of people swearing to uphold the Constitution while putting their hands on the scripture of a foreign god, we may have to be more careful in discerning the character of potential officeholders. Clearly, the people who voted for El-Sayed knew his loyalties and did not care, so having him use the Qur'an as the authority to which he is accountable would not shock them. The bottom line is not requiring men to swear on the Bible; it is an electorate that is wise and spiritually mature enough, and dedicated in their loyalty to the God of the Bible, that anyone who expresses loyalty to another god, even hidden, would be discerned by the subtleties of his past, his words, and his promises. That is not the case in 2026 America, and this is to our shame; it is time to change. We will pay a high price if we succumb to the temptation to worship other gods, and I do not want to see America degrade to the level that she will fall if she falls under the rule of foreign gods.

A religious test from the state would also be a weapon pointed back at us. A government competent to rule on which faiths are fit for office will, on some later Tuesday, rule on mine. As I wrote in *Transforming America*, a law barring anyone who calls himself a Muslim from office would punish a man for his relation to God rather than for his conduct or his politics, and it would also bar the very Muslims who are our allies, the ones who reject the program at personal cost. And while this is true, the continuation of the faith, and the blessings of God depend upon our loyalty to Him. If we place "no religious test" as our god and exalt it as a first principle, and we think we are virtuous because we have not compelled conscience, and should be rewarded as a society for being open-minded and tolerant, we will suffer when the eventual fruit comes from the worship of that false god. The lack of religious test is not an open door to anyone; it is a narrow door, and each man should be extremely discriminating of the character and philosophy of every man who serves in a public office, as it is they who will make society. Whether a man is pre- or post-trib, worships on Saturday or Sunday, sprinkles or immerses, uses grape juice or wine, believes we are cleansed after death or must cleanse before, will sleep or immediately be judged are the types of religious tests I believe the Founders referred to. The intramural squabbles over fine points not fully elaborated in scripture are the types of religious tests that should not be administered. But every voter should exercise and apply a religious test on every candidate.

So the state's question is settled. Michigan voters elected him, and the Constitution says what it says. But the Constitution restrains the *government*. It does not restrain the conscience of the *voter*, the pastor, or the citizen. That is where the real question lives. We as voters must be informed, spiritually mature, and discriminating, feeding on the Word of God daily so we can recognize a man's loyalty to other spirits.

## 2. The room and the sofa

The key to the whole subject came to me from Danny Burmawi, born in Jordan, raised a Muslim, and now a Christian who cannot go home. When an interviewer raised the objection that America has freedom of religion and the government does not critique theologies, Burmawi answered: Islam *has* a religion.

A room has a sofa, but the room is not the sofa. Islam contains prayer, fasting, almsgiving, pilgrimage, and a creed. That is a religion, and the First Amendment protects its free exercise. But it holds them inside a legal, political, military, and civilizational program whose stated goal is the rule of Allah's law over all mankind. Call the whole thing a religion, and the program walks past the Constitution's gate on the sofa's ticket.

This distinction resolves the apparent contradiction in my position. The state may not test a man's religion. It may, and must, test his allegiance. The question is not what he believes about God, but whether he holds that any religious law, Sharia included, should ever replace the Constitution as the law of this land.

And here is why the question is sharper for Islam than for Christianity, though both seek to bring all nations to their faith. The gospel spreads by persuasion; Christ conquered nothing and told Peter to put away his sword. The Christian who wields the sword in Christ's name can be condemned from inside his own faith, by its standard. In Islam, the standard is Muhammad, the perfect man, who was also a lawgiver, a ruler, and a general. Its legal tradition does not stop at the mosque door; it governs inheritance, marriage, apostasy, blasphemy, and the status of non-Muslims living under its rule. A program of that kind is not a relation between a soul and God. It is a relation between a party and a state.

I do not say Islam is nothing but that program. Its early Meccan teaching was a revolt against the idols of Arabia, and much of its moral teaching closely resembles the Torah. But the Medinan period turned toward rule and domination, and the classical doctrine that later verses supersede earlier ones means the Meccan mercy is read through the Medinan law, not the other way around. Many Muslims, and some of their scholars, dispute that doctrine. I am glad of everyone who does. But the tradition, not the dissenter, sets the default.

The strongest objection to all this is that Christians also hold a higher allegiance. We do. Peter told the council that we ought to obey God rather than men. So the question is never whether a man has a loyalty above the state; every serious believer does. The question is what that higher loyalty commands about the civil order. Christ commanded us to render unto Caesar the things that are Caesar's. When the crowd tried to take Him by force and make Him king, He withdrew from them.

*"My kingdom is not of this world: if my kingdom were of this world, then would my servants fight."* **(John 18:36)**

A Christian's higher loyalty forbids him to seize the state for Christ. The classical Islamic tradition's higher loyalty commands the state to be ruled by Allah's law. That is the difference, and it is why the question of allegiance is fair to put to a Muslim officeholder in a way it is not fair to put about a man's prayers.

I say this about a system, not about my neighbors. I have had Muslim patients I thoroughly enjoyed, and I have shared Thanksgiving tables with Muslim families. Contempt for Muslims as persons is sin. As I wrote in 2019, the Muslim is not our enemy; it is the god he worships against whom we fight. The prize is his heart.

## 3. What the record shows

I sort the evidence by type, not by which outlet reported it.

**Established from primary evidence (his own words, on video and in his campaign's transcript):**


 	- In March 2026, a man drove a truck into Temple Israel in West Bloomfield, Michigan, a synagogue with about 140 children inside, and injured a security guard. Authorities called it a targeted attack on Jews. The day after, Mr. El-Sayed released a video. He said the attack was heinous and that nothing justified it. He also said "violence is a cycle" and "hurt people hurt people," pointed out that the attacker had lost relatives in an airstrike in Lebanon, and placed the deeper cause on the war and on "the very people who launched that war." His campaign posted the full transcript on its own website.

 	- At an April press conference alongside streamer Hasan Piker, he repeated the line: "hurt people do hurt people."

 	- In late August, he told the Michigan Democratic Party's Jewish Caucus, "I made a mistake," and said he should have condemned the attack with a "full stop."

 	- He has campaigned with Hasan Piker, who once said America "deserved 9/11." Mr. El-Sayed has called that statement "dumb" but has not fully disowned Piker.


**His own statements on Sharia:**


 	- In 2009 he told the *New York Times* that when he stands before God he does not want to say he did things "the easy way instead of the Shariah-compliant way." His campaign confirms he still follows Sharia in his personal life, and says he believes in the separation of church and state.

 	- In 2018, asked whether Sharia in Michigan was a real concern, he cited his oath to the Constitution and said: "Sharia law is not in question in this state."

 	- In 2022, he criticized Oklahoma's constitutional ban on Sharia and tied opposition to Sharia to white supremacy. When Jesse Watters pressed him on that in August 2026, he answered by comparing Sharia to Catholic canon law: "Nobody is trying to push Sharia Law on anyone else."


**Established as documents, though what they mean is disputed:**


 	- His maternal half-sister, Eman Abdelhadi, a University of Chicago professor, wrote in 2012 and 2013 social media posts that "my family has long been MB," meaning the Muslim Brotherhood, and that she was praying for family members in Egypt who supported it. She also described their mother as active in the Brotherhood in college.

 	- On *Meet the Press* on October 11, Mr. El-Sayed answered "No" twice when asked whether he or his family had any ties to the Brotherhood. On NBC Nightly News on October 14, he called his sister's statements "hearsay" and repeated, "We have no ties."

 	- The same reporting that surfaced the posts states that there is no evidence Mr. El-Sayed was ever personally involved with the Brotherhood.


**Reported, but I could not confirm from primary sources:** the claims that he refused to condemn Hamas, refused to state Israel's right to exist as a Jewish state, and refused to condemn a campaign ad widely called antisemitic. I treat these as leads, not findings.

## 4. Weighing it

**On the family ties.** I will not hold a man guilty for his mother's college politics or his sister's opinions.

*"The son shall not bear the iniquity of the father, neither shall the father bear the iniquity of the son: the righteousness of the righteous shall be upon him, and the wickedness of the wicked shall be upon him."* **(Ezekiel 18:20)**

But Ezekiel speaks of guilt, not honesty. Mr. El-Sayed was asked a direct question on national television and answered with a flat "No" about his *family*, when his own sister had written the opposite in public. A man who means to be trusted would have said, "My sister has written that relatives in Egypt supported the Brotherhood. I have never been part of it, and I reject it." That would have been truthful and would have cost him nothing. The issue is not whom he is related to. It is whether his yes means yes.

**On "hurt people hurt people."** These were his own words, chosen deliberately and repeated. They contain a true observation: suffering often breeds violence. A physician sees that every day. But in the hours after a man tries to kill Jewish children because they are Jewish, explaining the attacker's grief and naming the people supposedly responsible for it moves part of the blame off the attacker and onto his victims' people. That is how an excuse is built while saying nothing excuses it.

*"Woe unto them that call evil good, and good evil; that put darkness for light, and light for darkness."* **(Isaiah 5:20)**

He has since apologized, and repentance is real; I will not refuse a man the chance to mean it. But an apology five months later, in a close race, to the group whose votes he needed, carries less weight than a full stop on the day itself would have.

**On Sharia.** Here the room and the sofa do their work. His personal observance, the interest-free mortgage, the prayers, the diet, is the sofa, and it is his right. "Nobody is trying to push Sharia on anyone" answers about the sofa. The nationis question is about the room. And his comparison to canon law dodges it: canon law is the internal rulebook of a church; it cannot become the penal code, and no Catholic jurisprudence claims the state. Classical Sharia does. When a man calls the effort to keep Sharia out of our law "white supremacy," he has told us he does not see the room at all, or does not want us to.

So I put to Senator-elect El-Sayed the same question I have asked every reader to put, respectfully, to any Muslim neighbor:

**"Will you say that Sharia should never replace the Constitution as the law of this land? And will you say so to your friends?"**

That is not a religious test. No government asks it, and nothing attaches to the answer but the truth. If he says yes, plainly and without the canon-law detour, and says it in Dearborn as clearly as on Fox, he is not part of the problem, and I will say so. If he will not, he has told us where he stands, without anyone asking him a thing about his God.

**On the alliance he represents.** Mr. El-Sayed is not only a Muslim; he is a progressive Democrat who campaigned with Hasan Piker and ran on Medicare for All and a wealth tax. He is a living example of what Burmawi calls the red-green alliance: a left that replaced class with identity, and an Islamist movement that fits its categories as non-Western and formerly colonized. The two share no vision of the good, only an enemy: the Christian West. I hold two readings of such alignments at once: it may be coordinated, or it may be convergence without coordination. Either way the outcome is the same. I said of Mayor Mamdani in September what I say now of Senator-elect El-Sayed: his sincerity does not change where the program leads. Pray for him by name, and oppose the program by name.

## 5. The classified-information question

Senators are not given security clearances in the normal sense. By long custom, members of Congress are treated as eligible for classified information because they hold elected office. The theory is that the voters are the background check, and that the executive branch must not be able to screen out the legislators who oversee it.

Sean Spicer's petition asks that Mr. El-Sayed undergo a formal background check, and Spicer added the most important sentence in the whole dispute: "Why can't we make all of them do it?"

That is the right principle. A rule written for the one Muslim senator is a religious test in disguise.

*"Thou shalt not respect the person of the poor, nor honour the person of the mighty: but in righteousness shalt thou judge thy neighbour."* **(Leviticus 19:15)**

The government's own clearance guidelines already make allegiance to the United States the first question. I would apply that question to every member of both houses equally: a uniform background investigation, blind to religion, for any member who sits on the Intelligence, Armed Services, or Foreign Relations committees or asks for compartmented access, with findings reported to the chamber's own security office so the separation of powers holds. No member loses his seat; the people's choice stands. The standard is conduct, associations, and allegiance: material support for designated terrorist groups, undisclosed foreign payments, lying to investigators, and refusal to renounce the replacement of the Constitution by any religious law. Is it righteous? Yes; it guards the nation by one rule. Is it loving? Yes, to the citizens whose lives depend on those secrets, and to the member, judged by the measure applied to his colleagues. Would I want it done to me? I would gladly submit to it.

## 6. The foreign god in the camp

Now I come to the point, and I will not gloss it.

The careful framing above- no religious test, scrutiny of conduct, one rule for all- is true. It is also the framing this nation has hidden behind for a generation while missing the larger matter. The real question is whether a man whose first allegiance is to Allah will govern according to the Constitution when the two conflict. I am not willing to assume the answer, and neither should any voter. The program Islam carries does not seek to coexist with the Christian culture that produced our liberties. It seeks to replace it.

I described in 2019 how that replacement proceeds, not by conquest but by a patient gradient: immigration, conversion, and multiplication, then political organization, then the pressure of a voting bloc. In Europe it is already well advanced. And I have to be honest, as I was in *Transforming America*, about the limit of the law here. Having large families, winning converts, and voting are lawful. No statute can reach them, and none should, because they are the same freedoms that protected the abolitionists and the early church. If I would not want the government to stop Christians from having children and voting their consciences, I cannot ask it to stop anyone else. The laws I have proposed are door locks. A lock does nothing for a house nobody is living in.

And this election shows exactly where the house stands empty. Muslims are roughly one percent of Americans and only a few percent of Michigan. Mr. El-Sayed won about fifty-one percent of nearly 3.7 million votes. Muslims did not elect him. A nominally Christian electorate elected him: people who believe they are voting for tolerance, or lower prices, or against the other party, and who have been taught that every religion is the same at its heart, so that a man's god is none of their business. They do not realize they are bringing a foreign god into the camp and laying the offering of their votes at its feet. The God revealed in Jesus Christ is not Allah, and the culture that grows from the one is not the culture that grows from the other.

This is the naturopathic point I made in 2019, and the same one I made about Iran this month: it is a spiritual problem with a physical symptom. Islam's rise in the West is a symptom; the illness is our own lack of dedication to the God of the Bible. The war with Iran will be won or lost first in American hearts, and so will this. Suppressing the symptom will fail, and treating it as the disease will make us cruel without making us safe. The cure is at the root.

Consider the Hagia Sophia. For nine centuries it was the greatest church in Christendom. When it was made a mosque, its Christian mosaics were not destroyed; they were plastered over, and today they are curtained during prayer. Christ is still on the walls, covered. That is what is happening to our own heritage. It is not being erased. It is being covered by our forgetting, while another faith fills the space we have left empty. The seven churches of Revelation stood in what is now Muslim-majority Turkey, and the warning to the first of them is the warning to us:

*"Remember therefore from whence thou art fallen, and repent, and do the first works; or else I will come unto thee quickly, and will remove thy candlestick out of his place, except thou repent."* **(Revelation 2:5)**

*"How long halt ye between two opinions? if the LORD be God, follow him: but if Baal, then follow him."* **(1 Kings 18:21)**

*"Thou shalt have no other gods before me."* **(Exodus 20:3)**

## 7. A word to my fellow Christians

The Quran on Mr. El-Sayed's table on January 3 is not the threat. Our lukewarmness is. We have laid down the sword of truth on the altar of political correctness, and we have let the word *Islamophobe* close a subject that a free people must keep open.

So do three things. Tell the truth about the program, without fear and without hatred, and have your answer ready when you are called a bigot for it: *I do not hate Muslims; I oppose a political project to replace the Constitution with Sharia, and I will defend your right to pray while I oppose it.* Love your Muslim neighbor in person, at your table, with questions before arguments and Jesus before any discussion of Muhammad; he may never have met a Christian who loves him and can tell him what the cross means. Use your voice and your vote: ask your representatives to oppose any law that defines criticism of Islam as a hate offense, to grant asylum to apostates from Islam as a named class of the persecuted, and to treat the persecution of Christians in Nigeria and Syria as the emergency it is. And return, as a people, to the God who made us free.

*"If my people, which are called by my name, shall humble themselves, and pray, and seek my face, and turn from their wicked ways; then will I hear from heaven, and will forgive their sin, and will heal their land."* **(2 Chronicles 7:14)**

Repentance is the house; the laws are only the locks. And the mosaics never came down. They are waiting under the plaster for a people willing to uncover them.

Pray for Senator-elect El-Sayed. Pray that he serves Michigan honestly, that he protects its Jews and Christians and Muslims equally, that he answers the one question plainly, and that he comes to know the One whose yes is always yes. Then watch his record, as you should every senator's, and hold him to it.

*"Choose you this day whom ye will serve… but as for me and my house, we will serve the LORD."* **(Joshua 24:15)**
